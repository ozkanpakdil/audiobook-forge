# Emergency Wound and Trauma Care — A Practical Manual for First Responders and Health Professionals

This manual is for the person who arrives first and has to act: the lay first aider at a road crash, the trained first responder, the emergency medical technician or paramedic on an ambulance, the nurse in a district hospital, and the physician or surgeon who receives what they send. It follows the injured patient from the moment of the injury to the operating theatre, covering wounds and bleeding, penetrating and firearm trauma, injuries of the chest, fractures of every limb, burns, amputations, head, neck and spinal injury, and the complications that kill after the injury itself: shock, compartment syndrome, crush syndrome and infection.

The manual keeps three levels of practice apart, and says which one it is speaking to at every step. There is what the lay first aider may do with bare hands and whatever is in the car, what the trained responder may do with a kit, a protocol and authorization, and what belongs to definitive hospital and surgical care. The same wound asks something different of each pair of hands, and knowing what one must not attempt is as much a part of competence as knowing what to do.

The principle that governs every chapter is that the first responder's job is not to make the injury look normal. The priorities, in the order they must be met, are to keep the patient alive, to control life-threatening bleeding, to keep the airway open and the breathing working, to prevent further injury, to protect wounds and exposed tissue, to immobilize what must be immobilized, to recognize shock and the other lethal complications, to keep the patient warm, to watch continuously, and to reach definitive care as quickly and safely as possible.

The manual has forty-nine chapters in seventeen parts, followed by four appendices: the emergency algorithms spoken as sequences of actions, the practical skills laboratory with its simulation scenarios, a quick-reference card for the problems met most often, and the closing statement of the core principle and the limits of each scope of practice.

This is educational material. It does not replace training, certification, supervision, local protocol or the law of the country in which you work. It teaches decisions rather than prescriptions, and it gives no dose of any drug. Procedures such as the extraction of a bullet, the surgical exploration of a wound, the reduction of a fracture, internal or external fixation, the repair of a vessel, tendon or nerve, fasciotomy and deep debridement belong to definitive surgical care: they are described here so that a responder recognizes when they are needed, never as first-aid measures.
