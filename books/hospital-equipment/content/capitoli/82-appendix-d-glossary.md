# Appendix D — Glossary of Devices and Acronyms

This appendix is the spoken glossary and index of the book, arranged from A to Z so an unfamiliar device or short form can be found in order. Each device entry names the object, states what it is, and explains the physical principle that makes it work. A safety hazard, a common operator error, or a characteristic alarm is named where the device is described, with the operator check and the consumable or maintenance demand. Short-form entries expand the letters in full and explain the concept in plain words, so no entry sends the listener to another entry. No brand name, trademark, or model number appears in this guide, because a generic name stays useful while a product name belongs to a moment. Every setting, pressure, energy level, temperature, sterilization cycle, or alarm threshold named below must be confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use.

An alarm is any audible or visible signal that a device has crossed a limit, and alarm fatigue grows from limits set too wide or too narrow. Artificial intelligence, known by the short form AI, is software that finds patterns in data to support a decision, and in medicine it is a second reader rather than a replacement. An autoclave sterilizes with saturated steam under pressure, because moist heat above the boiling point destroys the proteins of every organism including spores. A common cycle runs at 134 degrees Celsius for 4 minutes, and that cycle must be confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use. Its characteristic alarm reports a temperature or pressure not reached, or air trapped in the chamber, and the common operator error is a load packed so tightly that steam cannot reach its center. An automated external defibrillator, known by the short form AED, reads the heart rhythm through two pads and shocks only a rhythm a shock can correct. A bilevel positive airway pressure device, known by the short form BIPAP, gives a higher pressure on inhalation and a lower pressure on exhalation to help a patient move air.

Calibration compares a device with a known standard and adjusts it, and it is the only evidence that a displayed number still means what it says. A cannula is a short tube placed into a vein or a cavity, and its flow depends far more on internal diameter than on length. A centrifuge spins a tube of blood until denser cells pack at the bottom and lighter fluid rises, and balancing the opposite tube prevents a shattered rotor. Cleaning removes visible soil with water, detergent, and friction, and a surface still dirty cannot be reliably disinfected or sterilized. A consumable is any item used once and discarded, and opening a sterile package before it is needed ruins it.

Cardiopulmonary resuscitation, known by the short form CPR, combines chest compressions with rescue breathing when the heart has stopped. A central sterile services department, known by the short form CSSD, is where used instruments are collected, cleaned, inspected, packed, sterilized, and returned. Computed tomography, known by the short form CT, builds cross-sectional images from many X-ray views, and a computer reconstructs the interior from the shadows. Continuous positive airway pressure, known by the short form CPAP, is a steady pressure during spontaneous breathing that keeps small airways and air sacs open, and the field variant uses a bubble chamber whose depth of water sets the pressure. Continuous renal replacement therapy, known by the short form CRRT, is slow continuous dialysis for a patient too unstable for intermittent treatment.

Decontamination is the first step of reprocessing, the removal of dangerous contamination so later steps can be done safely. A defibrillator stores energy in a capacitor and releases it through pads, and a common adult setting of 150 to 200 joules must be confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use. A dialysis machine is an artificial kidney that runs blood along one side of a membrane and warmed salt solution along the other. Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine, known by the short form DICOM, is the standard that lets one imaging device write pictures another system can read. A disinfectant destroys organisms on a surface, and a contact time shorter than the label states is the common failure, which must be confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use. A disposable is an item made for one use, and treating a single-use device as reusable is among the most dangerous economies. Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry, known by the short form DEXA, measures bone density by passing two X-ray energies through bone, and a mispositioned limb is the usual source of error.

An electrocardiogram, known by the short form ECG, records the electrical activity of the heart from skin electrodes, and each wave sums the currents of millions of muscle cells. An electrode is the conductor that carries those tiny currents, and a dried gel or loose contact turns a clean trace into noise. An electroencephalogram, known by the short form EEG, records the small electrical rhythms of the brain, and it demands a quiet, still patient. An electromyogram, known by the short form EMG, records activity inside a muscle, and it separates a damaged nerve from a damaged muscle. An electronic health record, known by the short form EHR, is the digital file holding a patient's history, orders, results, and notes. An endotracheal tube is a plastic tube passed through the vocal cords into the windpipe, and its position must be reconfirmed after every move of the patient. Ethylene oxide is a gas used to sterilize items that heat would destroy, and it needs a sealed chamber and a defined aeration time, both confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use.

A failure mode is the way a device stops doing its job, and naming the likely failure modes in advance turns a repair into a diagnosis. The United States Food and Drug Administration, known by the short form FDA, reviews medical devices before sale, and its clearance is a floor rather than a guarantee of local suitability. A gas cylinder holds compressed or liquefied medical gas at high pressure, and its hazard is the sudden release of that energy if the valve is damaged. The global positioning system, known by the short form GPS, is a constellation of satellites that lets a receiver calculate its position, and in a mobile clinic it records where a patient was seen. Gloves are the barrier that protects patient and wearer, and wearing one pair from patient to patient turns the gloves into a vehicle.

Hand hygiene is the most effective single infection measure in any hospital, and its failure is rarely ignorance but omission at the moment of contact. A hazard is any condition of the device, the environment, or the task that can cause harm, and it is managed by removing it, then guarding it, then warning of it. Health Level Seven, known by the short form HL7, is a family of standards for exchanging health information between computer systems. Heating, ventilation and air conditioning, known by the short form HVAC, moves air, controls its temperature and humidity, and in special rooms controls pressure and filtration.

An infusion pump moves fluid into a vein at a set rate, and its characteristic occlusion alarm means the line is blocked, so the patient is checked before the alarm is silenced. An instrument count tallies every sponge, needle, and instrument before and after an operation, and it is the last defense against a retained object. An intensive care unit, known by the short form ICU, is a ward for patients who need continuous observation and life support. The International Electrotechnical Commission, known by the short form IEC, writes the standards for electrical safety of medical equipment. The International Organization for Standardization, known by the short form ISO, writes the standards for quality management and for many device requirements.

A laryngoscope is a handle with a battery and a blade that lifts the tongue to bring the vocal cords into view, and a dead battery wastes the most critical seconds. A light-emitting diode, known by the short form LED, turns current into light with little heat, which is why it is used in headlamps and phototherapy units. A liquid crystal display, known by the short form LCD, forms an image by controlling light through liquid crystals, and it fades in extreme cold. Magnetic resonance imaging, known by the short form MRI, builds pictures from hydrogen nuclei in a strong magnetic field, and a field of 1.5 tesla or 3 tesla must be confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use. The hazard of MRI is the projectile, because a ferromagnetic object carried into the room becomes a missile, so every person is screened at the door. Maintenance is the planned and corrective work that keeps a device in specification, and it includes the daily operator check, the scheduled service, the record of both, and the spare parts on the shelf. Medecins Sans Frontieres, known by the short form MSF, is an international medical humanitarian organization that publishes technical guidance for difficult settings. A monitor displays body signals continuously and alarms when a limit is crossed, and trusting a number without looking at the patient is the common error.

A nasogastric tube is a soft tube passed through the nose into the stomach to drain or feed, and its position must be confirmed before every use, because fluid delivered into the lung can be fatal. A nebulizer turns a liquid medicine into a fine mist for inhalation, and the operator check is that the mist is actually visible. A needle is a hollow metal shaft with a bevel at its point, and its gauge and length decide what it can reach and how fast fluid can flow. An occlusion alarm is the signal from an infusion device that the line is blocked, and the correct sequence is to look at the patient first, then the tubing, then the device. An oxygen concentrator removes nitrogen from room air through a zeolite sieve, and its characteristic alarm is a low concentration warning that means the sieve is saturated or the compressor is failing. An oxygen cylinder is a portable store of compressed oxygen, and its checks are the pressure gauge, the washer at the valve, and the flow leaving the regulator. Oxygen saturation is the percentage of hemoglobin carrying oxygen, measured by a pulse oximeter on a fingertip, and a reading below 90 per cent is the usual alarm limit, which must be confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use.

Patient-controlled analgesia, known by the short form PCA, is a pump that lets a patient press a button for a small dose within limits set by the prescriber, and a patient who is falling asleep cannot press the button. A patient monitor gathers heart rhythm, oxygen saturation, blood pressure, breathing rate, and temperature into one display, and its value depends on the sensors attached to the patient. A peripherally inserted central catheter, known by the short form PICC, is a long fine tube threaded into an arm vein until its tip sits near the heart. Personal protective equipment, known by the short form PPE, is the barrier set of gloves, gown, mask, eye protection, and apron. Picture archiving and communication system, known by the short form PACS, is the digital store and viewing network for medical images, so an image can be seen anywhere without a film being lost.

A pipette is a narrow tube that measures a small volume of liquid, and one read at the wrong level delivers the wrong volume. Polytetrafluoroethylene, known by the short form PTFE, is a smooth, chemically inert plastic used for catheters and needle coatings. Polyvinyl chloride, known by the short form PVC, is the soft, clear plastic of most intravenous tubing and blood bags. Positron emission tomography, known by the short form PET, detects the gamma rays released when a radiotracer decays, and the tracer follows a molecule the tissue uses, so active tissue lights up. A pressure cooker is the field sterilizer, a sealed pot that raises the boiling point of water so hotter steam reaches the instruments, and it demands a timer and a full drying period, with the cycle confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use.

Quality control runs a known sample through a device and confirms the expected result, and it is the only way an operator learns that a machine has drifted. Radiation is energy traveling as waves or particles, and the hazard is that it damages living cells, which is why distance, shielding, and time are the three protections. Radio frequency identification, known by the short form RFID, is a tag that answers a reader with a radio signal, and in a hospital it tracks equipment and confirms that an instrument has passed through a sterilization cycle. Radiography makes a shadow image by passing X-rays through the body onto a detector, and its field variant is a battery-powered source with a cassette developed by hand. A rapid diagnostic test is a single-use strip that detects a marker from a drop of blood or a swab in minutes. Reprocessing is the sequence of cleaning, disinfection, inspection, packing, and sterilization applied to a reusable device, and every step is wasted if the one before it was skipped. A rinse removes chemical residue, and a rinse in dirty water recontaminates the instrument just cleaned.

A sharps container is a rigid, puncture-resistant box for used needles and broken glass, and it must be replaced before it is three quarters full. A sphygmomanometer is the blood pressure instrument itself, whether a mercury column, an aneroid gauge, or an electronic device reading an oscillating cuff. Sterilization destroys all microbial life including spores, and the families met most often are steam under pressure, dry heat, chemical liquid or vapor, and ionizing radiation, with the chosen cycle confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use. A subscriber identity module, known by the short form SIM, is the small card that identifies a phone on a mobile network. Suction is a negative pressure that removes fluid, blood, or secretions from an airway or a wound, and its characteristic failure is a blocked catheter or a full canister. A syringe is a barrel and a plunger that measure and deliver a volume, and the error that causes the most harm is reading the scale from the wrong side of the plunger.

A thermometer converts temperature into a readable number, and the operator check is the site and the dwell time, because oral, rectal, axillary, and infrared readings are not interchangeable. A tourniquet is a band that stops venous return so a vein fills, and leaving it tight for minutes changes the chemistry of the blood behind it. A transducer converts one form of energy into another, so a pressure transducer turns fluid pressure into an electrical signal. A transfusion set is tubing with a filter that removes clots from blood, and its checks are the compatibility of the unit and the identity of the patient at the bedside. A tube is any hollow cylinder that carries something from one place to another, and its internal diameter sets its resistance to flow.

Ultrahigh frequency, known by the short form UHF, is a band of radio waves that penetrates buildings well and is used for short-range two-way radio. Ultrasound is sound above the range of human hearing, and a diagnostic machine builds a picture from the echoes returning from each boundary between tissues. An uninterruptible power supply, known by the short form UPS, is a battery and an inverter that carries a critical device through the seconds before a generator starts, and its battery must be tested on a schedule because it fails silently. A universal serial bus, known by the short form USB, is the common connector that lets a device exchange data and take power. A urinary catheter with an inflatable balloon drains the bladder, and the error that causes the most harm is inflating the balloon before confirming that the tip is in the bladder. A vacuum is a pressure below atmospheric pressure, piped to the wall to drive suction, so the operator checks the suction at the patient end.

A ventilator moves gas into and out of the lungs, and the distinction is between a pressure-controlled breath, in which the machine sets a pressure and the volume follows, and a volume-controlled breath, in which the volume is set and the pressure follows, with every pressure and volume limit confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use. Very high frequency, known by the short form VHF, is a band of radio waves that travels farther over open ground than UHF and is used for longer-range communication. A vital signs monitor is the small bedside unit that gathers the basic numbers, and it is only as good as the sensor on the patient. A warmer raises the temperature of a patient or a fluid, and every warmer needs a temperature limit and an alarm because the harm it can do is a burn, with every temperature setting confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use. A wheelchair is a mobility device whose safety depends on brakes and a seat that fits. A wound dressing keeps a wound moist, clean, and protected, and its failure modes are a dressing too tight, too loose, or left in place until the wound beneath has become infected. A wristband is the identity tag that links a patient to every sample, medicine, and record, and its checks are legibility and the correct patient.

To summarize, this glossary is the book in miniature, because every device here is a physical principle wrapped in a habit, a check, and a consumable. The listener who can say what a device does, why it works, what it looks like when it fails, and what must be confirmed before use already has most of what safe practice requires. The short forms and the device entries sit in one alphabetical place so that an unfamiliar string of letters or an unfamiliar object becomes a word with a meaning. Heard in sequence or opened in fragments, the appendix is meant to close the guide the way a good index opens a reference, by making the whole of it findable. Every number met anywhere in this book, whether a setting, a pressure, an energy level, a temperature, a sterilization cycle, or an alarm threshold, must be confirmed against the manufacturer's instructions and the local protocol before use.
