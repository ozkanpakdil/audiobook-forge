# Chapter 112 — Cardiovascular Anatomy

This chapter works through the clinical anatomy of the heart, the great vessels and the thoracic cavity, taking the listener from the chambers and valves to the arteries and veins of the trunk and limbs, and then through the chest wall, the pleura, the lungs, the mediastinum and the diaphragm. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is correct, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The items are written to be answered out loud. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 11101. Which chamber of the heart receives oxygenated blood returning from the lungs? The answer is the left atrium. The four pulmonary veins open into it, two from each lung. Blood then passes through the mitral valve into the left ventricle, which pumps it into the aorta and around the body.

Question 11102. Which vessel carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs: the aorta, the pulmonary trunk, a pulmonary vein, or the superior vena cava? The answer is the pulmonary trunk. It divides into the right and left pulmonary arteries, one for each lung. The pulmonary veins are the exception, carrying oxygenated blood back to the heart.

Question 11103. Which chamber of the heart has the thickest muscular wall? The answer is the left ventricle. It drives blood through the whole body, so it works against a much higher pressure than the right ventricle does. Its wall is about three times as thick as the wall of the right ventricle, which pumps only to the lungs.

Question 11104. The tricuspid valve guards the opening between which two chambers: the right atrium and the right ventricle, the left atrium and the left ventricle, the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk, or the left ventricle and the aorta? The answer is the right atrium and the right ventricle. Its three cusps are anchored by chordae tendineae to the papillary muscles.

Question 11105. Which chamber of the heart lies immediately behind the body of the sternum and the costal cartilages of the third to fifth ribs? The answer is the right ventricle. It forms most of the anterior surface of the heart, so a needle crossing the lower sternum reaches it first. The left ventricle lies behind it.

Question 11106. Which heart valve has only two cusps and guards the left atrioventricular opening? The answer is the mitral valve, also called the bicuspid valve. Its two cusps are named anterior and posterior, and they are tethered by chordae tendineae. It opens when the left atrium contracts and closes when the left ventricle contracts.

Question 11107. A described chest radiograph shows the heart as a shadow about one third to one half of the width of the chest, with its long axis pointing downward and to the left. Which chamber forms the lower left border of that shadow? The answer is the left ventricle. The right border of the shadow is formed by the right atrium.

Question 11108. A constructed illustration: a person has sudden central chest pain and injury of the front of the left ventricle. Which artery, running between the ventricles, is blocked? The answer is the anterior interventricular branch of the left coronary artery. It supplies the front of both ventricles and the front of the septum. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 11109. In most people, which artery supplies the posterior part of the interventricular septum and the back of the left ventricle? The answer is the right coronary artery, through its posterior interventricular branch. It also supplies the right atrium, most of the right ventricle, the sinoatrial node and the atrioventricular node. A left dominant circulation is a normal variant.

Question 11110. Which small group of specialized muscle cells in the wall of the right atrium sets the resting rhythm of the heart? The answer is the sinoatrial node. It lies at the junction of the superior vena cava and the right atrium. It fires faster than any other part of the conduction system, so it acts as the pacemaker.

Question 11111. True or false: the pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood toward the heart. The answer is true. They return blood that has been oxygenated in the lungs to the left atrium. They are the only veins in the body that carry oxygenated blood, just as the pulmonary arteries are the only arteries that carry deoxygenated blood.

Question 11112. Which structure lies immediately behind the left atrium and can be pressed on when that chamber enlarges: the esophagus, the trachea, the thymus, or the right main bronchus? The answer is the esophagus. It descends in the posterior mediastinum in contact with the back of the left atrium. An enlarged left atrium can press on it and make swallowing difficult.

Question 11113. Which artery supplies the sinoatrial node in about three fifths of people? The answer is the right coronary artery. It reaches the node through a small branch that circles the junction of the superior vena cava and the right atrium. In the remaining people the node is supplied by the left coronary artery, which is a normal variation.

Question 11114. Between which two structures does the atrioventricular node lie? The answer is the interatrial septum and the opening of the coronary sinus. It sits in the lower part of the interatrial septum, just above the attachment of the septal cusp of the tricuspid valve. The atrioventricular bundle leaves it and runs into the interventricular septum.

Question 11115. A described dissection shows a fibrous ring around each valve, the four rings fused into a single plate. What is that plate called, and what does it separate? The answer is the fibrous skeleton, which separates the atria from the ventricles. It anchors the valve cusps and insulates the atrial muscle from the ventricular muscle, so the two contract in sequence.

Question 11116. Where on the chest wall is the sound of the mitral valve best heard? The answer is the apex of the heart, in the fifth intercostal space just medial to the midclavicular line. The valve lies behind the sternum, but its sound is carried to the apex. The aortic valve is heard at the right edge of the sternum instead.

Question 11117. Which great vessel lies behind the pulmonary trunk and slightly to its right as both leave the heart? The answer is the ascending aorta. The pulmonary trunk arises in front of it and to its left. The aorta then arches backward over the left lung.

Question 11118. What is the name of the double-layered sac that encloses the heart and the roots of its great vessels? The answer is the pericardium. Its outer fibrous layer is tough and its inner serous layer is smooth. Between the two serous layers lies a thin film of fluid that lets the heart beat without friction.

Question 11119. A constructed illustration: fluid collects in the pericardial sac and compresses the heart, and a needle is passed below the xiphisternum. Which chamber, lying behind the lower sternum, is most at risk? The answer is the right ventricle, the most anterior chamber. The needle is angled toward the left shoulder. The procedure belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 11120. In a described transverse section through the chest, the right ventricle lies in front and the left ventricle behind. Which surface of the heart faces the diaphragm? The answer is the inferior surface, formed mainly by the two ventricles. It rests on the central tendon of the diaphragm, and the long axis runs downward, forward and to the left.

Question 11121. Which branch of the aortic arch arises first, on the right: the left common carotid artery, the brachiocephalic trunk, the left subclavian artery, or the left vertebral artery? The answer is the brachiocephalic trunk. It is the first and largest branch of the arch. The arch then gives off the left common carotid and the left subclavian arteries in turn.

Question 11122. Which is the first and largest branch of the aortic arch? The answer is the brachiocephalic trunk. It arises from the highest point of the arch and divides behind the right sternoclavicular joint. Its two branches are the right common carotid artery and the right subclavian artery, and the left side has no such trunk.

Question 11123. A described computed tomography section of the upper chest shows a vessel that arches backward over the left main bronchus and then descends beside the vertebral column. Which vessel is shown? The answer is the aortic arch, which continues as the descending thoracic aorta. The arch passes over the root of the left lung, and the descending part lies to the left of the vertebral bodies.

Question 11124. A described narrowing of the aorta lies just distal to the left subclavian artery. Which structure marks that site: the azygos vein, the left main bronchus, the ligamentum arteriosum, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the ligamentum arteriosum. It is the fibrous remnant of the ductus arteriosus, which joined the pulmonary trunk to the aorta there. The narrowing lies opposite it.

Question 11125. Which fibrous band connects the left pulmonary artery to the underside of the aortic arch? The answer is the ligamentum arteriosum. In the fetus it was the ductus arteriosus, which carried blood from the pulmonary trunk into the aorta and so past the lungs. After birth it closes and becomes a fibrous cord.

Question 11126. Which fetal vessel becomes the ligamentum arteriosum after birth? The answer is the ductus arteriosus. It shunted blood away from the fetal lungs, which were not yet working. Its closure in the first days of life is one of the changes that separate the pulmonary circulation from the systemic one.

Question 11127. True or false: the coronary arteries arise from the pulmonary trunk. The answer is false. They arise from the left and right aortic sinuses at the root of the aorta, just above the aortic valve. Blood enters them during diastole, when the aortic valve is closed and the sinuses fill.

Question 11128. Into which chamber of the heart does the coronary sinus empty? The answer is the right atrium. It opens just above and medial to the opening of the inferior vena cava, beside the valve of the inferior vena cava. It collects most of the venous blood returning from the wall of the heart.

Question 11129. A constructed illustration: a catheter is passed into a large vein at the root of the neck, where two veins join to form a single trunk. Which two veins join there? The answer is the internal jugular vein and the subclavian vein, which form the brachiocephalic vein. The right one is short and vertical, while the left crosses the midline behind the manubrium.

Question 11130. Which two veins unite to form the superior vena cava? The answer is the right and left brachiocephalic veins. They join behind the lower border of the first right costal cartilage. The superior vena cava then descends to empty into the right atrium, and the azygos vein joins it just above the heart.

Question 11131. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior interventricular branch with the front of the ventricles, the circumflex branch with the right ventricle, the right coronary artery with the left atrium, or the posterior interventricular branch with the left ventricle? The answer is the anterior interventricular branch with the front of the ventricles. It runs between the ventricles and supplies both of them.

Question 11132. Which coronary artery runs in the groove between the right and left ventricles on the front of the heart? The answer is the anterior interventricular branch of the left coronary artery. It descends toward the apex and turns around it to meet the posterior interventricular branch. The circumflex branch runs in the coronary sulcus on the left side instead.

Question 11133. A described angiogram of the lower limb shows a vessel that passes through an opening in the adductor magnus muscle to reach the back of the knee. Which artery is shown? The answer is the femoral artery, which becomes the popliteal artery as it passes through the adductor hiatus. Before that it lies in the adductor canal, deep to the sartorius muscle.

Question 11134. On the left side of the neck, which structure lies immediately lateral to the trachea and medial to the internal jugular vein? The answer is the left common carotid artery. It ascends in the carotid sheath alongside the vagus nerve, which lies between it and the internal jugular vein. The thyroid gland overlaps all three from the front.

Question 11135. Name the two arteries that arise from the left coronary artery, spoken as a list. The answer is the anterior interventricular branch and the circumflex branch. The anterior interventricular branch runs down the front of the heart between the ventricles, and the circumflex branch follows the coronary sulcus around the left side.

Question 11136. Which artery is felt as a pulse on the lateral side of the front of the wrist, just above the base of the thumb? The answer is the radial artery. It lies against the lower end of the radius, covered only by skin and fascia. The ulnar artery lies on the other side of the wrist and is deeper.

Question 11137. The femoral artery is the continuation of which vessel below the inguinal ligament? The answer is the external iliac artery. It enters the thigh at the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, where its pulse can be felt. The internal iliac artery stays in the pelvis and supplies its walls and organs.

Question 11138. A constructed illustration: a knee is dislocated backward, the pulse at the ankle is lost, and the foot is pale. Which artery, lying deepest in the popliteal fossa, is most likely injured? The answer is the popliteal artery. The popliteal vein and the tibial nerve lie superficial to it. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 11139. A described dissection of the thigh shows a triangle bounded above by the inguinal ligament, laterally by the sartorius and medially by the adductor longus. Which structure lies in the middle of that triangle? The answer is the femoral artery. The femoral vein lies medial to it and the femoral nerve lateral to it.

Question 11140. Which two arteries form the anastomosis around the scapula that can keep the arm supplied when the subclavian artery is blocked? The answer is the suprascapular artery and the circumflex scapular artery. The suprascapular artery comes from the thyrocervical trunk and the circumflex scapular artery from the subscapular artery. They meet on the back of the scapula.

Question 11141. True or false: an arterial anastomosis around a joint can keep blood flowing to the distal limb when the main artery is blocked. The answer is true, because the branches that meet around the joint provide an alternative route. The anastomoses around the scapula and the elbow are the classic examples, though the flow may be enough only for survival.

Question 11142. Within the femoral triangle, how does the femoral vein lie relative to the femoral artery? The answer is immediately medial to it. The artery lies lateral and the vein medial, with the femoral nerve further lateral still. This order is remembered as nerve, artery and vein from lateral to medial.

Question 11143. Which vein of the lower limb is the longest in the body and runs up the medial side of the leg and thigh? The answer is the great saphenous vein. It begins at the medial end of the dorsal venous arch of the foot and ends in the femoral vein below the inguinal ligament, joined by many tributaries along the way.

Question 11144. Which two veins unite behind the neck of the pancreas to form the portal vein? The answer is the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein. The portal vein carries blood from the gut to the liver. The inferior mesenteric vein usually joins the splenic vein rather than the portal vein itself.

Question 11145. A described transverse section through the chest shows three structures in front of the vertebral bodies: a vessel on the right, a vessel on the left, and a thin-walled duct between them. Which duct is it, and where does it end? The answer is the thoracic duct, which ends at the junction of the left subclavian and internal jugular veins.

Question 11146. A constructed illustration: a young athlete collapses during exertion, and a coronary artery arises from the wrong aortic sinus and passes between the great vessels. Which artery is most often involved? The answer is the left coronary artery arising from the right aortic sinus. Its course between them lets it be squeezed when the aorta expands. The right coronary artery can do the same, but less often.

Question 11147. Which of these pairs belongs together: the subclavian artery with the arm, the common carotid artery with the leg, the renal artery with the lung, or the coronary artery with the diaphragm? The answer is the subclavian artery with the arm. It continues as the axillary artery after crossing the first rib and then as the brachial artery.

Question 11148. Name the three veins that empty into the right atrium, spoken as a list. The answer is the superior vena cava, the inferior vena cava and the coronary sinus. The first two return blood from above and below the diaphragm. The coronary sinus returns most of the blood that has passed through the wall of the heart.

Question 11149. A described transverse section through the upper chest shows the trachea dividing into two tubes, with a large vessel lying above the division. Which structure is dividing, and what are the two tubes? The answer is the trachea, dividing into the right and left main bronchi. The right main bronchus is wider and more vertical, and the arch of the aorta lies above it.

Question 11150. In a left dominant coronary circulation, which artery gives off the posterior interventricular branch? The answer is the left coronary artery, through its circumflex branch. It then supplies the back of both ventricles and the posterior part of the interventricular septum. In most people the right coronary artery is dominant instead, and the pattern is a normal variation.

Question 11151. Which bone forms the front of the thoracic cage, between the clavicles above and the xiphoid process below? The answer is the sternum. Its upper part is the manubrium, its middle part is the body, and its pointed lower end is the xiphoid process. The manubrium joins the body at the sternal angle.

Question 11152. Which statement about the ribs is correct: 7 pairs reach the sternum directly, 12 pairs reach it directly, 5 pairs reach it directly, or 3 pairs are floating? The answer is that 7 pairs reach the sternum directly. The eighth, ninth and tenth pairs join the costal cartilages above them. The eleventh and twelfth pairs are free at the front.

Question 11153. Which ribs are described as floating, and where do their front ends lie? The answer is the eleventh and twelfth ribs, whose front ends lie free in the muscles of the abdominal wall. No costal cartilage joins them to the sternum. They lie over the upper part of the kidney behind them.

Question 11154. Which structure lies at the level of the sternal angle: the first costal cartilage, the second costal cartilage, the xiphoid process, or the twelfth thoracic vertebra? The answer is the second costal cartilage. The same level marks the fourth thoracic vertebra, the bifurcation of the trachea, and the upper border of the arch of the aorta.

Question 11155. Which structure lies immediately below the intercostal artery in the costal groove of a rib? The answer is the intercostal nerve. From above downward the groove holds the intercostal vein, then the artery, then the nerve. The three run between the internal and the innermost layers of the intercostal muscles.

Question 11156. What is the name of the dome-shaped muscle that separates the chest from the abdomen? The answer is the diaphragm. It has a right dome and a left dome, joined in the middle by a central tendon. Three openings pierce it, one for the inferior vena cava, one for the esophagus and one for the aorta.

Question 11157. A described radiograph of the chest shows a dome-shaped shadow on each side, higher on the right, with a sharp angle where it meets the chest wall. Which structures are shown? The answer is the two halves of the diaphragm. The right dome is higher because the liver lies beneath it, and the sharp angle is the costophrenic angle.

Question 11158. A constructed illustration: a tube is placed into the chest through the fifth intercostal space behind the midaxillary line, passing over the upper border of the sixth rib. Which structures is that route avoiding? The answer is the intercostal nerve, artery and vein of the fifth rib, which run along its lower border. The needle passes above the rib below to stay clear of them.

Question 11159. Which of these structures lies within the mediastinum: the left lung, the thoracic duct, the first rib, or the pectoralis major muscle? The answer is the thoracic duct. It ascends through the posterior mediastinum in front of the vertebral bodies. The lungs lie in the pleural cavities on either side, and the first rib and the pectoralis major lie outside the mediastinum.

Question 11160. What is the name of the membrane that lines the inside of the thoracic cavity and folds over the surface of the lung? The answer is the pleura. Its parietal layer lines the chest wall, the diaphragm and the mediastinum, and its visceral layer covers the lung. A thin film of fluid between them lets the lung slide as it expands.

Question 11161. True or false: the right lung has three lobes and the left lung has two. The answer is true. The right lung is divided by an oblique fissure and a horizontal fissure into upper, middle and lower lobes. The left lung is divided by the oblique fissure alone and has a cardiac notch.

Question 11162. Which large vein crosses the midline behind the manubrium, in front of the great vessels? The answer is the left brachiocephalic vein. It runs from the left side of the root of the neck across to join the right brachiocephalic vein. In a child the thymus lies in front of it. The two veins unite to form the superior vena cava.

Question 11163. The descending thoracic aorta comes to lie beside which structure, and on which side? The answer is the vertebral column, on its left side. It begins at the fourth thoracic vertebra and descends to the diaphragm. The esophagus lies in front of it and crosses to its right as it descends.

Question 11164. Which main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical, so that an inhaled object is more likely to enter it? The answer is the right main bronchus. It gives off its upper lobe branch above the pulmonary artery. The left main bronchus is longer and more horizontal, and it passes below the aortic arch.

Question 11165. A described radiograph of the chest shows a curved shadow filling the lowest part of one side of the chest, with the lung edge above it and a blunt costophrenic angle. Which space has filled with fluid? The answer is the pleural cavity, at its lowest point, the costodiaphragmatic recess. That recess dips below the lung, and fluid collects there first.

Question 11166. Which of these structures lies in the posterior mediastinum: the thymus, the thoracic duct, the phrenic nerve, or the left brachiocephalic vein? The answer is the thoracic duct. The thymus lies in the anterior mediastinum, the phrenic nerve runs in the middle mediastinum in front of the root of the lung, and the left brachiocephalic vein lies in the superior mediastinum.

Question 11167. Which structure lies behind the manubrium of the sternum in a young child, in front of the great vessels? The answer is the thymus. It lies in the anterior mediastinum and is largest in childhood. It shrinks with age and is replaced by fat, so in an adult it is barely visible.

Question 11168. Which nerve runs in front of the root of the lung and supplies the diaphragm? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It arises from the third, fourth and fifth cervical spinal nerves. It descends through the mediastinum, crosses in front of the root of the lung, and reaches the diaphragm from above.

Question 11169. A constructed illustration: a growth at the top of a lung presses on the chain of ganglia against the upper ribs, and the person has a drooping eyelid and a small pupil on that side. Which chain is compressed? The answer is the sympathetic trunk, which lies behind the parietal pleura and carries fibers to the head and the upper limb.

Question 11170. Which structure passes through the diaphragm at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra: the aorta, the esophagus, the inferior vena cava, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the esophagus. The inferior vena cava passes through the central tendon at the eighth thoracic vertebra, and the aorta passes behind the diaphragm at the twelfth. The vagus nerves accompany the esophagus.

Question 11171. A described computed tomography section at the level of the carina shows the two main bronchi separated by a ridge of tissue, with a tube lying immediately behind them. Which ridge is described? The answer is the carina, with the esophagus immediately behind it. The carina is the ridge at the last ring of the trachea.

Question 11172. Which structures lie between the internal and the innermost layers of the intercostal muscles? The answer is the intercostal nerve, artery and vein. They run in the costal groove along the lower border of the rib above. Their order from above downward is vein, artery and nerve. The innermost layer separates them from the pleura.

Question 11173. Which of these pairs belongs together: the thoracic duct with the left venous angle, the thoracic duct with the right venous angle, the right lymphatic duct with the left venous angle, or the azygos vein with the left atrium? The answer is the thoracic duct with the left venous angle, at the junction of the left subclavian and internal jugular veins. The right lymphatic duct ends at the right venous angle.

Question 11174. Which vein runs up the right side of the posterior mediastinum and arches over the root of the right lung? The answer is the azygos vein. It drains the posterior intercostal veins of the right side and the hemiazygos vein from the left. It ends in the superior vena cava.

Question 11175. Which structure lies highest in the root of the right lung: the pulmonary artery, the upper lobe bronchus, the pulmonary veins, or the bronchial arteries? The answer is the upper lobe bronchus, also called the eparterial bronchus because it lies above the pulmonary artery. Below the artery runs the bronchus to the middle and lower lobes, and the pulmonary veins lie lowest and in front.

Question 11176. A constructed illustration: a growth in the upper mediastinum causes a swollen face and distended veins over the chest wall, worst when the person lies flat. Which large vein, lying to the right of the midline behind the sternum, is compressed? The answer is the superior vena cava. It carries blood from the head, neck and upper limbs to the right atrium.

Question 11177. A described radiograph is taken with the beam passing from the front to the back, and the heart shadow looks wider than usual. Why does the heart look enlarged in that view? The answer is that the heart lies farther from the film, so its shadow is magnified. In the standard view the heart lies close to the film.

Question 11178. In the lower part of the posterior mediastinum, which structure lies in front of the descending thoracic aorta and slightly to its right? The answer is the esophagus. It descends in front of the aorta and then passes through the diaphragm with the vagus nerves. The thoracic duct lies behind the esophagus, between the aorta and the azygos vein.

Question 11179. Which of these structures is not found in the middle mediastinum: the heart, the phrenic nerve, the thoracic duct, or the ascending aorta? The answer is the thoracic duct, which lies in the posterior mediastinum. The middle mediastinum holds the pericardium, the heart, the ascending aorta, the pulmonary trunk, the phrenic nerves and the roots of the lungs.

Question 11180. Which muscle forms the front wall of the axilla and the anterior fold of the armpit? The answer is the pectoralis major. It arises from the clavicle, the sternum and the upper costal cartilages and inserts on the humerus. It adducts and medially rotates the arm, and its lower border forms the front fold of the armpit.

Question 11181. Which of these pairs belongs together: the phrenic nerve with the diaphragm, the vagus nerve with the diaphragm, the sympathetic trunk with the larynx, or the intercostal nerve with the pelvis? The answer is the phrenic nerve with the diaphragm. It carries fibers from the third, fourth and fifth cervical spinal nerves, while the vagus nerve supplies the heart, the lungs and the gut.

Question 11182. Name the three openings of the diaphragm, spoken as a list. The answer is the opening for the inferior vena cava, the opening for the esophagus and the opening for the aorta. The inferior vena cava passes through the central tendon at the eighth thoracic vertebra, the esophagus at the tenth, and the aorta behind the diaphragm at the twelfth.

Question 11183. Which statement about the opening for the inferior vena cava is correct: it lies in the central tendon, it lies between the crura, it lies behind the diaphragm, or it lies in the left muscular part? The answer is that it lies in the central tendon. It is the only opening in the central tendon, at the eighth thoracic vertebra. The aorta passes between the crura instead.

Question 11184. Which bone forms the upper part of the sternum, above the sternal angle? The answer is the manubrium. It joins the body of the sternum at the sternal angle. Its upper border carries the jugular notch, which can be felt at the base of the neck, and its sides join the clavicles and the first costal cartilages.

Question 11185. A described section of the chest shows the pleural cavity on each side separated from the mediastinum by a membrane that reflects over the root of the lung. Which membrane is described? The answer is the parietal pleura, which becomes continuous with the visceral pleura at the root of the lung. Below the root the two layers hang down as the pulmonary ligament.

Question 11186. Which nerve hooks around the aortic arch just behind the ligamentum arteriosum? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve. It arises from the left vagus nerve beside the arch and turns upward beneath it. It ascends in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus to reach the larynx. The right one hooks around the right subclavian artery.

Question 11187. Which statement about the pleura is correct: the parietal pleura is sensitive and the visceral pleura is not, both layers are equally sensitive, the visceral pleura alone is sensitive, or neither layer is sensitive? The answer is that the parietal pleura is sensitive and the visceral pleura is not. It is supplied by the intercostal and phrenic nerves, and the visceral layer has no pain fibers.

Question 11188. A constructed illustration: fluid in the chest is drained through the seventh intercostal space, with the needle passed just above the eighth rib. Why is it passed above that rib? The answer is that the intercostal nerve, artery and vein run along the lower border of the rib above the space. Passing above the rib below keeps the needle clear of them.

Question 11189. True or false: the parietal pleura covers the surface of the lung. The answer is false. The visceral pleura covers the surface of the lung and dips into the fissures. The parietal pleura lines the inside of the chest wall, the diaphragm and the mediastinum. The two layers are continuous at the root of the lung.

Question 11190. Which structures bound the superior thoracic aperture, the opening at the top of the chest? The answer is the first thoracic vertebra, the first ribs and the upper border of the manubrium. The opening is smaller than the lower opening of the chest. The trachea, the esophagus, the great vessels and the nerves of the upper limb all pass through it.

Question 11191. A described section through the lower chest shows two muscular pillars descending from the vertebral bodies and arching over the aorta. Which structures are shown? The answer is the right and left crura of the diaphragm, which bound the aortic opening behind the diaphragm. The right crus is the larger, and the aorta passes between them at the twelfth thoracic vertebra.

Question 11192. A described dissection shows an artery descending just lateral to the sternum, deep to the costal cartilages, with a nerve lateral to it. Which artery and which nerve are shown? The answer is the internal thoracic artery and the phrenic nerve. The artery arises from the subclavian artery, and the nerve crosses in front of the root of the lung.

Question 11193. Which bone of the thoracic cage carries a groove on its upper surface for the subclavian artery? The answer is the first rib. It is the shortest and most curved of the ribs, and it lies almost horizontally. The subclavian vessels and the lower trunk of the brachial plexus cross over it.

Question 11194. Which structure separates the first part of the subclavian artery from its third part: the clavicle, the anterior scalene muscle, the first rib, or the sternocleidomastoid? The answer is the anterior scalene muscle. The first part of the artery lies medial to it and the third part lateral to it. The trunks of the brachial plexus lie behind the artery.

Question 11195. Which of these pairs belongs together: the internal thoracic artery with the chest wall, the thoracic duct with the right atrium, the phrenic nerve with the abdominal wall, or the intercostal artery with the pleural cavity? The answer is the internal thoracic artery with the chest wall. It descends behind the costal cartilages and ends as the superior epigastric and musculophrenic arteries.

Question 11196. Which of these structures lies most posterior in the superior mediastinum: the trachea, the esophagus, the arch of the aorta, or the left brachiocephalic vein? The answer is the esophagus, which lies against the vertebral column. In front of it lie the trachea and the great vessels, and the thoracic duct ascends behind it.

Question 11197. A described computed tomography section through the lower chest shows one large vessel piercing the central tendon of the diaphragm and another passing behind it between the two crura. Which two vessels are shown? The answer is the inferior vena cava and the aorta. The esophagus passes through the muscular part at a level between them, so each opening lies at a different vertebral level.

Question 11198. A constructed illustration: sudden chest pain begins in the front of the chest and moves to the back between the shoulder blades. Which part of the aorta lies behind the heart and corresponds to the second site of pain? The answer is the descending thoracic aorta, which begins at the fourth thoracic vertebra and runs down to the diaphragm. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 11199. Which statement about the azygos vein is correct: it arches over the right lung root and ends in the superior vena cava, it arches over the left lung root, it ends in the left brachiocephalic vein, or it lies in the anterior mediastinum? The answer is that it arches over the right lung root and ends in the superior vena cava. It drains the posterior intercostal veins of the right side.

Question 11200. Below the fourth thoracic vertebra the thoracic duct lies on the right of the midline, and above it lies on the left. Which structure does it cross behind as it changes sides? The answer is the esophagus. It crosses behind it at about the fourth thoracic vertebra, then ascends on the left, so its position depends on the level of the section.

To summarize, the right atrium receives the superior vena cava, the inferior vena cava and the coronary sinus, while the left atrium receives the four pulmonary veins, and the left ventricle carries the thickest wall because it drives blood through the whole body. The coronary arteries arise from the aortic sinuses, the right coronary artery usually supplies the sinoatrial and atrioventricular nodes, and the anterior interventricular branch of the left coronary artery supplies the front of the ventricles and the front of the septum. The great vessels hold fixed relations, with the pulmonary trunk in front of the ascending aorta, the brachiocephalic trunk as the first branch of the arch, and the ligamentum arteriosum joining the left pulmonary artery to the arch. In the chest, the second costal cartilage marks the sternal angle, the intercostal bundle runs in the costal groove of the rib above the space, and the diaphragm opens for the inferior vena cava, the esophagus and the aorta. The parietal pleura is sensitive to pain while the visceral pleura is not, the right main bronchus is wider and more vertical than the left, and the thoracic duct crosses behind the esophagus before ending at the left venous angle.
