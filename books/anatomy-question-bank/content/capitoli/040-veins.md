# Chapter 40 — Veins

Veins return blood to the heart under low pressure, and the great veins that drain the body are mapped as carefully as the arteries beside them. This chapter follows that system in order: the superior vena cava, the inferior vena cava with its abdominal tributaries, the portal circulation to the liver, and the jugular and subclavian veins in the neck. It holds one hundred questions, and every question states its answer at once and explains why it is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3901. Which large vein is formed behind the first right costal cartilage and returns blood from the head, the neck, the upper limbs and the chest: the superior vena cava, the inferior vena cava, the portal vein, or the azygos vein? The answer is the superior vena cava. It is formed by the union of the two brachiocephalic veins and ends in the upper part of the right atrium. The azygos vein joins its back.

Question 3902. Name the chamber of the heart that the superior vena cava enters, and give the level of its opening. The answer is the right atrium, at the third right costal cartilage. The opening lies in the upper and posterior part of the chamber, and the blood then passes through the tricuspid valve.

Question 3903. A described radiograph of the chest, taken with the person lying flat, shows a vertical band forming the right border of the upper mediastinum, with the trachea to its left. Which structure forms that band, and why does it widen when the heart fails? The answer is the superior vena cava, which forms that border in the supine position and widens easily because its wall is thin and it has no valve.

Question 3904. The superior vena cava is about how long: 3 centimeters, 7 centimeters, 12 centimeters, or 17 centimeters? The answer is about 7 centimeters. Half of that length lies in the superior mediastinum and half in the middle mediastinum. The vein runs from the first right costal cartilage to the third.

Question 3905. Which vein arches forward over the root of the right lung and ends in the back of the superior vena cava? The answer is the azygos vein. It drains the posterior chest wall and the posterior intercostal spaces on both sides. It ends at the second right costal cartilage.

Question 3906. Which structure lies immediately behind the superior vena cava in the upper chest, and which nerve runs down its lateral side? The answer is the right main bronchus, with the right phrenic nerve beside it. The phrenic nerve passes in front of the root of the right lung, and the vagus nerve lies further back.

Question 3907. Which statement about the superior vena cava is correct: it lies to the left of the ascending aorta, it crosses in front of the trachea, it lies to the right of the ascending aorta, or it carries a valve? The answer is that it lies to the right of the ascending aorta. The trachea and the esophagus lie behind it, and the valve at the atrial opening belongs to the inferior vena cava.

Question 3908. A described venogram of the upper chest shows a wide channel on the right that receives a smaller vein arcing over the hilum of the lung. Which two veins are shown, and at what level do they meet? The answer is the superior vena cava and the azygos vein, meeting at the second right costal cartilage. The arch of the azygos vein passes over the root of the right lung.

Question 3909. In a described illustration, a person has swelling of the face and the neck with distended veins over the upper chest. Which vessel is obstructed: the superior vena cava, the inferior vena cava, the portal vein, or the splenic vein? The answer is the superior vena cava. Obstruction above the heart raises the pressure in the veins of the head, the neck and the arms, and blood returns by the azygos system.

Question 3910. Which two veins unite to form the superior vena cava, and where does that union take place? The answer is the right and left brachiocephalic veins, uniting behind the first right costal cartilage. Each is formed behind a sternoclavicular joint. The right one is nearly vertical, and the left one crosses the midline.

Question 3911. A constructed illustration: after a procedure in the upper chest, a described radiograph shows a widened mediastinum on the right, and the face is puffy. Which vein is compressed, and what does the widened shadow represent? The answer is the superior vena cava, and the shadow is the distended vein itself. The vein lies against the sternum, so its swelling shows on the film.

Question 3912. The left brachiocephalic vein crosses the midline in front of which structures: the trachea and the branches of the aortic arch, the esophagus and the descending aorta, the thoracic duct alone, or the root of the left lung? The answer is the trachea and the branches of the aortic arch. The left common carotid and left subclavian arteries arise from the arch behind the vein.

Question 3913. A described cross section of the upper chest shows a vein lying in front of the trachea and behind the sternum, crossing the midline to join its fellow. Which vein is described, and what lies immediately in front of it? The answer is the left brachiocephalic vein, with the thymus in front of it in the child. It is the longer of the two because it must cross the midline.

Question 3914. Which nerve descends in front of the root of the right lung and lies lateral to the superior vena cava? The answer is the right phrenic nerve, on its way to the diaphragm. The right vagus nerve lies behind the hilum instead, close to the trachea and the esophagus.

Question 3915. Which structure lies lateral to the superior vena cava in the middle mediastinum, and which lies medial to it? The answer is the right mediastinal pleura laterally and the ascending aorta medially. The right lung lies against the outer side of the vein, and the pericardium covers part of its lower portion.

Question 3916. The valve that guards the opening of a great vein into the right atrium is a remnant of which structure: the sinus venosus, the ductus venosus, the truncus arteriosus, or the septum primum? The answer is the sinus venosus. That valve belongs to the opening of the inferior vena cava. The superior vena cava has no valve at all.

Question 3917. True or false: the superior vena cava opens into the left atrium. The answer is false. It opens into the right atrium. The left atrium receives the four pulmonary veins from the lungs, and the two atria are separated by the interatrial septum.

Question 3918. Blood in the superior vena cava has just come from which of these: the lungs, the liver, the head and the upper limbs, or the kidneys? The answer is the head and the upper limbs. The superior vena cava drains everything above the diaphragm, while the hepatic veins drain the liver and the pulmonary veins drain the lungs.

Question 3919. Which vein drains the second, third and fourth intercostal spaces of the left side into the left brachiocephalic vein? The answer is the left superior intercostal vein, which runs across the arch of the aorta. On the right side the same spaces drain into the azygos vein.

Question 3920. A described dissection of the upper mediastinum shows a long vein lying to the left of the trachea and crossing in front of the branches of the aortic arch. Which vein is it, and which two arteries does it cross? The answer is the left brachiocephalic vein, crossing the left common carotid and the left subclavian arteries. It then joins the right brachiocephalic vein.

Question 3921. The inferior vena cava is formed by the union of which two veins, and at what level does that union lie: the two common iliac veins, the two renal veins, the two external iliac veins, or the two hepatic veins? The answer is the two common iliac veins. They unite in front of the fifth lumbar vertebra, to the right of the aortic bifurcation.

Question 3922. A constructed illustration: a described scan after an injury shows blood collecting behind the peritoneum on the right side of the lumbar spine. Which large vein lies in that position, and what is its relation to the aorta? The answer is the inferior vena cava, which lies to the right of the aorta and in front of the vertebral column. Its thin wall distends rather than contracts when torn.

Question 3923. Which vein returns blood from the lower half of the body to the right atrium, and on which side of the abdominal aorta does it ascend? The answer is the inferior vena cava, which ascends on the right of the aorta. It is the widest vein in the body, lying in front of the vertebral column behind the peritoneum.

Question 3924. The inferior vena cava passes through the diaphragm at the level of which vertebra: the eighth thoracic, the tenth thoracic, the twelfth thoracic, or the second lumbar? The answer is the eighth thoracic vertebra. Its opening lies in the central tendon, next to the right phrenic nerve, while the aorta passes behind the diaphragm at the twelfth thoracic vertebra.

Question 3925. Which structure crosses in front of the inferior vena cava in the upper part of the abdomen, and what lies behind the vein? The answer is the third part of the duodenum, with the head of the pancreas beside it. Behind the vein lie the bodies of the lumbar vertebrae and the right psoas muscle.

Question 3926. A described transverse section of the abdomen at the second lumbar vertebra shows a wide thin-walled vein to the right of the midline, in front of a vertebral body. Which vein is shown, and what lies on its left? The answer is the inferior vena cava, with the abdominal aorta on its left. The right psoas muscle lies behind it and the peritoneum in front.

Question 3927. Which veins open into the inferior vena cava just below the diaphragm and carry blood away from the liver? The answer is the hepatic veins. They are the largest tributaries of the vein and they open into it against the bare area of the liver. Blood reaches the liver by the portal vein and leaves by these veins.

Question 3928. Which renal vein is longer and crosses in front of the aorta: the right, the left, both equally, or neither? The answer is the left renal vein, passing in front of the aorta below the superior mesenteric artery. The right renal vein is short and opens directly into the inferior vena cava.

Question 3929. True or false: the left suprarenal vein drains directly into the inferior vena cava. The answer is false. It drains into the left renal vein, and only the right suprarenal vein opens directly into the inferior vena cava. The left gonadal vein behaves in the same way.

Question 3930. A constructed illustration: a described scan shows a mass at the back of the upper abdomen pressing on the vein that ascends to the right of the aorta. Which vein is compressed, and which channels may enlarge around it? The answer is the inferior vena cava, with the azygos and lumbar veins enlarging as collaterals. Blood then reaches the heart by the veins of the back.

Question 3931. Which of these statements is correct: the right gonadal vein ends in the inferior vena cava, the left gonadal vein ends in the inferior vena cava, the right suprarenal vein ends in the right renal vein, or the inferior phrenic veins end in the portal vein? The answer is that the right gonadal vein ends in the inferior vena cava. On the left, those veins end in the left renal vein.

Question 3932. Which vein receives the left gonadal vein and the whole of the left suprarenal vein? The answer is the left renal vein, which crosses in front of the aorta to reach the inferior vena cava. On the right side those veins open directly into the inferior vena cava, so the left renal vein is longer.

Question 3933. A described radiograph taken after contrast has been injected into a vein of the leg shows a wide channel running up the right side of the lumbar spine and through the diaphragm. Which vein is shown, and where does it enter the chest? The answer is the inferior vena cava, which enters through the central tendon at the eighth thoracic vertebra.

Question 3934. The right renal artery has which relation to the inferior vena cava: it passes in front of it, behind it, above it, or through it? The answer is that it passes behind it. The right renal artery is longer than the left, and the right gonadal artery crosses in front of the vein instead.

Question 3935. Which structures lie immediately behind the inferior vena cava as it ascends through the abdomen, and which crosses in front of it? The answer is the bodies of the lumbar vertebrae and the right psoas muscle, with the third part of the duodenum crossing in front. The right sympathetic trunk also lies behind the vein.

Question 3936. Which vein connects the lumbar veins with the azygos system on the right side? The answer is the ascending lumbar vein, which runs up in front of the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae. It joins the azygos vein above the diaphragm, linking the inferior vena cava with the chest.

Question 3937. Which of these pairs belongs together: the right gonadal vein with the left renal vein, the left suprarenal vein with the left renal vein, the right suprarenal vein with the right renal vein, or the left gonadal vein with the inferior vena cava? The answer is the left suprarenal vein with the left renal vein. On the right the corresponding veins open straight into the inferior vena cava.

Question 3938. How many veins unite to form the inferior vena cava, and where does that union lie: two, in front of the fifth lumbar vertebra; two, in front of the first lumbar vertebra; three, at the sacral promontory; or one, at the aortic bifurcation? The answer is two, in front of the fifth lumbar vertebra. That union lies behind the right common iliac artery.

Question 3939. A described cross section of the abdomen at the level of the umbilicus shows a wide vein on the right side of the vertebral column. Which vein is it, and what lies between it and the left side of the section? The answer is the inferior vena cava, with the aorta and the lumbar vertebrae between it and the left side.

Question 3940. A constructed illustration: a described scan of a thin person shows a vein compressed between the aorta behind and a branch of the aorta in front. Which vein is compressed, and which two vessels surround it? The answer is the left renal vein, caught between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery. It is longer than the right vein and crosses in front of the aorta.

Question 3941. Which vein drains the under surface of the diaphragm on the right side into the inferior vena cava? The answer is the right inferior phrenic vein. The left one opens into the left renal vein or into the inferior vena cava. Both run beside the phrenic nerves.

Question 3942. In the upper abdomen the inferior vena cava lies behind which structure: the peritoneum, the right psoas muscle, the lumbar vertebrae, or the right crus of the diaphragm? The answer is the peritoneum, because the vein is retroperitoneal. The other three lie behind it, and the duodenum and pancreas lie in front of it.

Question 3943. Which veins drain the posterior abdominal wall into the inferior vena cava, and with which system do they connect above the diaphragm? The answer is the lumbar veins, which connect through the ascending lumbar veins with the azygos system. There are usually four pairs, running behind the aorta and the inferior vena cava.

Question 3944. A described radiograph of the abdomen in a person lying flat shows the right border of a wide vein over the right side of the lumbar spine, with a smaller vein ending in it from the right. Which veins are shown, and what does the smaller one carry? The answer is the inferior vena cava and a right lumbar vein, which carries blood from the muscles of the back.

Question 3945. The hepatic veins differ from the other tributaries of the inferior vena cava in which way: their blood has already passed through a second set of capillaries, they carry oxygenated blood, they open into the left atrium, or they have valves? The answer is that their blood has passed through a second set of capillaries, having come from the gut by way of the liver. The other tributaries return blood after one passage.

Question 3946. Which vein is formed behind the neck of the pancreas by the union of the superior mesenteric vein and the splenic vein? The answer is the portal vein, which carries blood from the gut, the spleen and the pancreas to the liver. It runs in the free edge of the lesser omentum and has no valves.

Question 3947. True or false: blood from the gut passes through two sets of capillaries before it reaches the heart. The answer is true. It passes first through the capillaries of the gut wall and then through the sinusoids of the liver. The hepatic veins then carry it to the inferior vena cava.

Question 3948. In the free edge of the lesser omentum the portal vein lies in which relation to the bile duct and the proper hepatic artery: behind both, in front of both, between the two, or below both? The answer is behind both, with the bile duct in front and to its right and the artery in front and to its left. The three form the portal triad.

Question 3949. A constructed illustration: a person vomits blood and a described examination shows enlarged veins at the lower end of the esophagus. Which vein has enlarged, and why? The answer is the left gastric vein, whose esophageal tributaries have enlarged because the pressure in the portal system has risen. Those tributaries drain into the azygos system and reach the superior vena cava.

Question 3950. A described venogram of the abdomen shows a wide vein running up from behind the pancreas toward the liver, with a smaller vein joining the system behind the pancreas. Which veins are shown, and where do they meet? The answer is the portal vein and the inferior mesenteric vein, which joins the splenic vein behind the body of the pancreas.

Question 3951. Which vein carries blood from the spleen to the portal vein, and where does it run? The answer is the splenic vein, which runs behind the body and tail of the pancreas. It joins the superior mesenteric vein behind the neck of the pancreas and receives the short gastric veins.

Question 3952. Name the two veins whose union forms the portal vein, spoken as a list. The answer is the superior mesenteric vein and the splenic vein, which meet behind the neck of the pancreas. The inferior mesenteric vein often joins the splenic vein first, just before that meeting.

Question 3953. Which vein lies immediately to the right of the superior mesenteric artery in the root of the mesentery? The answer is the superior mesenteric vein, which ascends in front of the third part of the duodenum. The artery lies to its left, and the vein ends behind the neck of the pancreas.

Question 3954. Which of these pairs belongs together: the left gastric vein with the azygos system, the superior rectal vein with the internal jugular vein, the paraumbilical veins with the renal veins, or the splenic vein with the right atrium? The answer is the left gastric vein with the azygos system, by way of the veins of the esophagus. That connection opens when portal pressure rises.

Question 3955. Which of these veins carries blood toward the liver: the superior mesenteric vein, the inferior vena cava, the hepatic vein, or the renal vein? The answer is the superior mesenteric vein, which brings blood from the small intestine and the right half of the colon. The hepatic veins carry blood away from the liver.

Question 3956. Which vein drains the upper part of the anal canal into the portal system, and which veins drain the lower part into the systemic system? The answer is the superior rectal vein above and the middle and inferior rectal veins below. The two sets meet in the wall of the anal canal.

Question 3957. A described section through the lower end of the esophagus shows dilated veins in the wall beneath the mucosa, linking veins above with veins below. Which two venous systems meet there, and why do the veins dilate? The answer is the portal system, by way of the left gastric vein, and the systemic system, by way of the esophageal veins. They dilate when portal pressure rises.

Question 3958. The paraumbilical veins run in the round ligament of the liver and connect the portal vein with which veins of the abdominal wall: the superficial epigastric veins, the lumbar veins, the intercostal veins, or the internal thoracic veins? The answer is the superficial epigastric veins. When portal pressure rises they enlarge around the umbilicus, a pattern called a caput medusae.

Question 3959. True or false: the portal vein has valves that keep blood flowing toward the liver. The answer is false. The portal vein and its tributaries have no valves, so blood can flow either way when the pressure changes. That is why the connections with the systemic veins open.

Question 3960. A constructed illustration: a person with long-standing liver disease has dilated veins at the lower end of the esophagus, around the umbilicus and at the anal canal. How many places where portal and systemic veins meet are involved? The answer is three such places. At each one a portal tributary meets a systemic tributary, and a fourth route lies behind the peritoneum.

Question 3961. Which vein drains the upper part of the rectum into the portal system? The answer is the superior rectal vein, a tributary of the inferior mesenteric vein, which carries the blood to the splenic and portal veins. The lower rectum drains into the systemic veins instead.

Question 3962. A described specimen of the liver shows three structures entering the hilum, and the widest lies behind the other two. Which structure is the widest, and what are the other two? The answer is the portal vein, with the bile duct and the proper hepatic artery in front of it. All three run in the free edge of the lesser omentum.

Question 3963. Blood from the descending colon reaches the liver by which route: the superior mesenteric vein, the inferior mesenteric vein and then the splenic vein, the hepatic vein, or the lumbar veins? The answer is the inferior mesenteric vein and then the splenic vein. It drains the descending colon, the sigmoid colon and the upper rectum, and usually joins the splenic vein behind the pancreas.

Question 3964. Which vein runs immediately behind the body of the pancreas, and which vein joins it there? The answer is the splenic vein, which receives the inferior mesenteric vein and then joins the superior mesenteric vein behind the neck of the pancreas. It drains the spleen, the stomach and the pancreas.

Question 3965. Which statement about the portal triad is correct: the portal vein lies between the duct and the artery, the bile duct lies behind the portal vein, the portal vein lies behind the bile duct, or the hepatic artery lies behind the portal vein? The answer is that the portal vein lies behind the bile duct. The duct lies in front and to its right, the artery in front and to its left.

Question 3966. Which vein carries blood from the small intestine to the portal vein? The answer is the superior mesenteric vein, which receives the jejunal, ileal, ileocolic, right colic and middle colic veins. It lies to the right of the artery of the same name.

Question 3967. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superior rectal vein with the inferior mesenteric vein, the middle rectal vein with the portal vein, the inferior rectal vein with the splenic vein, or the paraumbilical veins with the azygos vein? The answer is the superior rectal vein with the inferior mesenteric vein. The middle and inferior rectal veins drain into the internal iliac veins.

Question 3968. Blood leaves the liver by which veins, and into which vessel do they open: the hepatic veins into the inferior vena cava, the portal vein into the inferior vena cava, the hepatic veins into the portal vein, or the splenic vein into the aorta? The answer is the hepatic veins, opening into the inferior vena cava just below the diaphragm. They carry blood that has already passed through the liver.

Question 3969. A described cast of the portal system shows a trunk running from the hilum of the liver down behind the pancreas, where two large veins join it, one from the left and one from below. Which vein is the trunk, and which two veins join it? The answer is the portal vein, joined by the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein.

Question 3970. A constructed illustration: a described scan of a person whose liver is firm and shrunken shows a dilated vein running up behind the pancreas, with a smaller channel beside the stomach. Which vein is dilated, and which connection has opened? The answer is the portal vein, with the left gastric and esophageal veins carrying blood to the azygos system. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3971. Which vein is the continuation of the sigmoid sinus below the jugular foramen? The answer is the internal jugular vein. It begins at the base of the skull in the superior bulb and descends in the carotid sheath. It ends behind the sternal end of the clavicle.

Question 3972. The internal jugular vein lies in which relation to the common carotid artery: lateral to it, medial to it, directly behind it, or directly in front of it? The answer is lateral to it, within the carotid sheath. The vagus nerve lies behind and between the two, and the sheath also contains the deep cervical lymph nodes.

Question 3973. True or false: the internal jugular vein has a valve at its lower end. The answer is true. A pair of valves lies in the inferior bulb, just above the point where the vein joins the subclavian vein. Valves are otherwise few in the veins of the neck.

Question 3974. Which structure lies between the internal jugular vein and the common carotid artery as they descend the neck? The answer is the vagus nerve, which lies behind and between them in the carotid sheath. The ansa cervicalis lies on the front of the sheath, and the deep cervical lymph nodes beside the vein.

Question 3975. Which of these veins is a tributary of the internal jugular vein: the facial vein, the external jugular vein, the anterior jugular vein, or the dorsal scapular vein? The answer is the facial vein. The internal jugular vein also receives the lingual, pharyngeal and thyroid veins, while the other three end elsewhere.

Question 3976. Which vein is formed near the angle of the jaw by the union of the posterior division of the retromandibular vein with the posterior auricular vein? The answer is the external jugular vein. It descends across the sternocleidomastoid muscle and ends in the subclavian vein, with a pair of valves just above its ending.

Question 3977. A described superficial dissection of the neck shows a vein crossing the sternocleidomastoid muscle, with a plexus of nerves appearing at the middle of the muscle's posterior border. Which structures are shown? The answer is the external jugular vein and the cervical plexus, with the great auricular nerve among its branches.

Question 3978. The external jugular vein ends in which vessel: the internal jugular vein, the subclavian vein, the brachiocephalic vein, or the axillary vein? The answer is the subclavian vein, above the middle of the clavicle. It has a pair of valves just above its ending.

Question 3979. Name the two veins that unite to form the external jugular vein, spoken as a list. The answer is the posterior division of the retromandibular vein and the posterior auricular vein, which meet below and behind the angle of the jaw. The vein then crosses the sternocleidomastoid muscle.

Question 3980. A constructed illustration: a small wound in the lower part of the neck lets air enter a vein, and the vein stands out as a cord over the muscle. Which vein is described, and why does air enter this one? The answer is the external jugular vein. It is superficial, and its wall is held open by the deep fascia, so it does not close when cut.

Question 3981. Which vein runs down the front of the neck close to the midline and often opens into the external jugular or subclavian vein? The answer is the anterior jugular vein, which begins below the chin and descends near the midline. The two are joined across the midline by the jugular arch.

Question 3982. Which vein drains the lower part of the thyroid gland and ends in the brachiocephalic vein: the superior thyroid vein, the middle thyroid vein, the inferior thyroid vein, or the facial vein? The answer is the inferior thyroid vein. The superior and middle thyroid veins end in the internal jugular vein, and the pattern varies.

Question 3983. Which of these pairs belongs together: the internal jugular vein with the subclavian vein, the external jugular vein with the internal jugular vein, the anterior jugular vein with the axillary vein, or the facial vein with the subclavian vein? The answer is the internal jugular vein with the subclavian vein. They unite behind the sternal end of the clavicle to form the brachiocephalic vein.

Question 3984. A described venogram of the neck shows a vein running from the base of the skull straight down to the root of the neck, where it joins another vein behind the inner end of the clavicle. Which vein is shown, and which veins does it receive? The answer is the internal jugular vein, receiving the facial and superior thyroid veins before it joins the subclavian vein.

Question 3985. Which structure lies immediately deep to the external jugular vein as the vein crosses the neck? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid muscle, which the vein crosses on its way down. The investing layer of deep fascia and the platysma lie superficial to it, and the vein runs from the jaw to the subclavian vein.

Question 3986. A constructed illustration: a described radiograph after a thin tube has been passed into a vein below the clavicle shows the tube running medially in front of a muscle in the root of the neck. Which vein holds it, and which muscle lies behind that vein? The answer is the subclavian vein, with the scalenus anterior behind it. The subclavian artery and the brachial plexus lie behind the same muscle.

Question 3987. The subclavian vein is the continuation of which vein, and where does that continuation begin: the axillary vein at the outer border of the first rib, the internal jugular vein at the jugular foramen, the brachiocephalic vein at the sternum, or the cephalic vein at the shoulder? The answer is the axillary vein, at the outer border of the first rib. The vein then runs medially in front of the scalenus anterior.

Question 3988. The subclavian vein ends where it joins which vein, and what do the two form: the internal jugular vein, forming the brachiocephalic vein; the external jugular vein, forming the superior vena cava; the cephalic vein, forming the axillary vein; or the basilic vein, forming the brachiocephalic vein? The answer is the internal jugular vein. The two form the brachiocephalic vein behind the sternal end of the clavicle.

Question 3989. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the root of the neck on the left side, lymph leaks from a duct that ends there. Which duct has been injured, and where does it end? The answer is the thoracic duct, which ends at the left venous angle where the internal jugular and subclavian veins meet. The right lymphatic duct ends on the right.

Question 3990. A described dissection of the root of the neck shows a vein in a groove on the upper surface of the first rib, in front of a small bony ridge, with an artery and a nerve plexus behind a muscle beside it. Which vein is shown? The answer is the subclavian vein, lying in the groove on the first rib in front of the scalene tubercle.

Question 3991. Which vein becomes the subclavian vein as it crosses the outer border of the first rib? The answer is the axillary vein. The subclavian vein then runs medially in front of the scalenus anterior, lying in a groove on the first rib, and joins the internal jugular vein.

Question 3992. Which structure lies immediately in front of the subclavian vein: the scalenus anterior muscle, the clavicle and the subclavius muscle, the brachial plexus, or the cervical pleura? The answer is the clavicle and the subclavius muscle. The vein lies in front of the scalenus anterior, and the artery and brachial plexus behind it.

Question 3993. Which muscle separates the subclavian vein from the subclavian artery at the root of the neck, and what lies behind the vein? The answer is the scalenus anterior muscle, which separates the two vessels. The vein lies in front of the muscle and the artery behind it, with the first rib and cervical pleura below.

Question 3994. Which of these pairs belongs together: the thoracic duct with the left venous angle, the thoracic duct with the right venous angle, the right lymphatic duct with the left venous angle, or the right lymphatic duct with the superior vena cava? The answer is the thoracic duct with the left venous angle. There the left internal jugular and left subclavian veins meet.

Question 3995. A described venogram of the upper limb shows contrast passing from a vein at the outer border of the first rib into a vein running medially in front of a muscle. Which vein has received the contrast: the subclavian vein, the internal jugular vein, the cephalic vein, or the axillary vein? The answer is the subclavian vein, which continues the axillary vein at that border. The scalenus anterior lies behind it.

Question 3996. A transverse section through the root of the neck shows the subclavian vein and the subclavian artery lying one behind the other. Which lies more anteriorly, and what separates them? The answer is that the vein lies in front and the artery behind, separated by the scalenus anterior muscle. The first rib lies below the vein.

Question 3997. Name two veins that join the subclavian vein near its end, spoken as a list. The answer is the external jugular vein and the dorsal scapular vein. The thoracic duct also ends at the left venous angle, close to the same point, and the anterior jugular vein may end there too.

Question 3998. Which statement about the valves of the subclavian vein is correct: it has a pair of valves near its termination, it has valves along its length, it has a single valve at the outer border of the first rib, or it has no valves? The answer is a pair of valves near its termination, close to the venous angle. Those valves keep blood from returning into the vein.

Question 3999. A described section of the root of the neck cuts across the great veins on one side and shows the internal jugular vein above and the subclavian vein below. In which plane does that section lie, and where do the two veins meet? The answer is a coronal plane. They meet at the venous angle, behind the sternal end of the clavicle.

Question 4000. Which two veins unite behind the sternal end of the clavicle to form a brachiocephalic vein? The answer is the internal jugular vein and the subclavian vein, whose meeting point is the venous angle. The left brachiocephalic vein then crosses the midline to join the right one.

To summarize, the superior vena cava is formed by the union of the two brachiocephalic veins behind the first right costal cartilage, runs to the right of the ascending aorta, receives the azygos vein from behind, and enters the right atrium at the third right costal cartilage. The inferior vena cava begins where the two common iliac veins meet in front of the fifth lumbar vertebra, ascends to the right of the aorta, receives the hepatic veins just below the diaphragm, and passes through the central tendon at the eighth thoracic vertebra, while the left renal vein crosses in front of the aorta. The portal vein is formed behind the neck of the pancreas, carries blood between two sets of capillaries, and has no valves, so the connections at the esophagus, the anal canal and the umbilicus open when the pressure inside it rises. The internal jugular vein continues the sigmoid sinus from the jugular foramen, lies lateral to the carotid artery, and joins the subclavian vein behind the sternal end of the clavicle, where the thoracic duct ends at the left venous angle, while the subclavian vein itself lies in front of the scalenus anterior in a groove on the first rib.
