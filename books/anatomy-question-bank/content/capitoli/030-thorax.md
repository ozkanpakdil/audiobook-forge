# Chapter 30 — Thorax

This chapter works through the muscular anatomy of the thorax and the abdomen, beginning with the diaphragm, the great dome of muscle that separates the two cavities, and then the muscles of the front abdominal wall, the rectus abdominis and the external oblique. It holds one hundred questions, and every one of them gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The chapter follows the diaphragm through its origins, its three great openings and the structures that pass through them, and then turns to the flat muscles that form the wall of the abdomen and the sheaths and ligaments they make. The questions are written to be answered out loud from the relations of each muscle rather than from a picture. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 2901. Which muscle forms the dome-shaped partition between the thoracic cavity above and the abdominal cavity below? The answer is the diaphragm. Its fibers arise from the sternum, the lower ribs and the lumbar vertebrae and converge upon a central tendon. When it contracts it flattens and enlarges the chest from above downward.

Question 2902. The central tendon of the diaphragm lies immediately below which structure, and which organ lies immediately below the tendon on the right side? The answer is the pericardium above and the bare area of the liver below. The heart rests upon the tendon, and the peritoneum does not cover the part of the liver that touches it.

Question 2903. A described radiograph of the chest taken at full inspiration shows one dome of the diaphragm standing higher than the other. Which dome is it, and why does it lie higher? The answer is the right dome, which is pushed upward by the liver beneath it. The left dome lies lower because the heart and the stomach occupy the space around it.

Question 2904. What is the name of the broad aponeurosis into which all the muscular fibers of the diaphragm insert? The answer is the central tendon. It lies high in the dome and is fused above with the pericardium. It is fibrous, so it does not shorten when the muscle contracts.

Question 2905. A described transverse section of the lower chest shows two curved domes of muscle meeting a fibrous sheet in the middle, with the heart above and the liver and the stomach below. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the diaphragm, which forms the floor of the chest and the roof of the abdomen at once. Its fibers rise from the body wall around it.

Question 2906. Which structures pass through the esophageal opening of the diaphragm, and which muscle fibers encircle that opening? The answer is the esophagus, the two vagal trunks and the esophageal branches of the left gastric vessels, encircled by a sling of fibers from the right crus. That sling contracts with the diaphragm and helps to close the opening.

Question 2907. A constructed illustration: irritation of the central part of the diaphragm causes pain felt at the tip of the shoulder. Which nerve carries that pain, and why is it felt so far from the diaphragm? The answer is the phrenic nerve, whose fibers enter the spinal cord at the third, fourth and fifth cervical segments, the same segments that serve the skin of the shoulder.

Question 2908. Which of these pairs belongs together: the caval opening with the eighth thoracic vertebra, the esophageal opening with the twelfth thoracic vertebra, the aortic opening with the tenth thoracic vertebra, or the caval opening with the tenth thoracic vertebra? The answer is the caval opening with the eighth thoracic vertebra. The esophagus passes at the tenth and the aorta at the twelfth.

Question 2909. Which nerve supplies the motor fibers of the diaphragm? The answer is the phrenic nerve, one for each half of the muscle. It arises in the neck from the third, fourth and fifth cervical spinal nerves and descends through the chest to reach the upper surface of the muscle. Division of one phrenic nerve paralyzes the same side.

Question 2910. True or false: the aorta pierces the muscular part of the diaphragm through an opening with a muscular margin. The answer is false. The aortic opening lies behind the diaphragm between the two crura, and the artery runs behind the muscle rather than through it. Because its margin is fibrous, contraction cannot pinch the aorta.

Question 2911. A described horizontal section across the chest at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra shows a muscular opening transmitting a tube with a plexus of nerves around it. Which opening is shown, and which of the three large openings lies highest? The answer is the esophageal opening, while the opening for the inferior vena cava lies highest, at the eighth thoracic vertebra.

Question 2912. Which of these structures passes through the opening in the central tendon of the diaphragm: the aorta, the esophagus, the inferior vena cava, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the inferior vena cava, which passes through the central tendon a little to the right of the midline and carries the right phrenic nerve with it. The aorta and the thoracic duct pass behind the muscle.

Question 2913. The right crus of the diaphragm is larger than the left and lies on which side of the esophageal opening, and from which bones does it arise? The answer is that it encircles the opening and arises from the bodies of the upper three lumbar vertebrae. Its fibers form the sling around the esophagus. The left crus is shorter.

Question 2914. What name is given to the two muscular pillars of the diaphragm that arise from the lumbar vertebrae? The answer is the crura. The right crus arises from the upper three lumbar vertebral bodies and the left from the upper two. Between them pass the aorta and the thoracic duct.

Question 2915. Which of these structures passes behind the medial arcuate ligament of the diaphragm: the sympathetic trunk, the subcostal nerve, the twelfth rib, or the kidney? The answer is the sympathetic trunk. The medial arcuate ligament is a thickening of fascia over the psoas major muscle. The subcostal nerve passes behind the lateral arcuate ligament instead.

Question 2916. A constructed illustration: a wound of the lower chest divides the phrenic nerve on one side, and on the next breath the injured dome of the diaphragm rises while the healthy dome descends. Which side moves upward, and why? The answer is the paralyzed side, which is pushed up by the pressure within the abdomen when the healthy half contracts.

Question 2917. Which muscle is the principal muscle of inspiration: the diaphragm, the rectus abdominis, the external oblique, or the internal intercostal muscles? The answer is the diaphragm. Its contraction lowers the domes and enlarges the chest from above downward. The flat abdominal muscles take part in forced expiration rather than quiet inspiration.

Question 2918. A described dissection of the diaphragm from below shows fibers running inward from the ribs, from the back of the xiphoid process and from the lumbar region, all meeting a trefoil-shaped sheet in the middle. Which parts of origin and which insertion are shown? The answer is the sternal, costal and lumbar parts, all inserting into the central tendon.

Question 2919. Which arched thickening of fascia passes over the psoas major muscle and gives origin to part of the diaphragm? The answer is the medial arcuate ligament. It is formed by the fascia over that muscle, and the sympathetic trunk passes behind it. The lateral arcuate ligament arches over the quadratus lumborum instead.

Question 2920. Which of these pairs belongs together: the central tendon with the inferior vena cava, the muscular part left of the midline with the aorta, the space between the crura with the esophagus, or the costal part with the thoracic duct? The answer is the central tendon with the inferior vena cava. The esophagus passes through the muscular part and the aorta between the crura.

Question 2921. What happens to the domes of the diaphragm during quiet inspiration: they flatten and descend, they rise, they stay still, or they move backward? The answer is that they flatten and descend. The muscle fibers shorten and draw the central tendon downward. This increases the vertical diameter of the chest.

Question 2922. Which structure lies immediately above the right dome of the diaphragm, and which organs lie immediately below the left dome? The answer is the right lung and pleura above, with the liver below it, and the stomach and the spleen below the left dome. The pleura dips into the recess around each dome.

Question 2923. A described radiograph shows a rounded shadow with a gas bubble and a fluid level beneath it on the left side, and the left dome of the diaphragm stands much higher than the right. Which organ lies below that dome, and what does the fluid level within it suggest? The answer is the stomach, and the level suggests that it is distended.

Question 2924. Which opening of the diaphragm transmits the thoracic duct together with the aorta? The answer is the aortic opening, which lies between the right and the left crus behind the muscle. The azygos vein also passes through it on the right side. Because it lies behind the muscle, it is never closed by contraction.

Question 2925. The diaphragm receives its motor supply from which nerve: the vagus nerve, the phrenic nerve, the intercostal nerves, or the sympathetic trunk? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It arises from the third, fourth and fifth cervical spinal nerves and runs down through the mediastinum. The lower intercostal nerves carry only sensation from the outer part of the muscle.

Question 2926. A constructed illustration: a newborn has difficulty in breathing, and a described radiograph shows loops of bowel in the left side of the chest. Through which gap in the diaphragm did the bowel most likely pass, and why is that side more often affected? The answer is the posterolateral gap, which closes last in development and lies on the left more often because the liver protects the right side.

Question 2927. Which statement about the costal part of the diaphragm is correct: its fibers interdigitate with those of the transversus abdominis, it arises from the outer surfaces of the lower six ribs, it arises from the first six ribs, or it inserts into the linea alba? The answer is that its fibers interdigitate with those of the transversus abdominis. The costal fibers arise from the inner surfaces of the lower six ribs.

Question 2928. Which arteries supply the upper surface of the diaphragm, and from which vessel do the inferior phrenic arteries arise? The answer is the pericardiophrenic and musculophrenic arteries above, and the inferior phrenic arteries from the abdominal aorta below. The musculophrenic artery runs along the costal margin and meets the other branches within the muscle.

Question 2929. True or false: the contraction of the diaphragm produces expiration. The answer is false, because its contraction produces inspiration. Expiration at rest follows the elastic recoil of the lungs and the chest wall as the muscle relaxes. A forced expiration uses the abdominal muscles, which push the relaxed diaphragm upward.

Question 2930. A described cross section of the lower chest at the level of the eighth thoracic vertebra shows a muscular sheet with a fibrous center, and the inferior vena cava is seen passing through that fibrous part. Which opening is shown, and which nerve runs beside the vein? The answer is the caval opening, with the right phrenic nerve beside it.

Question 2931. Which of these openings of the diaphragm lies most posterior: the opening for the inferior vena cava, the opening for the esophagus, the opening between the crura, or the gap between the sternal and the costal parts? The answer is the opening between the crura, through which the aorta passes. It lies against the vertebral column, behind the muscle.

Question 2932. The left phrenic nerve pierces the diaphragm and then runs on which surface of the muscle, and beside which structure does it reach the esophageal opening? The answer is the abdominal surface, where it lies beside the esophagus and the vagal trunks. Both phrenic nerves descend upon the pericardium before piercing the muscle.

Question 2933. What is the name of the fibrous sheet at the center of the diaphragm into which its muscle fibers insert? The answer is the central tendon. It lies at the summit of the dome and is fused with the pericardium above it. Because it is fibrous it does not shorten, and the muscle pulls it downward.

Question 2934. Which statement about the sternal part of the diaphragm is correct: it arises by two slips from the back of the xiphoid process, it arises from the sternum, it arises from the first costal cartilage, or it has no bony origin? The answer is that it arises by two slips from the back of the xiphoid process. Between them and the costal part lies a gap for the internal thoracic artery.

Question 2935. A constructed illustration: a person has a bout of hiccups after a large meal, with sudden involuntary contractions of the diaphragm followed by sharp closure of the vocal folds. Which muscle is contracting, and through which nerve does the reflex run? The answer is the diaphragm, acting through the phrenic nerve. The reflex may begin with irritation of the stomach below the muscle.

Question 2936. A described section of the trunk shows both domes of the diaphragm, with the heart above them and the liver and the stomach below. In which plane was this section cut, and what would a section at right angles to it show instead? The answer is a coronal plane, which divides the body into front and back parts. A transverse section would show a single horizontal level.

Question 2937. The diaphragm is attached to which of the following: the inner surfaces of the lower six ribs, the outer surfaces of the upper six ribs, the first lumbar vertebra only, or the clavicle? The answer is the inner surfaces of the lower six ribs and their costal cartilages. From there the fibers curve upward and inward to the central tendon. The outer surfaces give origin to other muscles.

Question 2938. A described radiograph taken at the end of a forced expiration shows both domes of the diaphragm much higher than on the earlier film. What has happened to the muscle, and which muscles helped to raise the domes? The answer is that the diaphragm has relaxed and been pushed upward by the abdominal muscles, chiefly the external oblique and the rectus abdominis.

Question 2939. Which opening of the diaphragm lies in the central tendon, and which large vein passes through it? The answer is the caval opening, transmitting the inferior vena cava. It lies a little to the right of the midline at the level of the eighth thoracic vertebra. Because it lies in fibrous tissue, the vein is not compressed when the muscle contracts.

Question 2940. Which of these pairs belongs together: the medial arcuate ligament with the psoas major, the lateral arcuate ligament with the aorta, the median arcuate ligament with the quadratus lumborum, or the medial arcuate ligament with the kidney? The answer is the medial arcuate ligament with the psoas major. The lateral ligament arches over the quadratus lumborum and the median ligament over the aorta.

Question 2941. Which of these statements describes the position of the diaphragm: it separates the thoracic cavity from the abdominal cavity, it lies entirely within the abdomen, it forms the roof of the thoracic cavity, or it lies below the pelvis? The answer is that it separates the thoracic cavity from the abdominal cavity. It is the floor of the chest and the roof of the abdomen at the same time.

Question 2942. Which structures lie immediately behind the lateral arcuate ligament of the diaphragm? The answer is the quadratus lumborum muscle with the subcostal nerve and vessels. The ligament is a thickening of the fascia over that muscle, and the twelfth rib lies behind it in turn. Its fibers run to the central tendon.

Question 2943. A constructed illustration: a person receives a blow to the left lower ribs, and a described radiograph later shows a raised left dome with bowel beneath it and a fracture of a lower rib. Which muscle has been torn, and through which gap can the abdominal contents now rise? The answer is the diaphragm, torn in its costal part near the posterolateral gap.

Question 2944. Which two arteries arise from the abdominal aorta and run upward onto the under surface of the diaphragm? The answer is the inferior phrenic arteries, one on each side. They lie below the muscle and supply its abdominal surface. Above, the muscle is supplied by branches of the internal thoracic artery.

Question 2945. Which of these structures passes through the esophageal opening of the diaphragm alongside the esophagus: the vagal trunks, the thoracic duct, the aorta, or the azygos vein? The answer is the vagal trunks, which lie upon the front and the back of the esophagus. The thoracic duct and the azygos vein pass through or behind the aortic opening.

Question 2946. A described specimen of the diaphragm viewed from below shows a fibrous trefoil in the center, and around it muscle fibers run in three directions from three different origins. Which origins are shown, and which one lies at the back? The answer is the sternal, costal and lumbar parts, with the lumbar part at the back upon the vertebral bodies.

Question 2947. Which statement about the levels of the three large openings of the diaphragm is correct: the caval opening lies highest, the aortic opening lies highest, the esophageal opening lies highest, or all three lie at the same level? The answer is that the caval opening lies highest. It lies at the eighth thoracic vertebra, the esophageal opening at the tenth and the aortic opening at the twelfth.

Question 2948. Name the three large openings of the diaphragm and one structure that passes through each, spoken as a list. The answer is the caval opening for the inferior vena cava, the esophageal opening for the esophagus and the vagal trunks, and the aortic opening for the aorta and the thoracic duct. Each opening is named for what it carries.

Question 2949. Which muscle forms the roof of the abdominal cavity and separates it from the chest? The answer is the diaphragm. Its dome rises on each side into the lower part of the chest. The liver and the stomach press against its under surface.

Question 2950. Which of these statements about the aortic opening is correct: it lies behind the diaphragm so that the muscle cannot compress the aorta, it lies in the central tendon, it is closed during inspiration, or it transmits the esophagus? The answer is that it lies behind the muscle, so contraction cannot compress the aorta. Flow through the aorta therefore continues throughout the breathing cycle.

Question 2951. Which muscles form the anterior wall of the abdomen, one on each side of the midline: the rectus abdominis and the pyramidalis, the diaphragm, the latissimus dorsi, or the serratus anterior? The answer is the rectus abdominis and the pyramidalis, with the three flat muscles of the abdominal wall behind them. The rectus runs vertically from the pubis to the chest.

Question 2952. Which muscle of the anterior abdominal wall is crossed by three fibrous bands, lies within a sheath whose posterior wall disappears below a certain line, and is the chief flexor of the trunk? The answer is the rectus abdominis. It runs from the pubic crest to the fifth, sixth and seventh costal cartilages. Its fibrous bands are the tendinous intersections.

Question 2953. A described dissection of the front of the abdomen shows a broad sheet of muscle whose fibers run downward and forward, with another sheet beneath it whose fibers run upward and forward. Which two muscles are shown? The answer is the external oblique upon the surface and the internal oblique deep to it. Their fibers cross at about a right angle.

Question 2954. Which of these structures marks the midline of the anterior abdominal wall: the linea alba, the inguinal ligament, the arcuate line, or the costal margin? The answer is the linea alba, the white fibrous band running from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis. The aponeuroses of the three flat muscles interlace within it, and it holds few blood vessels.

Question 2955. Which arteries lie posterior to the rectus abdominis and anastomose with each other within its sheath? The answer is the superior epigastric artery, descending behind the muscle from above, and the inferior epigastric artery, ascending behind it from below. They meet around the level of the umbilicus, so the muscle lies in front of both.

Question 2956. A constructed illustration: after a period of coughing, a person develops a painful swelling in the lower part of the rectus sheath, and a described scan shows a collection within the sheath below the umbilicus. Which vessel has most likely bled? The answer is the inferior epigastric artery, which runs behind the lower part of the rectus.

Question 2957. Which small triangular muscle lies in front of the lower end of the rectus abdominis and tenses the linea alba? The answer is the pyramidalis. It arises from the pubis and inserts into the linea alba. It is absent in a small proportion of people, which is a normal variation.

Question 2958. Which of these structures lies within the sheath of the rectus abdominis: the muscle with its arteries, nerves and lymph vessels, the peritoneal cavity, the kidney, or the small intestine? The answer is the muscle with its arteries, nerves and lymph vessels. The sheath is a fibrous compartment, and the peritoneum lies behind it, separated by the transversalis fascia.

Question 2959. True or false: the tendinous intersections of the rectus abdominis are firmly attached to the front wall of its sheath but not to the back wall. The answer is true. That attachment holds the muscle in place and produces the grooves seen upon the surface. The back of the muscle slides freely within the sheath.

Question 2960. Which of these pairs belongs together: the rectus abdominis with the lower six thoracic spinal nerves, the rectus abdominis with the phrenic nerve, the external oblique with the femoral nerve, or the diaphragm with the ilioinguinal nerve? The answer is the rectus abdominis with the lower six thoracic spinal nerves. The iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves supply only its lowest part.

Question 2961. What is the main action of the rectus abdominis: to flex the trunk forward, to extend the trunk, to rotate the trunk toward the same side, or to move the arm? The answer is to flex the trunk forward. Both muscles acting together bend the trunk, and one alone bends it toward its own side. They also compress the abdominal contents.

Question 2962. A described transverse section of the abdominal wall above the arcuate line shows a muscle enclosed between an anterior and a posterior layer of aponeurosis. Which muscle lies in that compartment, and which aponeuroses form the layer in front of it? The answer is the rectus abdominis, with the external oblique and the front lamina of the internal oblique in front of it.

Question 2963. Which fibrous line marks the lower limit of the posterior wall of the rectus sheath, below which the rectus lies directly upon the transversalis fascia? The answer is the arcuate line. It lies about midway between the umbilicus and the pubic symphysis. Below it all three aponeuroses pass in front of the muscle.

Question 2964. Which layer lies immediately posterior to the rectus abdominis below the arcuate line, and which structures lie behind that layer? The answer is the transversalis fascia, behind which lie the extraperitoneal fat and the peritoneum. Above the arcuate line the posterior sheath of the rectus separates the muscle from the same fascia.

Question 2965. Which of these muscles bends the trunk forward and compresses the abdominal contents: the rectus abdominis, the diaphragm, the serratus anterior, or the levator scapulae? The answer is the rectus abdominis. It is assisted in flexion by the internal and the external oblique muscles. All three also raise the pressure inside the abdomen.

Question 2966. A constructed illustration: a person of small build lifts a heavy weight, and the abdominal wall gives way along the midline between the two rectus muscles. Which structure has stretched, and why does this happen more often after pregnancy or repeated straining? The answer is the linea alba, which is widest and thinnest near the umbilicus.

Question 2967. Which statement about the nerve supply of the rectus abdominis is correct: it comes from the lower six thoracic spinal nerves, it comes from the phrenic nerve, it comes from the femoral nerve, or it comes from the obturator nerve? The answer is that it comes from the lower six thoracic spinal nerves, which run forward between the internal oblique and the transversus.

Question 2968. Which structures form the posterior wall of the rectus sheath above the arcuate line? The answer is the aponeurosis of the transversus abdominis together with the posterior lamina of the internal oblique. Both pass behind the muscle there. Below the arcuate line that wall is missing.

Question 2969. A described surface view of the abdomen shows three or four horizontal grooves crossing a vertical muscle on each side of the midline, with a furrow between the two muscles. Which muscle and which grooves are shown? The answer is the rectus abdominis with its tendinous intersections, and the furrow is the linea alba.

Question 2970. Which of these statements about the tendinous intersections of the rectus abdominis is correct: one lies at the level of the umbilicus, all of them lie below the umbilicus, there are always six of them, or they lie behind the muscle only? The answer is that one lies at the level of the umbilicus, with others at the xiphoid process and between the two. A fourth may lie below the umbilicus.

Question 2971. A described horizontal section of the abdominal wall below the arcuate line shows a vertical muscle lying upon a thin fibrous layer, with aponeuroses only in front of it. What lies behind the same muscle in a section taken above the arcuate line? The answer is a posterior sheath formed by the transversus abdominis and the internal oblique.

Question 2972. Which of these statements about the sheath of the rectus abdominis is correct: the external oblique aponeurosis always passes in front of the muscle, it always passes behind it, the transversus aponeurosis always passes behind it, or the internal oblique aponeurosis always passes behind it? The answer is that the external oblique aponeurosis always passes in front of the muscle.

Question 2973. Which muscle of the anterior abdominal wall has its fibers running vertically, and what is its lower attachment? The answer is the rectus abdominis, attached below to the pubic crest and the pubic symphysis. Above, it reaches the xiphoid process and the fifth to the seventh costal cartilages. It is the chief flexor of the trunk.

Question 2974. A constructed illustration: a person cannot rise from lying to sitting without using the arms, and the trunk cannot be bent against resistance. Which group of muscles is weak, and which nerves are likely to be affected? The answer is the muscles of the abdominal wall, chiefly the rectus abdominis, supplied by the lower six thoracic spinal nerves.

Question 2975. What effect does contraction of the rectus abdominis have upon the pelvis when the trunk is fixed: it tilts the pelvis backward, it tilts the pelvis forward, it rotates the pelvis about a vertical axis, or it has no effect? The answer is that it tilts the pelvis backward. The muscle pulls the front of the pelvis upward toward the chest.

Question 2976. From which surfaces of the ribs does the external oblique arise, and with which two muscles do its upper and lower fibers interdigitate? The answer is the outer surfaces of the lower eight ribs, interdigitating above with the serratus anterior and below with the latissimus dorsi. Its fibers then run downward and forward.

Question 2977. True or false: the external oblique is the most superficial of the three flat muscles of the abdominal wall. The answer is true. Its fibers run downward and forward, those of the internal oblique run upward and forward, and those of the transversus abdominis run across the abdomen. The three lie in that order from the surface inward.

Question 2978. A described dissection of the abdominal wall shows the outermost sheet of muscle with its fibers running in the direction of a hand placed in a pocket, and its lower border thickened into a band running from the front of the hip bone to the pubis. Which structures are shown? The answer is the external oblique with its aponeurosis, and the band is the inguinal ligament.

Question 2979. Which band forms the floor of the inguinal canal, running from the anterior superior iliac spine to the pubic tubercle? The answer is the inguinal ligament, the thickened lower border of the aponeurosis of the external oblique. The canal lies above it. Its medial end spreads into the lacunar ligament.

Question 2980. Which of these structures passes through the superficial inguinal ring: the spermatic cord in the male, the femoral artery, the inferior epigastric artery, or the obturator nerve? The answer is the spermatic cord in the male and the round ligament of the uterus in the female. The ring is a gap in the aponeurosis of the external oblique above and medial to the pubic tubercle.

Question 2981. Which of these pairs belongs together: the external oblique with the outer surfaces of the lower eight ribs, the external oblique with the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae, the internal oblique with the lower eight ribs only, or the transversus abdominis with the sternum? The answer is the external oblique with the outer surfaces of the lower eight ribs.

Question 2982. In which direction do the fibers of the external oblique run: downward and forward, upward and forward, horizontally across, or vertically upward? The answer is downward and forward. Both hands placed upon the chest with the fingers pointing down and inward follow the same direction. Contraction of the right external oblique therefore turns the trunk to the left.

Question 2983. A constructed illustration: a person develops a bulge in the groin above and medial to the pubic tubercle after lifting, and the swelling passes through a gap in the aponeurosis of the external oblique. Which structure has given way, and into which canal does it open? The answer is the superficial inguinal ring, opening into the inguinal canal.

Question 2984. Which muscle of the abdominal wall rotates the trunk toward the opposite side when it contracts on one side? The answer is the external oblique. Because its fibers run downward and forward, it pulls the chest toward the opposite hip. The internal oblique on the same side rotates the trunk toward the same side.

Question 2985. A described specimen of the groin shows a triangular opening in a fibrous sheet, with a cord passing through it and a nerve lying upon the cord. Which opening is shown, and which nerve lies upon the cord? The answer is the superficial inguinal ring, with the ilioinguinal nerve. The cord is the spermatic cord.

Question 2986. Which of these statements about the inguinal canal is correct: its anterior wall is formed by the aponeurosis of the external oblique, its posterior wall is the inguinal ligament, its roof is the peritoneum, or its floor is the internal oblique? The answer is that its anterior wall is formed by the aponeurosis of the external oblique. Its floor is the inguinal ligament.

Question 2987. Which structure lies immediately deep to the aponeurosis of the external oblique in the lateral part of the anterior wall of the inguinal canal, and which structure forms the medial part of its posterior wall? The answer is the internal oblique laterally, and the conjoint tendon medially. The conjoint tendon is formed together with the transversus abdominis.

Question 2988. Which crescent-shaped expansion of the inguinal ligament turns downward and backward to reach the pecten of the pubis? The answer is the lacunar ligament. It forms the medial boundary of the femoral canal, and the femoral vein lies just lateral to it. It also takes part in the floor of the inguinal canal.

Question 2989. Which of these muscles forms the anterior wall of the abdomen together with the internal oblique: the external oblique, the diaphragm, the quadratus lumborum, or the erector spinae? The answer is the external oblique, the most superficial of the three flat muscles. Its aponeurosis passes in front of the rectus abdominis at every level.

Question 2990. A described computed tomography section of the abdominal wall shows the outermost muscle layer as a thin sheet running downward and forward, with the internal oblique deep to it running upward and forward. Which two muscles are shown, and why is the pattern easy to tell apart? The answer is the external and the internal oblique muscles, whose fibers cross at about a right angle.

Question 2991. Name two muscles of the anterior abdominal wall whose fibers run obliquely but in opposite directions, spoken as a list. The answer is the external oblique and the internal oblique. The external oblique runs downward and forward and the internal oblique upward and forward. Their crossing strengthens the wall.

Question 2992. A described dissection of the groin shows a fibrous band running from the front of the hip bone to the pubic tubercle, with a canal lying above it and a nerve running through the canal. Which band and which canal are shown, and what is the nerve? The answer is the inguinal ligament and the inguinal canal, with the ilioinguinal nerve.

Question 2993. Which flat muscle of the abdominal wall lies deepest: the external oblique, the internal oblique, the transversus abdominis, or the rectus abdominis? The answer is the transversus abdominis. Its fibers run across the abdomen at right angles to the midline. Its aponeurosis forms part of the posterior sheath of the rectus above the arcuate line.

Question 2994. A constructed illustration: a blow to the side of the abdomen below the ribs is followed by pain on twisting the trunk, and the outer muscle layer of that region is bruised. Which muscle is injured, and which movement will hurt most? The answer is the external oblique, and rotation of the trunk toward the opposite side will hurt most.

Question 2995. A described radiograph after a strain shows a small avulsion of bone at the front of the hip bone, at the point where a long fibrous band begins. Which band begins there, and which aponeurosis forms it? The answer is the inguinal ligament, formed by the lower border of the aponeurosis of the external oblique, running to the pubic tubercle.

Question 2996. Which of these actions does the external oblique perform when it contracts on one side alone: it rotates the trunk toward the opposite side, it rotates the trunk toward the same side, it extends the trunk, or it abducts the arm? The answer is that it rotates the trunk toward the opposite side. Both muscles acting together flex the trunk and compress the abdominal contents.

Question 2997. Which muscle forms the greater part of the anterior abdominal wall and gives off the aponeurosis that forms the inguinal ligament? The answer is the external oblique. It arises from the outer surfaces of the lower eight ribs. Its fibers run downward and forward to the iliac crest, the linea alba and the pubis.

Question 2998. True or false: the aponeurosis of the external oblique passes behind the rectus abdominis below the arcuate line. The answer is false. It passes in front of the muscle at every level. Below the arcuate line all three aponeuroses lie in front, so the rectus rests directly upon the transversalis fascia.

Question 2999. Which of these structures lies at the medial end of the inguinal ligament and forms the medial wall of the femoral canal: the lacunar ligament, the conjoint tendon, the pectineal ligament, or the arcuate line? The answer is the lacunar ligament. It turns backward from the medial end of the inguinal ligament to the pecten of the pubis.

Question 3000. Name the three flat muscles of the abdominal wall in order from the surface inward, and state one action they share, spoken as a list. The answer is the external oblique, the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis, and all three compress the abdominal contents. They also help to expel air and to steady the trunk.

To summarize, the diaphragm is the dome of muscle that separates the chest from the abdomen, arising from the xiphoid process, the inner surfaces of the lower six ribs and the lumbar vertebrae, and inserting into the central tendon. Its three great openings are named for what they carry: the inferior vena cava passes through the central tendon at the level of the eighth thoracic vertebra, the esophagus with the vagal trunks passes through the muscular part at the tenth, and the aorta with the thoracic duct passes behind the muscle between the crura at the twelfth. The phrenic nerve from the third, fourth and fifth cervical spinal nerves is the only motor nerve of the diaphragm, and irritation of its central part is felt at the shoulder. The rectus abdominis runs vertically from the pubis to the fifth, sixth and seventh costal cartilages, is crossed by three tendinous intersections, and lies in a sheath whose posterior wall ends at the arcuate line. The external oblique is the most superficial of the flat muscles, arises from the lower eight ribs, runs downward and forward, and forms the inguinal ligament and the superficial inguinal ring. All the abdominal muscles compress the contents of the abdomen and raise the pressure within it, and this chapter is educational material that does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.
