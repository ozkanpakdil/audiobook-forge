# Chapter 32 — Upper Limb

The muscles of the upper limb work in groups, and this chapter takes them in the order in which a listener meets them from the shoulder outward. It begins with the four muscles of the rotator cuff that steady the head of the humerus in its shallow socket, then moves to the arm, where the flexors and the extensor of the elbow lie in two compartments, and ends in the forearm, whose two bones and many muscles turn the palm and move the hand. It holds one hundred questions, and every one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3101. Which of the four rotator cuff muscles is torn most often: the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor, or the subscapularis? The answer is the supraspinatus. Its tendon passes beneath the acromion and the coracoacromial ligament, where it is squeezed against the head of the humerus with every overhead movement. The other three lie further from that arch.

Question 3102. Which muscle of the rotator cuff arises from the fossa on the front of the scapula and inserts on the lesser tubercle of the humerus? The answer is the subscapularis. It is the only cuff muscle that lies on the anterior surface of the scapula. It rotates the arm medially and helps to hold the head of the humerus against the glenoid.

Question 3103. Which nerve supplies both the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus? The answer is the suprascapular nerve. It arises from the upper trunk of the brachial plexus and passes through the suprascapular notch beneath the transverse scapular ligament. It gives a branch to the supraspinatus and then continues into the infraspinous fossa.

Question 3104. Which muscle runs from the upper lateral border of the scapula to the lowest facet of the greater tubercle and rotates the arm laterally: the teres minor, the teres major, the infraspinatus, or the subscapularis? The answer is the teres minor. The teres major lies below it and rotates the arm medially, and the infraspinatus arises above it from the infraspinous fossa.

Question 3105. Which tendon of the rotator cuff lies immediately beneath the coracoacromial arch, and is therefore the one compressed when the arm is raised? The answer is the tendon of the supraspinatus. The arch is formed by the acromion and the coracoacromial ligament. Between the tendon and the arch lies the subacromial bursa, which is inflamed in the same movement.

Question 3106. A described magnetic resonance image of the shoulder, taken in the coronal plane, shows a gap filled with fluid in the tendon that crosses the top of the head of the humerus. Which tendon has torn? The answer is the supraspinatus, and fluid in the gap marks a full-thickness tear.

Question 3107. Which muscle arises from the whole of the infraspinous fossa and inserts on the middle facet of the greater tubercle? The answer is the infraspinatus. It is a lateral rotator of the arm and is supplied by the suprascapular nerve. It forms the rounded fullness below the spine of the scapula.

Question 3108. Which of these four rotator cuff muscles does not attach to the greater tubercle of the humerus: the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the subscapularis, or the teres minor? The answer is the subscapularis. Its tendon crosses the front of the joint to reach the lesser tubercle. The other three lie in a row on the greater tubercle, from above downward in that order.

Question 3109. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the shoulder, a person cannot begin to lift the arm away from the side, though a helper can raise it fully. Which structure is torn? The answer is the tendon of the supraspinatus, which takes the first part of abduction before the deltoid can act.

Question 3110. True or false: the tendons of the rotator cuff blend with the fibrous capsule of the shoulder joint and become inseparable from it near their insertions. The answer is true. The cuff forms a sleeve around the front, the top and the back of the joint. This fusion lets the cuff both move the arm and steady the head of the humerus.

Question 3111. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supraspinatus with the superior facet of the greater tubercle, the infraspinatus with the lesser tubercle, the subscapularis with the middle facet, or the teres minor with the highest facet? The answer is the supraspinatus with the superior facet, while the subscapularis takes the lesser tubercle.

Question 3112. Name the four muscles of the rotator cuff, spoken as a list. The answer is the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor, and the subscapularis. The first three arise from the back of the scapula and the last from its front. Together their tendons surround the shoulder joint and pull the head of the humerus into the glenoid.

Question 3113. Which nerve supplies the muscle that lies on the front of the scapula and rotates the arm medially: the suprascapular nerve, the axillary nerve, the upper and lower subscapular nerves, or the radial nerve? The answer is the upper and lower subscapular nerves. They arise from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus.

Question 3114. A described arthroscopic view of the shoulder shows a bare patch of bone on the greater tubercle where a tendon should be attached, with the tendon retracted toward the joint. Which structure has been lost? The answer is the insertion of the supraspinatus tendon. The bare patch is its footprint, left uncovered by the retracted end.

Question 3115. Which tendon passes through the gap between the supraspinatus and the subscapularis, a gap known as the rotator cuff interval? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps. It leaves the joint there and descends in the intertubercular sulcus. The coracohumeral ligament also crosses that interval and strengthens it.

Question 3116. Which muscle arises from the upper part of the lateral border of the scapula and inserts on the inferior facet of the greater tubercle? The answer is the teres minor. It is a lateral rotator of the arm and is supplied by the axillary nerve. It lies just below the infraspinatus and above the teres major.

Question 3117. A constructed illustration: a person reaches overhead and feels a sharp pain as the arm passes the middle of the arc. Which structures are being pinched, and in which space? The answer is the supraspinatus tendon and the subacromial bursa, in the subacromial space. The painful arc is the range in which the greater tubercle passes beneath the acromion.

Question 3118. Which muscle both forms part of the rotator cuff and starts the movement of raising the arm sideways: the infraspinatus, the supraspinatus, the subscapularis, or the teres major? The answer is the supraspinatus. It abducts the arm through the first part of the range, and the deltoid then carries the movement further. The teres major is not a cuff muscle and rotates the arm medially.

Question 3119. A described radiograph of the shoulder shows the head of the humerus sitting higher than its socket, almost touching the acromion. What does that position suggest? The answer is a tear of the rotator cuff. When the cuff no longer holds the head down against the pull of the deltoid, the head drifts upward. The normal space between the head and the acromion is lost.

Question 3120. True or false: the rotator cuff acts only as a passive tie that holds the head of the humerus in place and takes no active part in movement. The answer is false. The cuff is an active group of muscles that contracts during movement and steadies the head while the larger muscles move the arm. It is a dynamic stabilizer rather than a static ligament.

Question 3121. Which pair of rotator cuff muscles shares a single nerve supply, so that one lesion weakens both: the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus, the infraspinatus and the teres minor, the teres minor and the subscapularis, or the subscapularis and the supraspinatus? The answer is the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus, which share the suprascapular nerve.

Question 3122. Which structure lies immediately between the tendon of the supraspinatus and the arch of the acromion? The answer is the subacromial bursa. It is a flat sac that allows the tendon to glide beneath the bone. Because it communicates with the shoulder joint in many people, inflammation of one commonly affects the other.

Question 3123. Which artery runs with the suprascapular nerve through the suprascapular notch and supplies the muscles of the cuff on the back of the scapula? The answer is the suprascapular artery. It arises from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery. It reaches the back of the scapula above the transverse scapular ligament while the nerve passes below it.

Question 3124. How many muscles make up the rotator cuff: two, three, four, or five? The answer is four. They are the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor, and the subscapularis. Their tendons fuse with the capsule of the shoulder joint and form a cuff around the head of the humerus.

Question 3125. A constructed illustration: an older person has an old tear of the rotator cuff, and the head of the humerus now rides high. Which muscle is acting without its usual partner? The answer is the deltoid, whose upward pull is no longer balanced by the cuff.

Question 3126. A described transverse section through the shoulder shows a broad tendon wrapping the head of the humerus from the front, the top and the back. Which four muscles contribute to that wrapping? The answer is the subscapularis in front, the supraspinatus on top, and the infraspinatus with the teres minor behind. Their tendons merge with the capsule to form the cuff.

Question 3127. Which of these pairs belongs together: the subscapularis with lateral rotation, the infraspinatus with medial rotation, the supraspinatus with abduction, or the teres minor with extension of the elbow? The answer is the supraspinatus with abduction. The subscapularis rotates the arm medially, the infraspinatus and the teres minor rotate it laterally, and no cuff muscle acts on the elbow.

Question 3128. Which fossa of the scapula lies above the spine and gives origin to the muscle whose tendon passes under the acromion? The answer is the supraspinous fossa, and the muscle is the supraspinatus. The fossa is smaller than the infraspinous fossa below the spine. It is filled by the muscle, whose tendon then crosses the top of the joint.

Question 3129. Which movement is weakened first when the tendon of the supraspinatus is torn: rotation of the arm outward, the beginning of abduction of the arm, extension of the elbow, or pronation of the forearm? The answer is the beginning of abduction of the arm, which the deltoid can continue once it has started.

Question 3130. A described coronal section through the shoulder shows a bursa lying between a tendon and the underside of the acromion, and the bursa is thickened and filled with fluid. Which tendon lies deep to it? The answer is the supraspinatus. The bursa lies between the tendon and the arch formed by the acromion and the coracoacromial ligament. Thickening of the bursa narrows the space further.

Question 3131. Which rotator cuff muscle lies against the anterior surface of the scapula and is the only one to insert on the lesser tubercle: the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor, or the subscapularis? The answer is the subscapularis. It is a medial rotator of the arm, supplied by the upper and lower subscapular nerves. The other three all reach the greater tubercle behind it.

Question 3132. A described dissection of the shoulder has the deltoid reflected forward, exposing a tendinous sheet that covers the head of the humerus. Which sheet is exposed? The answer is the rotator cuff, with the subacromial bursa lying between the cuff and the acromion.

Question 3133. Which muscle lies immediately inferior to the infraspinatus on the back of the scapula, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the teres minor, supplied by the axillary nerve. It arises from the lateral border of the scapula below the infraspinatus. The two muscles work together as lateral rotators but have different nerve supplies.

Question 3134. Why does the tendon of the supraspinatus tear more often than the other cuff tendons: because it is the thinnest, because it passes beneath the acromion, because it has no nerve supply, or because it crosses two joints? The answer is that it passes beneath the acromion, and its blood supply is poor near the insertion, in the zone named for Codman.

Question 3135. The tendon of the supraspinatus crosses the top of the shoulder joint and ends on the superior facet of the greater tubercle. What does it lie against along the way? The answer is the fibrous capsule of the joint. Its fibers blend with the capsule before they reach the bone, and the subacromial bursa lies above it.

Question 3136. Which single bone forms the skeleton of the arm between the shoulder joint above and the elbow below? The answer is the humerus. Its rounded head fits into the glenoid cavity of the scapula, and its lower end joins the radius and the ulna. The arm is the segment above the elbow, while the forearm lies below it.

Question 3137. Which muscle of the arm both flexes the elbow and turns the palm to face upward: the brachialis, the triceps brachii, the biceps brachii, or the coracobrachialis? The answer is the biceps brachii. It is the strongest supinator when the elbow is flexed. The brachialis is a pure flexor, and the triceps brachii extends the elbow.

Question 3138. Which muscle arises from the coracoid process and inserts on the middle of the medial surface of the humerus? The answer is the coracobrachialis. It flexes and adducts the arm at the shoulder. The musculocutaneous nerve pierces it on its way down the arm.

Question 3139. Which nerve pierces the coracobrachialis muscle on its way into the arm: the axillary nerve, the musculocutaneous nerve, the radial nerve, or the ulnar nerve? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve. It enters the front of the arm through that muscle and then supplies the muscles of the anterior compartment. At the elbow it continues as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm.

Question 3140. Which tendon lies in the intertubercular sulcus on the front of the upper humerus? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps brachii. It leaves the shoulder joint and runs down the groove between the greater and the lesser tubercles. The transverse humeral ligament holds it in the groove.

Question 3141. Which muscle is the strongest flexor of the elbow, lying deep to the biceps brachii and inserting on the ulna? The answer is the brachialis. It arises from the front of the lower half of the humerus and inserts on the ulnar tuberosity. Because it attaches to the ulna rather than the radius, it flexes the elbow whatever the position of the forearm.

Question 3142. A constructed illustration: a person falls and breaks the shaft of the humerus at its middle, and afterwards the wrist cannot be straightened. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the radial nerve, in the groove on the back of the humerus. Its branches to the extensor muscles of the wrist lie beyond the fracture. The result is a wrist drop.

Question 3143. True or false: the median nerve gives off no branch to any muscle while it lies in the arm. The answer is true. In the arm it runs beside the brachial artery and gives no muscular branches at all. Its first muscular branches are given in the forearm, where it supplies most of the flexor group.

Question 3144. A described radiograph shows a fracture line through the upper humerus just below the rounded head, and the shaft is displaced. Which nerve is most at risk? The answer is the axillary nerve. It winds around the surgical neck, and the deltoid weakens when it is injured.

Question 3145. The triceps brachii has three heads. Which one arises from the back of the humerus below the groove that carries the radial nerve: the long head, the lateral head, the medial head, or the anconeus? The answer is the medial head. The lateral head arises above the groove, and the long head arises from the scapula.

Question 3146. Which of these pairs belongs together: the musculocutaneous nerve with the triceps brachii, the radial nerve with the coracobrachialis, the axillary nerve with the deltoid, or the median nerve with the biceps brachii? The answer is the axillary nerve with the deltoid. The musculocutaneous nerve supplies the coracobrachialis, the biceps and the brachialis, and the radial nerve supplies the triceps.

Question 3147. Which small muscle lies at the back of the elbow, arises from the lateral epicondyle of the humerus, and is supplied by the radial nerve? The answer is the anconeus. It inserts on the lateral side of the olecranon and the upper part of the ulna. It helps to extend the elbow and steadies the joint during the movement.

Question 3148. Which muscle forms the lateral boundary of the cubital fossa: the pronator teres, the brachioradialis, the biceps brachii, or the flexor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the brachioradialis. The pronator teres forms the medial boundary, and the line between the two epicondyles forms the base above. The biceps tendon and the brachial artery lie within the fossa.

Question 3149. A described radiograph of a child's elbow shows a fracture line just above the condyles of the humerus, with the lower fragment displaced backward. Which nerve and which artery lie most at risk? The answer is the median nerve and the brachial artery, both of which pass directly in front of the lower end of the humerus. The displaced fragment can stretch or tear them.

Question 3150. A described cross section through the middle of the arm shows two compartments separated by a band of deep fascia and a septum of bone. Which nerve lies in each? The answer is that the musculocutaneous nerve lies in the anterior compartment and the radial nerve behind, with the humerus between them.

Question 3151. The axillary nerve leaves the back of the axilla through a small space bounded by the teres minor above and the teres major below. What is that space called, and which vessel runs with the nerve? The answer is the quadrangular space, with the posterior circumflex humeral artery. Both pass around the surgical neck of the humerus.

Question 3152. At which point does the axillary artery become the brachial artery? The answer is at the lower border of the teres major muscle. Above that border the vessel is named the axillary artery. Below it, the brachial artery runs down the medial side of the arm beside the median nerve.

Question 3153. The musculocutaneous nerve ends in the forearm as which cutaneous nerve: the medial cutaneous nerve of the forearm, the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm, the posterior cutaneous nerve of the forearm, or the palmar branch of the median nerve? The answer is the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm, which emerges on the outer side of the arm above the elbow.

Question 3154. Name the three muscles of the anterior compartment of the arm, spoken as a list. The answer is the coracobrachialis, the biceps brachii, and the brachialis. All three are supplied by the musculocutaneous nerve. They flex the arm at the shoulder or the elbow, and the biceps also turns the palm upward.

Question 3155. From which point does the long head of the biceps brachii arise, and by what route does its tendon reach the arm: the coracoid process, the supraglenoid tubercle, the infraglenoid tubercle, or the greater tubercle? The answer is the supraglenoid tubercle. The tendon leaves the shoulder joint and runs down in the intertubercular sulcus. The short head arises from the coracoid process beside it.

Question 3156. True or false: the radial nerve supplies a small part of the brachialis muscle as well as the triceps. The answer is true. A branch of the radial nerve reaches the lateral part of the brachialis, while the musculocutaneous nerve supplies the rest. This double supply is one of the details that students most often miss.

Question 3157. A constructed illustration: a person who has used crutches under the arms for several weeks finds that the wrist hangs limp and the back of the hand feels numb. Which nerve has been compressed? The answer is the radial nerve in the axilla. The crutch presses it against the humerus, and the wrist drop follows.

Question 3158. A described specimen of the back of the arm shows a muscle with three heads, and when it is reflected the radial nerve and a deep artery come into view in a groove on the bone. Which muscle is it? The answer is the triceps brachii. The groove is the radial groove of the humerus, beside the deep artery of the arm.

Question 3159. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior compartment of the arm with the radial nerve, the posterior compartment of the arm with the musculocutaneous nerve, the anterior compartment of the arm with the musculocutaneous nerve, or the posterior compartment with the axillary nerve? The answer is the anterior compartment with the musculocutaneous nerve, and the posterior with the radial nerve.

Question 3160. Which bony prominence does the ulnar nerve lie behind as it passes from the arm into the forearm? The answer is the medial epicondyle of the humerus. The nerve crosses the back of the elbow in a groove behind that prominence. This is why a knock on the inner side of the elbow produces tingling in the little finger.

Question 3161. A fracture of the middle of the humeral shaft damages the radial nerve, yet the triceps still extends the elbow. Why is that: the triceps has another nerve, its branches leave the nerve above the groove, the triceps is not a true extensor, or the nerve recovers at once? The answer is that they leave the nerve above the groove, before its branches to the wrist extensors.

Question 3162. Which bony prominence can be felt on the inner side of the elbow, and which nerve lies behind it? The answer is the medial epicondyle of the humerus, with the ulnar nerve behind it. The epicondyle gives origin to the common flexor muscles of the forearm. The prominence is the reason the nerve is exposed to injury there.

Question 3163. The radial nerve leaves the back of the arm and reaches the front. Which structure does it pierce as it makes that crossing: the medial intermuscular septum, the lateral intermuscular septum, the bicipital aponeurosis, or the transverse humeral ligament? The answer is the lateral intermuscular septum, between the brachialis and the brachioradialis.

Question 3164. A described angiogram of the arm shows a single large vessel running down the medial side of the limb beside the median nerve, giving branches to the muscles of the arm. Which vessel is shown, and where does it end? The answer is the brachial artery, which ends in the cubital fossa by dividing into the radial and the ulnar arteries.

Question 3165. A constructed illustration: a person lifting a heavy object feels a sudden snap at the front of the shoulder, and afterwards the belly of the biceps bunches into a low swelling. Which structure has ruptured? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps. It lay in the intertubercular sulcus, and the short head remains attached.

Question 3166. Which muscle forms the rounded contour of the shoulder, is supplied by the axillary nerve, and abducts the arm: the supraspinatus, the teres major, the deltoid, or the coracobrachialis? The answer is the deltoid. Its middle fibers abduct the arm after the supraspinatus has begun the movement. Its anterior fibers flex and rotate the arm medially, and its posterior fibers do the reverse.

Question 3167. Which large vein runs up the medial side of the arm and joins the brachial vein near the axilla? The answer is the basilic vein. The cephalic vein runs on the lateral side instead and reaches the shoulder. In the cubital fossa the median cubital vein joins the two.

Question 3168. Which muscle of the arm inserts on the olecranon process of the ulna: the biceps brachii, the brachialis, the triceps brachii, or the coracobrachialis? The answer is the triceps brachii. Its three heads join in a common tendon that ends on the olecranon. The anconeus lies beside it and shares the same nerve supply.

Question 3169. Which of the two bones of the forearm lies on the lateral or thumb side? The answer is the radius. The ulna lies medially, on the little finger side. In the anatomical position the two bones lie parallel, and the radius turns around the ulna during pronation.

Question 3170. Which muscle both pronates the forearm and helps to flex the elbow: the pronator quadratus, the supinator, the pronator teres, or the flexor carpi radialis? The answer is the pronator teres. It arises from the medial epicondyle and the coronoid process and inserts on the lateral surface of the radius. The pronator quadratus pronates without flexing the elbow.

Question 3171. Which flat muscle lies deep in the front of the distal forearm, directly on the two bones, and pronates the hand? The answer is the pronator quadratus. It runs from the front of the ulna to the front of the radius. It is the main pronator during slow, unresisted movement.

Question 3172. All the muscles of the front of the forearm are supplied by the median nerve except two. Which muscle is supplied by the ulnar nerve: the pronator teres, the flexor carpi radialis, the flexor carpi ulnaris, or the flexor digitorum superficialis? The answer is the flexor carpi ulnaris. The ulnar half of the flexor digitorum profundus is the other exception.

Question 3173. In which direction do the fibers of the interosseous membrane of the forearm run, and what force do they carry? The answer is that they run downward and medially from the radius to the ulna. The hand loads the radius, and the membrane passes that force to the ulna.

Question 3174. A described cross section through the middle of the forearm shows two bones with a membrane between them. Which bone lies on which side, and what lies between them? The answer is that the radius lies laterally and the ulna medially, with the interosseous membrane stretched between them. Muscle fills the space in front of both.

Question 3175. Which muscle of the forearm sends tendons to the middle phalanges of the fingers? The answer is the flexor digitorum superficialis. Each of its tendons splits into two slips to allow the deeper tendon to pass through. It flexes the middle joints of the fingers, and its tendons pass beneath the flexor retinaculum at the wrist.

Question 3176. The flexor digitorum profundus has a double nerve supply. Which part of it is supplied by the ulnar nerve: the lateral half, the medial half, the whole muscle, or none of it? The answer is the medial half, which moves the little finger and the ring finger. The lateral half is supplied by the median nerve.

Question 3177. A constructed illustration: an older person falls on the outstretched hand, and afterwards the lower end of the radius is displaced backward, giving the wrist a fork-shaped outline. Which bone is fractured? The answer is the distal radius, displaced backward and outward, and the ulnar styloid may be broken too.

Question 3178. True or false: every muscle of the front of the forearm is supplied by the median nerve. The answer is false. The flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial half of the flexor digitorum profundus are supplied by the ulnar nerve. All the others are supplied by the median nerve or by its branches.

Question 3179. Which muscle wraps around the upper part of the radius, arises from the lateral epicondyle and the supinator crest of the ulna, and turns the palm upward? The answer is the supinator. It is supplied by the deep branch of the radial nerve. That nerve passes beneath the fibrous arch at the upper edge of the muscle.

Question 3180. Which of these pairs belongs together: the flexor carpi radialis with the ulnar nerve, the flexor digitorum superficialis with the radial nerve, the flexor carpi ulnaris with the ulnar nerve, or the pronator quadratus with the radial nerve? The answer is the flexor carpi ulnaris with the ulnar nerve, the one muscle of the superficial group supplied by that nerve.

Question 3181. At the wrist, the radial artery is felt for its pulse between which two tendons: the flexor carpi radialis and the brachioradialis, the palmaris longus and the flexor carpi ulnaris, the extensor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis, or the flexor digitorum superficialis and the flexor digitorum profundus? The answer is between the flexor carpi radialis and the brachioradialis.

Question 3182. A described radiograph of the wrist shows a break across the lower end of the radius, with the distal fragment tilted backward. Which normal relation has altered? The answer is that the normal forward tilt of the lower end of the radius is reversed, giving the fork-shaped outline.

Question 3183. A constructed illustration: after a forearm injury a person cannot bend the tip of the thumb or the tip of the index finger, yet sensation in the hand is normal. Which nerve is damaged? The answer is the anterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the median nerve. It supplies the deep flexors and carries no sensory fibers.

Question 3184. The median nerve enters the forearm between which two structures before it passes beneath the arch of the flexor digitorum superficialis? The answer is the two heads of the pronator teres, the humeral head and the ulnar head. It then runs down the middle of the forearm. The ulnar nerve enters between the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris instead.

Question 3185. Which tendon forms the medial boundary of the small triangular hollow at the base of the thumb? The answer is the tendon of the extensor pollicis longus. The lateral boundary is formed by the tendons of the abductor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis. The radial artery crosses the floor of that hollow, over the scaphoid.

Question 3186. Which muscle of the forearm flexes the elbow most effectively when the forearm is midway between pronation and supination: the biceps brachii, the brachialis, the brachioradialis, or the pronator teres? The answer is the brachioradialis. The biceps works best with the palm upward, and the brachialis flexes in every position but has its best leverage in full supination.

Question 3187. Turning the palm to face the floor and then back to face the ceiling happens in which plane, and about which axis? The answer is the transverse plane, about a longitudinal axis that runs from the center of the head of the radius above to the head of the ulna below. The radius turns around the ulna. The elbow itself does not move.

Question 3188. Which structure must the deep branch of the radial nerve pass beneath as it enters the back of the forearm: the arcade of Frohse, the bicipital aponeurosis, the flexor retinaculum, or the interosseous membrane? The answer is the arcade of Frohse. It is the fibrous upper edge of the supinator, and the nerve beyond it is named the posterior interosseous nerve.

Question 3189. A constructed illustration: a fracture of the upper ulna is found together with a dislocation of the head of the radius at the elbow. Why do those two injuries belong together? The answer is that the broken ulna allows the head of the radius to leave the joint, and it will not return while the ulna remains bent.

Question 3190. A described transverse section is taken through the middle third of the forearm. Which structure lies directly between the two bones, and in which direction does it pass? The answer is the interosseous membrane, which passes downward and medially from the radius to the ulna. It divides the forearm into an anterior and a posterior compartment. Vessels pass through openings in it.

Question 3191. A described dissection of the back of the forearm has the superficial extensor muscles turned aside, revealing a deep group around the lower end of the radius. Which muscles are in that group? The answer is the supinator, the abductor pollicis longus, the extensor pollicis brevis, the extensor pollicis longus, and the extensor indicis.

Question 3192. Supination is strongest with the elbow bent to a right angle. Which fact explains that: the biceps brachii is a powerful supinator, the supinator muscle crosses only one joint, the pronator quadratus relaxes, or the radius is fixed by the wrist? The answer is that the biceps brachii is a powerful supinator and is at its best length when the elbow is flexed.

Question 3193. A constructed illustration: a person who repeatedly lifts a young child feels pain over the outer side of the wrist when the thumb is extended and the hand turns inward. Which tendons are inflamed? The answer is the abductor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis, in the first compartment beneath the extensor retinaculum.

Question 3194. Which artery runs down the forearm in company with the ulnar nerve, and between which two heads do both pass at the elbow? The answer is the ulnar artery, which passes between the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris with the nerve. Below the elbow it gives the common interosseous artery. It ends in the hand as the superficial palmar arch.

Question 3195. Which phalanx does the tendon of the flexor digitorum superficialis reach: the proximal, the middle, the distal, or none? The answer is the middle phalanx. Each tendon splits into two slips that attach to the sides of that bone. The deeper muscle, the flexor digitorum profundus, continues through the split to reach the distal phalanx.

Question 3196. Which nerve supplies the deep muscles on the back of the forearm, after it has passed beneath the supinator? The answer is the posterior interosseous nerve, the continuation of the deep branch of the radial nerve. It carries no cutaneous sensation in the forearm. It ends as a small filament over the back of the wrist.

Question 3197. Name the three muscles whose tendons bound the small hollow at the base of the thumb, spoken as a list. The answer is the abductor pollicis longus, the extensor pollicis brevis, and the extensor pollicis longus. The first two form the lateral boundary, and the third forms the medial boundary. The radial artery crosses the floor.

Question 3198. Which muscle of the forearm is absent in some people, and is often used as a graft: the flexor carpi radialis, the palmaris longus, the pronator teres, or the flexor digitorum superficialis? The answer is the palmaris longus. It arises from the medial epicondyle and ends in the palmar aponeurosis of the hand.

Question 3199. A described dissection of the back of the wrist shows a broad band of fibrous tissue holding six sets of tendons against the bones, each set in its own sheath. What is that band called, and how many compartments does it form? The answer is the extensor retinaculum, which forms six compartments. The tendons pass beneath it in sheaths lined with synovial membrane.

Question 3200. A constructed illustration: a fracture of the lower radius comes with disruption of the joint between the two forearm bones at the wrist. Which pair is described: a broken radius with a disrupted distal radioulnar joint, a broken ulna with a dislocated radial head, a broken scaphoid alone, or a dislocated wrist with no fracture? The answer is the broken radius with the disrupted distal radioulnar joint.

To summarize, the rotator cuff is four muscles whose tendons fuse with the capsule of the shoulder joint: the supraspinatus, which begins abduction beneath the acromion, the infraspinatus and the teres minor, which rotate the arm laterally, and the subscapularis, which rotates it medially. In the arm, the anterior compartment is supplied by the musculocutaneous nerve and the triceps by the radial nerve in the groove on the back of the humerus, while the brachial artery and the median nerve descend on the medial side. In the forearm, the median nerve supplies every flexor except the flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial half of the flexor digitorum profundus, and the radial nerve supplies the extensors through its deep branch. The radius turns around the ulna in the transverse plane, and the interosseous membrane carries the load from the radius to the ulna.
