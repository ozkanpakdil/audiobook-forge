# Chapter 113 — Abdominal Anatomy

The abdomen holds the organs of digestion, the urinary passages and the great vessels that serve them, and it is the region where an injury can hide behind an intact wall. This chapter covers the peritoneum and its folds, the liver and the biliary tree, the stomach and the intestines, the pancreas and the spleen, the kidneys and the ureters, and the vessels and nerves of the back wall; its second half turns to the structures that are torn, compressed or obstructed when a person arrives acutely unwell. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question, then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind to the end. Every clinical item here is a constructed illustration rather than a real case, and where management is mentioned it belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 11201. A described dissection shows a double fold of peritoneum hanging from the greater curvature of the stomach and draping over the coils of the small intestine. Which structure is described? The answer is the greater omentum. It is the largest fold of peritoneum, and its fatty apron lies between the abdominal wall and the viscera.

Question 11202. Which layer of the peritoneum is sensitive to pain: the visceral layer, the parietal layer, the greater omentum, or the mesentery? The answer is the parietal layer. The visceral peritoneum is supplied by autonomic nerves and feels only stretch, while the parietal layer carries somatic nerves and localizes pain sharply.

Question 11203. Which fold of peritoneum runs from the left side of the stomach to the spleen and carries the short gastric vessels and the left gastroepiploic vessels? The answer is the gastrosplenic ligament. It is part of the greater omentum, and the spleen lies behind its lateral end.

Question 11204. The celiac trunk supplies the derivatives of which part of the embryonic gut: the foregut, the midgut, the hindgut, or the whole gut? The answer is the foregut. It feeds the stomach, the duodenum as far as the major papilla, the liver, the gallbladder, the pancreas and the spleen.

Question 11205. Which part of the liver rests directly against the diaphragm with no covering of visceral peritoneum, and is therefore named the bare area? The answer is the posterior part of the right lobe. The coronary ligament marks its margins, and the inferior vena cava grooves the liver close beside it.

Question 11206. Deep to the neck of the pancreas, the splenic vein joins the superior mesenteric vein to form which vessel? The answer is the portal vein. The gland lies in front of the junction, so the vein is created behind the pancreas and then runs upward to the liver.

Question 11207. A described section of the upper abdomen shows a long lobulated gland whose head is clasped by the curve of the duodenum and whose tail reaches the spleen. Which organ is shown, and which duct runs along it? The answer is the pancreas, with the pancreatic duct. That duct joins the common bile duct at the major duodenal papilla.

Question 11208. Which fold of peritoneum runs from the liver to the lesser curvature of the stomach, and what is its free right edge called? The answer is the lesser omentum, whose free edge is the hepatoduodenal ligament. The bile duct and the hepatic artery lie in it in front of the portal vein.

Question 11209. Within the free edge of the lesser omentum, which structure lies most posterior: the common bile duct, the proper hepatic artery, the portal vein, or the lymph nodes? The answer is the portal vein. The bile duct lies anterior and to its right, and the hepatic artery anterior and to its left.

Question 11210. A constructed illustration: several hours after a fatty meal, a person has severe pain in the right upper abdomen that is also felt beneath the right shoulder. Which organ is inflamed, and which nerve carries the pain? The answer is the gallbladder, with the right phrenic nerve. The gallbladder touches the diaphragm, and irritation of that surface is felt at the shoulder.

Question 11211. Which part of the stomach lies highest in the standing body: the pylorus, the fundus, the body, or the pyloric canal? The answer is the fundus. It rises above the level of the opening of the esophagus, and swallowed gas collects there, which is why it may be seen on a chest radiograph.

Question 11212. Which ring of thickened circular muscle guards the outlet of the stomach and controls the passage of food into the duodenum? The answer is the pyloric sphincter. The pylorus lies to the right of the midline at the level of the first lumbar vertebra.

Question 11213. A described contrast study of the upper gut shows a loop of bowel that curves around the head of the pancreas and ends where a duct opens on its medial wall. Which part of the gut is shown, and what is that opening called? The answer is the duodenum, opening at the major duodenal papilla.

Question 11214. Which part of the duodenum passes across the front of the aorta and is itself crossed in front by the superior mesenteric artery? The answer is the third, or horizontal, part. It lies between the aorta behind and the superior mesenteric artery in front, and it is a place where the bowel can be compressed.

Question 11215. Which part of the small intestine has a thick wall, tall folds and a mesentery clear enough to show its vessels: the jejunum or the ileum? The answer is the jejunum. It occupies the upper part of the peritoneal cavity, while the ileum lies below it and is thinner walled.

Question 11216. Which feature marks the ileum rather than the jejunum: tall circular folds, aggregated lymphoid nodules, a thick wall, or few arterial arcades? The answer is aggregated lymphoid nodules. They lie in the wall on the side opposite the mesentery and grow more numerous toward the large intestine.

Question 11217. True or false: the appendix opens into the cecum a little below the point where the ileum joins it. The answer is true. The opening lies on the posteromedial wall of the cecum, below the ileocecal junction, and the appendix hangs free from that point in a variable direction.

Question 11218. Which surface point for the appendix lies one third of the way along a line from the right anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus? The answer is McBurney point. It marks the base of the appendix, and tenderness there is one of the signs of appendicitis.

Question 11219. A constructed illustration: a young person has pain that begins around the navel and settles in the right lower abdomen, where the abdominal wall is guarded. Which organ lies beneath that point? The answer is the appendix. Its visceral afferents follow the sympathetic nerves to the tenth thoracic segment, which is why the early pain is felt near the navel.

Question 11220. Which of these parts of the large intestine has no mesentery and lies behind the peritoneum: the appendix, the transverse colon, the sigmoid colon, or the ascending colon? The answer is the ascending colon. It is covered by peritoneum only in front, and the descending colon has the same arrangement.

Question 11221. At what level does the left kidney lie, and which kidney sits a little lower? The answer is the level of the twelfth thoracic to the third lumbar vertebra, with the right kidney lower. The liver occupies the space above the right kidney and pushes it downward.

Question 11222. A described scan of the upper abdomen shows a small golden gland sitting like a cap on the upper pole of each kidney, enclosed with the kidney in the same fat and fascia. Which glands are shown? The answer is the suprarenal glands. The right one is triangular and the left one is crescent shaped.

Question 11223. Which structure lies in front of the upper part of the left kidney and shares the same bed behind the peritoneum: the cecum, the liver, the pancreas, or the appendix? The answer is the pancreas. Its body and tail lie across the left kidney, and the spleen rests against the upper lateral border of that kidney.

Question 11224. Which artery supplies the middle part of the suprarenal gland and arises directly from the abdominal aorta? The answer is the middle suprarenal artery. The gland also receives superior branches from the inferior phrenic artery and inferior branches from the renal artery. Those three sources overlap, which is why the gland survives when one of them is tied.

Question 11225. Which kidney lies lower in the body: the right, the left, both at the same level, or the left in men and the right in women? The answer is the right. The liver above it takes the space that the left kidney uses, so the right kidney lies about 2 centimeters lower.

Question 11226. Which of these pairs belongs together: the left renal vein with the inferior vena cava, the left gonadal vein with the inferior vena cava, the right gonadal vein with the left renal vein, or the sigmoid vein with the portal vein? The answer is the left renal vein with the inferior vena cava. The left gonadal and left suprarenal veins join it, while the right gonadal vein joins the inferior vena cava.

Question 11227. Which vein crosses in front of the aorta and behind the superior mesenteric artery, and what may happen to it there? The answer is the left renal vein. It is longer than the right one because it must cross the midline, and the artery above it can narrow it. The narrowing is a described variation rather than a disease in most people.

Question 11228. A described operation shows a tube crossing the brim of the pelvis in front of the common iliac bifurcation. Which structure is it: the ureter, the femoral nerve, the inferior epigastric artery, or the round ligament of the uterus? The answer is the ureter. It is the structure most easily caught at that crossing.

Question 11229. A described scan of the back wall of the abdomen shows the aorta dividing into two vessels in front of the fourth lumbar vertebra, and each of those vessels dividing again at the brim of the pelvis. Which vessels are shown? The answer is the two common iliac arteries. Each one divides into an external and an internal iliac artery at the brim.

Question 11230. Which is the lowest of the unpaired branches of the abdominal aorta: the celiac trunk, the superior mesenteric artery, the inferior mesenteric artery, or the middle suprarenal artery? The answer is the inferior mesenteric artery. The middle suprarenal artery is a paired branch, and it arises above the inferior mesenteric artery.

Question 11231. Up to which point does the inferior mesenteric artery supply the gut: the first part of the duodenum, the middle of the transverse colon, the cecum, or the upper rectum? The answer is the upper rectum. Its territory begins at the splenic flexure and runs to the upper part of the rectum.

Question 11232. At which point along the colon is the blood supply least secure, where the territory of the superior mesenteric artery meets that of the inferior mesenteric artery? The answer is the splenic flexure. The marginal artery is often thin there, so that segment is the most vulnerable when flow falls. This is a normal arrangement and not a disease.

Question 11233. A described contrast study of the colon shows a segment that crosses the abdomen from right to left, bears pouches along its length and carries small fatty tags. Which part of the colon is shown? The answer is the transverse colon. It hangs from a mesentery that allows it to move, and its right end lies below the liver.

Question 11234. Which midline band of fibrous tissue runs from the pubic symphysis to the xiphoid process and is formed by the interlocking aponeuroses of the abdominal muscles? The answer is the linea alba. It is narrow below the umbilicus and broad above it, and it marks the midline of the abdominal wall.

Question 11235. Which structure passes through the inguinal canal in the male: the round ligament of the uterus, the spermatic cord, the femoral nerve, or the saphenous vein? The answer is the spermatic cord. In the female the canal carries the round ligament of the uterus, and the femoral nerve passes below the inguinal ligament instead.

Question 11236. In the inguinal region, does the inferior epigastric artery lie medial or lateral to the deep inguinal ring? The answer is that the artery lies medial to the ring. The ring is a defect above the middle of the inguinal ligament, and it lies lateral to the artery. A hernia that enters it therefore passes lateral to the artery.

Question 11237. A constructed illustration: a person lifts a heavy load and feels a dragging pain in the groin, and a swelling appears above and medial to the pubic tubercle and passes into the scrotum. Which kind of hernia is described? The answer is an indirect inguinal hernia. It enters the deep inguinal ring, lies lateral to the inferior epigastric artery, and follows the spermatic cord.

Question 11238. In the femoral triangle, which of the three main structures lies most lateral: the femoral nerve, the femoral artery, or the femoral vein? The answer is the femoral nerve. From lateral to medial they are the nerve, the artery and the vein, and the lymph nodes lie in the canal medial to the vein.

Question 11239. A described dissection of the front of the thigh shows three structures arranged from lateral to medial below the inguinal ligament. Which order is correct: vein, artery, nerve; nerve, vein, artery; nerve, artery, vein; or artery, nerve, vein? The answer is nerve, artery, vein. The femoral nerve lies outside the femoral sheath, while the artery and the vein lie within it.

Question 11240. A described cross section of the front of the abdomen, below the umbilicus, shows the rectus muscle in a sheath whose front wall is made of two aponeuroses while its back wall is thin. Which layer forms that back wall? The answer is the transversalis fascia. Below the arcuate line all the aponeuroses pass in front of the muscle, so the fascia lies directly behind it.

Question 11241. True or false: the inguinal ligament is formed by the folded lower border of the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle. The answer is true. It runs from the anterior superior iliac spine to the pubic tubercle, and the deep fascia of the thigh hangs from its lower edge.

Question 11242. Which opening in the diaphragm lies at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra and carries the aorta and the thoracic duct? The answer is the aortic hiatus. It lies behind the diaphragm between its two crura, so the aorta passes behind the muscle rather than through it.

Question 11243. Which structures pass through the esophageal opening of the diaphragm, and at which vertebral level does that opening lie? The answer is the esophagus and the two vagus nerves, at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra. The opening lies in the muscular part of the diaphragm, in front of the aorta.

Question 11244. Which structures pass through the opening in the central tendon of the diaphragm: the aorta and the thoracic duct, the esophagus and the vagus nerves, the inferior vena cava and the right phrenic nerve, or the sympathetic trunk and the splanchnic nerves? The answer is the inferior vena cava and the right phrenic nerve. That opening lies at the eighth thoracic vertebra, highest of the three.

Question 11245. Which of these pairs belongs together: the caval opening with the tenth thoracic vertebra, the esophageal opening with the eighth thoracic vertebra, the aortic opening with the twelfth thoracic vertebra, or the aortic opening with the eighth thoracic vertebra? The answer is the aortic opening with the twelfth thoracic vertebra. The caval opening lies at the eighth and the esophageal opening at the tenth.

Question 11246. A described dissection behind the aorta at the level of the first lumbar vertebra shows a sac into which several lymph trunks from the lower limbs and the gut drain. Which structure is shown, and into what does it continue upward? The answer is the cisterna chyli, which continues as the thoracic duct. It lies behind the aorta and in front of the vertebral column.

Question 11247. A constructed illustration: a person with long-standing liver disease has dilated veins running outward from the umbilicus, and the superficial veins of the abdominal wall are engorged. Which veins have opened up? The answer is the paraumbilical veins, which join the superficial veins of the abdominal wall. Blood that cannot pass through the liver is carried to the body wall instead.

Question 11248. Name three places where the veins of the portal system join the veins of the body wall, spoken as a list. The answer is the lower esophagus, the umbilicus and the rectum. There the left gastric veins meet the esophageal veins, the paraumbilical veins meet the veins of the wall, and the superior rectal vein meets the middle and inferior rectal veins.

Question 11249. True or false: the psoas major muscle arises from the sides of the lumbar vertebral bodies and from the discs between them. The answer is true. It also takes origin from the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae, and it passes under the inguinal ligament to reach the lesser trochanter of the femur.

Question 11250. Which vessel is formed at the level of the fifth lumbar vertebra by the union of the two common iliac veins, and lies to the right of the aorta: the portal vein, the inferior vena cava, the left renal vein, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the inferior vena cava. It ascends in front of the vertebral column and passes through the diaphragm to reach the heart.

Question 11251. A described specimen of the airway shows that one main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical than the other. Which side is a swallowed object more likely to enter? The answer is the right side. The right main bronchus continues the line of the trachea more directly, so a foreign body follows it into the right lung.

Question 11252. Within an intercostal space, where does the bundle of nerve and vessels lie: deep to the under surface of the rib above, deep to the upper surface of the rib below, in the middle of the space, or outside the rib cage? The answer is deep to the under surface of the rib above. The bundle rests in the groove there, and the inner intercostal muscle separates it from the pleura.

Question 11253. A described chest radiograph shows one side of the chest more translucent than the other, with the lung collapsed toward the midline and the heart and trachea pushed to the opposite side. Which condition is shown? The answer is a tension pneumothorax, with air under pressure in the pleural cavity. The pressure pushes the mediastinum across, and that is what makes the injury dangerous.

Question 11254. Which chamber of the heart lies immediately behind the body of the sternum and the costal cartilages beside it, and is therefore the chamber most exposed to a wound of the front of the chest? The answer is the right ventricle. It forms most of the anterior surface of the heart, with the right atrium to its right.

Question 11255. In cardiac tamponade, which chambers of the heart are compressed first: the left atrium and left ventricle, the right atrium and right ventricle, the aorta and the pulmonary trunk, or the coronary veins? The answer is the right atrium and the right ventricle. They are the thinnest walled and the lowest pressured chambers, so they fill last when the sac is tight.

Question 11256. A constructed illustration: a narrow wound enters the left side of the chest beside the sternum in the fourth intercostal space and reaches the heart. Which chamber is most likely to be wounded? The answer is the right ventricle. It lies immediately behind the sternum and the left fourth costal cartilage, so a wound beside the sternum reaches it first.

Question 11257. A described scan of the chest after a sudden stop in a road collision shows a widened upper mediastinum, with blood around the aorta just beyond the point where the left subclavian artery leaves it. Which part of the aorta is injured? The answer is the isthmus, just distal to the left subclavian artery. There the aorta is fixed against the vertebral column by the ligamentum arteriosum.

Question 11258. Which organ lies beneath the left ninth to the eleventh ribs and is the organ most often injured in a blunt blow to the upper abdomen? The answer is the spleen. It is a soft organ with a thin capsule, and it lies in the left upper quadrant behind the stomach and the ribs.

Question 11259. Which structure lies immediately beneath the left lower ribs in the midaxillary line, so that a fracture of those ribs may tear it: the liver, the spleen, the right kidney, or the gallbladder? The answer is the spleen. It is protected by the ninth, tenth and eleventh ribs, and its long axis follows the line of the tenth rib.

Question 11260. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the left lower chest, a person has pain in the left upper abdomen and also complains of pain at the tip of the left shoulder, worse on breathing in. Which organ is likely injured, and which nerve explains the shoulder pain? The answer is the spleen, with the left phrenic nerve. Blood under the diaphragm irritates that nerve, and the pain is felt over the shoulder.

Question 11261. Which part of the pancreas lies against the spleen, so that the two are often injured together in a blow to the left upper abdomen? The answer is the tail of the pancreas. It reaches the spleen along the splenic vessels, and the spleen lies behind its lateral end. Both rest at the level of the eleventh thoracic vertebra.

Question 11262. In a road collision a lap belt crosses the abdomen and presses a structure against the vertebral column. Which structure lies in that position and is injured: the stomach, the gallbladder, the third part of the duodenum, or the appendix? The answer is the third part of the duodenum. It crosses in front of the spine, and the pancreas lies behind it in the same line of compression.

Question 11263. The abdomen is divided by two planes crossing at the umbilicus into four quadrants. In which quadrant does the spleen lie? The answer is the left upper quadrant. It sits behind the stomach and the left lower ribs, above and to the left of the umbilicus.

Question 11264. A described wound track enters the left side of the chest at the level of the eighth rib and passes downward and medially. Which two cavities could lie in that path, and which structure separates them? The answer is the pleural cavity and the peritoneal cavity, separated by the diaphragm. The left dome of the diaphragm rises to about the level of the eighth rib in the midclavicular line.

Question 11265. A fracture of the pelvis tears vessels on its inner surface and causes heavy bleeding. Which vessels are the usual source: the femoral artery and vein, the presacral venous plexus and the superior gluteal artery, the obturator and sciatic nerves, or the inferior epigastric vessels? The answer is the presacral venous plexus and the superior gluteal artery. Both lie against the bone inside the pelvis.

Question 11266. Which part of the urethra is torn when the pelvis is fractured, at the point where the urethra passes through the perineal membrane? The answer is the membranous part, the short segment between the prostate and the bulb of the penis. It is the fixed point of the urethra, so it tears when the ring of the pelvis gives way.

Question 11267. In the female pelvis, which structure lies immediately lateral to the cervix of the uterus and passes over the ureter, close to it? The answer is the uterine artery. The ureter passes beneath the artery about 2 centimeters lateral to the cervix, which is why the two are described as water running under a bridge.

Question 11268. A constructed illustration: a person falls astride a hard edge and cannot pass urine, with blood at the urinary opening and bruising of the perineum and scrotum. Which part of the urethra is torn? The answer is the bulbar part in the perineum. It is the fixed part of the anterior urethra, and blood and urine spread into the perineum and the scrotum.

Question 11269. A described radiograph of the pelvis shows breaks through both the superior and the inferior pubic rami on each side, with the fragments undisturbed. Which kind of injury is shown, and which structure is at risk? The answer is a straddle fracture, with the bulbar urethra at risk. The bony ring gives way at its weakest points, which are the pubic rami.

Question 11270. When a full bladder ruptures after a blow to the lower abdomen, why does urine usually enter the peritoneal cavity: because the peritoneum covers the dome of the bladder, because the bladder lies behind the peritoneum, because the urethra is torn, or because the rectum is perforated? The answer is that the peritoneum covers its dome. A full bladder rises above the pubic symphysis, and the peritoneum over its dome tears with it.

Question 11271. True or false: the right kidney lies a little lower than the left because the liver occupies the space above it. The answer is true. The right kidney is pressed downward by the liver, so its upper pole lies about 2 centimeters lower than the upper pole of the left kidney.

Question 11272. Which structure of the abdomen lies behind the peritoneum and may be injured in a blunt blow to the front of the abdomen, with signs that appear only hours later: the stomach, the transverse colon, the duodenum, or the jejunum? The answer is the duodenum. It lies against the vertebral column behind the peritoneum, so blood and gut contents collect in the tissue there.

Question 11273. In an emergency, which artery can be pressed against the superior ramus of the pubis at the midpoint of the inguinal ligament? The answer is the femoral artery. It lies at that point between the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic symphysis, with the femoral vein medial to it.

Question 11274. Which of these pairs belongs together: a blow to the left lower ribs with the spleen, a blow to the right lower ribs with the spleen, a fractured pelvis with the appendix, or a stab below the left costal margin with the gallbladder? The answer is a blow to the left lower ribs with the spleen. The spleen lies beneath the ninth to the eleventh ribs on the left.

Question 11275. Name three structures that lie behind the peritoneum in the abdomen, spoken as a list. The answer is the kidneys, the pancreas and the duodenum. The ureters, the suprarenal glands, the abdominal aorta, the inferior vena cava and the ascending and descending parts of the colon lie there too.

Question 11276. After a severe injury, pressure rises inside the abdomen and the diaphragm is pushed upward. Which structure reflects that pressure because it lies in the pelvis with a soft wall: the stomach, the bladder, the rectum, or the gallbladder? The answer is the bladder. It lies in the pelvis, and the pressure within it follows the pressure in the abdomen around it.

Question 11277. Which artery supplies the appendix, and from which vessel does it arise? The answer is the appendicular artery, from the ileocolic artery. It runs in the mesentery of the appendix, along the free edge of that fold, and it belongs to the superior mesenteric territory.

Question 11278. A described dissection of the right lower abdomen shows the appendix hanging over the muscle that runs down the back wall of the abdomen, with its tip pointing toward the brim of the pelvis. On which muscle does it rest? The answer is the psoas major. It flexes the hip joint and steadies the lumbar spine, and it passes beneath the inguinal ligament.

Question 11279. A described upright radiograph of the chest shows a crescent of gas under both domes of the diaphragm, with no gas in the stomach below the left dome. What does that gas indicate, and where has it come from? The answer is free gas in the peritoneal cavity, from a perforated hollow organ. Gas rises to the highest part of the abdomen, which is under the diaphragm.

Question 11280. Which part of the duodenum is the usual site of perforation: the anterior wall of the first part, the posterior wall of the first part, the anterior wall of the third part, or the medial wall of the second part? The answer is the anterior wall of the first part. That part is mobile and lies in front of the pancreas, so its contents spill into the abdominal cavity.

Question 11281. A constructed illustration: a person has sudden severe pain in the upper middle of the abdomen, the abdominal wall becomes rigid, and the radiograph shows gas under the diaphragm. Which part of the gut has perforated? The answer is the first part of the duodenum, on its anterior wall. That segment is the most mobile part of the duodenum, and it lies in front of the pancreas.

Question 11282. A described contrast study of the upper gut shows a small crater in the wall of the first part of the duodenum, with the fold pattern drawn toward it. Which structure is shown, and on which wall does it lie? The answer is an ulcer of the duodenum on the anterior wall. That wall faces the peritoneal cavity, which is why it is the site that perforates.

Question 11283. The superior mesenteric artery supplies the gut from which point to which point: from the stomach to the cecum, from the second part of the duodenum to the proximal two thirds of the transverse colon, from the cecum to the rectum, or from the rectum to the anal canal? The answer is from the second part of the duodenum to the proximal two thirds of the transverse colon.

Question 11284. Which kind of hernia passes through the narrow channel bounded in front by the inguinal ligament and behind by the pectineal ligament, and is the most likely to strangulate? The answer is a femoral hernia. Its neck is the femoral ring, a narrow opening that admits only a finger, and the firm lacunar ligament lies at its medial edge.

Question 11285. Which ligament forms the medial boundary of the femoral ring, and what is the effect of that boundary on a hernia passing there? The answer is the lacunar ligament, the reflected part of the inguinal ligament. That sharp edge narrows the ring and can cut into the bowel within it. The femoral vein lies lateral to the ring and the pectineal ligament behind it.

Question 11286. A constructed illustration: a person has a firm, tender lump in the groin that lies below and lateral to the pubic tubercle and does not descend into the scrotum. Which kind of hernia is described? The answer is a femoral hernia. It passes below the inguinal ligament through the femoral canal, while an inguinal hernia lies above and medial to the pubic tubercle.

Question 11287. True or false: a direct inguinal hernia passes through the deep inguinal ring. The answer is false. A direct hernia pushes through the medial part of the posterior wall of the inguinal canal, medial to the inferior epigastric artery, while an indirect hernia is the one that enters the deep ring.

Question 11288. A described dissection of the inguinal region shows a peritoneal sac entering the abdominal wall lateral to the inferior epigastric artery and then running down the canal. Which kind of hernia does the sac represent? The answer is an indirect inguinal hernia. It enters the deep inguinal ring, which lies lateral to the artery, and it travels within the coverings of the spermatic cord.

Question 11289. The weak triangle in the posterior wall of the inguinal canal is bounded by the inferior epigastric artery, the inguinal ligament and the rectus sheath. In which position does each lie: artery laterally, ligament below and sheath medially; artery medially, ligament above and sheath laterally; artery below, ligament laterally and sheath above; or artery above, ligament medially and sheath below? The answer is artery laterally, ligament below and sheath medially.

Question 11290. On which ribs does the spleen rest, and at which line is its surface marking drawn? The answer is the ninth, tenth and eleventh ribs on the left, in the midaxillary line. Its long axis follows the tenth rib, and the organ descends when a person breathes in.

Question 11291. Name three structures that may be injured when a lap belt compresses the abdomen against the vertebral column, spoken as a list. The answer is the duodenum, the pancreas and the mesentery of the small intestine. The duodenum and the pancreas lie directly in front of the spine, and the mesentery can be torn by the sudden change in speed.

Question 11292. Which solid organ is most often injured in a blunt injury to the abdomen: the liver, the spleen, the kidney, or the pancreas? The answer is the spleen. It lies in the left upper quadrant behind the ribs and the stomach, and it is a soft organ with a thin capsule that tears easily. The liver is injured second most often.

Question 11293. A described ultrasound examination of the abdomen shows dark fluid lying between the liver and the right kidney, in the space where the peritoneum reflects from one to the other. Which space is shown, and why does fluid collect there? The answer is the hepatorenal pouch. It is the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity when a person lies on the back, so free fluid gathers there first.

Question 11294. Which of these pairs belongs together: the hepatorenal pouch with a person lying on the back, the rectouterine pouch with a person lying face down, a ruptured spleen with right shoulder pain, or a ruptured bladder with pain under the left ribs? The answer is the hepatorenal pouch with a person lying on the back, because that pouch is the most dependent part of the peritoneal cavity.

Question 11295. In which quadrant of the abdomen does the spleen lie: the right upper, the left upper, the right lower, or the left lower? The answer is the left upper quadrant. It lies behind the stomach and beneath the left dome of the diaphragm, above the level of the umbilicus.

Question 11296. A constructed illustration: a person has sudden lower abdominal pain and faints, and the abdomen is tender with signs of bleeding inside. Which structure has ruptured, and in which pouch does the blood collect first? The answer is a pregnancy in a uterine tube, with blood collecting in the rectouterine pouch. That pouch is the lowest part of the female peritoneal cavity.

Question 11297. Which of these pairs belongs together: a fractured pelvis with the membranous urethra, a straddle injury with the membranous urethra, a fractured pelvis with the bulbar urethra, or a stab wound of the loin with the appendix? The answer is a fractured pelvis with the membranous urethra. That part is fixed at the pelvic floor and tears when the ring of the pelvis breaks.

Question 11298. Which organ rises out of the pelvis and can be felt above the pubic symphysis when it is full, and lies behind that joint? The answer is the urinary bladder. When empty it lies entirely within the pelvis, and as it fills it rises into the abdomen and pushes the peritoneum off the front of the abdominal wall.

Question 11299. When the abdominal aorta ruptures below the kidneys, where does the blood usually collect: in the peritoneal cavity, in the retroperitoneal tissue, in the pleural cavity, or in the pelvis only? The answer is in the retroperitoneal tissue. The aorta lies behind the peritoneum against the vertebral column, so blood spreads through the loose tissue there and may present as back pain.

Question 11300. A described wound enters the left side of the chest in the eighth intercostal space in the midaxillary line and passes downward through the diaphragm. Which organ lies beneath that point: the stomach, the spleen, the left lobe of the liver, or the left kidney? The answer is the spleen. The left dome of the diaphragm rises to that level, and the spleen lies below it against the ninth to eleventh ribs.

To summarize, the peritoneum has a parietal layer that localizes pain sharply and a visceral layer that feels only stretch, and its folds, the greater omentum, the lesser omentum, the gastrosplenic ligament and the mesenteries, carry the vessels of the gut. The foregut is supplied by the celiac trunk, the midgut by the superior mesenteric artery down to the proximal two thirds of the transverse colon, and the hindgut by the inferior mesenteric artery, with the watershed at the splenic flexure. The bare area of the liver, the portal vein formed behind the neck of the pancreas, the third part of the duodenum that crosses the aorta, and the ascending colon that lies behind the peritoneum are relations worth hearing again. The right kidney lies lower than the left, the left renal vein crosses in front of the aorta, and the ureter crosses the pelvic brim in front of the common iliac bifurcation. In emergency anatomy the spleen is the organ most often injured by a blunt blow to the left upper abdomen, the right ventricle lies behind the sternum in the path of a wound beside it, the third part of the duodenum and the pancreas are compressed against the spine by a lap belt, and the membranous urethra is torn by a fractured pelvis while the bulbar urethra is torn in a straddle injury.
