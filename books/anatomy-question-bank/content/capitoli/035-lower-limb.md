# Chapter 35 — Lower Limb

This chapter covers the muscles of the lower limb below the hip: the hamstrings at the back of the thigh, the quadriceps at the front, the muscles grouped into the compartments of the leg, and the small muscles of the foot. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The muscles are named with their attachments, their nerve supply and their actions, and each clinical scenario is described as a constructed illustration rather than as a real patient. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3401. Which group of muscles occupies the back of the thigh, running from the ischial tuberosity down to the bones of the leg below the knee? The answer is the hamstrings. The group holds the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus. All three extend the hip and flex the knee, and the biceps femoris lies laterally while the other two lie medially.

Question 3402. Which of these structures gives origin to the hamstring muscles: the ischial tuberosity, the anterior superior iliac spine, the pubic symphysis, or the greater trochanter? The answer is the ischial tuberosity. All three arise from it, the biceps femoris sharing a common tendon with the semitendinosus while the semimembranosus takes origin by a flat expansion. The short head of the biceps femoris has an extra origin from the back of the femur.

Question 3403. Which hamstring muscle ends on the head of the fibula? The answer is the biceps femoris. Its tendon passes down the lateral side of the knee and splits around the fibular collateral ligament before it reaches the head of the fibula. A small slip commonly continues into the lateral condyle of the tibia, and that slip is a normal variation.

Question 3404. In the popliteal fossa, which muscles form the upper medial boundary and which muscle forms the upper lateral boundary? The answer is the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus medially and the biceps femoris laterally. The two medial tendons can be felt as cords beside the popliteal vessels. The fossa is bounded below by the two heads of the gastrocnemius.

Question 3405. Which nerve supplies the hamstring muscles: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the tibial division of the sciatic nerve, or the common fibular nerve? The answer is the tibial division of the sciatic nerve, for all of them except the short head of the biceps femoris. The femoral nerve supplies the front of the thigh, and the obturator nerve supplies the adductor group on the medial side.

Question 3406. Which hamstring muscle has a long rounded tendon that can be felt as a cord behind the medial side of the knee? The answer is the semitendinosus. Its name comes from that long tendon, which is about half the length of the muscle. It inserts on the upper part of the medial surface of the tibia, where it joins the pes anserinus.

Question 3407. True or false: the short head of the biceps femoris is supplied by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve. The answer is false. The short head is supplied by the common fibular division of the sciatic nerve, while the long head is supplied by the tibial division. Both heads share the same action at the knee joint.

Question 3408. A described dissection of the back of the thigh shows a muscle with two heads of origin, one from the ischial tuberosity and one from the back of the femur. Which muscle is shown, and which nerve supplies each head? The answer is the biceps femoris, with the long head supplied by the tibial division and the short head by the common fibular division of the sciatic nerve.

Question 3409. A constructed illustration: a runner feels a sudden tearing pain in the back of the thigh during a sprint, and a tender swelling appears. Which muscle is most often injured in that way? The answer is the biceps femoris, the most commonly strained of the hamstrings. The injury usually lies at the musculotendinous junction, and its management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 3410. Which of these actions belongs to the hamstrings: extension of the hip and flexion of the knee, flexion of the hip and extension of the knee, abduction of the hip, or dorsiflexion of the ankle? The answer is extension of the hip and flexion of the knee. The muscles therefore act at two joints, and only the short head of the biceps femoris acts at the knee alone.

Question 3411. Which muscle of the back of the thigh is the only one of the three that has two heads of origin? The answer is the biceps femoris. Its long head arises from the ischial tuberosity and its short head from the linea aspera of the femur. The other two hamstrings each arise by a single head.

Question 3412. The sciatic nerve runs down the back of the thigh. Which muscle does it lie immediately deep to at the upper end of its course? The answer is the long head of the biceps femoris. The nerve emerges from below the gluteus maximus and passes down upon the adductor magnus. It is crossed obliquely by the lower fibers of the gluteus maximus.

Question 3413. Which of these muscles rotates the flexed knee laterally: the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus, the semimembranosus, or the gracilis? The answer is the biceps femoris. The semitendinosus and the semimembranosus rotate the flexed knee medially because they lie on the medial side of the axis of the joint. Rotation of the knee is possible only while the joint is flexed.

Question 3414. A constructed illustration: an adolescent sprinter feels a sudden pain at the ischial tuberosity and cannot sprint, and the bony point is tender with a gap beneath it. What has most likely happened? The answer is an avulsion of the ischial apophysis together with the hamstring origin. In a young person the growth plate is the weakest link, so the bone gives way before the tendon.

Question 3415. Which muscle group of the back of the thigh is tested by asking a person to bend the knee against resistance while lying face downward? The answer is the hamstrings. The examiner feels the tendons tighten at the back of the knee as the resistance is applied. The same movement is used when the sciatic nerve and its divisions are examined.

Question 3416. A described transverse section of the mid thigh shows a large nerve lying against the back of a broad muscle, with a two-headed muscle behind it. Which structures are described? The answer is the sciatic nerve, the adductor magnus and the biceps femoris. The nerve lies between the adductor magnus in front of it and the hamstrings behind it.

Question 3417. Which statement about the hamstring part of the adductor magnus is correct: it is supplied by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve, it is supplied by the obturator nerve, it lies lateral to the biceps femoris, or it inserts on the head of the fibula? The answer is that the ischiocondylar part is supplied by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve, exactly like the hamstrings beside it.

Question 3418. Name the three muscles whose tendons form the pes anserinus on the medial side of the knee, spoken as a list. The answer is the sartorius, the gracilis and the semitendinosus. Their tendons join in a flat expansion that inserts on the upper medial surface of the tibia. The name means the goose foot, after the shape of the joined tendons.

Question 3419. A described dissection behind the knee shows a tendon that splits around a ligament on the lateral side and then attaches to a small bone below the joint. Which muscle and which ligament are shown? The answer is the biceps femoris and the fibular collateral ligament. The tendon divides so that it can rotate about the ligament during bending of the knee.

Question 3420. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the expansion of the semimembranosus tendon at the back of the knee, and what does it do? The answer is the popliteus, which unlocks the knee by rotating the femur laterally on a fixed tibia at the start of flexion. The expansion of the semimembranosus forms the oblique popliteal ligament over it.

Question 3421. A described dissection of the popliteal fossa shows a fat-filled diamond with a nerve lying nearest the skin, a vein and an artery beneath it, and a muscle forming the floor. Which muscle forms the floor? The answer is the popliteus. The nerve is the tibial nerve, and the vessels are the popliteal vein and the popliteal artery.

Question 3422. Which statement about the hamstrings is correct: the short head of the biceps femoris crosses the hip joint, the semitendinosus lies lateral to the biceps femoris, the semimembranosus lies deep to the semitendinosus, or the hamstrings are supplied by the obturator nerve? The answer is that the semimembranosus lies deep to the semitendinosus. The short head crosses only the knee, and the whole group is supplied by the sciatic nerve.

Question 3423. A constructed illustration: a deep cut across the back of the thigh in its lower third leaves a person unable to move the foot and without feeling over the sole. Which structure has most likely been divided? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It lies just deep to the long head of the biceps femoris at that level, where it is sheltered only by muscle.

Question 3424. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the biceps femoris with the head of the fibula, the semitendinosus with the greater trochanter, the semimembranosus with the tibial tuberosity, or the hamstrings with the femoral nerve? The answer is the biceps femoris with the head of the fibula. The semitendinosus ends on the medial tibia and the semimembranosus on the medial tibial condyle.

Question 3425. A constructed illustration: the examiner asks a person to bend the knee against resistance and palpates two cords at the medial side of the popliteal fossa. Which two structures are felt: the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus, the biceps femoris and the iliotibial tract, the gracilis and the sartorius, or the two heads of the gastrocnemius? The answer is the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus, the first lying superficial to the second.

Question 3426. Which muscle group forms the front of the thigh and is the great extensor of the knee? The answer is the quadriceps femoris. It has four parts, the rectus femoris and the three vastus muscles, and they share a common tendon that encloses the patella. All four parts are supplied by the femoral nerve.

Question 3427. Which nerve supplies the quadriceps femoris: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the common fibular nerve? The answer is the femoral nerve, which arises from the second, third and fourth lumbar spinal nerves. It also supplies the skin over the front of the thigh through its saphenous branch, which reaches the medial side of the leg.

Question 3428. Which part of the quadriceps is the only one that crosses the hip joint as well as the knee? The answer is the rectus femoris. It arises from the anterior inferior iliac spine and from a groove above the rim of the acetabulum. The other three parts arise from the femur, so they act at the knee alone.

Question 3429. In the front of the thigh, which part of the quadriceps lies immediately on each side of the rectus femoris? The answer is the vastus lateralis on the lateral side and the vastus medialis on the medial side. Both arise from the back of the femur and form the sides of the large muscle mass of the front of the thigh.

Question 3430. From which point does the rectus femoris arise: the anterior inferior iliac spine, the anterior superior iliac spine, the ischial tuberosity, or the pubic tubercle? The answer is the anterior inferior iliac spine, together with a reflection from the groove above the rim of the acetabulum. The anterior superior iliac spine gives origin to the sartorius instead.

Question 3431. A described dissection of the front of the thigh shows one muscle arising from the front of the hip bone and another lying directly upon the front of the femur. Which two parts of the quadriceps are shown? The answer is the rectus femoris and the vastus intermedius. The vastus lateralis and the vastus medialis lie beside them on either side.

Question 3432. Which part of the quadriceps lies deepest, directly upon the front and lateral surfaces of the femur? The answer is the vastus intermedius. It is hidden by the rectus femoris above it and by the other two vastus muscles beside it. Its fibers end in the deep surface of the quadriceps tendon.

Question 3433. A constructed illustration: the tendon below the kneecap is tapped and the knee jerks. Which muscle contracts, and which nerve carries that reflex? The answer is the quadriceps femoris and the femoral nerve. The response is lost when the third and fourth lumbar segments of the spinal cord or the nerve itself are damaged.

Question 3434. The quadriceps tendon attaches to which structures: the base of the patella and then the tibial tuberosity through the patellar ligament, the head of the fibula, the pes anserinus, or the medial malleolus? The answer is the base of the patella and then the tibial tuberosity. The patella is a sesamoid bone that lies within the tendon.

Question 3435. True or false: the patella is a sesamoid bone that develops inside the tendon of the quadriceps femoris. The answer is true. It lies in front of the knee joint and increases the leverage of the quadriceps. Its posterior surface carries the thickest layer of articular cartilage in the body.

Question 3436. Which small muscle lies deep in the lower part of the front of the thigh, arising from the front of the femur below the vastus intermedius and ending in the synovial membrane of the knee joint? The answer is the articularis genus. It pulls the synovial membrane upward during extension so that the membrane is not trapped in the joint.

Question 3437. A described radiograph of the knee seen from the side shows a small sesamoid bone in front of the joint, with a band above it and another band below it. Which structures are shown? The answer is the patella, the quadriceps tendon above it and the patellar ligament below it. The shadow of the femur lies behind the bone.

Question 3438. Which part of the quadriceps is most often strained in a sprinting injury, because it acts at both the hip and the knee: the rectus femoris, the vastus lateralis, the vastus medialis, or the vastus intermedius? The answer is the rectus femoris. Working across two joints makes it act over a longer range than the vastus muscles. The injury usually occurs at the musculotendinous junction.

Question 3439. The femoral artery passes down the front of the thigh toward the opening in the adductor magnus. Which part of the quadriceps lies immediately behind it in the lower part of its course? The answer is the vastus medialis. The artery rests upon that muscle within the adductor canal, whose roof is a strong membrane blending with the muscle.

Question 3440. Name the four parts of the quadriceps femoris spoken as a list. The answer is the rectus femoris, the vastus lateralis, the vastus medialis and the vastus intermedius. The first of them crosses the hip joint, and the other three arise from the femur itself. All four straighten the knee.

Question 3441. The patellar ligament runs from the lower point of the patella to which structure: the tibial tuberosity, the head of the fibula, the adductor tubercle, or the medial malleolus? The answer is the tibial tuberosity. It is the continuation of the quadriceps tendon after that tendon has passed over the patella. The medial and lateral patellar retinacula pass down beside it.

Question 3442. A constructed illustration: a person falls onto a flexed knee, and afterward the knee cannot be straightened actively while a gap can be felt above the patella. Which structure is torn? The answer is the quadriceps tendon. The patella is pulled downward by the intact patellar ligament, and management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3443. Which part of the quadriceps arises from the intertrochanteric line and the medial lip of the linea aspera and sends an expansion to the medial side of the knee? The answer is the vastus medialis. Its lowest fibers run almost horizontally and are thought to resist lateral displacement of the patella. That expansion forms the medial patellar retinaculum.

Question 3444. Which statement about the medial and lateral patellar retinacula is correct: they are expansions of the vastus medialis and the vastus lateralis, they are parts of the joint capsule alone, they are formed by the sartorius and the iliotibial tract, or they are ligaments running from the patella to the femur? The answer is that they are expansions of the vastus medialis and the vastus lateralis.

Question 3445. A described transverse section of the lower thigh shows the femur surrounded by muscle on three sides, with one thin sheet lying directly upon the bone and two larger masses beside it. Which parts of the quadriceps are shown? The answer is the vastus intermedius upon the bone, with the vastus lateralis and the vastus medialis beside it.

Question 3446. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the rectus femoris with the anterior inferior iliac spine, the vastus lateralis with the ischial tuberosity, the vastus medialis with the greater trochanter, or the vastus intermedius with the ischial spine? The answer is the rectus femoris with the anterior inferior iliac spine. The vastus lateralis and the vastus medialis arise from the back of the femur instead.

Question 3447. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the knee a person cannot actively straighten the knee, yet the hip can still be flexed. Which structure is most likely torn: the patellar ligament, the fibular collateral ligament, the iliotibial tract, or the medial meniscus? The answer is the patellar ligament, because the rectus femoris remains attached above and can still flex the hip.

Question 3448. A constructed illustration: an active adolescent reports pain and a bony swelling at the tibial tuberosity that worsens with sport and eases with rest. Which structure is involved, and why is that age group affected? The answer is the tibial tuberosity at the insertion of the patellar ligament, where the growing apophysis is pulled upon. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 3449. A described dissection separates the quadriceps tendon into layers above the patella. A superficial band comes from a muscle that crosses the hip, and a deep sheet comes from a muscle lying on the femur. Which two parts of the quadriceps are shown? The answer is the rectus femoris and the vastus intermedius.

Question 3450. Which of these muscles does not belong to the quadriceps femoris: the rectus femoris, the vastus medialis, the sartorius, or the vastus intermedius? The answer is the sartorius. It is the longest muscle of the body, runs from the hip bone to the medial side of the tibia, and is supplied by the femoral nerve as well.

Question 3451. Which compartment of the leg lies in front of the interosseous membrane and holds the muscles that lift the foot? The answer is the anterior compartment. Its muscles dorsiflex the ankle and extend the toes, and all of them are supplied by the deep fibular nerve.

Question 3452. Which nerve supplies the muscles of the anterior compartment of the leg: the tibial nerve, the deep fibular nerve, the superficial fibular nerve, or the sural nerve? The answer is the deep fibular nerve. It reaches the compartment beside the anterior tibial artery after winding around the neck of the fibula.

Question 3453. Which muscle lies immediately lateral to the anterior border of the tibia and is the strongest dorsiflexor of the ankle? The answer is the tibialis anterior. It arises from the upper lateral surface of the tibia and inserts on the medial cuneiform bone and the base of the first metatarsal. It also inverts the foot.

Question 3454. Which of these muscles lies in the lateral compartment of the leg: the fibularis longus, the tibialis posterior, the flexor digitorum longus, or the extensor hallucis longus? The answer is the fibularis longus, together with the fibularis brevis. Both evert the foot and both are supplied by the superficial fibular nerve.

Question 3455. In the lower part of the front of the leg, which structures lie between the tibialis anterior and the extensor hallucis longus? The answer is the anterior tibial artery and the deep fibular nerve. The vessels and the nerve run down that interval and cross the front of the ankle beneath the extensor retinaculum.

Question 3456. A described dissection of the calf shows a large muscle with two heads, a broad flat muscle deep to it, and a small muscle with a slender tendon between the two heads. Which three muscles are shown? The answer is the gastrocnemius, the soleus and the plantaris. All three of them are supplied by the tibial nerve.

Question 3457. Which muscle of the calf arises from the upper part of the fibula and from the soleal line of the tibia and does not cross the knee joint? The answer is the soleus. It lies deep to the gastrocnemius and joins it in the calcaneal tendon. Its many short fibers give it great power in standing.

Question 3458. A constructed illustration: after a long march a person has a tight and painful front of the leg, pain when the toes are passively curled, and numbness in the web space between the first two toes. Which compartment and which nerve are involved? The answer is the anterior compartment and the deep fibular nerve that supplies it.

Question 3459. Which structure passes forward through the interosseous membrane from the posterior compartment of the leg into the anterior compartment: the anterior tibial artery, the posterior tibial artery, the fibular artery, or the small saphenous vein? The answer is the anterior tibial artery. It passes through the upper part of the membrane and then descends upon it. The deep fibular nerve joins it lower down in the leg.

Question 3460. True or false: the calcaneal tendon is the common tendon of the gastrocnemius and the soleus. The answer is true. The two muscles are often called the triceps surae, and the plantaris tendon joins them on the medial side. The tendon inserts on the middle of the back of the calcaneus.

Question 3461. Which muscle forms the chief bulk of the calf and arises by two heads from the lower end of the femur? The answer is the gastrocnemius. It crosses the knee joint as well as the ankle, so it also helps to flex the knee. The small saphenous vein ascends between its two heads.

Question 3462. A described dissection behind the medial malleolus shows four tendons and a nerve passing into the sole. Which tendon lies closest to the bone? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior. Behind it in order lie the flexor digitorum longus, the posterior tibial artery, the tibial nerve and the flexor hallucis longus.

Question 3463. Which muscle of the deep posterior compartment is the chief invertor of the foot and inserts on the tuberosity of the navicular bone: the tibialis posterior, the flexor hallucis longus, the popliteus, or the flexor digitorum longus? The answer is the tibialis posterior. It arises from the back of the tibia, from the fibula and from the interosseous membrane. Its tendon also sends slips to the cuneiform bones and several metatarsals.

Question 3464. Which structure lies immediately behind the posterior tibial artery as both pass behind the medial malleolus? The answer is the tibial nerve. The order from the medial side is the tibialis posterior, the flexor digitorum longus, the artery, the nerve and the flexor hallucis longus. All of them pass beneath the flexor retinaculum.

Question 3465. Which muscle of the leg arises from the posterior surface of the fibula and the interosseous membrane and inserts on the base of the distal phalanx of the great toe? The answer is the flexor hallucis longus. Its tendon grooves the back of the talus and passes below the sustentaculum tali. It is a powerful flexor of the great toe.

Question 3466. Which statement about the nerves of the popliteal fossa is correct: the common fibular nerve lies medial to the tibial nerve, the tibial nerve supplies the short head of the biceps femoris, the common fibular nerve winds around the neck of the fibula, or the popliteal artery lies superficial to the tibial nerve? The answer is that the common fibular nerve winds around the neck of the fibula.

Question 3467. A constructed illustration: a blow lands on the lateral side of the knee and the head of the fibula, and afterward the foot cannot be lifted and the toes drag on the ground. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the common fibular nerve, which winds around the fibular neck at that point. The illustration describes a pattern of injury, not a real patient.

Question 3468. A described dissection of the calf shows a muscle lying deep to the gastrocnemius whose fibers run downward and backward into a broad aponeurosis that joins the calcaneal tendon. Which muscle is described? The answer is the soleus. It arises from the soleal line of the tibia and from the upper part of the fibula.

Question 3469. Which movement of the foot is lost when the muscles of the lateral compartment of the leg are paralyzed: eversion, inversion, dorsiflexion, or plantarflexion? The answer is eversion. The fibularis longus and the fibularis brevis turn the sole outward and are supplied by the superficial fibular nerve. The tibialis anterior and the tibialis posterior are the chief invertors of the foot.

Question 3470. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the tibialis anterior with the deep fibular nerve, the fibularis longus with the tibial nerve, the gastrocnemius with the superficial fibular nerve, or the tibialis posterior with the deep fibular nerve? The answer is the tibialis anterior with the deep fibular nerve. The other three muscles are supplied by the tibial nerve or the superficial fibular nerve.

Question 3471. What is the name of the strong fibrous band that runs down the back of the leg and attaches to the heel? The answer is the calcaneal tendon, also called the Achilles tendon. It is the thickest and strongest tendon in the body. The gastrocnemius, the soleus and the plantaris all contribute to it.

Question 3472. Which statement about the deep fibular nerve is correct: it passes deep to the extensor retinaculum and ends in the web space between the first two toes, it supplies the skin of the lateral side of the foot, it passes behind the lateral malleolus, or it gives off the sural nerve? The answer is that it passes deep to the retinaculum to the first web space.

Question 3473. Which vessel runs down the back of the leg between the two heads of the gastrocnemius, accompanied by the sural nerve, and ends in the popliteal vein? The answer is the small saphenous vein. It begins behind the lateral malleolus and ascends in the subcutaneous tissue. The sural nerve lies beside it and supplies the skin of the lateral border of the foot.

Question 3474. A described dissection shows the popliteal artery dividing at the lower border of the popliteus, with one branch passing forward through the interosseous membrane and the other continuing down the back of the leg. Which two vessels are shown? The answer is the anterior tibial artery and the posterior tibial artery.

Question 3475. Which of these muscles inserts on the calcaneus by the calcaneal tendon: the tibialis anterior, the gastrocnemius, the tibialis posterior, or the fibularis longus? The answer is the gastrocnemius. It acts with the soleus to plantarflex the ankle. The tibialis posterior inserts on the navicular bone and the fibularis longus on the base of the first metatarsal.

Question 3476. A constructed illustration: a laceration behind the knee leaves a person unable to stand on tiptoe and without feeling over the sole, while the foot can still be lifted. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the tibial nerve. The ability to lift the foot remains because the anterior compartment is supplied by the deep fibular nerve.

Question 3477. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the flexor hallucis longus with the base of the distal phalanx of the great toe, the flexor digitorum longus with the calcaneus, the tibialis posterior with the head of the fibula, or the popliteus with the tibial tuberosity? The answer is the flexor hallucis longus with the base of the distal phalanx of the great toe.

Question 3478. True or false: the plantaris has a small fleshy belly and a long slender tendon that inserts on the calcaneus, and it is absent in some people. The answer is true. The muscle is a feeble flexor of the knee and a weak plantarflexor, and its absence causes no loss of function. Its slender tendon is sometimes used by surgeons as a graft.

Question 3479. Which muscle of the body has the longest tendon in proportion to its belly: the plantaris, the gastrocnemius, the tibialis posterior, or the flexor hallucis longus? The answer is the plantaris. Its small belly lies above the lateral head of the gastrocnemius, and its slender tendon runs down between the two heads to the calcaneus. It is supplied by the tibial nerve.

Question 3480. A described specimen of the front of the ankle shows a muscle whose tendon crosses the joint to reach the medial border of the foot. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the tibialis anterior. Its tendon is the most medial of the tendons on the front of the ankle, and it inserts on the medial cuneiform and the first metatarsal.

Question 3481. Which statement about the deep fascia of the leg is correct: it forms septa that attach to the fibula and divide the leg into compartments, it covers only the back of the leg, it is continuous with the plantar aponeurosis alone, or it does not exist in the leg? The answer is that it forms septa attached to the fibula.

Question 3482. A transverse section of the leg at mid calf shows three compartments grouped around the fibula. Which compartment lies in front of the interosseous membrane, which lies on the outer side of the fibula, and which lies behind it? The answer is the anterior compartment in front, the lateral compartment on the outer side, and the posterior compartment behind.

Question 3483. What is the name of the thick fibrous band that stretches from the calcaneus to the bases of the toes and forms the tough sole of the foot? The answer is the plantar aponeurosis. It protects the vessels, the nerves and the muscles beneath it and helps to maintain the arches of the foot.

Question 3484. Which muscle of the first layer of the sole runs from the calcaneus to the medial side of the great toe: the abductor hallucis, the quadratus plantae, the adductor hallucis, or the flexor digiti minimi brevis? The answer is the abductor hallucis. It is supplied by the medial plantar nerve, a branch of the tibial nerve.

Question 3485. Which nerve enters the sole between the abductor hallucis and the flexor digitorum brevis and supplies the muscles of the medial side? The answer is the medial plantar nerve. It is a branch of the tibial nerve and also supplies the skin of the medial three and a half toes.

Question 3486. Which muscle of the sole lies in the second layer, arises by two heads from the calcaneus, and inserts into the tendon of the flexor digitorum longus, correcting its oblique pull: the quadratus plantae, the flexor digitorum brevis, the abductor hallucis, or the adductor hallucis? The answer is the quadratus plantae. It is supplied by the lateral plantar nerve and helps the long flexor to straighten its line of pull.

Question 3487. A described dissection of the sole shows the tendon of a long flexor dividing into four parts, and four small muscles arising from those tendons and passing to the extensor expansions of the toes. Which muscles are described? The answer is the lumbricals. The first is supplied by the medial plantar nerve and the other three by the lateral plantar nerve.

Question 3488. Which structures lie immediately deep to the plantar aponeurosis in the sole of the foot? The answer is the muscles of the first layer: the abductor hallucis, the flexor digitorum brevis and the abductor digiti minimi. The medial and lateral plantar nerves and vessels lie between the first layer and the second.

Question 3489. A constructed illustration: a person has pain under the heel that is worst on the first steps of the morning, with tenderness at the medial tubercle of the calcaneus. Which structure is involved? The answer is the plantar aponeurosis at its attachment. The condition is an overuse traction injury, and its management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 3490. Which statement about the interossei of the foot is correct: the dorsal interossei abduct the toes and the plantar interossei adduct them, both groups abduct the toes, both groups adduct the toes, or the dorsal group flexes the toes? The answer is that the dorsal interossei abduct and the plantar interossei adduct. Both groups are supplied by the lateral plantar nerve.

Question 3491. Which muscle of the sole flexes the great toe and has two heads, with a sesamoid bone in each of its tendons? The answer is the flexor hallucis brevis. It lies in the third layer and is supplied by the medial plantar nerve. The sesamoid bones lie beneath the head of the first metatarsal bone.

Question 3492. Which statement about the nerve supply of the lumbrical muscles is correct: all four are supplied by the medial plantar nerve, all four are supplied by the lateral plantar nerve, the first is supplied by the medial plantar nerve and the remaining three by the lateral plantar nerve, or all four are supplied by the deep fibular nerve? The answer is that the first lumbrical is supplied by the medial plantar nerve.

Question 3493. A described specimen of the sole shows a swollen thickening of an interdigital nerve between the heads of the third and fourth metatarsals. Which structure is described? The answer is an enlargement of a plantar digital nerve, a common cause of pain in the forefoot. The specimen is described as an illustration of the normal course of the nerve.

Question 3494. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the abductor hallucis with the medial plantar nerve, the abductor digiti minimi with the medial plantar nerve, the first lumbrical with the lateral plantar nerve, or the dorsal interossei with the medial plantar nerve? The answer is the abductor hallucis with the medial plantar nerve. The others are supplied by the lateral plantar nerve.

Question 3495. In which layer of the sole do the tendons of the fibularis longus and the tibialis posterior lie: the first, the second, the third, or the fourth? The answer is the fourth layer, the deepest one, which rests against the metatarsals and the interosseous muscles. The fibularis longus tendon crosses the sole to the first metatarsal.

Question 3496. Name two muscles of the sole that are supplied by the lateral plantar nerve, spoken as a list. The answer is the abductor digiti minimi and the quadratus plantae, and the interossei and the lateral three lumbricals may also be named. The medial plantar nerve supplies the muscles of the medial side instead.

Question 3497. Which tendon crosses the sole from the lateral side to insert on the base of the first metatarsal bone and the medial cuneiform, lying in the deepest layer of the sole? The answer is the tendon of the fibularis longus. It also helps to support the transverse arch of the foot, and its muscle lies in the lateral compartment of the leg.

Question 3498. True or false: the tendons of the flexor digitorum longus pass through openings in the tendons of the flexor digitorum brevis. The answer is true. The brevis tendon is split into two slips that let the long tendon pass through and then reunite behind it. The long tendon continues to the base of the distal phalanx.

Question 3499. In the sole the muscles are arranged in four layers. Which layer lies immediately deep to the plantar aponeurosis, and which lies against the metatarsal bones and the long tendons? The answer is the first layer in the first case and the fourth layer in the second, with the nerves and vessels running between the first and second layers.

Question 3500. Which small muscle lies on the dorsum of the foot, arises from the upper surface of the calcaneus, and divides into four tendons that join the long extensor tendons? The answer is the extensor digitorum brevis. It also has a part that goes to the great toe. It is supplied by the deep fibular nerve.

To summarize, the hamstrings are the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus, they arise from the ischial tuberosity, they extend the hip and flex the knee, and all of them except the short head of the biceps femoris are supplied by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve. The quadriceps femoris is the rectus femoris with the three vastus muscles, it is supplied by the femoral nerve, it extends the knee, and the rectus femoris alone crosses the hip and also flexes it. The leg is divided by fascia and septa into an anterior compartment supplied by the deep fibular nerve, a lateral compartment supplied by the superficial fibular nerve, and a posterior compartment supplied by the tibial nerve, with the gastrocnemius and the soleus forming the calf and joining in the calcaneal tendon. The sole of the foot holds four layers of muscle, the first three supplied mainly by the medial and lateral plantar nerves, and the fourth layer carrying the interossei together with the tendons of the fibularis longus and the tibialis posterior.
