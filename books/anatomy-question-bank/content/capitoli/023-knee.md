# Chapter 23 — Knee

This chapter covers the knee and the ankle and the foot, the two regions where the lower limb meets the ground and takes the whole weight of the body. It begins with the ligaments of the knee, the medial collateral ligament and the lateral collateral ligament, then the menisci, the patella and the joint capsule, and it goes on to the bones, the ligaments and the arches of the ankle and the foot. It holds one hundred questions, and every question states its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. Every item can be answered by ear alone, without a picture or a list to consult. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 2201. Which ligament of the knee runs from the medial epicondyle of the femur downward and forward to the medial surface of the tibia? The answer is the medial collateral ligament, also called the tibial collateral ligament. It is a broad flat band on the inner side of the joint. It checks a force that pushes the knee inward and opens the inner side of the joint.

Question 2202. Which of these four structures does the medial collateral ligament blend with: the lateral meniscus, the medial meniscus, the patellar tendon, or the iliotibial band? The answer is the medial meniscus. Its deep fibers attach to the outer margin of that plate of fibrocartilage. That attachment explains why the meniscus tears when the ligament is injured.

Question 2203. A surgeon passes a finger deep to the medial collateral ligament at the joint line. Which structure lies immediately deep to the ligament there? The answer is the medial meniscus, with the joint capsule between the two. The deep fibers of the ligament are fused to the capsule and the meniscus, so the medial meniscus is far less mobile than the lateral one.

Question 2204. A constructed illustration: a player takes a blow to the outer side of the knee, and the joint opens on the inner side. Which ligament is most likely torn, and what may it drag with it? The answer is the medial collateral ligament, often with the medial meniscus. The outside force drives the knee inward and stretches the inner side, and the bond between the two explains their injury together.

Question 2205. True or false: the medial collateral ligament lies outside the joint capsule and is not bathed in synovial fluid. The answer is true. It is an extracapsular ligament on the inner side of the knee. Its deep fibers do blend with the capsule, but the ligament itself lies outside the synovial cavity.

Question 2206. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the knee, cut from front to back, shows a dark band running from the inner side of the femur to the inner side of the tibia. Which structure is shown? The answer is the medial collateral ligament. Ligaments return little signal on magnetic resonance imaging and appear dark. Its tibial attachment lies well below the joint line.

Question 2207. Which movement does the medial collateral ligament mainly restrain: inward angulation of the knee, outward angulation, straightening of the knee, or rotation of the hip? The answer is inward angulation of the knee. The ligament tightens when the leg is pushed inward at the knee. Outward angulation is checked by the ligament on the outer side.

Question 2208. Within the medial collateral ligament, which part sends fibers that attach directly to the medial meniscus? The answer is the deep part, which blends with the joint capsule. It is these deep fibers, not the superficial band, that bind the meniscus. Their pull is what tears the meniscus along with the ligament.

Question 2209. Which statement distinguishes the medial collateral ligament from the lateral one: it is a rounded cord, it blends with the joint capsule, it lies outside the capsule, or it attaches to the head of the fibula? The answer is that it blends with the joint capsule. The ligament on the outer side is a rounded cord standing clear of the capsule.

Question 2210. Name the two bones to which the medial collateral ligament attaches, spoken as a list. The answer is the femur and the tibia. It begins on the medial epicondyle of the femur. It ends on the medial surface of the tibia, well below the joint line, so the whole inner side of the joint is braced.

Question 2211. Along the inner side of the knee, which three tendons spread out over the medial collateral ligament, and which structure lies deepest beneath them? The answer is the sartorius, the gracilis and the semitendinosus, with the ligament deepest. The three tendons fan out over the ligament as the pes anserinus. The ligament lies beneath that fan and takes its pull.

Question 2212. Which cord-like ligament runs from the lateral epicondyle of the femur down to the head of the fibula? The answer is the lateral collateral ligament, also called the fibular collateral ligament. It is a rounded cord on the outer side of the knee. It lies free of the joint capsule and is separated from the lateral meniscus.

Question 2213. Which of these bones does the lateral collateral ligament attach to below: the tibia, the fibula, the patella, or the first metatarsal? The answer is the fibula. Its lower end joins the head of the fibula, the slender bone on the outer side of the leg. The ligament sends no fibers to the tibia at all.

Question 2214. A constructed illustration: a blow lands on the inner side of the knee, and the outer side of the joint opens. Which ligament is torn, and which nerve may be stretched at the same time? The answer is the lateral collateral ligament, with the common fibular nerve. That nerve winds around the neck of the fibula just below the ligament, where it may be stretched.

Question 2215. Which structure lies immediately deep to the lateral collateral ligament and separates it from the lateral meniscus? The answer is the tendon of the popliteus muscle. It passes upward and inward through the gap between the ligament and the meniscus. That gap is one reason the outer meniscus is injured less often than the inner one.

Question 2216. A described dissection of the outer side of a knee shows a cord standing clear of the capsule, with a tendon slipping beneath it. Which ligament and which tendon are shown? The answer is the lateral collateral ligament and the tendon of the popliteus. The cord is separate from the capsule, unlike the ligament on the inner side.

Question 2217. True or false: the lateral collateral ligament attaches to the lateral meniscus. The answer is false. The lateral meniscus sends no fibers to that ligament. The tendon of the popliteus muscle lies between the two, and that separation keeps them apart. The medial meniscus, by contrast, is bound to the ligament on the inner side.

Question 2218. Which statement about the lateral collateral ligament is correct: the biceps femoris tendon splits around it, the medial meniscus blends with it, it fuses with the joint capsule, or it attaches to the tibia? The answer is that the biceps femoris tendon splits around it. The two parts of that tendon pass on either side of the cord, which stays free of the capsule.

Question 2219. Which ligament of the knee is the chief restraint against the knee bending outward, so that the joint opens on the outer side? The answer is the lateral collateral ligament. It tightens when the leg is pushed outward at the knee. The ligament on the inner side checks the opposite movement.

Question 2220. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lateral collateral ligament with the head of the fibula, the medial collateral ligament with the head of the fibula, the patellar tendon with the femur, or the iliotibial band with the medial tibia? The answer is the lateral collateral ligament with the head of the fibula. The medial ligament ends on the tibia instead.

Question 2221. A constructed illustration: after a knee is forced outward, the outer side of the joint is unstable and the foot cannot be lifted at the ankle. Which ligament and which nerve are involved? The answer is the lateral collateral ligament and the common fibular nerve. The nerve lies against the neck of the fibula just below the ligament, where it is easily stretched.

Question 2222. What name is given to the two C-shaped plates of fibrocartilage that sit on the upper end of the tibia within the knee? The answer is the menisci. Each lies on the articular surface of the tibia, one on either side. They deepen that surface and spread the load across it.

Question 2223. Which of these statements describes the shape of the medial meniscus: it is nearly a complete ring, it is a C-shaped plate, it is a flat disc, or it is a triangular bone? The answer is that it is a C-shaped plate. It is longer and more open than the lateral one. The lateral meniscus comes closer to a closed ring.

Question 2224. Why is the lateral meniscus more mobile: it is thinner, it is not tethered to the capsule as firmly, it has no blood supply, or it lies on the femur? The answer is that it is not tethered to the capsule as firmly. On the outer side the popliteus tendon separates it from the capsule, while the medial meniscus is bound along its whole rim.

Question 2225. Which structures hold the outer rim of each meniscus to the margin of the tibia? The answer is the coronary ligaments, short bands of the joint capsule. They run from the rim of the meniscus to the tibial margin. The medial meniscus is also bound to the deep fibers of the medial collateral ligament.

Question 2226. A constructed illustration: a person twists the knee while the foot stays fixed, and the joint locks so that it cannot be straightened. Which structure is most likely torn, and what is the mechanism? The answer is a meniscus, torn by rotation of the bent knee. The torn fragment slips between the surfaces and blocks extension. Locking of the joint is the sign described.

Question 2227. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the knee, cut across the joint, shows two dark triangular shapes with their points toward the middle of the joint. Which structures are shown? The answer is the menisci, seen in cross section. Their dark appearance follows from the dense fibrocartilage of which they are made.

Question 2228. A described section cut across the knee at the joint line shows a wedge of fibrocartilage on either side, fixed to the tibia below and free above. In which plane was the section cut, and why is the inner wedge less mobile? The answer is a transverse plane. The inner meniscus is bound to the capsule and to the deep fibers of the medial collateral ligament.

Question 2229. Which part of a meniscus heals after a tear: the outer rim, the inner edge, the whole plate, or none of it? The answer is the outer rim. That rim receives a blood supply from the capsule and is called the red zone. The inner part has no vessels of its own and is called the white zone.

Question 2230. True or false: a meniscus covers the whole of the articular surface of the tibia. The answer is false. Each meniscus covers only the outer part of that surface. The central area of the tibia is left uncovered by fibrocartilage. The plates deepen the surface rather than cover it.

Question 2231. Name the two horns of a meniscus, spoken as a list, and say where each is fixed. The answer is the anterior horn and the posterior horn. The anterior horn is fixed in front of the tibial surface and the posterior horn behind it. Short attachments anchor both ends to the bone.

Question 2232. A fragment of meniscus lies between the femur and the tibia and blocks extension, while its ends stay attached. Which injury is described: a bucket-handle tear, a radial tear, a horizontal tear, or a flap tear? The answer is a bucket-handle tear. A long strip splits from the inner edge, displaces into the joint, and keeps a bridge at each end.

Question 2233. Which bone is the largest sesamoid bone in the body, lying within the tendon of the quadriceps muscle in front of the knee? The answer is the patella. It is a triangular bone with its base above and its apex below. It is the only bone that develops inside a tendon.

Question 2234. Which of these structures continues below the apex of the patella to the tibial tuberosity: the quadriceps tendon, the patellar ligament, the iliotibial band, or the medial collateral ligament? The answer is the patellar ligament. It is the continuation of the quadriceps tendon beyond the bone. The quadriceps tendon itself attaches to the base above.

Question 2235. Which structure lies immediately behind the patella, between it and the femur, and what is its function? The answer is the articular cartilage of the patella, which faces the groove on the femur. It is the thickest layer of articular cartilage in the body. It spreads the pull of the quadriceps across the front of the knee.

Question 2236. A constructed illustration: a person kneels on a hard floor for a long time, and a fluid-filled swelling appears over the front of the knee. Which bursa is involved? The answer is the prepatellar bursa, which lies between the skin and the front of the patella. It is the bursa that takes the pressure of kneeling.

Question 2237. A described radiograph taken along the shaft of a bent knee shows the patella as a rounded shadow sitting between two ridges on the lower end of the femur. Which joint is shown, and which part of the patella takes the greater load? The answer is the joint between the patella and the femur. Its outer facet takes the greater load.

Question 2238. Which part of the patella meets the femur only when the knee is fully bent: the base, the medial facet, the odd facet, or the apex? The answer is the odd facet. It lies on the far inner edge of the articular surface. It comes into contact only in deep flexion, which is why it wears early in some people.

Question 2239. True or false: the patella increases the leverage of the quadriceps muscle. The answer is true. Standing away from the joint, it holds the tendon clear of the femur. That distance gives the muscle a longer lever arm, so the same pull has a greater turning effect.

Question 2240. Which of these pairs belongs together: the base of the patella with the quadriceps tendon, the apex of the patella with the quadriceps tendon, the base with the tibial tuberosity, or the apex with the femur? The answer is the base of the patella with the quadriceps tendon. The apex joins the patellar ligament below. The tendon above and the ligament below are one continuous band.

Question 2241. Which muscle of the thigh sends fibers into the inner border of the patella and pulls it inward during extension? The answer is the vastus medialis, whose lowest fibers are named the vastus medialis oblique. Those fibers slant down to the inner edge of the bone. They resist the outward drift of the patella.

Question 2242. When the angle between the line from the hip bone to the patella and the line from the patella to the tibial tuberosity is larger than usual, which is most likely: outward dislocation of the patella, inward dislocation, quadriceps paralysis, or a detached patellar ligament? The answer is outward dislocation of the patella. A larger angle directs the pull of the quadriceps more outward.

Question 2243. With the knee fully straight, the patella rests high against the front of the femur, and with the knee fully bent it lies lower within the groove. In which direction does the bone move as the knee bends, and why? The answer is downward, because the condyles of the femur roll backward over the tibia. The patella is carried with the quadriceps tendon.

Question 2244. What is the name of the fibrous sleeve that surrounds the knee joint, attached above to the femur and below to the tibia? The answer is the joint capsule. It is a strong fibrous layer lined on the inside by synovial membrane. It is incomplete in front, where the patella and its ligaments lie.

Question 2245. Which of these structures produces the synovial fluid of the knee: the joint capsule, the synovial membrane, the menisci, or the articular cartilage? The answer is the synovial membrane. It lines the inside of the capsule and covers the structures that lie within the joint. It secretes the fluid that lubricates the surfaces.

Question 2246. Which ligaments of the knee lie within the capsule but outside its synovial lining? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament and the posterior cruciate ligament. They are intracapsular but extrasynovial, wrapped in a fold of the synovial membrane. The synovial fluid therefore does not bathe them directly.

Question 2247. A constructed illustration: a knee that has been inflamed for weeks is swollen with clear fluid, and the fluid cannot be felt outside the capsule. Which structure produced the fluid? The answer is the synovial membrane, which lines the capsule. The fibrous capsule confines the fluid to the joint cavity. Its shape explains why the swelling appears where the capsule is thinnest.

Question 2248. A described view through an instrument inside the knee shows a smooth white lining over the inner wall of the cavity, with folds of tissue passing around two crossing ligaments in the middle. What is the white lining, and what are the folds? The answer is the synovial membrane, with the folds that wrap the cruciate ligaments. The membrane lines the cavity except at the articular surfaces.

Question 2249. Which opening in the capsule of the knee allows the tendon of a muscle to pass out of the joint: the opening for the popliteus tendon, the opening for the patellar ligament, the opening for the iliotibial band, or the opening for the medial collateral ligament? The answer is the opening for the popliteus tendon. No other muscle tendon leaves this joint.

Question 2250. True or false: the fibrous capsule of the knee is strengthened at the back by the oblique popliteal ligament. The answer is true. That ligament crosses the back of the joint and blends with the capsule. The back of the capsule is also reinforced by the tendon of the semimembranosus muscle.

Question 2251. Which bursa of the knee lies between the tendon of the semimembranosus muscle and the medial head of the gastrocnemius muscle? The answer is the semimembranosus bursa. It lies in the inner part of the back of the knee. It often communicates with the joint cavity, which is why a swelling there can follow an effusion.

Question 2252. Which structure is a large pouch of the synovial cavity of the knee that extends upward beneath the quadriceps muscle: the prepatellar bursa, the suprapatellar bursa, the deep infrapatellar bursa, or the semimembranosus bursa? The answer is the suprapatellar bursa. Being part of the cavity, it fills when the joint fills.

Question 2253. Which of these pairs belongs together: the prepatellar bursa with the skin over the patella, the prepatellar bursa with the joint cavity, the suprapatellar bursa with the tibia, or the deep infrapatellar bursa with the femur? The answer is the prepatellar bursa with the skin over the patella. It lies in the loose tissue just beneath the skin. It does not normally open into the joint.

Question 2254. A constructed illustration: weeks after a knee injury the joint remains swollen, the pouch above the patella bulges, and the knee is held slightly bent. Why does the knee rest in that position? The answer is that the capsule is most relaxed in slight flexion, so the cavity holds the most fluid there. Bending the knee a little keeps the pressure lowest.

Question 2255. Which bone of the ankle carries the weight of the body from the leg to the foot, sitting between the two malleoli? The answer is the talus. It has no muscle attached to it. It passes the whole weight of the body to the heel and the forefoot.

Question 2256. Which three bones form the ankle joint: the tibia, the fibula and the talus; the tibia, the fibula and the calcaneus; the talus, the calcaneus and the navicular; or the tibia, the talus and the navicular? The answer is the tibia, the fibula and the talus. Those two bones form the socket that holds the talus.

Question 2257. A described radiograph taken from the front shows a square socket formed by two bones above and a rounded bone held inside it. Which joint is shown, and which bone lies within the socket? The answer is the ankle joint, with the talus within the socket. The socket is formed by the tibia and the fibula. The rounded upper surface of the talus fits it closely.

Question 2258. The deltoid ligament spreads from the medial malleolus in several bands. Which bone does its deepest and strongest band reach, and what does that band hold? The answer is the talus, which the band holds against the inner side of the socket. The other bands reach the calcaneus and the navicular. The deep band is the strongest part of the ligament.

Question 2259. A constructed illustration: a foot rolls inward under the body, the outer side of the ankle swells, and the joint is tender in front of the outer malleolus. Which ligament is most likely torn? The answer is the anterior talofibular ligament. It is the weakest and the most often injured of the ligaments on the outer side, and the strain falls on it first.

Question 2260. Which bone forms the heel and is the largest bone of the foot? The answer is the calcaneus. It lies below the talus and projects backward to form the heel. The tendon of the calf muscles attaches to its back. It is the first bone of the foot to meet the ground in standing and walking.

Question 2261. Which ligament of the ankle lies furthest back in the group on the outer side, so that it is torn last when the foot rolls inward: the anterior talofibular ligament, the calcaneofibular ligament, the posterior talofibular ligament, or the deltoid ligament? The answer is the posterior talofibular ligament. In the common sprain the front ligament tears first.

Question 2262. A described stress radiograph taken with the foot pushed inward shows a wide gap on the outer side of the ankle. Which ligaments have failed, and what else must be considered? The answer is the anterior talofibular ligament with the calcaneofibular ligament. A gap that wide means more than one ligament has torn, so injury to the ligaments joining the two leg bones must be excluded.

Question 2263. Which structures pass behind the medial malleolus, and in what order from front to back? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior, the tendon of the flexor digitorum longus, the posterior tibial artery with its veins, the tibial nerve, and the tendon of the flexor hallucis longus. All lie in a fibrous tunnel beneath the flexor retinaculum.

Question 2264. Which bone of the foot lies between the talus behind it and the cuneiform bones in front, and receives the head of the talus in a socket? The answer is the navicular. It lies on the inner side of the midfoot. Its tuberosity can be felt on the inner border of the foot.

Question 2265. Which of these bones belongs to the midfoot rather than the hindfoot: the calcaneus, the talus, the cuboid, or the first metatarsal? The answer is the cuboid. The talus and the calcaneus form the hindfoot. The cuboid lies in front of the calcaneus and joins the fourth and fifth metatarsals.

Question 2266. Which of these pairs belongs together: the deltoid ligament with the medial malleolus, the deltoid ligament with the lateral malleolus, the anterior talofibular ligament with the medial malleolus, or the calcaneofibular ligament with the tibia? The answer is the deltoid ligament with the medial malleolus. The three ligaments of the outer side all arise from the lateral malleolus.

Question 2267. A constructed illustration: the foot is forced outward, the inner side of the ankle is injured, and the outer malleolus is broken. Which ligament and which bone are involved, and why do the two go together? The answer is the deltoid ligament and the lateral malleolus. The strong inner ligament resists the outward drive, and the outer bone gives way as the talus presses against it.

Question 2268. Which bony shelf of the calcaneus supports the talus from below and gives attachment to a strong ligament of the ankle? The answer is the sustentaculum tali. It projects inward from the inner side of the calcaneus. The spring ligament runs forward from it to the navicular bone.

Question 2269. Which statement about the joint below the ankle is correct: it is the main joint of turning the sole, it is a hinge that only bends the foot, it lies between the calcaneus and the navicular, or it is the same as the ankle joint? The answer is that it turns the sole inward and outward. It lies between the talus and the calcaneus.

Question 2270. A described radiograph of the foot taken from the side shows a large bone at the back forming the heel, a smaller bone above it, and a row of bones running forward. Which bones are shown, and which of them lies highest? The answer is the calcaneus, the talus and the metatarsals, with the talus highest. The arch descends from the talus forward to the toes.

Question 2271. The plantar fascia spreads from the heel to the front of the foot. Which structures lie immediately deep to it along the sole? The answer is the short muscles of the foot and the tendons that they cover. The fascia forms a tough band beneath the skin of the sole. It helps to hold up the arch of the foot.

Question 2272. What are the five long bones of the foot that run from the midfoot forward to the toes called? The answer is the metatarsals. Each has a base behind and a head in front. The first is the shortest and the thickest, and it carries the great toe.

Question 2273. Which of these numbers is the number of metatarsal bones in one foot: four, five, seven, or fourteen? The answer is five. The tarsal bones behind them number seven. The bones of the toes number fourteen, and the metatarsals themselves run from the midfoot forward.

Question 2274. A constructed illustration: a runner feels a sudden snap at the back of the heel, cannot rise on the toes, and a gap can be felt in the band above the heel. Which structure has ruptured? The answer is the calcaneal tendon, also called the Achilles tendon. It is formed by the calf muscles and attaches to the calcaneus, and the foot can no longer be pushed down.

Question 2275. Which tendon passes behind the medial malleolus and attaches mainly to the tuberosity of the navicular, holding up the arch of the foot? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior. It is the strongest support of the medial arch. When it fails, the arch drops and the foot flattens.

Question 2276. Which of these bones forms the keystone of the medial longitudinal arch: the calcaneus, the talus, the navicular, or the first metatarsal? The answer is the talus. It receives the weight from the leg and passes it to the arch behind and in front. The arch itself is completed by the calcaneus behind and by the navicular, the cuneiforms and the first three metatarsals in front.

Question 2277. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the ankle, cut across the joint just above the two malleoli, shows a dark band joining the tibia to the fibula. Which structure is shown, and what does it do? The answer is the tibiofibular ligament, which binds the two bones together. It holds the socket closed so that the talus cannot wedge the bones apart.

Question 2278. Which tendon turns under the cuboid bone on the outer side of the foot and crosses the sole to the inner border? The answer is the tendon of the fibularis longus muscle. It runs behind the lateral malleolus and grooves the underside of the cuboid. It ends on the first metatarsal and the medial cuneiform and supports the arches.

Question 2279. How many phalanges does the great toe have, and what are they called? The answer is two, the proximal phalanx and the distal phalanx. Each of the other toes has three. The missing middle bone is what makes the great toe short and thick.

Question 2280. Which small bones lie within the tendons beneath the head of the first metatarsal: the cuneiform bones, the sesamoid bones, the accessory navicular, or the cuboid? The answer is the sesamoid bones. Two of them lie beneath the head of the first metatarsal, within the tendon of the flexor hallucis brevis.

Question 2281. Which of these pairs belongs together: the medial longitudinal arch with the talus, the lateral longitudinal arch with the talus, the transverse arch with the calcaneus alone, or the medial arch with the cuboid alone? The answer is the medial longitudinal arch with the talus. The lateral arch is formed by the calcaneus, the cuboid and the fourth and fifth metatarsals.

Question 2282. Name three bones that take part in the medial longitudinal arch of the foot, spoken as a list. The answer is the calcaneus, the navicular and the first metatarsal. The talus rests upon the arch between them. The arch is held by the plantar fascia and by the tendons that run along the sole.

Question 2283. A described ultrasound scan of the sole of the foot shows a thick band running from the heel forward, swollen where it attaches to the heel. Which structure is shown, and why does it become painful? The answer is the plantar fascia, inflamed at its attachment to the calcaneus. It is stretched with every step as the arch flattens.

Question 2284. Which ligament runs from the sustentaculum tali to the navicular and is the chief support of the head of the talus? The answer is the plantar calcaneonavicular ligament, also called the spring ligament. It lies beneath the head of the talus. If it stretches, the head of the talus drops and the arch of the foot gives way.

Question 2285. Why has the talus no muscle attached to it: because it is covered by cartilage, because the socket of the ankle holds it, because it is too small for a tendon, or because it fuses to the calcaneus? The answer is that the socket of the ankle holds it. Almost all of its surface is articular, so tendons pass over it.

Question 2286. A described computed tomography scan of the ankle shows a fragment of bone separated from the back of the tibia and a second fragment from the outer malleolus. Which injury is shown, and which structure between the two leg bones must be assessed? The answer is a fracture of both malleoli, with the tibiofibular ligament between the bones. If that ligament is torn, the socket is unstable.

Question 2287. Which of these structures occupies the channel between the talus and the calcaneus on the outer side of the foot: the tarsal tunnel, the sinus tarsi, the spring ligament, or the plantar fascia? The answer is the sinus tarsi. It is a funnel-shaped channel running inward between the two bones. It contains fat, vessels and ligaments and can be felt in front of the outer malleolus.

Question 2288. Which ligament, shaped like a triangle, spreads from the medial malleolus to the talus, the calcaneus and the navicular? The answer is the deltoid ligament. It is the great ligament of the inner side of the ankle. It is much stronger than the separate ligaments of the outer side.

Question 2289. Which movements take place at the ankle joint: bending and straightening of the foot, turning the sole inward and outward, both of these, or neither of these? The answer is bending and straightening of the foot. The ankle is a hinge between the leg and the talus. Turning the sole inward and outward takes place mainly at the joint below it.

Question 2290. A break of the fibula high near the knee, far from the ankle, with tenderness on the inner side of the ankle, is explained by which mechanism: the force traveled up from the ankle, a direct blow, a knee dislocation, or an ankle that escaped injury? The answer is that the force traveled up from the ankle. The inner ligament of the ankle tore first, and the strain passed up the bone.

Question 2291. A described radiograph of an ankle shows a clear space between the lower ends of the tibia and the fibula, wider than it should be. Which structure has failed, and what happens to the socket? The answer is the tibiofibular ligament, which has torn. The socket widens and the talus can shift within it.

Question 2292. Which muscle of the outer side of the leg ends on the base of the fifth metatarsal, and what movement does it produce? The answer is the fibularis brevis, which turns the sole outward. The fibularis longus lies beside it and crosses the sole. Both lie in the outer compartment of the leg.

Question 2293. Why can the foot turn inward and outward as well as bend up and down: because the ankle has two axes, because the joint below it has one sloping axis, because the midfoot joints are rigid, or because the ankle is a ball and socket? The answer is the joint below the ankle, with its sloping axis. Being oblique, that axis turns the sole and swings the front of the foot.

Question 2294. A described photograph of the inner side of an ankle shows a rounded bony knob with a fan-shaped band spreading downward from it in three directions. Which bone forms the knob, and which ligament is the fan? The answer is the medial malleolus of the tibia, with the deltoid ligament. The band reaches the talus, the calcaneus and the navicular.

Question 2295. Which statement about the bones of the foot is correct: the first metatarsal carries two sesamoid bones beneath its head, the fifth metatarsal has no tuberosity, the cuboid lies in front of the navicular, or the talus takes no part in the arches? The answer is that the first metatarsal carries two sesamoid bones beneath its head. The cuboid lies in front of the calcaneus, not the navicular.

Question 2296. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the foot shows a fibrous band running from the heel to the toes, and another band rising behind the ankle. Which structures are shown, and which one is inflamed in a painful heel? The answer is the plantar fascia below and the calcaneal tendon behind. The fascia is the one that becomes inflamed at the heel.

Question 2297. Why does a fracture of the neck of the talus threaten the bone: because the bone has no vessels of its own, because its vessels enter through the neck, because it lies outside the ankle joint, or because it is covered by the plantar fascia? The answer is that its vessels enter through the neck. A fracture there can cut them off, and the bone may die and collapse.

Question 2298. Which extra piece of bone, present in some people, lies at the inner border of the foot above the tuberosity of the navicular and may be mistaken for a fracture? The answer is the accessory navicular bone. It is a common variant within the tendon of the tibialis posterior and can become painful after a strain.

Question 2299. Which statement about the fibula at the ankle is correct: it bears almost none of the weight and still completes the socket, it bears half of the body weight, it forms the medial malleolus, or it takes no part in the joint? The answer is that it bears almost none of the weight and still completes the socket. The tibia carries the load through the talus.

Question 2300. A described cross section through the lower leg shows a triangular bone on the inner side and a slender bone on the outer side, joined by a strong membrane. What is the membrane, and what does it do at the ankle? The answer is the interosseous membrane, which joins the two bones along their shafts. It holds them together as the socket grips the talus.

To summarize, the knee is braced by the medial collateral ligament, which blends with the capsule and the medial meniscus, and by the lateral collateral ligament, a cord separated from the lateral meniscus by the popliteus tendon. The menisci are C-shaped plates of fibrocartilage that deepen the tibial surface, heal only at their outer rim, and tear with rotation of the bent knee. The patella is a sesamoid bone in the quadriceps tendon that increases the leverage of that muscle and glides in the femoral groove, its odd facet meeting the femur only in deep flexion. The capsule is lined by synovial membrane, carries the cruciate ligaments outside that lining, and sends the suprapatellar pouch upward beneath the quadriceps. At the ankle the tibia and the fibula grip the talus in a socket, the deltoid ligament holds the inner side, and the three ligaments of the outer side are injured in the common sprain.
