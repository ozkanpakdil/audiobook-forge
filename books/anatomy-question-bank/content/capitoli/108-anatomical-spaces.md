# Chapter 108 — Anatomical Spaces

Anatomical spaces are the rooms of the body: the fossae of the skull, the compartments of the mediastinum, the recesses of the peritoneal cavity, and the fossae and canals of the limbs. This chapter describes the walls, the contents, and the openings of those spaces, together with the fascial compartments whose septa divide the limbs into groups of muscles. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. Most of these items therefore ask for a boundary or a relation rather than for a name alone. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10701. Which cone-shaped bony space of the skull holds the eyeball, its muscles, and the optic nerve, and is bounded above by the frontal bone and below by the maxilla? The answer is the orbit. Its roof separates it from the anterior cranial fossa, and its floor separates it from the maxillary sinus. The medial wall is the thinnest wall and the lateral wall the thickest.

Question 10702. Which of these openings lies between the lateral wall and the floor of the orbit: the optic canal, the superior orbital fissure, the inferior orbital fissure, or the infraorbital foramen? The answer is the inferior orbital fissure. It carries the maxillary nerve and the infraorbital vessels, which run forward in the floor of the orbit and leave by the infraorbital foramen below the eye.

Question 10703. The superior orbital fissure lies between which two parts of the sphenoid bone: the body and the lesser wing, the lesser wing and the greater wing, the greater wing and the frontal bone, or the maxilla and the zygomatic bone? The answer is between the lesser wing and the greater wing. The oculomotor, trochlear, ophthalmic, and abducens nerves pass through it.

Question 10704. A described coronal section through the face shows a narrow cavity divided in the middle by a vertical plate of bone and cartilage, with three curled bony shelves on each side wall and an opening below each shelf. Which cavity is described? The answer is the nasal cavity. The vertical plate is the nasal septum, and the curled shelves are the superior, middle, and inferior nasal conchae, which overhang the meatuses beneath them.

Question 10705. Which structure forms the midline partition of the nasal cavity, built from the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid above and the vomer below? The answer is the nasal septum. The septal cartilage fills the front of the partition between those two bones. A deviated septum is a common variant and narrows one nasal passage without disturbing the other.

Question 10706. Which of these air-filled cavities opens into the sphenoethmoidal recess above the superior concha: the frontal sinus, the maxillary sinus, the sphenoidal sinus, or the anterior ethmoidal air cells? The answer is the sphenoidal sinus. The posterior ethmoidal air cells open into the superior meatus just below it. The frontal, maxillary, and anterior ethmoidal cells all open into the middle meatus.

Question 10707. A described radiograph of the face after a blow to the eye shows the floor of the orbit sagging downward into the maxillary sinus, with a soft tissue shadow hanging from the broken edge. Which structure is most likely trapped there, and which nerve is at risk? The answer is the inferior rectus muscle, with the infraorbital nerve at risk. The thin floor gives way before the bony rim does.

Question 10708. The pterygopalatine fossa communicates with the orbit, the nasal cavity, the infratemporal fossa, and the middle cranial fossa. Which of these openings carries the maxillary nerve into the back of the fossa: the inferior orbital fissure, the sphenopalatine foramen, the foramen rotundum, or the pterygoid canal? The answer is the foramen rotundum.

Question 10709. Which small pyramid-shaped space lies behind the maxilla, in front of the pterygoid plates, and below the apex of the orbit, and contains the maxillary nerve and the pterygopalatine ganglion? The answer is the pterygopalatine fossa. Every wall of it is pierced by an opening, which is why a lesion there can cause pain in the face, the nose, and the palate at the same time.

Question 10710. Which of these structures lies in the infratemporal fossa, below and behind the maxilla: the maxillary artery, the internal carotid artery, the ophthalmic artery, or the facial artery? The answer is the maxillary artery. It crosses the fossa and gives off the middle meningeal and inferior alveolar branches there. The facial artery crosses the lower border of the mandible at the front.

Question 10711. A described lateral view of the skull shows a broad shallow depression on the side of the head, bounded above by a curved line and below by an arch of bone that runs forward to the cheek. Which space is shown, and which muscle fills it? The answer is the temporal fossa, filled by the temporalis muscle, which passes deep to the zygomatic arch to reach the jaw.

Question 10712. A constructed illustration: a deep cut in front of the ear divides branches of a nerve that fan out across the side of the face, and the forehead on that side cannot be wrinkled. Which space has been entered, and which nerve is injured? The answer is the parotid space and the facial nerve. The nerve divides inside the parotid gland, so a wound there can split it into separate branches.

Question 10713. Which of these pairs belongs together: the temporal fossa with the temporalis muscle, the pterygopalatine fossa with the masseter muscle, the infratemporal fossa with the orbicularis oculi, or the orbit with the medial pterygoid muscle? The answer is the temporal fossa with the temporalis muscle. The masseter lies outside the ramus of the mandible, and the pterygoid muscles lie in the infratemporal fossa.

Question 10714. Which space lies in the floor of the mouth above the mylohyoid muscle, between the tongue and the mandible, and holds the sublingual gland and the deep part of the submandibular gland? The answer is the sublingual space. It wraps around the back of the mylohyoid to reach the submandibular space, so infection can pass between the two.

Question 10715. Which muscle forms the floor of the submandibular space: the geniohyoid, the mylohyoid, the hyoglossus, or the digastric? The answer is the mylohyoid. It runs from the mandible to the hyoid bone and separates the sublingual space above from the submandibular space below. The digastric lies below it and does not form the floor.

Question 10716. True or false: the retropharyngeal space lies between the buccopharyngeal fascia in front and the alar fascia behind, and the danger space lies behind the alar fascia. The answer is true. The alar fascia is the thin sheet that divides the two. Because both spaces reach the upper chest, infection in the neck can travel down into the mediastinum.

Question 10717. Which space of the neck lies immediately behind the alar fascia and in front of the prevertebral fascia, and runs from the base of the skull down to the diaphragm? The answer is the danger space. It communicates freely with the posterior mediastinum, which is why infection in it travels so far before it is noticed.

Question 10718. Name the three main structures that lie together inside the carotid sheath of the neck. The answer is the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, and the vagus nerve. The artery lies medially, the vein laterally, and the nerve between and behind them.

Question 10719. A described cross section of the neck shows a wedge of fat lying between the muscles of the pharynx medially and the muscles of the jaw laterally, with the carotid sheath behind it. Which space is described, and why is it called the crossroads of the neck? The answer is the parapharyngeal space, which touches the parotid, the masticator, and the visceral spaces.

Question 10720. A constructed illustration: a collection of pus forms beside a tonsil and pushes the soft palate and the uvula toward the opposite side of the throat. Which space is involved, and which large vessel lies just outside its wall? The answer is the peritonsillar space, with the internal carotid artery nearby. That artery lies only a short distance lateral to the tonsil.

Question 10721. Which space lies in the middle of the chest between the two pleural sacs, behind the sternum and in front of the vertebral column? The answer is the mediastinum. It is divided into a superior part above the sternal angle and an inferior part below.

Question 10722. Which of these structures lies in the middle mediastinum: the heart and the pericardium, the thymus, the esophagus, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the heart and the pericardium. The middle mediastinum also holds the ascending aorta, the pulmonary trunk, and the phrenic nerves. The thymus lies in the anterior mediastinum, and the esophagus and thoracic duct lie behind the pericardium.

Question 10723. A described sagittal section of the chest shows a muscular tube running down behind the heart, with a large vessel to its left, a smaller tube behind it, and a chain of nodes beside it. Which part of the mediastinum is shown, and which structures lie in it? The answer is the posterior mediastinum, with the esophagus, the descending aorta, the thoracic duct, and the azygos vein.

Question 10724. A horizontal plane is drawn through the sternal angle and the disc between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae. Which important levels does that plane mark? The answer is the plane between the superior mediastinum and the inferior mediastinum, and the level of the bifurcation of the trachea and of the second costal cartilage. It is also where the arch of the aorta begins and ends.

Question 10725. A described frontal radiograph of the chest shows a dark space at each side of the heart that reaches down into the angle between the diaphragm and the chest wall. Which space is shown, and what is the lowest part of it called? The answer is the pleural cavity, and its lowest part is the costodiaphragmatic recess, which the lung does not fill during quiet breathing.

Question 10726. During quiet breathing, which space remains unoccupied by lung at the lower border of the chest: the costomediastinal recess, the costodiaphragmatic recess, the oblique sinus, or the transverse sinus? The answer is the costodiaphragmatic recess. The lower border of the lung never reaches the lowest point of the pleural cavity.

Question 10727. When fluid collects in the pleural cavity, which of these places is the lowest point of the space in a person sitting upright: the costodiaphragmatic recess, the costomediastinal recess, the root of the lung, or the oblique sinus? The answer is the costodiaphragmatic recess. It is the most dependent part of the pleural cavity when the body is vertical.

Question 10728. Which passage of the pericardial cavity lies behind the aorta and the pulmonary trunk and in front of the superior vena cava, and joins the two sides of the cavity? The answer is the transverse sinus of the pericardium, through which a tube can be passed behind the great vessels. The oblique sinus lies behind the left atrium instead.

Question 10729. Which of these boundaries defines the oblique sinus: the reflection of serous pericardium around the four pulmonary veins and the inferior vena cava, the gap between the aorta and the pulmonary trunk, the fold over the superior vena cava, or the space between the two layers of fibrous pericardium? The answer is the reflection around the pulmonary veins and the inferior vena cava. The sinus lies behind the left atrium.

Question 10730. Which recess of the mediastinum lies to the right of the midline behind the esophagus, bounded above by the azygos vein and in front by the right atrium, and appears as a soft tissue line on a frontal chest radiograph? The answer is the azygoesophageal recess. It is a pleural reflection rather than part of the esophagus, and enlarged nodes can obliterate it.

Question 10731. True or false: the mediastinum is a single closed cavity with its own lining, separate from the two pleural cavities. The answer is false. The mediastinum is a region between the pleural sacs, not a cavity with a lining of its own, and its structures lie in loose connective tissue.

Question 10732. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior mediastinum with the thymus, the middle mediastinum with the esophagus, the posterior mediastinum with the heart, or the superior mediastinum with the rectum? The answer is the anterior mediastinum with the thymus. The heart lies in the middle mediastinum and the esophagus in the posterior part.

Question 10733. Which of these structures lies in the superior mediastinum: the arch of the aorta, the left ventricle, the right atrium, or the pericardium? The answer is the arch of the aorta. The superior mediastinum also holds the thymus, the trachea, the esophagus, and the thoracic duct.

Question 10734. A described transverse section at the top of the chest shows a kidney-shaped opening bounded behind by a vertebra, on each side by a first rib, and in front by the top of the sternum. Which opening is shown, and what passes through it? The answer is the superior thoracic aperture, through which pass the trachea, the esophagus, the great vessels, and the nerves to the upper limb.

Question 10735. Which part of the peritoneal cavity lies below the diaphragm and in front of the stomach and the transverse colon, and reaches the space behind the stomach through the epiploic foramen? The answer is the greater sac, which forms most of the peritoneal cavity. The lesser sac lies behind the stomach and the lesser omentum and is much smaller.

Question 10736. Which of these structures forms the anterior wall of the lesser sac: the stomach and the lesser omentum, the pancreas, the diaphragm, or the left kidney? The answer is the stomach and the lesser omentum. The posterior wall is formed by the pancreas and the posterior abdominal wall.

Question 10737. Which structures bound the epiploic foramen: in front the free edge of the lesser omentum with the portal vein, behind the peritoneum over the inferior vena cava, above the caudate lobe of the liver, and below which part of the gut? The answer is below the first part of the duodenum. Those four boundaries make the foramen the only natural way into the lesser sac.

Question 10738. A constructed illustration: a loop of bowel slips through the opening behind the free edge of the lesser omentum and becomes trapped in the space behind the stomach. Which opening is involved, and which structure in its front wall is at risk of compression? The answer is the epiploic foramen, with the portal vein at risk.

Question 10739. Which space lies between the diaphragm above and the liver below on the right side of the abdomen, and is divided by the falciform ligament into a right and a left part? The answer is the subphrenic space. The right part is divided again by the coronary ligament into an anterior and a posterior part.

Question 10740. A described frontal view of the abdominal cavity shows a channel running down the outer side of the ascending colon on the right and another down the outer side of the descending colon on the left. Which channels are shown, and where does fluid in them collect? The answer is the paracolic gutters. Fluid in the right gutter runs down toward the pelvic cavity.

Question 10741. Which of these recesses of the peritoneal cavity lies between the right kidney and the right lobe of the liver: the hepatorenal pouch, the splenorenal recess, the rectouterine pouch, or the vesicouterine pouch? The answer is the hepatorenal pouch. It is the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity in a person lying face upward, so fluid gathers there first.

Question 10742. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supracolic compartment with the stomach and the liver, the infracolic compartment with the rectum, the lesser sac with the spleen alone, or the greater sac with the kidneys? The answer is the supracolic compartment with the stomach and the liver. It lies above the transverse colon, and the infracolic compartment below it holds the coils of the small intestine.

Question 10743. A constructed illustration: after an infection within the abdomen, fluid gathers in the lowest part of the female peritoneal cavity, behind the uterus and in front of the rectum. Which space is involved, and which two organs lie immediately in front of it and behind it? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, with the uterus in front and the rectum behind.

Question 10744. Which pouch of the peritoneal cavity lies between the rectum behind and the uterus in front in the female, and is the lowest point of the cavity when she lies face upward? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, also called the pouch of Douglas. In the male the corresponding lowest point is the rectovesical pouch.

Question 10745. Which of these peritoneal pouches lies between the urinary bladder and the uterus: the rectouterine pouch, the vesicouterine pouch, the hepatorenal pouch, or the omental bursa? The answer is the vesicouterine pouch. It is shallower than the rectouterine pouch, which lies behind the uterus instead.

Question 10746. True or false: the kidneys, the ureters, and the ascending and descending parts of the colon lie behind the peritoneum and are therefore called retroperitoneal. The answer is true. They rest against the posterior abdominal wall and are covered by peritoneum only on the front. The stomach and the spleen lie within the peritoneal cavity instead.

Question 10747. Which space surrounds the kidney and its capsule, holds the perinephric fat, is bounded by the renal fascia, and encloses the adrenal gland as well? The answer is the perirenal space. It is closed above and open below toward the pelvis. The fat lying outside the renal fascia occupies the pararenal space.

Question 10748. A described transverse section through the abdomen shows a large vessel resting on the bodies of the vertebrae, a gland crossing in front of it, and two bean-shaped organs on either side behind a thin membrane. Which region is shown, and which great vessels lie in it at that level? The answer is the retroperitoneum, with the aorta and the inferior vena cava.

Question 10749. Which wedge-shaped space of fat lies on each side of the anal canal between the skin below and the levator ani above, and allows the canal to expand as the bowel opens? The answer is the ischioanal fossa. Its medial wall is the anal canal and the levator ani, and its lateral wall carries the pudendal canal.

Question 10750. Which of these structures runs in the pudendal canal on the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa: the internal pudendal artery and the pudendal nerve, the sciatic nerve, the obturator nerve, or the femoral artery? The answer is the internal pudendal artery and the pudendal nerve.

Question 10751. Which space lies immediately behind the pubic symphysis and in front of the urinary bladder, is filled with loose fat and veins, and can be entered without opening the peritoneal cavity? The answer is the retropubic space, also called the space of Retzius. It lies below and outside the peritoneum, which is why the bladder can be reached there.

Question 10752. Which of these spaces lies immediately outside the renal fascia and contains only fat: the perirenal space, the pararenal space, the lesser sac, or the rectovesical pouch? The answer is the pararenal space. The perirenal space, by contrast, lies inside the renal fascia and holds the kidney, the adrenal gland, and the perinephric fat.

Question 10753. Which of these spaces of the pelvis lies between the urinary bladder and the pubic bones: the rectovesical pouch, the retropubic space, the rectouterine pouch, or the ischioanal fossa? The answer is the retropubic space. It lies outside the peritoneal cavity and below the peritoneum, while the rectovesical pouch lies behind the bladder in the male.

Question 10754. Which pyramid-shaped space lies between the upper part of the arm and the side of the chest, with its apex pointing up toward the root of the neck and its base formed by the skin of the armpit? The answer is the axilla. It holds the axillary vessels and lymph nodes and the cords and branches of the brachial plexus.

Question 10755. Which of these structures lies in the axilla: the axillary artery and the brachial plexus, the femoral nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the median cubital vein? The answer is the axillary artery and the brachial plexus. The cords of the plexus surround the second part of the artery.

Question 10756. Which space of the shoulder region is bounded above by the teres minor, below by the teres major, medially by the long head of the triceps, and laterally by the surgical neck of the humerus? The answer is the quadrangular space. The axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery pass through it.

Question 10757. A constructed illustration: a fall on the shoulder drives the head of the humerus downward and the person cannot lift the arm away from the side, with numbness over the outer part of the shoulder. Which nerve is injured, and through which space does it run? The answer is the axillary nerve, which passes through the quadrangular space.

Question 10758. A described dissection of the back of the shoulder shows two gaps, one bounded above by the teres minor and below by the teres major, and a second beside the humerus with the teres major above it. Which two spaces are shown, and what passes through each? The answer is the triangular space, with the circumflex scapular artery, and the triangular interval, with the radial nerve.

Question 10759. Which of these pairs belongs together: the quadrangular space with the axillary nerve, the triangular space with the radial nerve, the cubital fossa with the sciatic nerve, or the carpal tunnel with the ulnar nerve? The answer is the quadrangular space with the axillary nerve. The radial nerve passes through the triangular interval, and the ulnar nerve passes outside the carpal tunnel.

Question 10760. Which triangular hollow lies on the front of the elbow, bounded above by a line between the two epicondyles and on each side by the muscles of the forearm? The answer is the cubital fossa. The biceps tendon lies in the middle of it, with the brachial artery medial to the tendon and the median nerve medial to the artery.

Question 10761. Which of these structures lies in the cubital fossa: the brachial artery, the axillary artery, the radial artery at the wrist, or the ulnar nerve at the elbow? The answer is the brachial artery. It divides into the radial and the ulnar arteries at the lower border of the fossa.

Question 10762. Which structures pass through the carpal tunnel, and which nerve lies outside it? The answer is the tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis, the flexor digitorum profundus, and the flexor pollicis longus, together with the median nerve, while the ulnar nerve passes outside. The tunnel is bounded by the carpal bones and the flexor retinaculum.

Question 10763. A constructed illustration: a person wakes with numbness in the thumb, the index finger, the middle finger, and half of the ring finger, and the muscles at the base of the thumb are wasted. Which nerve is compressed, and through which space does it pass? The answer is the median nerve, which passes through the carpal tunnel beneath the flexor retinaculum.

Question 10764. A described dissection of the front of the wrist shows a small channel on the little finger side of the flexor retinaculum, between it and a small hook of bone, with a nerve and an artery running through it. Which channel is shown, and what does it carry? The answer is the ulnar canal, carrying the ulnar nerve and the ulnar artery.

Question 10765. Which triangular hollow lies on the back of the hand at the base of the thumb, is bounded by the tendons of two muscles, and becomes deepest when the thumb is pulled away from the palm? The answer is the anatomical snuffbox. The radial artery crosses its floor. The scaphoid and the trapezium are the bones beneath that floor.

Question 10766. Which tendon forms the medial boundary of the anatomical snuffbox: the extensor pollicis longus, the abductor pollicis longus, the extensor pollicis brevis, or the extensor indicis? The answer is the extensor pollicis longus. The abductor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis form the lateral boundary.

Question 10767. True or false: the radial artery and the scaphoid bone both lie in the floor of the anatomical snuffbox, so a fracture of the scaphoid can cause tenderness there. The answer is true. The hollow is felt between the thumb tendons, and the artery crosses the bone. Tenderness in the hollow after a fall is a reason to look for a fracture.

Question 10768. Which triangular space lies in the upper part of the front of the thigh, bounded above by the inguinal ligament, laterally by the sartorius, and medially by the adductor longus? The answer is the femoral triangle. From lateral to medial its contents are the femoral nerve, the femoral artery, and the femoral vein, with lymph nodes beside the vein.

Question 10769. Which structure lies most laterally within the femoral triangle: the femoral nerve, the femoral artery, the femoral vein, or the femoral canal? The answer is the femoral nerve. It lies outside the femoral sheath and lateral to the artery. The femoral canal is the smallest and most medial compartment of the sheath.

Question 10770. Which compartment of the femoral sheath lies most medially, contains lymph nodes and fat, and is the route by which a femoral hernia descends? The answer is the femoral canal. Its upper opening is the femoral ring, bounded in front by the inguinal ligament and medially by the lacunar ligament. The femoral vein lies just lateral to it.

Question 10771. A constructed illustration: a soft swelling appears below and lateral to the pubic tubercle in the groin crease and enlarges on coughing. Through which opening has it passed, and which structure lies immediately medial to that opening? The answer is the femoral ring, with the lacunar ligament immediately medial to it.

Question 10772. A described dissection of the medial side of the thigh shows a narrow tunnel running from the apex of the femoral triangle to an opening in a broad tendon above the knee, carrying an artery, a vein, and a nerve. Which tunnel is shown, and what forms its roof? The answer is the adductor canal, roofed by the sartorius and the vasto adductor membrane.

Question 10773. Which diamond-shaped hollow lies behind the knee, bounded above by the tendons of the hamstring muscles and below by the two heads of the gastrocnemius? The answer is the popliteal fossa. It contains the popliteal artery and vein, the tibial and common fibular nerves, and fat, and its floor is the back of the femur and the knee joint.

Question 10774. Which of these structures lies deepest in the popliteal fossa: the popliteal artery, the popliteal vein, the tibial nerve, or the small saphenous vein? The answer is the popliteal artery, which lies against the capsule of the knee joint. The popliteal vein lies nearer the skin than the artery, and the tibial nerve lies nearest the skin of the three.

Question 10775. Name the two nerves and the main artery that lie in the popliteal fossa. The answer is the tibial nerve, the common fibular nerve, and the popliteal artery. The tibial nerve runs straight down through the fossa and into the back of the leg. The common fibular nerve follows the tendon of the biceps femoris toward the neck of the fibula.

Question 10776. Which fascial compartment of the arm lies in front of the humerus and between the medial and lateral intermuscular septa, and holds the biceps brachii, the brachialis, and the coracobrachialis? The answer is the anterior compartment of the arm. All three muscles are supplied by the musculocutaneous nerve.

Question 10777. Which nerve supplies the muscles of the posterior compartment of the arm: the musculocutaneous nerve, the radial nerve, the median nerve, or the ulnar nerve? The answer is the radial nerve. It runs in the spiral groove against the back of the humerus between the heads of the triceps, so a fracture of the middle of the shaft can injure it.

Question 10778. Which structure separates the anterior and the posterior compartments of the arm on the medial side, and which nerve crosses it from behind to reach the front? The answer is the medial intermuscular septum, with the ulnar nerve. The nerve passes from the posterior compartment through the septum just above the elbow.

Question 10779. A described transverse section of the forearm shows a thick sleeve of fascia, two bones joined by a membrane, and a group of muscles in front of the membrane whose tendons point toward the wrist. Which compartment is described, and which nerve supplies most of it? The answer is the anterior compartment, supplied mainly by the median nerve.

Question 10780. Which compartment of the hand lies between the metacarpal bones, is closed by deep fascia in front and behind, and holds the muscles that spread the fingers apart and draw them together? The answer is the interosseous compartment. The dorsal interossei abduct the fingers and the palmar interossei adduct them, and the ulnar nerve supplies both groups.

Question 10781. Which of these structures lies in the posterior compartment of the forearm: the extensor digitorum, the flexor digitorum profundus, the pronator teres, or the palmaris longus? The answer is the extensor digitorum. The compartment is supplied by the radial nerve through its deep branch, the posterior interosseous nerve. The other three muscles lie in the anterior compartment.

Question 10782. A constructed illustration: after a fracture of the upper forearm the limb becomes tense and painful when the fingers are stretched, and the radial pulse is still present. Which compartment is under pressure, and which nerves are threatened? The answer is the anterior compartment of the forearm, where the median and ulnar nerves and the flexor muscles are at risk.

Question 10783. True or false: the deep fascia of the thigh, the fascia lata, is thickened on the lateral side as the iliotibial tract, which receives the insertion of the gluteus maximus and the tensor fasciae latae. The answer is true. The tract runs down to the lateral condyle of the tibia, and the fascia sends septa inward to form the thigh compartments.

Question 10784. Which compartment of the thigh lies in front of the femur and between the lateral and medial intermuscular septa, holds the quadriceps femoris and the sartorius, and is supplied by the femoral nerve? The answer is the anterior compartment of the thigh. Its muscles extend the knee, and the femoral vessels run in its upper part.

Question 10785. Which nerve supplies the muscles of the medial compartment of the thigh: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the tibial nerve? The answer is the obturator nerve. It enters the thigh through the obturator canal and supplies the adductor group. The femoral nerve supplies the anterior compartment instead.

Question 10786. Which opening in the tendon of the adductor magnus carries the femoral artery and vein out of the adductor canal into the popliteal fossa, where the artery takes the name popliteal? The answer is the adductor hiatus. It lies just above the knee, and the vessels change their name as they pass through it.

Question 10787. A described transverse section through the middle of the thigh shows a large group of muscles behind a single bone, separated from the front and the medial groups by septa, with a long nerve running down among them. Which compartment is shown, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the posterior compartment, supplied by the tibial part of the sciatic nerve.

Question 10788. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the front of the leg the limb becomes tense and shiny, and the person cannot lift the foot or the toes and cannot feel the skin between the first two toes. Which compartment is under pressure, and which nerve is failing? The answer is the anterior compartment of the leg, with the deep fibular nerve.

Question 10789. Which compartment of the leg holds the fibularis longus and the fibularis brevis, lies against the lateral surface of the fibula, and is supplied by the superficial fibular nerve? The answer is the lateral compartment. Its tendons pass behind the lateral malleolus, and the tendon of the longus crosses the sole of the foot.

Question 10790. Which of these nerves supplies the deep posterior compartment of the leg: the tibial nerve, the superficial fibular nerve, the deep fibular nerve, or the saphenous nerve? The answer is the tibial nerve. It runs down with the posterior tibial vessels and supplies the flexor muscles of the ankle and the toes. The deep fibular nerve supplies the anterior compartment instead.

Question 10791. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior compartment of the leg with the deep fibular nerve, the lateral compartment with the tibial nerve, the posterior compartment with the superficial fibular nerve, or the anterior compartment of the forearm with the radial nerve? The answer is the anterior compartment of the leg with the deep fibular nerve. The lateral compartment is supplied by the superficial fibular nerve.

Question 10792. Name three structures that pass through the carpal tunnel. The answer is the median nerve and the tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis and the flexor digitorum profundus. The tendon of the flexor pollicis longus passes through it as well. The ulnar nerve and the ulnar artery pass outside it in the ulnar canal.

Question 10793. Which of these muscles lies in the deep part of the posterior compartment of the leg: the flexor hallucis longus, the gastrocnemius, the soleus, or the plantaris? The answer is the flexor hallucis longus. It lies against the back of the fibula and the interosseous membrane with the tibialis posterior and the flexor digitorum longus. The other three lie in the superficial part.

Question 10794. A described dissection behind the medial malleolus shows a tunnel roofed by a thick band of fascia, with a nerve, an artery, and several long tendons passing through it into the sole. Which tunnel is shown, and why is it a site of nerve compression? The answer is the tarsal tunnel, the narrow gateway between the leg and the sole, where the tibial nerve divides.

Question 10795. Which statement about the interosseous membrane of the leg is correct: it separates the anterior compartment from the posterior compartment and lets the anterior tibial vessels through an opening above, it separates the lateral compartment from the anterior compartment, it is pierced by nerves only, or it is absent below the ankle? The answer is the first statement.

Question 10796. Which compartment of the foot lies in the middle of the sole between the medial and the lateral compartments, holds the flexor digitorum brevis and the tendons of the long flexors, and is roofed by a septum? The answer is the central compartment of the sole. The interosseous compartment lies above it, and the medial compartment holds the muscles of the great toe.

Question 10797. Which of these structures lies in the lateral compartment of the sole: the abductor digiti minimi, the abductor hallucis, the flexor digitorum brevis, or the dorsal interossei? The answer is the abductor digiti minimi, which lies along the outer border of the foot with the flexor digiti minimi brevis and the opponens digiti minimi. The abductor hallucis lies in the medial compartment.

Question 10798. A constructed illustration: a person reports burning pain and tingling along the sole and the outer side of the heel, worse after standing, with weakness of the small muscles of the foot. Which nerve is compressed, and where does it lie? The answer is the tibial nerve, in the tarsal tunnel behind and below the medial malleolus.

Question 10799. In a transverse section through the middle of the leg, which compartment lies immediately behind the interosseous membrane, and which compartment lies between the anterior compartment and the skin on the outer side of the limb? The answer is the deep posterior compartment behind the membrane, and the lateral compartment on the outer side.

Question 10800. Which compartment of the hand holds the muscles of the thumb and lies on the outer side of the palm beneath the thenar fascia: the thenar compartment, the hypothenar compartment, the adductor compartment, or the midpalmar space? The answer is the thenar compartment. The hypothenar compartment lies on the little finger side, and the adductor compartment lies deeper against the palm.

To summarize, an anatomical space is defined by its walls, its contents, and the openings that connect it to its neighbors, and that triad is what every question in this chapter tested. The orbit, the nasal cavity, the fossae of the head and neck, the divisions of the mediastinum, the greater and lesser sacs with the epiploic foramen, the peritoneal and pelvic recesses, and the fossae and canals of the limbs each have a short list of boundaries worth saying aloud. The fascial compartments of the limbs are closed rooms bounded by deep fascia, intermuscular septa, bones, and interosseous membranes, and each contains a named group of muscles with one dominant nerve: the musculocutaneous nerve in the front of the arm, the radial nerve behind it, the obturator nerve in the medial thigh, and the deep fibular nerve in the front of the leg with the tibial nerve behind it. Pressure inside a compartment threatens its muscles and its nerve, while disease in a space threatens whatever passes through it, so the boundaries and the contents are the facts to keep.
