# Chapter 13 — Pectoral Girdle

The pectoral girdle is the bony ring that carries the upper limb, and this chapter takes it apart piece by piece. It covers the clavicle, the scapula with its spine and fossae, the acromion, the coracoid process, and the glenoid cavity, in that order, with the relations and muscle attachments of each. It holds one hundred questions, and every one of them gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. The girdle has only two bones and two small joints, yet it is the most mobile part of the skeleton, which is why its anatomy is worth hearing said aloud. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 1201. Which long bone lies across the root of the neck just beneath the skin and forms the only bony link between the upper limb and the trunk? The answer is the clavicle. Its medial end meets the manubrium of the sternum and its lateral end meets the acromion. Because it lies under the skin along its whole length, it is easily felt and easily broken.

Question 1202. Which part of the clavicle is enlarged and roughly quadrilateral: the medial end, the lateral end, the middle third, or the whole shaft? The answer is the medial end. It is the thicker end, and it articulates with the manubrium of the sternum at the sternoclavicular joint. The lateral end is flattened where it meets the acromion.

Question 1203. Which curve of the clavicle is convex forward, the medial part or the lateral part? The answer is the medial part. The bone is shaped like a flattened S, with the medial two thirds convex forward and the lateral one third concave forward. That reversed curve is one reason the bone breaks so often in its middle.

Question 1204. A described radiograph of the shoulder region shows a horizontal bone whose lateral third carries a small downward projection near its posterior border. Which bony point is shown? The answer is the conoid tubercle. It lies on the inferior surface of the lateral third of the clavicle at the back, and the conoid ligament attaches to it. The trapezoid line lies in front of it.

Question 1205. Which of these structures occupies the groove on the inferior surface of the middle third of the clavicle: the subclavius muscle, the pectoralis major, the deltoid, or the trapezius? The answer is the subclavius. It lies in the subclavian groove and pulls the clavicle downward and forward. The other three muscles attach nearer the ends of the bone.

Question 1206. Which muscle arises from the anterior surface of the medial half of the clavicle, close to the sternum? The answer is the pectoralis major. Its clavicular head takes origin from the front of the medial half of the bone. The sternocleidomastoid attaches higher, on the superior surface of the medial third.

Question 1207. Which structures pass behind the clavicle in the narrow space between the bone and the first rib? The answer is the subclavian artery and vein together with the brachial plexus. They cross the first rib behind the middle of the clavicle. The vein lies in front of the artery, and the plexus lies behind the artery.

Question 1208. Which bone of the pectoral girdle lies most anteriorly and can be felt along its whole length: the clavicle, the scapula, the humerus, or the first rib? The answer is the clavicle. The scapula lies behind the ribs, so only its spine and its acromion can be felt. The humerus is covered by muscle.

Question 1209. A constructed illustration: a cyclist falls on the point of the shoulder, and afterward the shoulder on that side droops and the arm cannot be raised. Which bone of the girdle is most likely broken, and at which part? The answer is the clavicle, usually at its middle third. That part lies between the two curves and is the weakest point of the bone.

Question 1210. Which bone of the human body is the first to begin ossification, starting in the sixth week of development? The answer is the clavicle. It begins to form in membrane rather than in cartilage, which is unusual for a long bone. Its medial end adds a secondary center much later.

Question 1211. The clavicle acts as a strut. Which of these is the main consequence of losing it: the shoulder falls forward and toward the midline, the arm cannot bend at the elbow, the scapula fuses to the ribs, or the hand loses its grip? The answer is that the shoulder falls forward and toward the midline. Without the strut the limb moves inward, and the scapula is carried with it.

Question 1212. True or false: the clavicle articulates directly with the first rib. The answer is false. It articulates with the manubrium of the sternum at its medial end and with the acromion of the scapula at its lateral end. The first rib lies below it, joined to it only by the costoclavicular ligament.

Question 1213. A described CT scan of the shoulder shows the medial end of the clavicle sitting in a shallow socket on the upper corner of the sternum, with a disc between the two surfaces. Which joint is shown, and what kind of joint is it? The answer is the sternoclavicular joint, a synovial saddle joint with an articular disc. It is the only synovial joint between the upper limb and the axial skeleton.

Question 1214. In the sternoclavicular joint, which structure is the chief restraint against upward displacement of the medial end of the clavicle: the costoclavicular ligament, the interclavicular ligament, the articular disc, or the joint capsule? The answer is the costoclavicular ligament. It runs from the first rib and its cartilage to the impression on the inferior surface of the medial end. The disc and the capsule help, but this ligament is the strongest.

Question 1215. Which ligament attaches at the rough oval depression on the inferior surface of the medial end of the clavicle, and what is that depression called? The answer is the costoclavicular ligament, attaching at the impression for the costoclavicular ligament, also called the rhomboid fossa. It is the strongest bond between the clavicle and the first rib.

Question 1216. Which large nervous structure passes immediately behind the middle of the clavicle and lies at risk when that bone is fractured? The answer is the brachial plexus. It leaves the neck between the scalene muscles and runs behind the clavicle into the armpit. The subclavian artery and vein travel in the same narrow space.

Question 1217. Which muscle attaches to the posterior border of the lateral third of the clavicle: the trapezium, the trapezius, the deltoid, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the trapezius. The deltoid takes origin from the anterior border of the same lateral third. The two muscles therefore pull the outer end of the clavicle in opposite directions.

Question 1218. A constructed illustration: after a fall, a patient holds the arm still, and the outer fragment of a broken clavicle lies lower and nearer the midline than the inner fragment. Which two forces explain that displacement? The answer is the pull of the sternocleidomastoid on the inner fragment and the weight of the arm on the outer one. The inner fragment is lifted, and the outer one sags.

Question 1219. Which joint does the lateral end of the clavicle form, and with which bone: the acromioclavicular joint with the scapula, the sternoclavicular joint with the sternum, the glenohumeral joint with the humerus, or the costoclavicular joint with the first rib? The answer is the acromioclavicular joint with the scapula. It is a small plane joint at the top of the shoulder.

Question 1220. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the shoulder shows a small fibrous band passing from the coracoid process upward and laterally to the underside of the clavicle. Which ligament is shown, and what does it prevent? The answer is the coracoclavicular ligament. Its two parts, the conoid and the trapezoid, hold the lateral end of the clavicle down. It stops the clavicle from riding up over the acromion.

Question 1221. Which muscle takes origin from the superior surface of the medial third of the clavicle and helps to turn the face to the opposite side? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid. Its clavicular head begins on that part of the bone and joins the sternal head. The two heads act on the head and the neck, not on the arm.

Question 1222. During raising the arm above the head, what movement does the clavicle perform about its own long axis: it rotates backward, it stays still, it rotates forward, or it dislocates? The answer is that it rotates backward. The clavicle turns at the sternoclavicular joint as the scapula rotates upward. That rotation adds range to the lift of the arm.

Question 1223. Which of these pairs belongs together: the conoid tubercle with the conoid ligament, the conoid tubercle with the trapezoid ligament, the subclavian groove with the trapezoid ligament, or the rhomboid fossa with the conoid ligament? The answer is the conoid tubercle with the conoid ligament. The trapezoid ligament attaches to the trapezoid line, and the rhomboid fossa holds the costoclavicular ligament.

Question 1224. Which muscle lies immediately above the medial end of the clavicle, and which joint lies immediately below its lateral end? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid above the medial end and the acromioclavicular joint below the lateral end. Both can be felt through the skin in a thin person. The clavicle separates them.

Question 1225. A constructed illustration: a large newborn cannot pass through the birth canal, and after delivery the baby does not move one arm and a crack is felt over the clavicle. Which bone is injured, and why is that bone the usual one? The answer is the clavicle, compressed against the maternal bone as the shoulders are delivered. It lies just under the skin, so it is the first bone to give way.

Question 1226. A described radiograph taken from above the shoulder shows the clavicle crossing the upper chest, and a second line of bone running from its middle toward the neck. Which two bones are shown, and what does the second line represent? The answer is the clavicle and the first rib. The first rib lies below and behind the clavicle, so it is seen through it. A break would interrupt its smooth upper border.

Question 1227. Which statement about the ossification of the clavicle is correct: it begins in membrane, it begins in cartilage, it has no secondary center, or it ossifies from the sternal end outward? The answer is that it begins in membrane. It is the first bone to ossify and the only long bone to form in this way. A secondary center appears at the sternal end much later.

Question 1228. Name the two ligaments that together form the coracoclavicular ligament, spoken as a list. The answer is the conoid ligament and the trapezoid ligament. They run from the coracoid process of the scapula to the inferior surface of the lateral third of the clavicle. Together they suspend the weight of the upper limb from the clavicle.

Question 1229. Which flat triangular bone lies on the back of the chest wall, covering the upper ribs, and moves with the arm? The answer is the scapula. It has three borders and three angles, and its lateral angle carries the socket for the head of the humerus. It is held in place by muscles rather than by a strong joint.

Question 1230. Over which ribs does the body of the scapula lie: the first to the fourth, the second to the seventh, the fifth to the tenth, or the eighth to the twelfth? The answer is the second to the seventh. The medial border runs down beside the vertebral column. The inferior angle rests at the level of the seventh rib.

Question 1231. Which bony ridge runs across the back of the scapula and separates two hollows? The answer is the spine of the scapula. It rises from the flat surface and turns forward at its outer end to form the acromion. The hollows above and below it hold the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus.

Question 1232. A described radiograph of the back of the shoulder shows a triangular plate of bone with a horizontal ridge dividing it into a smaller upper part and a larger lower part. Which bone is shown, and what is the ridge? The answer is the scapula, with its spine. The upper part is the supraspinous fossa and the lower part the infraspinous fossa.

Question 1233. At the upper border of the scapula, which structure passes below the superior transverse scapular ligament: the suprascapular nerve, the suprascapular artery, the axillary nerve, or the dorsal scapular nerve? The answer is the suprascapular nerve. The suprascapular artery passes above the ligament. The nerve then supplies the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus.

Question 1234. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the scapula and separates the bone from the ribs? The answer is the serratus anterior. It fills the plane between the costal surface of the scapula and the chest wall. Its slips arise from the upper ribs and reach the medial border.

Question 1235. Which hollow on the scapula faces the ribs and gives origin to the subscapularis? The answer is the subscapular fossa. It covers most of the costal surface of the bone. The subscapularis fills it and rotates the arm inward. Its ridges mark the attachment of the muscle.

Question 1236. Which rotator cuff muscle arises from the upper surface of the lateral border of the scapula: the supraspinatus, the teres minor, the subscapularis, or the teres major? The answer is the teres minor. It arises from the upper part of the dorsal surface of the lateral border and rotates the arm outward. The teres major arises lower down and is not part of the cuff.

Question 1237. True or false: the scapula is joined to the vertebral column by a synovial joint. The answer is false. The scapula has no joint with the axial skeleton at all. It is tethered by muscles, and its only bony path to the trunk runs through the clavicle and the sternoclavicular joint.

Question 1238. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the armpit, a patient cannot push the arm forward against a wall, and the medial border of the scapula stands away from the chest. Which muscle is paralyzed, and which nerve was injured? The answer is the serratus anterior, with the long thoracic nerve. That nerve runs down the chest wall on the surface of the muscle, where it is easily stretched.

Question 1239. Which muscle arises from the superior angle and the upper part of the medial border of the scapula and lifts the whole bone? The answer is the levator scapulae. It descends from the transverse processes of the upper cervical vertebrae. It helps to raise the point of the shoulder.

Question 1240. The inferior angle of the scapula lies at the level of which rib and which vertebral spine: the fifth rib and the fourth thoracic spine, the seventh rib and the seventh thoracic spine, the ninth rib and the ninth thoracic spine, or the eleventh rib and the eleventh thoracic spine? The answer is the seventh rib and the seventh thoracic spine. That landmark is used to count ribs from behind.

Question 1241. A described cross section through the back of the chest shows a triangular bone lying against the ribs, with a muscle in the hollow on each side of a central ridge. Which hollow lies above the ridge and which below? The answer is the supraspinous fossa above and the infraspinous fossa below. The ridge between them is the spine of the scapula.

Question 1242. Which muscle takes origin from the ventral surface of the medial border of the scapula: the rhomboid major, the serratus anterior, the levator scapulae, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the serratus anterior. It arises by finger-like slips from the upper ribs and pulls the scapula around the chest wall. The rhomboids lie on the dorsal surface of the same border.

Question 1243. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supraspinous fossa with the infraspinatus, the subscapular fossa with the supraspinatus, the infraspinous fossa with the subscapularis, or the supraspinous fossa with the supraspinatus? The answer is the supraspinous fossa with the supraspinatus. The infraspinous fossa holds the infraspinatus, and the subscapular fossa holds the subscapularis.

Question 1244. Which nerve supplies the rhomboid muscles and helps to hold the scapula against the chest wall? The answer is the dorsal scapular nerve. It arises from the fifth cervical spinal nerve root and runs down deep to the rhomboids. Injury weakens retraction of the scapula and lets the bone slide a little laterally.

Question 1245. Which part of the scapula lies immediately below the acromion, and which part lies immediately medial to the acromion? The answer is the glenoid cavity below it and the spine of the scapula medial to it. The acromion is the outer end of the spine. These three landmarks are felt as one continuous line on the top of the shoulder.

Question 1246. A described CT scan of the shoulder shows the scapula as a triangular plate on the back of the chest, with a ridge from its medial border to the shoulder socket and a notch near its upper border. Which notch is it, and what passes through it? The answer is the suprascapular notch, with the suprascapular nerve passing through it. The artery crosses above the ligament that roofs it.

Question 1247. A constructed illustration: a patient has a drooping shoulder and cannot shrug it, and the scapula on that side sits lower and rotated. Which muscle is paralyzed, and which nerve is damaged? The answer is the trapezius, with the eleventh cranial nerve. That nerve is superficial in the back of the neck, where it can be injured by a cut or by an operation there.

Question 1248. A described radiograph of the shoulder shows the coracoid process overlapping the upper part of the glenoid, with the spine of the scapula above and behind it. Which of these landmarks is the most anterior? The answer is the coracoid process. It hooks forward and laterally, so it lies in front of the socket. That is why it is felt in the groove between the deltoid and the chest.

Question 1249. During full elevation of the arm, what share of the movement takes place at the shoulder joint and what share at the scapula: about three to one, about two to one, about one to one, or about one to three? The answer is about two to one. For every 15 degrees of arm lift, roughly 10 occur at the shoulder joint and 5 at the scapula.

Question 1250. Which muscle arises from the subscapular fossa and forms the anterior wall of the rotator cuff? The answer is the subscapularis. It is the only one of the four cuff muscles that lies in front of the joint. It rotates the arm inward and is supplied by the upper and lower subscapular nerves.

Question 1251. True or false: the scapula lies flat against the rib cage and cannot be moved independently of the humerus. The answer is false. The scapula glides on the chest wall and can be drawn forward, backward, up, and down. It must also turn upward before the arm can pass above the head.

Question 1252. Which muscle both protracts the scapula and turns it upward, and is therefore needed for lifting the arm above the head: the rhomboid major, the levator scapulae, the serratus anterior, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the serratus anterior. Working with the trapezius, it rotates the scapula so that the socket faces upward. Its paralysis prevents full elevation of the arm.

Question 1253. Which structure lies immediately deep to the medial border of the scapula and separates the bone from the ribs? The answer is the serratus anterior. It fills that plane, so the bone slides on muscle rather than on bone. Loose tissue between the muscle and the chest wall allows the scapula to glide.

Question 1254. A described specimen of the scapula is held with its smooth concave costal surface facing the viewer. Which hollow is seen, and what does it hold? The answer is the subscapular fossa, which holds the subscapularis. The ridges on its surface mark the attachment of the muscle fibers. The dorsal surface is the one divided by the spine.

Question 1255. From above downward, which two muscles attach to the dorsal surface of the lateral border of the scapula: the teres minor and the teres major, the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus, the subscapularis and the rhomboid major, or the deltoid and the trapezius? The answer is the teres minor above and the teres major below it. The long head of the triceps passes between them.

Question 1256. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the outstretched hand, a patient has deep shoulder pain, numbness over the outer upper arm, and a weak deltoid. Which nerve is injured, and which part of the girdle should be examined for a break? The answer is the axillary nerve, with the glenoid cavity and the surgical neck of the humerus. It winds close to the lower rim of the socket.

Question 1257. Name the two muscles that turn the scapula upward so that the arm can be raised above the head, spoken as a list. The answer is the trapezius and the serratus anterior. They work as a pair, one pulling at the spine and the other at the inferior angle. Without that rotation the arm cannot pass the vertical.

Question 1258. Which single feature explains why the scapula is so mobile: it joins the trunk through the clavicle and muscles only, it has a wide synovial joint with the ribs, it is fused to the first rib, or it hangs by ligaments from the vertebrae? The answer is that it joins the trunk through the clavicle and muscles only. There is no wide joint, so the bone can glide in many directions.

Question 1259. Which bony point forms the outer end of the spine of the scapula and is felt at the top of the shoulder? The answer is the acromion. It is flattened and turns forward and upward over the socket. It gives the shoulder its square outline and meets the clavicle.

Question 1260. With which bone does the acromion articulate: the humerus, the clavicle, the first rib, or the coracoid process? The answer is the clavicle. The two bones meet at the acromioclavicular joint at the top of the shoulder. The head of the humerus lies below the acromion and does not touch it.

Question 1261. A described radiograph of the shoulder shows a bony shelf lying over the top of the rounded head of the humerus, with a clear gap between the two. Which part of the scapula forms that shelf? The answer is the acromion. The clear gap beneath it holds the supraspinatus tendon and a bursa. Loss of that gap suggests that the head has moved upward.

Question 1262. What is the name of the angle at which the spine of the scapula turns forward to become the acromion? The answer is the acromial angle. It is felt as a bony corner at the back of the shoulder. The acromion then continues forward and slightly upward above the joint.

Question 1263. Which two muscles take origin from the borders of the acromion: the trapezius and the deltoid, the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus, the rhomboid major and the rhomboid minor, or the latissimus dorsi and the teres major? The answer is the trapezius and the deltoid. The trapezius attaches to the posterior border and the deltoid to the anterior and lateral borders. The two muscles therefore surround the point of the shoulder.

Question 1264. Which ligament stretches from the tip of the coracoid process to the acromion, and what does the arch it forms protect? The answer is the coracoacromial ligament, which forms the coracoacromial arch. The arch lies above the head of the humerus and the tendon of the supraspinatus. It helps to stop the head from moving upward.

Question 1265. True or false: the acromion always ossifies as part of the spine of the scapula and never has a separate center. The answer is false. It has its own center or centers, which may stay unfused as a separate piece. That normal variant is called an os acromiale and can be mistaken for a fracture.

Question 1266. A constructed illustration: a middle-aged patient has pain when the arm is lifted to the side, worst in the middle of the arc, and the pain eases when the arm is raised fully. Which tendon and which bursa lie in the narrow space most likely involved? The answer is the supraspinatus tendon with the subacromial bursa. Both lie just below the acromion, in the space roofed by that bone.

Question 1267. What kind of joint is the acromioclavicular joint: a plane synovial joint with a disc, a ball and socket joint, a cartilaginous joint, or a fibrous joint? The answer is a plane synovial joint with an articular disc. Its surfaces glide on each other during shrugging. Movement there adds to the range of the scapula.

Question 1268. Which of these pairs belongs together: the acromion with the clavicle, the acromion with the first rib, the coracoid process with the clavicle, or the glenoid cavity with the acromion? The answer is the acromion with the clavicle, which meet at the acromioclavicular joint. The coracoid process reaches the clavicle only by a ligament.

Question 1269. Which muscles attach to the posterior and anterior borders of the acromion, and what attaches to its medial border? The answer is the trapezius behind, the deltoid in front, and the coracoacromial ligament on the medial border near the tip. Those attachments fix the acromion on all sides.

Question 1270. A described MRI scan of the shoulder shows a bright collection of fluid in the space between the underside of the acromion and the tendon below it. Which space is shown, and which tendon lies in it? The answer is the subacromial bursa, with the tendon of the supraspinatus. The space is roofed by the acromion and the coracoacromial ligament.

Question 1271. Which of these structures prevents the head of the humerus from dislocating upward: the acromion with the coracoacromial arch, the glenoid labrum, the long head of the biceps, or the capsule of the joint? The answer is the acromion with the coracoacromial arch. It forms a bony and fibrous roof over the head. The labrum deepens the socket but does not stop upward movement.

Question 1272. Which structures lie immediately deep to the acromion, in order from the bone downward? The answer is the subacromial bursa, then the tendon of the supraspinatus, then the head of the humerus. The long head of the biceps also passes through the same space on its way to the socket.

Question 1273. Which bony process of the scapula projects forward and laterally below the outer end of the clavicle and is shaped like a bent finger? The answer is the coracoid process. It arises from the upper part of the lateral angle of the scapula. Three muscles and several ligaments attach to it.

Question 1274. Which two muscles arise from the tip of the coracoid process: the coracobrachialis and the short head of the biceps, the long head of the biceps and the brachialis, the pectoralis major and the deltoid, or the subscapularis and the teres major? The answer is the coracobrachialis and the short head of the biceps. Both pass into the arm and help to flex the shoulder.

Question 1275. Which notch lies on the superior border of the scapula, immediately medial to the base of the coracoid process? The answer is the suprascapular notch. The superior transverse scapular ligament closes it into a foramen. The suprascapular nerve passes through it, and the artery passes above the ligament.

Question 1276. Which muscle from the chest wall attaches to the coracoid process and pulls the point of the shoulder forward and downward? The answer is the pectoralis minor. It arises from the third, fourth, and fifth ribs. It inserts on the medial border and the upper surface of the process.

Question 1277. A described specimen of the scapula shows a strong hook of bone rising above the socket and pointing forward, with a notch just behind its base. Which process is shown, and which notch lies behind it? The answer is the coracoid process, with the suprascapular notch medial to its base. The hook gives attachment to three muscles.

Question 1278. Which two ligaments run from the coracoid process to the inferior surface of the clavicle: the conoid and the trapezoid, the acromioclavicular and the coracoacromial, the costoclavicular and the interclavicular, or the glenohumeral and the coracohumeral? The answer is the conoid and the trapezoid. Together they form the coracoclavicular ligament, which suspends the limb from the clavicle.

Question 1279. Which structure lies immediately lateral to the base of the coracoid process, and which lies immediately medial to it? The answer is the glenoid cavity laterally and the suprascapular notch medially. The coracoid therefore sits at the front of the upper end of the socket. Behind it runs the upper border of the scapula.

Question 1280. True or false: the coracoid process can be felt through the skin in the groove between the front of the shoulder and the chest. The answer is true. It lies deep in that groove, below the outer third of the clavicle. It can be rolled under the finger in a thin person.

Question 1281. Which ligament arises from the lateral border of the coracoid process and blends with the capsule of the shoulder joint: the coracohumeral ligament, the coracoacromial ligament, the conoid ligament, or the transverse humeral ligament? The answer is the coracohumeral ligament. It passes to the greater and lesser tubercles of the humerus. It strengthens the upper part of the capsule.

Question 1282. A described radiograph of the shoulder taken from the side shows a strong hook of bone lying in front of the socket while another process lies above it. Which two processes of the scapula frame the joint in that view? The answer is the coracoid process in front and the acromion above. Between them runs the coracoacromial ligament.

Question 1283. Which statement about the coracoid process is correct: it is the point of the shoulder, it is felt in the groove between the deltoid and the chest, it articulates with the humerus, or it gives origin to the pectoralis major? The answer is that it is felt in the groove between the deltoid and the chest. It does not articulate with the humerus, and it gives origin to the pectoralis minor.

Question 1284. A constructed illustration: after a direct blow to the front of the shoulder, the tip of a hook-shaped process under the outer clavicle is tender, and the patient cannot pull the arm forward against resistance. Which process is injured? The answer is the coracoid process, from which the coracobrachialis and the short head of the biceps arise. The pectoralis minor also attaches there.

Question 1285. Which of these pairs belongs together: the coracoid tip with the short head of the biceps, the coracoid tip with the long head of the biceps, the coracoid base with the pectoralis major, or the coracoid process with the trapezius? The answer is the coracoid tip with the short head of the biceps. The long head of the biceps arises inside the joint from the supraglenoid tubercle.

Question 1286. Which shallow socket on the scapula receives the head of the humerus? The answer is the glenoid cavity, also called the glenoid fossa. It lies at the lateral angle of the scapula, facing outward. It is much smaller than the head that fits into it.

Question 1287. Which bone contributes the socket of the shoulder joint: the humerus, the clavicle, the scapula, or the first rib? The answer is the scapula. Its glenoid cavity forms the socket, and the head of the humerus forms the ball. The clavicle lies above the joint but takes no part in it.

Question 1288. Which ring of fibrocartilage is attached to the rim of the glenoid cavity and deepens it? The answer is the glenoid labrum. It rounds off the edge of the socket and grips the head of the humerus. It also blends with the capsule and with the origin of the long head of the biceps.

Question 1289. The glenoid cavity faces in which direction: straight up, laterally and slightly forward, straight back, or medially? The answer is laterally and slightly forward. The scapula lies on the back of the chest, so its socket must turn around the rib cage to face the arm. The upper end of the socket is narrower than the lower end.

Question 1290. Which tendon arises from the supraglenoid tubercle just above the glenoid cavity: the long head of the biceps, the short head of the biceps, the long head of the triceps, or the coracobrachialis? The answer is the long head of the biceps. It passes through the joint and then lies in a groove on the humerus. The short head arises from the coracoid process instead.

Question 1291. Which structure lies immediately below the glenoid cavity, and which muscle arises from it? The answer is the infraglenoid tubercle, from which the long head of the triceps arises. It lies at the upper end of the lateral border of the scapula. The tubercle above the socket is the supraglenoid one.

Question 1292. A described radiograph of the shoulder shows the rounded head of the humerus overlapping a shallow socket on a triangular bone, and the socket is narrower at the top than at the bottom. Which socket is shown, and what deepens its rim? The answer is the glenoid cavity, deepened by the glenoid labrum. The narrow upper end carries the supraglenoid tubercle.

Question 1293. Which feature of the glenoid cavity best explains why the shoulder dislocates more easily than any other joint: the socket is shallow and holds only about one third of the head, it is filled with fat, it faces backward, or it is lined by cartilage? The answer is the shallow socket, which holds only about one third of the head. The labrum and the cuff help, but the fit stays loose.

Question 1294. A constructed illustration: a patient falls with the arm forced up and back, and afterward the shoulder looks square and the deltoid cannot be felt to contract. Which nerve is usually injured in this kind of dislocation, and what is lost? The answer is the axillary nerve, with weakness of the deltoid and loss of feeling over the upper outer arm. The nerve winds close to the lower part of the socket.

Question 1295. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supraglenoid tubercle with the long head of the biceps, the supraglenoid tubercle with the triceps, the infraglenoid tubercle with the biceps, or the infraglenoid tubercle with the coracobrachialis? The answer is the supraglenoid tubercle with the long head of the biceps. The infraglenoid tubercle belongs to the long head of the triceps.

Question 1296. Name the four muscles of the rotator cuff, spoken as a list. The answer is the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the subscapularis, and the teres minor. They arise from the scapula and blend with the capsule around the head of the humerus. They hold the head in the socket during movement.

Question 1297. The glenoid cavity is described as pear-shaped. Which of these statements is correct: it is wider below and narrower above, it is wider above and narrower below, it is perfectly round, or it is oval with its long axis across? The answer is that it is wider below and narrower above. The narrow upper part carries the supraglenoid tubercle. The lower front rim is the part torn in anterior dislocation.

Question 1298. A described cross section through the shoulder passes from front to back and shows the socket cut in half with the head of the humerus seated in it. In which plane was the section taken? The answer is a sagittal plane through the joint. It shows the socket and the head from the side, with the coracoid process in front and the acromion above.

Question 1299. A described MRI scan of the shoulder shows a dark triangular rim around the edge of the socket, continuous with the capsule above and torn below. Which structure is shown, and where is the tear? The answer is the glenoid labrum, torn at its lower front part. That tear follows the head of the humerus as it slips out of the socket.

Question 1300. A constructed illustration: a young athlete dislocates the shoulder several times, and each time the arm is forced outward and back. Which part of the glenoid rim is repeatedly injured, and which piece of bone may be chipped? The answer is the lower front part of the rim, with a chip of the bony edge. The damaged rim then fails to hold the head.

To summarize, the clavicle is the only bony link between the upper limb and the trunk and the first bone to begin ossification, and it breaks most often at its middle third, where the sternocleidomastoid lifts the inner fragment and the weight of the arm drops the outer one. The scapula is a flat triangular bone lying over the second to the seventh ribs, with no joint to the axial skeleton, held by muscles alone, and carrying the spine, the supraspinous and infraspinous fossae behind and the subscapular fossa in front. The acromion and the coracoid process are the two hooks of the upper lateral scapula, the acromion roofing the shoulder and the coracoid giving origin to the coracobrachialis and the short head of the biceps, while the glenoid cavity is the shallow socket deepened by the labrum and steadied by the rotator cuff. The suprascapular nerve passes through the suprascapular notch below the ligament, the axillary nerve winds close to the lower rim of the socket, and the long thoracic nerve runs on the serratus anterior, so each of these nerves marks a place where the girdle is vulnerable.
