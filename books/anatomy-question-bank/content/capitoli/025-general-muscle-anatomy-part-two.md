# Chapter 25 — General Muscle Anatomy, Part Two

This chapter continues the general study of muscle by asking how muscles work against one another and how they are packed into groups inside the body. It deals first with antagonists, the muscles that oppose a movement, then with synergists and fixators, the muscles that help a movement or steady its base, and then with the fascial compartments that hold muscles into working sets. The second half turns to the face, where the muscles of facial expression lie in the superficial fascia and move the skin rather than a joint. The chapter holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind from one item to the next. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 2401. Which muscle is the antagonist of the biceps brachii at the elbow? The answer is the triceps brachii. The biceps brachii lies on the front of the arm and flexes the elbow, and the triceps brachii lies on the back and extends it, so the two pull in opposite directions.

Question 2402. At the knee, which group of muscles opposes the quadriceps femoris: the adductor group, the hamstrings, the gluteal muscles, or the calf muscles? The answer is the hamstrings. The quadriceps femoris extends the knee and the hamstrings flex it, while the gluteal muscles act at the hip and the calf muscles at the ankle.

Question 2403. Which group opposes the flexor muscles that bend the wrist: the extensor muscles on the back of the forearm, the thenar muscles of the thumb, the interosseous muscles of the hand, or the muscles of the little finger? The answer is the extensors on the back of the forearm. They straighten the wrist against the flexors, while the interosseous muscles move the fingers.

Question 2404. When a muscle contracts and its antagonist at the same joint relaxes at the same moment, what is that arrangement called? The answer is reciprocal inhibition. Signals from the contracting muscle quiet the motor neurons that drive its opponent, which saves work at the joint and is built into walking and reaching.

Question 2405. A described dissection of the arm shows a two-headed muscle on the front of the humerus and a three-headed muscle on the back, with the elbow bent. Which of the two is the antagonist of that movement? The answer is the three-headed muscle, the triceps brachii. It extends the elbow and must lengthen for the joint to bend, while the two-headed muscle is the agonist.

Question 2406. Which muscle at the front of the hip opposes the gluteus maximus when the thigh is lifted forward? The answer is the iliopsoas. It runs from the lumbar vertebrae and the inner surface of the pelvis to the lesser trochanter of the femur, so it flexes the hip while the gluteus maximus extends it.

Question 2407. Which two muscles oppose the middle fibers of the deltoid, which lift the arm sideways: the pectoralis major and the latissimus dorsi, the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus, the rhomboid muscles and the trapezius, or the serratus anterior and the levator scapulae? The answer is the pectoralis major and the latissimus dorsi. Both draw the arm down and inward, while the supraspinatus assists the deltoid.

Question 2408. Which muscles oppose the serratus anterior, which draws the shoulder blade forward: the rhomboid major and rhomboid minor, the pectoralis minor, the subclavius, or the levator scapulae? The answer is the rhomboid major and rhomboid minor. They pull the shoulder blade back toward the vertebral column, while the pectoralis minor draws it forward and so assists the serratus anterior.

Question 2409. Which muscle crosses the front of the hip and opposes the hamstrings when the knee is straightened? The answer is the rectus femoris. It is the only part of the quadriceps femoris that crosses the hip as well as the knee, so it extends the knee and also lifts the thigh.

Question 2410. True or false: a muscle is called an antagonist only when it lies on the opposite side of a joint from the muscle it opposes. The answer is false. An antagonist is named by the movement it opposes, and its position about the joint is not the test.

Question 2411. Which muscle opposes the masseter when the lower jaw is opened: the temporalis, the medial pterygoid, the lateral pterygoid, or the buccinator? The answer is the lateral pterygoid. The masseter, the temporalis and the medial pterygoid all raise the jaw, while the lateral pterygoid pulls the head of the mandible forward and opens the mouth.

Question 2412. Which muscle on the front of the leg opposes the gastrocnemius and the soleus: the tibialis anterior, the fibularis longus, the flexor digitorum longus, or the plantaris? The answer is the tibialis anterior. It turns the sole inward as it lifts the foot, while the two calf muscles pull the heel up and point the foot down.

Question 2413. A constructed illustration: a person cannot lift the foot after an injury to the front of the leg. Which group has lost its pull, leaving the calf unopposed? The answer is the anterior compartment of the leg, whose muscles dorsiflex the ankle. With that group silent the foot droops. Examination and management belong to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2414. Which muscle inside the orbit opposes the orbicularis oculi and raises the upper eyelid? The answer is the levator palpebrae superioris. The orbicularis oculi closes the lids and the levator lifts the upper lid, and the two differ in nerve supply as well.

Question 2415. Which muscle opposes the genioglossus, which pulls the tongue forward: the styloglossus, the hyoglossus, the palatoglossus, or the geniohyoid? The answer is the styloglossus. It runs from the styloid process to the side of the tongue and draws it up and backward, so the two pull in opposite directions.

Question 2416. Which of these four pairs is a pair of antagonists: the biceps brachii with the brachialis, the quadriceps femoris with the hamstrings, the gastrocnemius with the soleus, or the deltoid with the supraspinatus? The answer is the quadriceps femoris with the hamstrings. The other three pairs work together, since both members of each bend, point or lift in the same direction.

Question 2417. Name the group of muscles that acts as the antagonist of the quadriceps femoris, spoken as a single group. The answer is the hamstrings. The group lies at the back of the thigh and holds the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus, which cross both the hip and the knee.

Question 2418. A described dissection of the forearm shows a thick muscle mass on the front and another on the back, separated by deep fascia and by the two bones with their interosseous membrane. What relationship do the two masses have at the wrist? The answer is that they are antagonists. The front group flexes the wrist and fingers and the back group extends them.

Question 2419. What name is given to a muscle that works alongside the prime mover and helps it produce the same movement? The answer is a synergist. The prime mover is the muscle chiefly responsible for the movement, and its synergists add their pull in the same direction.

Question 2420. Which muscle lies on the front of the lower half of the humerus and joins the biceps brachii in bending the elbow? The answer is the brachialis. It arises from the front of the humerus and inserts on the ulna, and because it lies close to the joint it is the strongest of the elbow flexors.

Question 2421. Which four muscles make up the quadriceps femoris: the rectus femoris with the three vastus muscles, the biceps femoris with the semitendinosus, the sartorius with the gracilis, or the gluteus maximus with the gluteus medius? The answer is the rectus femoris with the vastus lateralis, the vastus medialis and the vastus intermedius. All four straighten the knee.

Question 2422. Which three muscles form the hamstrings at the back of the thigh: the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus, the adductor magnus with the gracilis, the gluteus maximus with the piriformis, or the sartorius with the popliteus? The answer is the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus. All three arise from the ischial tuberosity and act at the knee and the hip.

Question 2423. Which small muscle lies above the spine of the scapula and begins the act of lifting the arm, working with the deltoid? The answer is the supraspinatus. It passes under the acromion to the greater tubercle of the humerus, and the deltoid continues the movement once it has begun.

Question 2424. What name is given to a muscle that steadies the origin of the prime mover so that the prime mover can act from a firm base? The answer is a fixator. It produces no movement at the joint it crosses, but it makes the movement elsewhere efficient.

Question 2425. A constructed illustration: a person reaches forward with both arms, and the shoulder blades are held firmly against the chest wall so that the deltoid pulls from a fixed base. Which muscles steady the shoulder blades there? The answer is the trapezius, the rhomboid muscles and the serratus anterior. They are the fixators of the movement.

Question 2426. A described dissection of the back of the chest shows a large triangular muscle running from the lower vertebrae to the arm, a smaller muscle beside it and a broad muscle above. Which of these shares the work of pulling the arm down and back? The answer is the latissimus dorsi, with the teres major beside it. The broad muscle above is the trapezius.

Question 2427. Which two muscles on the back of the leg point the foot down and raise the heel: the tibialis anterior and the extensor digitorum longus, the gastrocnemius and the soleus, the flexor hallucis longus and the tibialis posterior, or the fibularis longus and the fibularis brevis? The answer is the gastrocnemius and the soleus. Both reach the calcaneus by the same tendon, and the soleus works hardest when a person stands still.

Question 2428. Which muscle lies in the deep part of the back of the forearm and joins the extensor digitorum in straightening the index finger? The answer is the extensor indicis. It reaches the index finger alone, while the extensor digitorum straightens all four fingers.

Question 2429. True or false: a synergist always produces the same movement as the prime mover it helps. The answer is false. Some synergists cancel a movement that the prime mover would otherwise produce, and so make the wanted movement cleaner. The muscles that steady the shoulder blade during a lift work in that way.

Question 2430. Which of these pairs belongs together: a fixator with a muscle that steadily holds a base, a synergist with a muscle that opposes a movement, an antagonist with a muscle that helps a movement, or a prime mover with a muscle that only steadies a joint? The answer is a fixator with a muscle that steadily holds a base. The other three pair the wrong term with the wrong description.

Question 2431. A described illustration shows a person lifting a heavy box, with the back straight and the shoulder blades drawn together before the elbows bend. Which muscles have acted before the elbows bend, and what is their role? The answer is the muscles that steady the shoulder blades, acting as fixators. They hold the scapulae so that the arm muscles pull from a firm base.

Question 2432. Name two muscles that act as synergists with the biceps brachii when the elbow is bent against resistance, spoken as a list. The answer is the brachialis and the brachioradialis. Both bend the elbow, and neither supinates the forearm as the biceps brachii does.

Question 2433. Which group of muscles steadies the head of the humerus in its socket while the deltoid lifts the arm: the rotator cuff, the thenar muscles, the adductor muscles of the thigh, or the muscles of the abdominal wall? The answer is the rotator cuff, formed by the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor and the subscapularis. They hold the head in place while the deltoid lifts the shaft.

Question 2434. A constructed illustration: the deltoid is healthy but the arm cannot be lifted, because the shoulder blade slides away from the chest wall whenever it is raised. Which structures have failed there? The answer is the fixators of the shoulder blade, chiefly the serratus anterior with the trapezius and the rhomboid muscles.

Question 2435. Which muscles lie in the anterior compartment of the arm, on the front of the humerus? The answer is the biceps brachii, the brachialis and the coracobrachialis. All three are supplied by the musculocutaneous nerve, and the triceps brachii lies behind them in the posterior compartment.

Question 2436. Which nerve supplies the muscles of the anterior compartment of the arm: the radial nerve, the musculocutaneous nerve, the median nerve, or the ulnar nerve? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve. It pierces the coracobrachialis and runs between the biceps brachii and the brachialis, while the radial nerve supplies the posterior compartment.

Question 2437. What structure divides the muscles of a limb into compartments and gives each compartment its own nerve and blood supply? The answer is the deep fascia with the intermuscular septa that run from it to the bone. The septa make closed spaces, and each space carries the vessels and nerves for the muscles within it.

Question 2438. Which muscles lie in the anterior compartment of the leg: the gastrocnemius and the soleus, the tibialis anterior with the long extensors of the toes, the fibularis longus and the fibularis brevis, or the tibialis posterior with the long flexors of the toes? The answer is the tibialis anterior with the extensor digitorum longus and the extensor hallucis longus, which lift the foot and the toes.

Question 2439. Which muscles lie in the lateral compartment of the leg, and which nerve supplies them: the tibialis anterior with the deep fibular nerve, the fibularis longus and fibularis brevis with the superficial fibular nerve, the gastrocnemius with the tibial nerve, or the extensor digitorum longus with the medial plantar nerve? The answer is the fibularis longus and fibularis brevis with the superficial fibular nerve, which turn the sole outward.

Question 2440. A described cross section of the leg shows the tibia and the fibula with a membrane between them, and muscle masses in front of, behind and beside the bones, each wrapped in its own sheet of deep fascia. Which compartment lies between the two bones in front of the membrane? The answer is the anterior compartment. The posterior compartment lies behind both bones.

Question 2441. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the front of the leg, the tissue in one closed fascial space swells and the toes hurt when straightened. Which space is involved, and why does the pressure rise? The answer is the anterior compartment of the leg, a closed space bounded by deep fascia and bone. The fascia cannot stretch, so swelling raises the pressure. Assessment and management belong to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2442. Which muscles form the deep part of the posterior compartment of the leg, the part against the back of the two bones? The answer is the tibialis posterior, the flexor digitorum longus and the flexor hallucis longus, with the popliteus at the top. All are supplied by the tibial nerve.

Question 2443. In which part of the leg does the tibialis posterior lie: the anterior compartment, the lateral compartment, the deep part of the posterior compartment, or the superficial part of the posterior compartment? The answer is the deep part of the posterior compartment. Its tendon passes behind the medial malleolus and turns the sole inward while supporting the arch.

Question 2444. Which structure separates the anterior compartment of the leg from the lateral compartment, and how is it attached? The answer is the anterior intermuscular septum, which runs from the deep fascia inward to the anterior border of the fibula. Its fibers are attached to the bone along the length of the compartment.

Question 2445. True or false: the compartments of the leg each receive their own nerve, so that weakness of one nerve weakens a recognizable group of muscles. The answer is true. The anterior compartment takes the deep fibular nerve, the lateral compartment the superficial fibular nerve, and the posterior compartments the tibial nerve.

Question 2446. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior compartment of the leg with the deep fibular nerve, the lateral compartment with the tibial nerve, the posterior compartment with the superficial fibular nerve, or the anterior compartment with the medial plantar nerve? The answer is the anterior compartment with the deep fibular nerve. The lateral compartment takes the superficial fibular nerve.

Question 2447. A described cross section of the thigh shows one large muscle mass in front, another behind and a third group along the inner side, separated by septa that run from a strong band of fascia around the limb to the femur. Which compartment holds the muscles that straighten the knee? The answer is the anterior compartment, which holds the quadriceps femoris. The posterior compartment holds the hamstrings.

Question 2448. A described transverse section through the middle of the leg is read with the fibula on the outer side and the tibia on the inner side. Which compartment lies against the outer surface of the fibula, and which lies behind both bones? The answer is that the lateral compartment lies against the fibula and the posterior compartment lies behind both bones.

Question 2449. Name the three compartments of the thigh and the action for which each is best known, spoken as a list. The answer is the anterior compartment, which straightens the knee, the posterior compartment, which bends the knee and extends the hip, and the medial compartment, which draws the thigh inward.

Question 2450. A constructed illustration: a blow to the outer side of the knee leaves the foot unable to turn outward and the skin over the outer leg numb. Which nerve has been injured, and why is it exposed there? The answer is the common fibular nerve, as it winds around the neck of the fibula, where it lies against bone. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2451. Which cranial nerve supplies the muscles of facial expression, the muscles that move the skin of the face? The answer is the seventh cranial nerve, the facial nerve. Its branches spread through the face from the parotid region, and the muscles it supplies pull on the skin rather than on a joint.

Question 2452. Which muscle closes the eyelids: the levator palpebrae superioris, the orbicularis oculi, the corrugator supercilii, or the procerus? The answer is the orbicularis oculi. Its fibers form a ring about the eye, and the levator palpebrae superioris does the opposite and lifts the upper lid.

Question 2453. Which muscle closes and purses the lips: the buccinator, the orbicularis oris, the mentalis, or the risorius? The answer is the orbicularis oris. Its fibers form a ring about the mouth, while the buccinator presses the cheek against the teeth and the mentalis moves the chin.

Question 2454. Which muscle forms the wall of the cheek, presses it against the teeth and keeps food from collecting beside the gum? The answer is the buccinator. It runs from the back of the jaw forward to the angle of the mouth and is supplied by the seventh cranial nerve.

Question 2455. Which muscle runs from the cheekbone to the angle of the mouth and pulls that angle upward and outward in a smile? The answer is the zygomaticus major. The zygomaticus minor lies just above it and lifts the upper lip, and both are supplied by the seventh cranial nerve.

Question 2456. A described dissection of the face shows a ring of muscle about the mouth and, at the corner, a small knot where several flat muscles meet and interlace. What is that knot called, and which muscles meet there? The answer is the modiolus, where the orbicularis oris, the buccinator, the levator and depressor muscles of the angle and the zygomaticus major meet.

Question 2457. Which muscle lifts the eyebrows and wrinkles the forehead: the occipital belly of the occipitofrontalis, the frontal belly of the occipitofrontalis, the corrugator supercilii, or the procerus? The answer is the frontal belly, which lies over the forehead. The occipital belly lies at the back of the head and pulls the scalp backward.

Question 2458. Which small muscle lies at the inner end of the eyebrow and draws it toward the midline and downward, making vertical folds above the nose? The answer is the corrugator supercilii. It lies deep to the frontal belly, and its pull produces the frown lines between the eyebrows.

Question 2459. Which muscle lies over the bridge of the nose and wrinkles the skin there: the procerus, the nasalis, the levator labii superioris, or the depressor septi? The answer is the procerus. It reaches the skin between the eyebrows, while the nasalis lies lower and compresses the nostril.

Question 2460. Which broad sheet of muscle lies in the superficial fascia of the neck, tenses the skin of the neck and pulls the corner of the mouth downward? The answer is the platysma. It arises from the fascia over the chest and shoulder and is supplied by the cervical branch of the seventh cranial nerve.

Question 2461. Which muscle protrudes the lower lip, turns it outward and wrinkles the skin of the chin: the depressor labii inferioris, the mentalis, the depressor anguli oris, or the orbicularis oris? The answer is the mentalis. The depressor labii inferioris pulls the lower lip down rather than forward.

Question 2462. Which muscle lies over the back of the head and pulls the scalp backward, working across a broad fibrous sheet with the muscle over the forehead? The answer is the occipital belly of the occipitofrontalis. The sheet between the two bellies is the epicranial aponeurosis.

Question 2463. Which muscle lies over the side of the nose and compresses the nostril: the procerus, the nasalis, the levator labii superioris alaeque nasi, or the depressor septi? The answer is the nasalis, whose compressor part squeezes the nostril. The levator labii superioris alaeque nasi widens it instead.

Question 2464. Which of these pairs belongs together: the orbicularis oris with closing the lips, the orbicularis oculi with lifting the eyebrow, the mentalis with closing the eyelids, or the buccinator with turning the sole of the foot? The answer is the orbicularis oris with closing the lips. The orbicularis oculi closes the eyelids and the buccinator presses the cheek against the teeth.

Question 2465. True or false: the muscles of facial expression lie in the superficial fascia and are attached to the skin at one or both ends. The answer is true. They arise from bone or fascia and insert into the skin, which is why their contraction moves the skin and produces an expression.

Question 2466. Which branch of the seventh cranial nerve supplies the frontal belly and the upper part of the orbicularis oculi: the temporal branch, the zygomatic branch, the buccal branch, or the cervical branch? The answer is the temporal branch. It crosses the zygomatic arch to the forehead, and the zygomatic branch supplies the lower part of the orbicularis oculi.

Question 2467. The duct of the parotid gland crosses the cheek and pierces which muscle, and opposite which tooth does it open into the mouth? The answer is the buccinator, opposite the second upper molar tooth. The duct crosses the masseter first and then turns inward through the cheek.

Question 2468. Which muscle lies below the eye and runs down to the upper lip, lifting it and deepening the fold from the side of the nose to the corner of the mouth? The answer is the levator labii superioris. It arises from the lower margin of the orbit.

Question 2469. Which muscle of the cheek lies deepest, against the lining of the mouth: the buccinator, the risorius, the zygomaticus major, or the levator anguli oris? The answer is the buccinator. It forms the deep wall of the cheek and is pierced by the duct of the parotid gland.

Question 2470. A described dissection of the cheek shows a small duct running forward across a muscle at the side of the jaw, then turning inward through the cheek wall opposite the upper molar teeth. Which muscle does the duct cross, and which does it pierce? The answer is that it crosses the masseter and pierces the buccinator.

Question 2471. Immediately deep to the platysma in the neck lie the investing layer of deep cervical fascia and, beneath it, which large muscle running from the chest to the mastoid process? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid. The platysma pulls on the skin, while that muscle turns the head.

Question 2472. Which muscle draws the corner of the mouth straight outward and produces a flat, mirthless grin: the risorius, the zygomaticus major, the depressor anguli oris, or the levator anguli oris? The answer is the risorius, a slender band running across the cheek. The zygomaticus major lifts the corner instead of pulling it straight out.

Question 2473. Which muscle runs from the front of the lower jaw to the lower lip and pulls that lip downward and a little outward? The answer is the depressor labii inferioris. The depressor anguli oris lies beside it and pulls the whole corner of the mouth down.

Question 2474. Which muscle lies deep to the levator labii superioris and lifts the angle of the mouth: the levator anguli oris, the zygomaticus minor, the depressor anguli oris, or the buccinator? The answer is the levator anguli oris. It arises from the canine fossa of the upper jaw.

Question 2475. The seventh cranial nerve leaves the skull through which opening, and which gland does it enter immediately afterward? The answer is the stylomastoid foramen, and it enters the parotid gland. Inside the gland it splits into its five main branches for the face.

Question 2476. A described section through the scalp shows a broad sheet of fibrous tissue between a muscle at the back and a muscle at the front, with the skin above and the bone below. What is the sheet called? The answer is the epicranial aponeurosis, which the frontal belly pulls forward with the scalp and the occipital belly pulls backward.

Question 2477. Which muscle widens the nostril: the compressor part of the nasalis, the levator labii superioris alaeque nasi, the depressor septi, or the procerus? The answer is the levator labii superioris alaeque nasi, whose long name records its two actions. Its outer slip raises the upper lip and pulls the wing of the nose upward.

Question 2478. True or false: the buccinator is one of the muscles of mastication, because it lies beside the teeth and keeps food between them. The answer is false. The buccinator is a muscle of facial expression, supplied by the seventh cranial nerve, and it does not move the joint of the jaw.

Question 2479. Which muscle runs from the lower border of the jaw up to the corner of the mouth and pulls that corner downward: the depressor anguli oris, the depressor labii inferioris, the mentalis, or the platysma? The answer is the depressor anguli oris. The depressor labii inferioris moves only the lower lip.

Question 2480. Which of these pairs belongs together: the frontal belly with the occipital belly as antagonists across the scalp, the orbicularis oris with the buccinator as antagonists, the mentalis with the depressor labii inferioris as antagonists, or the procerus with the corrugator supercilii as antagonists? The answer is the frontal belly with the occipital belly. The other pairs lie side by side and help one another.

Question 2481. A described cross section through the cheek at the level of the upper molar teeth shows the skin outside, a layer of fat, muscles of the face, then the buccinator, and the lining of the mouth innermost. Which muscle forms the deepest part of the wall, and what passes through it? The answer is the buccinator, pierced by the duct of the parotid gland.

Question 2482. A described transverse section through the head is read with the nose in front and the ears behind. Which muscles of the face lie superficial to the buccinator, and where is the parotid gland? The answer is that the muscles of facial expression lie superficial to the buccinator, and the parotid gland lies at the side, in front of the ear.

Question 2483. Which muscle of facial expression is supplied by the cervical branch of the seventh cranial nerve? The answer is the platysma. It is the only muscle of facial expression that lies in the neck, and its pull tenses the skin of the neck and draws the corner of the mouth downward.

Question 2484. Which branch of the seventh cranial nerve supplies the depressor anguli oris, the depressor labii inferioris and the mentalis: the temporal branch, the zygomatic branch, the marginal mandibular branch, or the cervical branch? The answer is the marginal mandibular branch. It runs forward close to the lower border of the jaw, where it is easily injured.

Question 2485. A described dissection in front of the ear shows a gland wrapped around the back of the jaw, and emerging from its front edge a nerve that divides into branches running to the forehead, across the cheek and down to the neck. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the seventh cranial nerve, which supplies the muscles of facial expression.

Question 2486. Which artery crosses the lower border of the jaw just in front of the masseter, and which branch of the seventh cranial nerve runs near it there? The answer is the facial artery, with the marginal mandibular branch of the facial nerve nearby. The nerve passes forward over the jaw to the muscles of the lower lip.

Question 2487. A described dissection shows two bellies of a thin flat muscle joined by a broad fibrous sheet that covers the top of the head, with the skin moving freely over the whole arrangement. What is this arrangement? The answer is the occipitofrontalis with its epicranial aponeurosis. The muscle inserts into the skin, so the scalp moves with either belly.

Question 2488. Which group of muscles is derived from the second pharyngeal arch and is therefore supplied by the seventh cranial nerve: the muscles of the tongue, the muscles of facial expression with the stapedius and the posterior belly of the digastric, the muscles of mastication, or the muscles of the pharynx? The answer is the muscles of facial expression with the stapedius and the posterior belly of the digastric.

Question 2489. Which muscle of facial expression arises not from bone but from the fascia over the chest and the shoulder? The answer is the platysma. Its fibers end in the skin of the neck, the lower jaw and the corner of the mouth, and no part of it is attached to bone.

Question 2490. Which of these muscles is not supplied by the seventh cranial nerve: the stapedius, the posterior belly of the digastric, the tensor tympani, or the stylohyoid? The answer is the tensor tympani, which is supplied by a branch of the fifth cranial nerve. The other three belong to the second pharyngeal arch.

Question 2491. A described dissection of the eyelid region shows a ring of muscle about the opening of the eye and, above it inside the orbit, a slender muscle running forward to the upper lid. Which closes the eye, and which lifts the lid? The answer is that the orbicularis oculi closes the eye and the levator palpebrae superioris lifts the lid.

Question 2492. The duct of the parotid gland leaves the front of the gland and runs forward across the face. Which muscle does it cross first at the side of the jaw, and where does it turn inward? The answer is that it crosses the masseter, then turns inward at the front edge of that muscle to pierce the buccinator.

Question 2493. A described dissection shows a nerve emerging from an opening behind the ear and entering a gland, inside which it divides into five branches that fan out over the face. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the seventh cranial nerve, whose branches supply the muscles of facial expression.

Question 2494. Which muscle of the face is a slender band over the cheek that is absent in some people and very thin in others? The answer is the risorius, which draws the corner of the mouth straight outward when it is present. Such differences in the facial muscles are normal variations and not signs of disease.

Question 2495. A constructed illustration: the whole of one side of a person's face is weak, the forehead cannot be wrinkled and the eye cannot be closed, while the arm and the leg move normally. Which nerve is involved, and why does the forehead share in the weakness? The answer is the seventh cranial nerve of that side, which supplies the forehead as well as the lower face.

Question 2496. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the upper neck below the angle of the jaw, the lower lip on one side does not pull down and the corner of the mouth droops on smiling. Which branch has been injured? The answer is the marginal mandibular branch of the seventh cranial nerve, which crosses that region close to the jaw.

Question 2497. A constructed illustration: one cheek is weak, food collects between the cheek and the teeth on that side, and the person cannot blow out a candle. Which muscle is chiefly affected, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the buccinator, supplied by the buccal branch of the seventh cranial nerve.

Question 2498. A constructed illustration: the eye on one side cannot be shut, it waters, and its surface feels gritty because the lid does not sweep across it. Which part of which muscle has lost its nerve supply? The answer is the palpebral part of the orbicularis oculi, supplied by the seventh cranial nerve. Care of the eye belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 2499. A constructed illustration: a person cannot purse the lips, whistle or hold a thread between the lips, although the jaw and the tongue move normally. Which muscle is chiefly at fault? The answer is the orbicularis oris, the ring of muscle about the mouth, supplied by the buccal branches of the seventh cranial nerve.

Question 2500. A described dissection of the neck and the lower face shows a thin sheet of muscle running from the fascia over the chest up into the jaw and the corner of the mouth. What is this muscle, and what does it do to the skin? The answer is the platysma, which tenses the skin of the neck and pulls the corner of the mouth downward.

To summarize, an antagonist is defined by the movement it opposes and not by its position, and reciprocal inhibition is the automatic quieting of an antagonist while its partner contracts. A synergist adds its pull to the prime mover or cancels an unwanted movement, and a fixator steadies the base from which the prime mover acts. Muscles are grouped into compartments by deep fascia and intermuscular septa, and each compartment holds a recognizable set of muscles with its own nerve and vessels, as the compartments of the leg and the thigh show. The muscles of facial expression lie in the superficial fascia, insert into the skin and are supplied by the seventh cranial nerve, and the frontal belly, the corrugator supercilii, the procerus, the orbicularis oculi, the nasalis, the orbicularis oris, the buccinator, the zygomaticus major, the levator and depressor muscles of the mouth, the mentalis and the platysma each move a particular area of skin. The buccinator lies deepest in the cheek, the duct of the parotid gland pierces it opposite the second upper molar tooth, and the branches of the seventh nerve cross the face from the parotid gland.
