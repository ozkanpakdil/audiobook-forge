# Chapter 46 — Spleen

This chapter follows the lymph of the body from the abdomen to the great veins and then turns to the organ that filters the blood. It covers the thoracic duct, its origin, its course through the chest and the neck, its relations and its ending, and then the spleen, its position, its coverings, its blood supply, its development and the clinical anatomy that follows from all of them. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 4501. Which lymphatic channel is the largest in the body, and where does it end? The answer is the thoracic duct, which ends at the left venous angle. It begins in the abdomen, runs up through the chest, and carries lymph from both legs, the pelvis, the abdomen, the left arm, and the left side of the head and chest.

Question 4502. Where does the thoracic duct begin: in the pelvis, in the abdomen behind the diaphragm, in the chest behind the heart, or in the root of the neck? The answer is in the abdomen behind the diaphragm. Its lower end is a dilated sac at the level of the lower border of the twelfth thoracic vertebra. That sac is the cisterna chyli.

Question 4503. What is the name of the dilated sac at the lower end of the thoracic duct, and which trunks empty into it? The answer is the cisterna chyli, which receives the intestinal trunk and the two lumbar trunks. The intestinal trunk carries fat-rich lymph from the small intestine. The lumbar trunks drain the lower limbs, the pelvis, and the abdominal wall.

Question 4504. Which vein receives the thoracic duct at the root of the neck, and what is that junction called? The answer is the left brachiocephalic vein, at the point where the left internal jugular vein joins the left subclavian vein. That point is the left venous angle. The duct enters it from below and behind.

Question 4505. Which of these regions does the thoracic duct not drain: the left leg, the left arm, the right side of the head, or the left side of the abdomen? The answer is the right side of the head. The right side of the head and neck, the right upper limb, and the right half of the chest drain into the right lymphatic duct. Everything else in the body drains into the thoracic duct.

Question 4506. A constructed illustration: during an operation in the upper chest a surgeon divides a thin-walled channel behind the esophagus, and milky fluid collects in the left pleural cavity. Which channel is described? The answer is the thoracic duct. It carries chyle, the fat-rich lymph from the intestine, so a leak into the chest produces a collection of that fluid. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 4507. A described section of the chest shows a small thin-walled tube in front of a vertebral body, behind the esophagus, with the aorta on its left and the azygos vein on its right. Which structure is shown? The answer is the thoracic duct. It ascends through the posterior mediastinum in exactly that plane. A section taken higher, at the level of the sternal angle, would show it crossing to the left.

Question 4508. Which structure forms the lower end of the thoracic duct: the superior vena cava, the cisterna chyli, the portal vein, or the inferior mesenteric vein? The answer is the cisterna chyli. It is a sac-like dilatation behind the right crus of the diaphragm. The other three carry blood rather than lymph.

Question 4509. In the posterior mediastinum, which two structures lie immediately in front of and immediately behind the thoracic duct? The answer is the esophagus in front and the vertebral column behind. The descending aorta lies to its left and the azygos vein to its right. The duct therefore occupies a narrow plane between the gullet and the spine.

Question 4510. True or false: the thoracic duct carries the lymph of the right upper limb. The answer is false. The right upper limb, the right side of the head and neck, and the right half of the chest drain into the right lymphatic duct. The two ducts are unequal partners, the left one serving about three quarters of the body.

Question 4511. Through which opening in the diaphragm does the thoracic duct pass on its way from the abdomen to the chest? The answer is the aortic hiatus. That opening also transmits the aorta and the azygos vein, and it lies behind the diaphragm at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra. The duct runs up behind the aorta through the same gap.

Question 4512. At which level does the thoracic duct cross from the right of the midline to the left: the fourth thoracic vertebra, the eighth thoracic vertebra, the twelfth thoracic vertebra, or the second lumbar vertebra? The answer is the fourth thoracic vertebra. The crossing lies behind the esophagus at the level of the sternal angle. Above that level the duct is a left-sided structure.

Question 4513. A described lymphangiogram shows a channel that begins as a sac in the upper abdomen, runs upward to the right of the midline, and then turns toward the left side of the neck. What explains the turn? The answer is the normal course of the thoracic duct, which crosses the midline in the upper chest at the level of the fourth thoracic vertebra. The turn is anatomy and not disease.

Question 4514. At the root of the neck the arch of the thoracic duct ends at the left venous angle. Which structure crosses immediately in front of that arch? The answer is the left common carotid artery, together with the left internal jugular vein. Both run in the carotid sheath. The vertebral vessels and the sympathetic trunk lie behind the duct.

Question 4515. A described dissection of the lower chest shows, where one thoracic duct is expected, two parallel thin-walled channels. Which explanation fits best: an enlarged azygos vein, a duplicated thoracic duct, a dilated esophagus, or a replaced right subclavian artery? The answer is a duplicated thoracic duct, a normal variant found in a small minority of people. The two channels usually join above the diaphragm and end together at the left venous angle.

Question 4516. A constructed illustration: a thin-walled channel in the left side of the root of the neck is torn during a procedure on the great veins, and the field fills with milky fluid. Which structure is injured? The answer is the thoracic duct, which ends at the left venous angle. It reaches that point after crossing the midline lower down. The repair belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 4517. Which lymphatic trunk joins the thoracic duct from the left lung and the left half of the chest wall? The answer is the left bronchomediastinal trunk. A matching trunk on the right joins the right lymphatic duct instead. Each trunk carries lymph from the lung, the mediastinum, and the chest wall of its own side.

Question 4518. Which statement about the valves of the thoracic duct is correct: it contains no valves, its valves lie only at its lower end, its valves are numerous along its length and direct lymph toward the neck, or its valves allow flow in either direction? The answer is that its valves are numerous along its length and direct lymph toward the neck. Movement and breathing drive the flow, and the valves keep it one way.

Question 4519. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the cisterna chyli with the pelvis, the thoracic duct with the right upper limb, the right lymphatic duct with the right side of the head, or the lumbar trunks with the left arm? The answer is the right lymphatic duct with the right side of the head. The cisterna chyli lies in the upper abdomen, and the lumbar trunks drain the lower limbs.

Question 4520. Between which two structures does the thoracic duct lie as it ascends through the posterior mediastinum, and why does that position matter at operation? The answer is between the esophagus in front and the vertebral column behind, with the aorta on its left and the azygos vein on its right. Its thin wall makes it easy to overlook.

Question 4521. Which statement about the cisterna chyli is correct: it lies in the chest, it receives the intestinal and lumbar trunks, it drains into the right venous angle, or it is a distinct sac in every person? The answer is that it receives the intestinal and lumbar trunks. It lies in the abdomen behind the right crus of the diaphragm. In many people it is a plexus rather than a distinct sac.

Question 4522. Which channel carries the fat-rich lymph absorbed from a meal in the small intestine, and what is that lymph called? The answer is the thoracic duct, and the lymph is called chyle. Chyle reaches the duct through the intestinal trunk and the cisterna chyli. It looks milky because of the fat it carries.

Question 4523. A described radiograph taken after a lymph vessel has been filled with contrast medium shows a channel ending in a small ampulla behind the medial end of the left clavicle. What is shown? The answer is the terminal part of the thoracic duct, just before it enters the left venous angle. The duct ends in a small dilatation guarded by a pair of valves.

Question 4524. A constructed illustration: an operation high in the left chest leaves a milky collection in the left pleural cavity, while the same operation on the right side does not. Which anatomical fact explains this? The answer is that the thoracic duct crosses to the left at the level of the fourth thoracic vertebra. Below that level it lies on the right of the midline, and above it lies on the left.

Question 4525. Which statement best explains why the thoracic duct is easily torn during a procedure on the left side of the neck: it is the largest vessel in the neck, it ends at the left venous angle behind the carotid sheath, it is fixed to the prevertebral fascia, or it carries bright red blood? The answer is that it ends at the left venous angle behind the carotid sheath. It is thin walled and easily missed.

Question 4526. Which organ is the largest single mass of lymphoid tissue in the body, and where does it lie? The answer is the spleen, which lies in the left hypochondrium. It sits between the fundus of the stomach and the diaphragm, deep to the ninth to the eleventh ribs. In the adult it is about 12 centimeters long and about 150 grams in weight.

Question 4527. In which region of the abdomen does the spleen lie: the right hypochondrium, the left hypochondrium, the umbilical region, or the left iliac region? The answer is the left hypochondrium. It lies in the left upper quadrant, behind the lower left ribs. Its long axis runs along the line of the tenth rib.

Question 4528. A described radiograph of the abdomen shows a soft tissue shadow in the left upper quadrant, between the stomach bubble and the left chest wall, with a rounded lower border and a notch on its upper edge. Which organ produces the shadow? The answer is the spleen. The notch on the superior border reflects the lobular way it develops.

Question 4529. Which ligament joins the spleen to the greater curvature of the stomach, and which vessels does it carry? The answer is the gastrosplenic ligament, which carries the short gastric vessels. It runs between the hilum of the spleen and the fundus of the stomach. The splenic vessels travel in the separate splenorenal ligament behind it.

Question 4530. Which organ lies immediately in front of and medial to the spleen, in contact with its gastric impression? The answer is the stomach. Its fundus and upper body rest against the visceral surface of the spleen. The spleen therefore lies behind the stomach and to its left.

Question 4531. Which artery supplies the spleen: the left gastric artery, the splenic artery, the superior mesenteric artery, or the left renal artery? The answer is the splenic artery. It arises from the celiac trunk and runs a tortuous course along the upper border of the pancreas. It enters the spleen at its hilum.

Question 4532. Which vein begins at the hilum of the spleen, runs to the right behind the body of the pancreas, and joins the superior mesenteric vein? The answer is the splenic vein. That junction forms the portal vein behind the neck of the pancreas. The splenic vein has no valves along its course.

Question 4533. On the visceral surface of the spleen, which organ lies in the renal impression and which lies in the colic impression? The answer is the left kidney in the renal impression and the left colic flexure in the colic impression. The gastric impression lies above them for the stomach. The splenic hilum occupies the middle of the visceral surface.

Question 4534. A constructed illustration: a person is struck on the left lower chest and later reports pain at the tip of the left shoulder. Which organ is most likely injured, and why is the pain felt there? The answer is the spleen. Blood irritates the diaphragm, and the phrenic nerve carries that sensation to the same spinal segments that supply the skin of the shoulder.

Question 4535. Which structure lies immediately below and medial to the spleen, in contact with its renal impression: the left kidney, the tail of the pancreas, the left suprarenal gland, or the left ureter? The answer is the left kidney. Its upper pole lies in the renal impression on the visceral surface. The tail of the pancreas lies against the hilum.

Question 4536. A constructed illustration: a young person with an enlarged spleen is struck in the left upper abdomen and collapses. Which organ has most likely ruptured, and why does it rupture more easily when enlarged? The answer is the spleen. An enlarged organ has a stretched capsule, and its lower pole extends below the rib cage, where the ribs no longer protect it.

Question 4537. A constructed illustration: a person with longstanding liver disease has an enlarged spleen and dilated veins at the lower esophagus. Which venous arrangement explains both findings? The answer is the splenic vein, which joins the superior mesenteric vein to form the portal vein. Raised pressure in that system passes back to the spleen and makes it enlarge, and the same pressure reaches the lower esophagus through the left gastric vein.

Question 4538. Which structure lies immediately behind the spleen, separating it from the left lung and the left pleura? The answer is the diaphragm. The convex upper surface of the spleen rests against it. A wound of the left lower chest can therefore injure the spleen and the pleura together.

Question 4539. Which surface of the spleen carries the impressions of the stomach, the left kidney and the left colic flexure? The answer is the visceral surface. It faces downward and medially toward the abdominal cavity. The gastric impression is the largest of the three, and the hilum lies in the middle.

Question 4540. Which statement about the coverings of the spleen is correct: it lies behind the peritoneum, it is covered by peritoneum except at its hilum, it has no peritoneal covering, or it lies within the lesser sac? The answer is that it is covered by peritoneum except at its hilum. It is therefore an intraperitoneal organ. The peritoneum reaches it as the gastrosplenic and splenorenal ligaments.

Question 4541. A described cross section of the upper abdomen shows an oval organ on the left, behind the stomach and above the left kidney, receiving a tortuous artery at its medial border. Which organ is shown? The answer is the spleen. Its medial border is the hilum, where the splenic vessels enter and leave. The stomach lies in front of it and the kidney below it.

Question 4542. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the gastrosplenic ligament with the short gastric vessels, the splenorenal ligament with the hepatic artery, the falciform ligament with the spleen, or the phrenicocolic ligament with the splenic artery? The answer is the gastrosplenic ligament with the short gastric vessels. The splenorenal ligament carries the splenic vessels and the tail of the pancreas.

Question 4543. Which statement about the blood held by the spleen is correct: it holds about half of the blood volume of the body, it holds a store of blood that can be shifted into the circulation, it holds blood only in its white pulp, or it receives blood only by way of the portal vein? The answer is that it holds a store of blood that can be shifted into the circulation. It acts as a reservoir that contracts when more circulating blood is needed.

Question 4544. A constructed illustration: a person whose spleen has been removed develops a severe infection with an encapsulated bacterium. Which function of the spleen does this illustrate? The answer is the filtration and immune function of its white pulp. The spleen removes organisms from the blood and mounts a rapid response to those with capsules. Loss of that function raises the risk of such infections.

Question 4545. True or false: the spleen lies in the left hypochondrium with its long axis along the line of the tenth rib. The answer is true. The organ follows the tenth rib from the spine toward the left costal margin. That is why a normal spleen remains hidden beneath the rib cage.

Question 4546. Which part of the spleen removes aged red blood cells from the circulation, and what is the other main part called? The answer is the red pulp, and the other part is the white pulp. Blood seeps through the red pulp, where macrophages take up old and damaged red cells. The white pulp is lymphoid tissue gathered around the central arteries.

Question 4547. A described computed tomography scan of the upper abdomen shows an organ of soft tissue density between the stomach and the left kidney, with the left crus of the diaphragm behind it. What is shown, and what lies in the impression below its hilum? The answer is the spleen, with the left kidney in the renal impression below the hilum. The gastric impression faces the stomach.

Question 4548. Which statement about accessory spleens is correct: they lie inside the main capsule, they are small masses of splenic tissue near the hilum or in the gastrosplenic ligament, they are always malignant, or they are the main organ divided in two? The answer is that they are small masses of splenic tissue near the hilum or in the gastrosplenic ligament. They come from lobules that failed to join the main mass during development.

Question 4549. Name two structures that lie immediately related to the visceral surface of the spleen, spoken as a list. The answer is the stomach and the left kidney. The gastric impression and the renal impression on the visceral surface hold them. The left colic flexure also touches the organ at the colic impression.

Question 4550. On a transverse section of the abdomen at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra, in which direction does the spleen lie from the stomach, and in which direction from the left kidney? The answer is behind and to the left of the stomach, and above and lateral to the left kidney. The spleen rests against the diaphragm in the left upper quadrant.

Question 4551. In which part of the embryo does the spleen first appear, and what carries it to its adult position? The answer is the mesoderm of the dorsal mesogastrium, carried to the left by the rotation of the stomach. It forms in the fifth week between the two layers of that mesentery. The same rotation moves the pancreas and helps to create the lesser sac.

Question 4552. What is the normal length of the adult spleen: about 3 centimeters, about 12 centimeters, about 25 centimeters, or about 40 centimeters? The answer is about 12 centimeters. It is about 7 centimeters wide and 3 to 4 centimeters thick, and it weighs about 150 grams. The organ is largest in early adult life and shrinks gradually after middle age.

Question 4553. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan shows a large mass in the left upper quadrant between the stomach and the left kidney, supplied by a branch of the celiac trunk, with the tail of the pancreas against its medial border. Which organ is shown? The answer is the spleen. Its position and its blood supply fit the organ exactly. A pancreatic mass would lie more central.

Question 4554. Which branch of the splenic artery runs in the gastrosplenic ligament to the fundus of the stomach, and which one runs along the greater curvature? The answer is the short gastric arteries and the left gastroepiploic artery. Both arise from the splenic artery. They reach the stomach through the gastrosplenic ligament and the greater omentum.

Question 4555. Which of these structures is not related to the spleen: the diaphragm, the left kidney, the tail of the pancreas, or the right colic flexure? The answer is the right colic flexure. It lies on the right side of the abdomen below the liver. The left colic flexure, and not the right, leaves the colic impression on the spleen.

Question 4556. Which statement about the splenic vein is correct: it lies in front of the pancreas, it runs behind the pancreas and joins the superior mesenteric vein, it drains into the inferior vena cava, or it carries lymph rather than blood? The answer is that it runs behind the pancreas and joins the superior mesenteric vein. That junction forms the portal vein behind the neck of the pancreas. Blood from the spleen therefore passes through the liver.

Question 4557. Which artery arises from the celiac trunk, runs a tortuous course to the left, and enters the spleen at its hilum? The answer is the splenic artery. It is the largest branch of the celiac trunk. Its tortuous course lets it stretch as the spleen moves with breathing.

Question 4558. A described section of the abdomen shows an organ wrapped in peritoneum except where vessels enter its medial border, with a fold of peritoneum running from that border to the greater curvature of the stomach. What is that fold called? The answer is the gastrosplenic ligament. It carries the short gastric vessels. The peritoneum leaves the spleen bare only at the hilum.

Question 4559. Which of these functions belongs to the spleen: the production of bile, the storage of bile, the removal of aged red blood cells from the blood, or the absorption of fat from the intestine? The answer is the removal of aged red blood cells from the blood. The red pulp filters the blood and its macrophages take up worn-out cells. Before birth the spleen can also form blood cells.

Question 4560. A constructed illustration: a person has a fracture of the left ninth, tenth and eleventh ribs after a fall, and the left side of the abdomen becomes tender. Which organ lies directly beneath those ribs and is at risk? The answer is the spleen. It lies against the diaphragm under the ninth to the eleventh ribs. A fractured rib on that side can tear its capsule.

Question 4561. Which statement about the development of the spleen is correct: it develops from the endoderm of the gut, it develops in the dorsal mesogastrium and comes to lie on the left, it develops in the ventral mesogastrium, or it develops from the neural crest? The answer is that it develops in the dorsal mesogastrium and comes to lie on the left. Rotation of the stomach carries it to the left side of the abdomen.

Question 4562. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the red pulp with immune responses, the white pulp with filtration of blood, the splenic capsule with smooth muscle, or the splenic hilum with the tail of the liver? The answer is the splenic capsule with smooth muscle. The white pulp is the lymphoid part, and the red pulp filters blood. The hilum transmits the splenic vessels.

Question 4563. The tail of the pancreas reaches the hilum of the spleen inside which ligament, and which organ lies immediately behind that point? The answer is the splenorenal ligament, with the left kidney behind it. The splenic vessels run in the same ligament. Injury to the tail there can release pancreatic enzymes into the surrounding tissue.

Question 4564. What name is given to the notch on the superior border of the spleen, and what does it indicate about the development of the organ? The answer is the splenic notch, which marks a line of incomplete fusion between lobules. Several notches may be present. They are normal, and they help to identify the organ at operation and on imaging.

Question 4565. Which statement about the relations of the spleen is correct: its upper surface touches the left pleura through the diaphragm, its medial border touches the liver, its lower pole rests on the right kidney, or its anterior surface touches the bladder? The answer is that its upper surface touches the left pleura through the diaphragm. That relation explains why an inflamed spleen can irritate the pleura.

Question 4566. A described dissection of the hilum of the spleen shows a tortuous artery lying above a wider thin-walled vein, with a lobulated yellowish mass behind them. Which structures are shown? The answer is the splenic artery above the splenic vein, with the tail of the pancreas behind them. The artery is tortuous and the vein is wider and smooth. The yellowish mass is the tail of the pancreas.

Question 4567. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the gastric impression with the left kidney, the renal impression with the stomach, the colic impression with the left colic flexure, or the splenic hilum with the left ureter? The answer is the colic impression with the left colic flexure. The gastric impression holds the stomach and the renal impression the left kidney. The hilum holds the splenic vessels.

Question 4568. Which statement about the branches of the splenic artery inside the spleen is correct: they join one another freely, they behave as end arteries and each supplies its own segment, they drain straight into the splenic vein without a capillary bed, or they supply the left kidney as well? The answer is that they behave as end arteries and each supplies its own segment. A block of one branch therefore damages only part of the organ.

Question 4569. A constructed illustration: a person with an enlarged spleen has sudden pain in the left upper abdomen after a minor blow, and the blood pressure falls. Which structure has most likely torn? The answer is the capsule of the spleen. When the organ enlarges, the capsule is stretched and the lower pole comes to lie below the rib cage. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 4570. True or false: an enlarged spleen descends below the left costal margin and can then be felt on examination. The answer is true. The organ moves downward along its own oblique axis as it enlarges. In health it stays beneath the rib cage and cannot be felt.

Question 4571. Which vessel carries blood from the spleen to the liver? The answer is the splenic vein, which joins the superior mesenteric vein to form the portal vein. It runs behind the pancreas on its way to the right, and it has no valves.

Question 4572. A described computed tomography scan shows a notched organ against the left chest wall, with the stomach in front of it and the left kidney below and behind it. Which organ is shown? The answer is the spleen, whose long axis follows the tenth rib. The notch lies on its superior border.

Question 4573. Which organ lies immediately in front of the spleen: the left kidney, the stomach, the pancreas, or the left ureter? The answer is the stomach. Its fundus and upper body rest against the gastric impression. The kidney lies below and behind.

Question 4574. Name the two peritoneal ligaments that attach the spleen, spoken as a list. The answer is the gastrosplenic ligament and the splenorenal ligament. The first carries the short gastric vessels, and the second carries the splenic vessels and the tail of the pancreas.

Question 4575. On a transverse section at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra, does the spleen lie in front of or behind the stomach, and in front of or behind the upper pole of the left kidney? The answer is behind the stomach and in front of the upper pole of the left kidney. The kidney lies against the posterior wall.

Question 4576. Which peritoneal ligament carries the splenic vessels from the hilum toward the midline? The answer is the splenorenal ligament, which runs from the hilum to the left kidney. The splenic artery, the splenic vein and the tail of the pancreas all pass through it.

Question 4577. Which statement about the white pulp of the spleen is correct: it filters aged red cells, it is lymphoid tissue around the central arteries, it stores fat, or it produces insulin? The answer is that it is lymphoid tissue around the central arteries. It holds both T lymphocytes and B lymphocytes.

Question 4578. A described specimen shows an organ with a smooth convex surface, and on the other side three shallow impressions around a central hilum. Which organ is this, and which impression is the largest? The answer is the spleen, and the gastric impression is the largest. The renal and the colic impressions lie below it.

Question 4579. Which statement about the spleen and the blood is correct: it makes red blood cells throughout adult life, it stores platelets and can release them when they are needed, it makes plasma proteins, or it destroys white cells only? The answer is that it stores platelets and can release them when they are needed. In disease it can also resume making red cells.

Question 4580. Which structure lies immediately in front of the splenic vein as it runs behind the body of the pancreas, and which vessel lies above it? The answer is the body of the pancreas in front, with the splenic artery above. The left kidney lies behind the vein.

Question 4581. Which statement about the tail of the pancreas is correct: it lies in the gastrosplenic ligament, it lies in the splenorenal ligament close to the splenic hilum, it lies in the lesser omentum, or it lies behind the right kidney? The answer is that it lies in the splenorenal ligament close to the splenic hilum. It is the only part of the pancreas that reaches the spleen.

Question 4582. Which structure forms the upper limit of the spleen, separating it from the left lung? The answer is the diaphragm. The convex upper surface of the spleen lies against it. Above the diaphragm lie the left pleura and the base of the left lung.

Question 4583. A described ultrasound image shows a rounded organ in the left upper abdomen that moves downward as the person breathes in, with a notch on its upper border. Which organ is shown? The answer is the spleen, which moves because it lies against the diaphragm. The notch on its superior border is normal.

Question 4584. In which quadrant of the abdomen does the spleen lie: the right upper quadrant, the left upper quadrant, the right lower quadrant, or the left lower quadrant? The answer is the left upper quadrant. It lies in the left hypochondrium, above the left colic flexure.

Question 4585. A constructed illustration: a person with fever and an enlarged spleen feels pain at the left shoulder when the abdomen is pressed. Which nerve carries that sensation? The answer is the phrenic nerve, which supplies the diaphragm over the spleen. The same spinal segments supply the skin of the shoulder.

Question 4586. Which statement about the position of the spleen in relation to the ribs is correct: it lies deep to the ninth to the eleventh ribs on the left, it lies deep to the fifth to the seventh ribs on the left, it lies below the twelfth rib, or it lies deep to the ribs on the right? The answer is that it lies deep to the ninth to the eleventh ribs on the left. Its long axis follows the tenth rib.

Question 4587. True or false: the splenic vein carries blood from the spleen directly to the inferior vena cava. The answer is false. The splenic vein joins the superior mesenteric vein to form the portal vein. Blood from the spleen therefore passes through the liver first.

Question 4588. Which vessel runs behind the body of the pancreas while the splenic artery runs above the gland, and where does that vessel end? The answer is the splenic vein, which ends where it joins the superior mesenteric vein. The artery lies along the upper border of the pancreas.

Question 4589. What name is given to a spleen that is displaced from its usual position because its supporting ligaments are lax? The answer is a wandering spleen. The organ can then lie lower in the abdomen or even in the pelvis. The condition may be discovered when the organ twists.

Question 4590. Which statement about the venous drainage of the spleen is correct: the splenic vein runs in front of the pancreas, it receives the inferior mesenteric vein before it joins the superior mesenteric vein, it drains into the left renal vein, or it has many valves? The answer is that it receives the inferior mesenteric vein before it joins the superior mesenteric vein. Together they form the portal vein.

Question 4591. A described specimen of the spleen is cut across, and the cut surface shows small pale nodules scattered through a dark red background. What are the pale nodules? The answer is the white pulp, with the red pulp around it. The white pulp is lymphoid tissue around the central arteries.

Question 4592. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the upper surface of the spleen with the left pleura, the visceral surface with the right lung, the gastric impression with the left ureter, or the renal impression with the colon? The answer is the upper surface of the spleen with the left pleura. The two are separated by the diaphragm.

Question 4593. Which function of the spleen explains the finding of abnormal particles inside red cells in a person whose spleen has been removed: the storage of fat, the removal of particles from red cells in the red pulp, the production of bile, or the secretion of hormones? The answer is the removal of particles from red cells in the red pulp. Without the organ they remain in the circulating red cells.

Question 4594. A described venogram shows the splenic vein blocked near the hilum, with contrast medium flowing backward along veins that run in the gastrosplenic ligament toward the fundus of the stomach. Which veins are shown? The answer is the short gastric veins, which drain the fundus into the splenic vein. When that vein is blocked they dilate.

Question 4595. True or false: accessory spleens near the hilum can enlarge and take over part of the work of the spleen after the main organ is removed. The answer is true. They are small masses of splenic tissue in the gastrosplenic ligament or near the hilum. Their presence is common and is not a disease.

Question 4596. Which border of the spleen carries the notches that help to identify it, and what do they mark? The answer is the superior border, and the notches mark the lines along which its lobules fused. They are a normal feature of the adult organ.

Question 4597. A described arteriogram shows a tortuous artery running to the left along the upper border of the pancreas, breaking into branches that fan out at the medial border of an organ. Which vessel and organ are shown? The answer is the splenic artery and the spleen, whose branches enter at the hilum and supply separate segments.

Question 4598. Which statement about the spleen and the left colic flexure is correct: the flexure leaves an impression on the visceral surface, the flexure lies above the spleen, the flexure is attached to the hilum, or the flexure lies behind the spleen? The answer is that the flexure leaves an impression on the visceral surface. It lies below and in front of the lower pole.

Question 4599. Name the three impressions found on the visceral surface of the spleen, spoken as a list. The answer is the gastric impression, the renal impression and the colic impression. The gastric impression is the largest, and the renal impression holds the left kidney.

Question 4600. Which ligament runs from the left colic flexure to the diaphragm and helps to support the lower pole of the spleen? The answer is the phrenicocolic ligament, which forms a shelf beneath the lower pole. The spleen rests upon it.

To summarize, the thoracic duct begins at the cisterna chyli in the abdomen behind the diaphragm, passes upward through the aortic hiatus, and crosses to the left at the level of the fourth thoracic vertebra before it ends at the left venous angle. It drains both lower limbs, the pelvis, the abdomen, the left arm, and the left side of the head and chest, and it carries chyle after a fatty meal. The spleen lies in the left hypochondrium between the stomach and the diaphragm, covered by peritoneum except at its hilum, attached by the gastrosplenic and splenorenal ligaments, supplied by the splenic artery and drained by the splenic vein to the portal vein. Its red pulp filters the blood and its white pulp mounts immune responses, and the ninth to the eleventh ribs protect it.
