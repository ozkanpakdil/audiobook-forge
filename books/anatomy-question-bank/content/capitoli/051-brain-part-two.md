# Chapter 51 — Brain, Part Two

This chapter continues the survey of the brain by looking at its deepest parts: the brainstem, the basal nuclei, the thalamus and the hypothalamus. It passes from the stalk of the brain, with its medulla, pons and midbrain, to the masses of gray matter that shape movement, and then to the thalamus and the small control center beneath it. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The structures named here are the ones a clinician follows when a stroke, a tremor or a disorder of hormone control has to be explained aloud. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 5001. Which paired ridge on the anterior surface of the medulla oblongata carries the fibers that run from the cerebral cortex to the spinal cord? The answer is the pyramid. The corticospinal fibers gather into a compact bundle that raises the surface into a ridge. Most of them cross to the opposite side at the lower end of the medulla.

Question 5002. Which of these is the uppermost part of the brainstem: the medulla oblongata, the pons, the midbrain, or the spinal cord? The answer is the midbrain. The brainstem runs upward from the spinal cord as medulla, pons and midbrain in that order. The midbrain therefore lies closest to the thalamus.

Question 5003. What is the name of the broad rounded prominence of the brainstem that lies between the medulla oblongata below and the midbrain above? The answer is the pons. Its name means bridge, because a great band of its fibers crosses to the cerebellum behind it. The basilar artery lies in a groove along its front surface.

Question 5004. Which cranial nerve nucleus lies in the midbrain at the level of the superior colliculus and supplies the muscle that narrows the pupil: the third, the fourth, the sixth, or the twelfth cranial nerve? The answer is the third cranial nerve. It also supplies most of the other muscles that move the eye and the muscle that raises the upper eyelid. The fourth and sixth nerves supply one muscle each.

Question 5005. A described transverse section of the brainstem shows two thick columns in front, a dark band behind them, a rounded red nucleus on each side, and a narrow channel in the middle. Which part of the brainstem is shown? The answer is the midbrain at the level of the superior colliculus. The columns are the cerebral peduncles and the dark band is the substantia nigra.

Question 5006. Which structures lie immediately behind the cerebral aqueduct in the midbrain? The answer is the superior and the inferior colliculi. Together they form the tectum, the roof of the midbrain. The superior colliculus lies above the inferior one on each side of the midline.

Question 5007. A constructed illustration: a small blockage of an artery leaves a person weak on the opposite side, with a weak tongue on the side of the lesion and loss of position sense on the other side. Which structure in the medulla is involved? The answer is the pyramid. In the medial medullary syndrome the corticospinal fibers, the twelfth cranial nerve and the medial lemniscus are affected together.

Question 5008. Where do most fibers of the corticospinal tract cross to the opposite side: in the midbrain, in the pons, in the upper medulla, or in the lower medulla? The answer is the lower medulla. The crossing is visible on the surface as the decussation of the pyramids. A small number of fibers stay uncrossed and descend in the anterior corticospinal tract.

Question 5009. True or false: the brainstem is made up of the midbrain, the pons and the medulla oblongata. The answer is true. Those three parts join the spinal cord below to the diencephalon above. The cerebellum lies behind the brainstem and is not counted as part of it.

Question 5010. A described section through the back of the head shows a diamond-shaped cavity whose floor is formed by the pons and the medulla and whose roof is formed by the cerebellum. Which cavity is shown? The answer is the fourth ventricle. It is continuous below with the central canal of the spinal cord and above with the cerebral aqueduct. Cerebrospinal fluid fills it.

Question 5011. What is the name of the two thick columns on the front of the midbrain, one on each side of the midline? The answer is the cerebral peduncles. They carry the descending fibers from the cerebral cortex to the brainstem and the spinal cord. The interpeduncular fossa lies in the midline between them.

Question 5012. Which cranial nerve nucleus lies in the pons and supplies the muscles that produce the movements of the face: the third, the fifth, the seventh, or the twelfth cranial nerve? The answer is the seventh cranial nerve. Its fibers loop around the nucleus of the sixth cranial nerve before they leave the brainstem. The fifth cranial nerve supplies the muscles of chewing instead.

Question 5013. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superior colliculus with hearing, the inferior colliculus with sight, the superior colliculus with visual reflexes, or the inferior colliculus with smell? The answer is the superior colliculus with visual reflexes. It receives fibers from the retina and turns the head and the eyes toward a moving object. The inferior colliculus belongs to the pathway for hearing.

Question 5014. Which vessel runs in the midline along the anterior surface of the pons? The answer is the basilar artery. It is formed where the two vertebral arteries join at the lower border of the pons. It lies in a groove on the surface of the pons and gives off branches to the brainstem and the cerebellum.

Question 5015. What is the name of the oval swelling on the lateral side of the upper medulla, just behind the pyramid? The answer is the olive. It is produced by the inferior olivary nucleus, a folded mass of gray matter beneath the surface that sends a large bundle of fibers to the cerebellum.

Question 5016. Which tract in the lateral part of the medulla carries pain and temperature sense from the opposite side of the body: the posterior column, the spinothalamic tract, the corticospinal tract, or the medial lemniscus? The answer is the spinothalamic tract. Its fibers have already crossed in the spinal cord, so those in the medulla come from the opposite side. The posterior column carries fine touch and position sense instead.

Question 5017. A constructed illustration: a blockage of the posterior inferior cerebellar artery leaves a person dizzy, hoarse and unable to swallow, with loss of pain and temperature sense on the face and on the opposite side of the body. Which part of the brainstem is involved? The answer is the lateral part of the medulla, where the vestibular nuclei, the nucleus ambiguus and the spinothalamic tract lie close together.

Question 5018. A described transverse section of the brainstem shows four rounded bumps on the back, two above and two below, with a narrow channel in front of them. At which level was the cut made? The answer is the midbrain, at the level of the colliculi. The four bumps are the superior and inferior colliculi on each side. The channel in front of them is the cerebral aqueduct.

Question 5019. Name two cranial nerves whose fibers leave the brainstem at the junction of the pons and the medulla, spoken as a list. The answer is the sixth cranial nerve and the seventh cranial nerve. The sixth lies nearer the midline and turns the eye outward. The seventh lies farther out and moves the muscles of the face.

Question 5020. Which cranial nerve nucleus lies in the midbrain and supplies the muscle that raises the upper eyelid: the third, the fourth, the sixth, or the seventh cranial nerve? The answer is the third cranial nerve. A lesion of that nerve leaves the eyelid drooping and the pupil wide. The fourth cranial nerve supplies the superior oblique muscle instead.

Question 5021. Which nucleus in the medulla receives the fibers of taste from the back of the tongue and from the throat? The answer is the nucleus of the solitary tract. Taste fibers that travel with the facial, glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves all end in it. The same nucleus also receives information from the internal organs.

Question 5022. A described magnetic resonance image of the lower part of the head shows a rounded structure passing through the foramen magnum, with the cerebellum behind it and the vertebral arteries beside it. Which structure is shown? The answer is the medulla oblongata. It is the lowest part of the brainstem and continues below as the spinal cord. The foramen magnum marks the boundary between the two.

Question 5023. The superior cerebellar peduncles carry fibers between the cerebellum and which part of the brainstem? The answer is the midbrain. They leave the cerebellum, run upward and enter the midbrain just below the colliculi. The middle and inferior cerebellar peduncles join the pons and the medulla instead.

Question 5024. A constructed illustration: a person has a drooping eyelid, a wide pupil and an eye that rests turned down and outward. Which cranial nerve is most likely affected? The answer is the third cranial nerve, in a described palsy. The muscles that raise the eyelid and most of the muscles that move the eye lose their supply. The pupil widens because the small fibers that narrow it are lost.

Question 5025. Which of these is the chief function of the reticular formation of the brainstem: fine touch, hearing, wakefulness, or smell? The answer is wakefulness. The reticular formation is a network of cells and short fibers spread through the brainstem. Its ascending fibers keep the cerebral cortex alert, and its descending fibers adjust muscle tone and posture.

Question 5026. True or false: every corticospinal fiber crosses to the opposite side in the medulla oblongata. The answer is false. Most fibers cross at the decussation of the pyramids, but a small group continues uncrossed in the anterior corticospinal tract. Those fibers cross lower down, in the segment of the spinal cord where they end.

Question 5027. Which part of the brainstem continues below into the spinal cord through the foramen magnum? The answer is the medulla oblongata. It is the lowest part of the brainstem and the highest part of the central nervous system within the skull. Its cavity is continuous with the central canal of the spinal cord.

Question 5028. A described transverse section of the lowest part of the brainstem shows a closed central canal, a pair of ridges in front, and a large nucleus on each side behind them. Which part is shown? The answer is the lower medulla oblongata. The ridges are the pyramids, and the nuclei behind them belong to the posterior column pathway.

Question 5029. Which tract lies close to the midline in the medulla and carries fine touch and position sense from the opposite side of the body: the spinothalamic tract, the medial lemniscus, the anterior corticospinal tract, or the inferior cerebellar peduncle? The answer is the medial lemniscus. Its fibers come from the nucleus gracilis and the nucleus cuneatus and cross in the midline as the internal arcuate fibers.

Question 5030. Which depression lies between the two cerebral peduncles on the front of the midbrain, and what emerges from it? The answer is the interpeduncular fossa, from which the third cranial nerve emerges. Small vessels also pass through its floor. The cerebral peduncles form its two sides.

Question 5031. A constructed illustration: a blockage of the basilar artery leaves a person awake but unable to move the limbs or to speak, with only the eyes moving up and down. Which part of the brainstem is destroyed? The answer is the front of the pons, where the descending fibers from the cortex lie close together, while the reticular formation behind them keeps a person awake.

Question 5032. Which of these pairs belongs together: the medulla with the visual cortex, the midbrain with hearing, the medulla with the centers for breathing and for the heart, or the pons with smell? The answer is the medulla with the centers for breathing and for the heart. Those centers lie in the reticular formation of the medulla. The midbrain serves the visual and hearing reflexes instead.

Question 5033. Which dark band of gray matter lies in the midbrain just behind the cerebral peduncle and sends dopamine to the striatum? The answer is the substantia nigra. Its name means black substance, from the pigment in its cells. Its loss is the central change in parkinsonism.

Question 5034. Which brainstem nucleus contains the cells that send parasympathetic fibers to the heart, the lungs and the gut: the nucleus of the third cranial nerve, the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus, the nucleus ambiguus, or the facial nucleus? The answer is the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus, in the medulla. Its fibers leave with the tenth cranial nerve.

Question 5035. A described specimen shows the front of the whole brainstem, with a pair of ridges beside the midline below, a broad bulge above them, and two thick columns above that. Which three parts are shown, in order from below? The answer is the medulla oblongata, the pons and the midbrain. The ridges below are the pyramids and the columns above are the cerebral peduncles.

Question 5036. Which part of the brainstem lies immediately above the medulla oblongata and immediately below the midbrain? The answer is the pons. It lies in the middle of the three parts of the brainstem. The fourth ventricle lies behind it and the cerebellum lies beyond that.

Question 5037. Which cranial nerve nucleus lies beside the midline in the medulla and supplies the muscles of the tongue: the fifth, the seventh, the twelfth, or the third cranial nerve? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve. It lies close to the midline behind the pyramid, and its fibers leave between the pyramid and the olive. The other three lie higher in the brainstem and farther from the midline.

Question 5038. Which band of fibers crosses the midline in the tegmentum at the junction of the pons and the midbrain and connects the two red nuclei? The answer is the decussation of the superior cerebellar peduncles. The fibers come from the cerebellum, mainly from the dentate nucleus, and cross on their way to the thalamus. The crossing is a useful landmark at that junction.

Question 5039. Which large gray mass of the basal nuclei is shaped like the letter C and follows the curve of the lateral ventricle? The answer is the caudate nucleus. Its head lies in front of the thalamus, its body runs along the roof of the ventricle, and its tail curves down into the temporal lobe. It belongs to the input side of the basal nuclei.

Question 5040. Which of these structures is part of the lentiform nucleus: the caudate nucleus, the putamen, the amygdala, or the claustrum? The answer is the putamen. The lentiform nucleus is a wedge of gray matter made of the putamen and the globus pallidus. The caudate nucleus lies separately, in the wall of the lateral ventricle.

Question 5041. Which part of the lentiform nucleus is the smaller, paler, medial part that forms the main output of the basal nuclei? The answer is the globus pallidus. It lies medial to the putamen and is divided into an internal and an external segment. Its fibers pass to the thalamus.

Question 5042. True or false: the putamen and the globus pallidus together make up the lentiform nucleus. The answer is true. The two lie side by side and together form a wedge whose point faces the midline. The internal capsule separates the wedge from the caudate nucleus and the thalamus.

Question 5043. Which of these structures is not part of the basal nuclei: the caudate nucleus, the putamen, the globus pallidus, or the thalamus? The answer is the thalamus. The thalamus is the great sensory relay and lies medial to the basal nuclei. The basal nuclei are the caudate nucleus, the putamen and the globus pallidus, with the amygdala and the claustrum often named beside them.

Question 5044. What is the name of the thin sheet of gray matter that lies between the putamen and the insula, separated from each of them by a narrow band of white matter? The answer is the claustrum. The band between it and the putamen is the external capsule, and the band outside it is the extreme capsule. Its function is still debated.

Question 5045. Which mass of gray matter lies deep in the temporal lobe, in front of the inferior horn of the lateral ventricle, and belongs to the limbic system: the amygdala, the claustrum, the globus pallidus, or the pulvinar? The answer is the amygdala. It is often grouped with the basal nuclei because of its position. It is concerned with emotion, especially fear, and with the sense of smell.

Question 5046. Which part of the substantia nigra sends dopamine to the striatum, and which part sends fibers to the thalamus and the colliculi? The answer is the pars compacta for dopamine and the pars reticulata for the other fibers. The compact part lies behind and its cells carry pigment. The reticular part lies in front of it and acts as one of the output nuclei of the basal nuclei.

Question 5047. The internal capsule lies between which two groups of structures? The answer is the caudate nucleus and the thalamus on its medial side and the lentiform nucleus on its lateral side. Its fibers run up and down between the cortex and the brainstem. A small lesion there can therefore cause a large paralysis.

Question 5048. A described coronal section through the brain shows a wedge of gray matter whose broad base faces outward and whose narrow point faces the midline, with white matter in front of it and behind it. Which structure is shown? The answer is the lentiform nucleus. The white matter in front of it is the anterior limb of the internal capsule. The white matter behind it is the posterior limb.

Question 5049. Which of these pairs belongs together: the amygdala with the occipital lobe, the caudate nucleus with the cerebellum, the amygdala with the temporal lobe, or the globus pallidus with the frontal pole? The answer is the amygdala with the temporal lobe. It lies in the deep part of that lobe, near the inferior horn of the lateral ventricle. The caudate nucleus follows the lateral ventricle instead.

Question 5050. From which of these does the striatum receive its main input: the cerebral cortex, the spinal cord, the cerebellum, or the retina? The answer is the cerebral cortex. Fibers arrive from many areas of the cortex and end in the caudate nucleus and the putamen. The cerebellum sends its fibers to the thalamus instead.

Question 5051. Name the two parts of the substantia nigra, spoken as a list. The answer is the pars compacta and the pars reticulata. The compact part lies behind and holds the pigmented cells that make dopamine. The reticular part lies in front and forms one of the output nuclei of the basal nuclei.

Question 5052. A described transverse section through the diencephalon shows a small round nucleus above the midbrain, medial to the internal capsule, with the substantia nigra behind it. Which nucleus is shown? The answer is the subthalamic nucleus. It lies at the junction of the midbrain and the diencephalon. It belongs to the indirect pathway of the basal nuclei.

Question 5053. A constructed illustration: a small hemorrhage damages a nucleus just above the midbrain, and the person develops sudden violent flinging movements of the arm and the leg on the opposite side. Which nucleus is involved? The answer is the subthalamic nucleus. Its usual activity excites the output nuclei of the basal nuclei, so its loss releases the movement. The illustration is a teaching example and not a real case.

Question 5054. Which of these does an active direct pathway do: excite the internal segment of the globus pallidus, inhibit it and release the thalamus, excite the subthalamic nucleus, or inhibit the cerebral cortex? The answer is that it inhibits the internal segment of the globus pallidus and so releases the thalamus. That nucleus normally holds the thalamus in check. The indirect pathway does the opposite.

Question 5055. Which structure does the indirect pathway use to raise the output of the internal segment of the globus pallidus: the subthalamic nucleus, the amygdala, the claustrum, or the red nucleus? The answer is the subthalamic nucleus. It excites the internal segment, which then holds the thalamus back and makes movement harder. Loss of the subthalamic nucleus therefore releases movement.

Question 5056. The tail of the caudate nucleus lies in the roof of which cavity? The answer is the inferior horn of the lateral ventricle. The tail curves downward and forward through the temporal lobe and ends beside the amygdala. The body of the nucleus lies along the roof of the central part of the ventricle instead.

Question 5057. A constructed illustration: an adult develops jerking movements of the limbs, a change of personality and failing memory, and imaging shows that the caudate nucleus has shrunk on both sides. Which nucleus is affected, and why does the movement disorder appear? The answer is the caudate nucleus, whose loss removes part of the indirect pathway that normally restrains movement.

Question 5058. Which of these explains why a small lesion in the internal segment of the globus pallidus reduces rigidity and tremor: because that nucleus is the main output of the basal nuclei, because it is their main input, because it makes dopamine, or because it begins the corticospinal tract? The answer is that it is the main output, and its loss releases the thalamus. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 5059. A described specimen of the insula shows a thin gray sheet with a band of white matter on each side, the inner band separating it from the putamen and the outer band separating it from the cortex. Which structure lies between the two bands? The answer is the claustrum. The inner band is the external capsule and the outer band is the extreme capsule.

Question 5060. Which nucleus lies where the head of the caudate nucleus meets the putamen, below the front of the internal capsule, and is concerned with reward and with addiction? The answer is the nucleus accumbens. It forms part of the ventral striatum. It lies close to the midline in the front part of the base of the brain.

Question 5061. Which structure forms the medial boundary of the putamen: the globus pallidus, the claustrum, the caudate nucleus, or the thalamus? The answer is the globus pallidus. The putamen and the globus pallidus lie side by side within the lentiform nucleus. The claustrum lies lateral to the putamen and the thalamus lies beyond the internal capsule.

Question 5062. In a coronal section through the brain at the level of the interventricular foramen, which of these lists the structures in the correct order from medial to lateral: thalamus, internal capsule, lentiform nucleus, insula, or insula, thalamus, lentiform nucleus, internal capsule? The answer is thalamus, internal capsule, lentiform nucleus, insula. The insula lies on the surface of the hemisphere.

Question 5063. Which nucleus of the thalamus receives fine touch and position sense from the opposite side of the body and sends them to the cortex? The answer is the ventral posterior lateral nucleus. It lies in the lateral group of thalamic nuclei. Fibers from the face end in the ventral posterior medial nucleus beside it.

Question 5064. Which of these is the relay for vision on its way to the cortex: the medial geniculate body, the lateral geniculate body, the pulvinar, or the anterior nucleus? The answer is the lateral geniculate body. It lies under the back of the thalamus and receives fibers from the retina. The medial geniculate body serves hearing instead.

Question 5065. What is the name of the small swelling below the pulvinar that receives fibers from the hearing pathway? The answer is the medial geniculate body. It sends its fibers to the hearing area of the temporal lobe. The lateral geniculate body beside it belongs to vision, and the two together are called the metathalamus.

Question 5066. A described horizontal section through the middle of the brain shows a band of gray matter joining the two thalami across the midline, surrounded by the cavity of the third ventricle. Which structure is shown? The answer is the interthalamic adhesion, also called the massa intermedia. It is present in most brains but not in all of them. It carries no large bundle of fibers.

Question 5067. Which thalamic nucleus receives fine touch from the face and sends it to the cortex: the ventral posterior medial nucleus, the ventral posterior lateral nucleus, the ventral anterior nucleus, or the pulvinar? The answer is the ventral posterior medial nucleus. The face is represented there, next to the hand and the body in the nucleus beside it. The ventral posterior lateral nucleus carries the rest of the body instead.

Question 5068. True or false: the thalamus lies on each side of the third ventricle. The answer is true. The two thalami form the greater part of the wall of that cavity. They are joined across the middle in most brains by the interthalamic adhesion.

Question 5069. Which two thalamic nuclei receive fibers from the basal nuclei and the cerebellum and send them to the motor cortex: the ventral anterior and ventral lateral nuclei, the anterior and medial nuclei, the pulvinar and the lateral geniculate body, or the ventral posterior nuclei? The answer is the ventral anterior and ventral lateral nuclei. They lie in the front part of the lateral group. The ventral posterior nuclei carry sensation instead.

Question 5070. Which nucleus of the thalamus lies in the front part of its dorsal surface, receives fibers from the mammillary body, and belongs to the circuit of memory? The answer is the anterior nucleus. It lies just behind the interventricular foramen, inside the internal medullary lamina. Its fibers pass back to the cingulate gyrus.

Question 5071. Which large nucleus forms the back end of the thalamus and is concerned with vision and with the direction of attention? The answer is the pulvinar. It is the largest of the thalamic nuclei and lies above and behind the medial geniculate body. Its connections are mainly with the association areas of the cortex.

Question 5072. Which sheet of white matter divides the thalamus into an anterior part, a medial part and a lateral part: the internal medullary lamina, the external capsule, the corpus callosum, or the cerebral peduncle? The answer is the internal medullary lamina. It is a thin band of fibers that divides the gray matter into the three parts. Small intralaminar nuclei lie within it.

Question 5073. Which cavity lies between the two halves of the thalamus, and what forms its floor? The answer is the third ventricle, whose floor is formed by the hypothalamus. The thalamus forms its two walls. Its roof is a thin sheet of tissue that carries a small knot of vessels.

Question 5074. A described horizontal section through the brain shows a large egg-shaped mass of gray matter beside the midline, with white matter along its outer border and the head of the caudate nucleus in front of it. Which structure is shown? The answer is the thalamus. The white matter along its outer border is the posterior limb of the internal capsule.

Question 5075. Which group of thalamic nuclei lies within the internal medullary lamina and is concerned with awareness and with pain: the intralaminar nuclei, the anterior nucleus, the pulvinar, or the lateral geniculate body? The answer is the intralaminar nuclei, of which the centromedian nucleus is the largest. They project widely to the cerebral cortex and to the striatum. They are not part of the main sensory relay.

Question 5076. A constructed illustration: a small lesion destroys the lateral geniculate body on one side. Which loss of sight follows? The answer is loss of the opposite half of the visual field in both eyes. Fibers from the same half of each retina end in that body. The pupil still reacts to light, because the fibers that serve that reflex leave the pathway before they reach it.

Question 5077. A described magnetic resonance image shows a small lesion in the lateral part of the thalamus on one side, and the person has lost all forms of sensation on the opposite side of the body and the face. Which nucleus is damaged? The answer is the ventral posterior nucleus, whose lateral part serves the body and whose medial part serves the face. Every sensory pathway except smell relays there.

Question 5078. A constructed illustration: months after a small stroke in the thalamus, a person has severe burning pain on the opposite side of the body that is made worse by touch and by cold. Which structure was damaged? The answer is the thalamus, in the thalamic pain syndrome. The pain follows damage to the sensory nuclei and their cortical connections. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 5079. The thalamus is supplied mainly by branches of which artery: the middle cerebral artery, the posterior cerebral artery, the anterior cerebral artery, or the basilar artery? The answer is the posterior cerebral artery. Its small branches enter the back of the thalamus and supply most of it. The front and medial parts receive small branches from the back of the internal carotid artery.

Question 5080. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lateral geniculate body with hearing, the medial geniculate body with sight, the pulvinar with smell, or the medial geniculate body with hearing? The answer is the medial geniculate body with hearing. It receives fibers from the inferior colliculus and sends them to the temporal lobe. The lateral geniculate body belongs to vision instead.

Question 5081. Name the two nuclei of the metathalamus, spoken as a list. The answer is the lateral geniculate body and the medial geniculate body. They lie beneath the back of the thalamus, one for sight and one for hearing. The pulvinar lies above them.

Question 5082. In a horizontal section through the diencephalon, which of these lists the structures in the correct order from the midline outward: third ventricle, thalamus, internal capsule, lentiform nucleus, or thalamus, third ventricle, lentiform nucleus, internal capsule? The answer is third ventricle, thalamus, internal capsule, lentiform nucleus. The ventricle lies in the midline and the lentiform nucleus lies furthest out. The internal capsule separates the thalamus from the lentiform nucleus.

Question 5083. Which nucleus of the hypothalamus lies above the optic chiasm and makes antidiuretic hormone? The answer is the supraoptic nucleus. Its fibers run down the stalk to the back part of the pituitary gland. The hormone is stored and released there into the blood, where it controls the amount of water lost in the urine.

Question 5084. Where does the hypothalamus lie: above the thalamus, below the thalamus and in front of the midbrain, behind the cerebellum, or inside the temporal lobe? The answer is below the thalamus and in front of the midbrain. It forms the floor of the third ventricle, and the pituitary gland hangs beneath it on a stalk.

Question 5085. What are the names of the two rounded bodies that hang from the back of the floor of the hypothalamus? The answer is the mammillary bodies. They lie behind the infundibulum, one on each side of the midline. They receive fibers from the fornix and belong to the circuit of memory.

Question 5086. Which structure lies in front of the infundibulum and above the pituitary gland: the optic chiasm, the mammillary body, the pineal gland, or the splenium of the corpus callosum? The answer is the optic chiasm, where fibers from the two eyes cross. The infundibulum is the stalk that carries fibers down to the pituitary gland. The mammillary bodies lie behind the stalk.

Question 5087. True or false: the back part of the pituitary gland stores hormones that are made in the hypothalamus. The answer is true. The supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei make the hormones, and their fibers carry them down the stalk. The front part of the gland makes its own hormones instead.

Question 5088. Which nucleus of the hypothalamus lies beside the third ventricle above the supraoptic nucleus and makes oxytocin? The answer is the paraventricular nucleus. Its fibers run down the stalk to the back part of the pituitary gland. The same nucleus also sends fibers to the brainstem and the spinal cord.

Question 5089. Which part of the hypothalamus is most concerned with losing heat and with lowering body temperature: the posterior part, the anterior part, the lateral part, or the mammillary bodies? The answer is the anterior part. The posterior part acts in the opposite way and helps to conserve heat. The two together keep the temperature of the body steady.

Question 5090. Which nucleus of the hypothalamus is classically described as the center for the feeling of fullness after a meal: the lateral nucleus, the ventromedial nucleus, the arcuate nucleus, or the suprachiasmatic nucleus? The answer is the ventromedial nucleus. The lateral nucleus is described as the center that drives feeding instead. The two are spoken of as a pair that balances appetite.

Question 5091. Which small nucleus of the hypothalamus lies near the median eminence and sends its fibers into the portal vessels: the arcuate nucleus, the supraoptic nucleus, the suprachiasmatic nucleus, or the posterior nucleus? The answer is the arcuate nucleus. It lies in the tuberal region, close to the infundibulum. Its fibers end on the vessels of the median eminence.

Question 5092. A described sagittal section through the middle of the brain shows a small block of tissue below the thalamus, with the optic chiasm in front, a stalk in the middle and two rounded bodies behind. Which region is shown? The answer is the hypothalamus. The stalk is the infundibulum and the rounded bodies are the mammillary bodies.

Question 5093. Which tract carries fibers from the mammillary body to the anterior nucleus of the thalamus? The answer is the mammillothalamic tract. It runs upward and forward through the wall of the third ventricle. The fibers continue from the thalamus to the cingulate gyrus.

Question 5094. A described magnetic resonance image of the head shows a mass above the sella, growing on the stalk of the pituitary gland, with the optic chiasm stretched over it. Which region is compressed, and which structure is usually affected first? The answer is the suprasellar region, with the optic chiasm affected first. Loss of the outer parts of the visual fields follows.

Question 5095. A constructed illustration: after an operation near the base of the brain, a person passes large volumes of dilute urine and is constantly thirsty. Which nuclei have lost their function? The answer is the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus. Their fibers carry antidiuretic hormone down the stalk to the back part of the pituitary gland. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 5096. A constructed illustration: a child has attacks of laughter that cannot be controlled, together with a small mass hanging from the region of the tuber cinereum. Which region is involved, and why does the laughing appear? The answer is the hypothalamus, in a described malformation that joins the tuber cinereum. The mass lies within a circuit that normally holds emotional expression in check.

Question 5097. Which nucleus of the hypothalamus lies just above the optic chiasm and sets the daily rhythm of sleeping and waking: the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the supraoptic nucleus, the arcuate nucleus, or the posterior nucleus? The answer is the suprachiasmatic nucleus. It receives fibers directly from the retina by a special pathway. It then adjusts the rhythm of the rest of the body.

Question 5098. A described specimen shows the under surface of the brain with a small gray area behind the infundibulum and in front of the mammillary bodies, dotted with fine vessels. Which part of the hypothalamus is shown? The answer is the tuber cinereum, with the median eminence on its surface. The fine vessels are the portal vessels that carry releasing factors to the pituitary gland.

Question 5099. Which tract descends to end in the mammillary body, and what does it carry? The answer is the fornix, which carries fibers from the hippocampus. It curves downward and forward behind the anterior commissure and ends in the mammillary body. The mammillary body then sends fibers onward to the thalamus.

Question 5100. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supraoptic nucleus with oxytocin, the paraventricular nucleus with antidiuretic hormone, the ventromedial nucleus with thirst, or the supraoptic nucleus with antidiuretic hormone? The answer is the supraoptic nucleus with antidiuretic hormone. Both nuclei make both hormones, but the supraoptic nucleus is the classic source. The lateral nucleus is linked with thirst instead.

To summarize, the brainstem is the midbrain, the pons and the medulla oblongata, and it carries the cranial nerve nuclei, the long ascending and descending tracts, and the centers for breathing and for the heart. The basal nuclei, built from the caudate nucleus, the putamen and the globus pallidus, shape movement through the direct and indirect pathways that pass by way of the thalamus, and their damage produces tremor, rigidity, involuntary movement or loss of control. The thalamus relays every sensation except smell, serves sight and hearing through the geniculate bodies and movement through the ventral anterior and ventral lateral nuclei, and wraps around the third ventricle. The hypothalamus, small and placed below the thalamus, governs temperature, thirst, hunger, sleep, the daily rhythm and the hormones released through the pituitary gland. Naming each structure aloud with its neighbor fixes the deep brain in memory, and this chapter is educational material that does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection or the judgment of a teacher.
