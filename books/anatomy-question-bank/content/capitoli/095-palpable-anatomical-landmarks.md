# Chapter 95 — Palpable Anatomical Landmarks

This chapter walks the surface of the body with the hands, naming the bony points, cartilages, tendons, vessels and muscles that can be felt through the skin. It covers the landmarks of the head and the neck, the trunk, the upper limb and the lower limb, and then the steps of an anatomical examination built on them. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right. Every clinical item is a constructed illustration, and every measurement describes ordinary anatomy. This is educational material and does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9401. Which bony point can be felt in the midline at the back of the skull, just above the neck? The answer is the external occipital protuberance. It is a raised bump where the ligament of the neck and the trapezius muscle attach. It is also called the inion.

Question 9402. A bony prominence is felt behind and below the ear, and it is tender when the head turns. Which structure is it? The answer is the mastoid process of the temporal bone. It gives attachment to the sternocleidomastoid muscle, and air cells hollow it out and connect it with the middle ear.

Question 9403. Where does the mastoid process lie in relation to the external acoustic opening? The answer is behind and below it, with the sternocleidomastoid muscle attached to its tip. The ear canal opens in front of the process. The styloid process projects down and forward from its medial side.

Question 9404. At the level of which vertebra does the hyoid bone lie: the second cervical, the third cervical, the fifth cervical, or the first thoracic? The answer is the third cervical vertebra. The hyoid is a small curved bone above the thyroid cartilage. It is the only bone of the body that does not articulate with another bone.

Question 9405. Which ring of cartilage lies immediately below the laryngeal prominence and is the only complete ring of the airway? The answer is the cricoid cartilage. It lies at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra, narrow in front and broad behind. The windpipe begins below it.

Question 9406. A constructed illustration: a midline depression is felt between the laryngeal prominence and the cricoid cartilage. Which structure lies there? The answer is the cricothyroid membrane, which joins the two cartilages in the midline. Only skin and a thin layer of fascia cover it. Any decision to use it for emergency access belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9407. Which statement about the laryngeal prominence is correct: it lies at the fourth and fifth cervical vertebrae, it lies at the second cervical vertebra, it is formed by the cricoid cartilage, or it cannot be felt in the adult? The answer is that it lies at the fourth and fifth cervical vertebrae. It is the front edge of the thyroid cartilage.

Question 9408. A described photograph of the side of the neck shows a long strap of muscle from behind the ear to the top of the chest, dividing the neck into two triangles. Which muscle is it? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid. It arises from the mastoid process and inserts on the manubrium and the clavicle. The great vessels of the neck lie deep to it.

Question 9409. Where is the pulse of the common carotid artery best felt? The answer is at the level of the upper border of the thyroid cartilage, in the groove between the larynx and the sternocleidomastoid muscle. The artery lies just medial to the muscle. Lower in the neck it lies deeper.

Question 9410. Which structure sits at the fork of the common carotid artery and is supplied by a branch of the ninth cranial nerve? The answer is the carotid sinus, a small dilatation at the beginning of the internal carotid artery. The sinus nerve of the glossopharyngeal nerve supplies it. The carotid body lies close by and receives branches from the same nerve.

Question 9411. Which spinous process is the most prominent one at the base of the neck when the head is bent forward? The answer is the spine of the seventh cervical vertebra, the vertebra prominens. The cervical spines above it are short and often split. The first thoracic spine may be equally prominent in some people.

Question 9412. A finger pressed upward behind the angle of the mandible meets a hard transverse bar. Which structure is it? The answer is the transverse process of the atlas, the first cervical vertebra. The atlas has no body and no spinous process, so this process is the part reached. The transverse process of the axis lies below it.

Question 9413. True or false: the spinous process felt at the base of the neck is always the first thoracic vertebra. The answer is false. The most prominent spine there is usually the seventh cervical vertebra, because its spine is long and unpaired. In some people the first thoracic spine is equally prominent, so the level is settled by counting.

Question 9414. Which bony angle can be felt at the lower back corner of the jaw, below the ear? The answer is the angle of the mandible. The masseter muscle covers its outer surface, and the parotid gland wraps around its posterior border. It is the point where the body of the mandible meets its vertical ramus.

Question 9415. Which structure lies immediately in front of the external acoustic opening and glides forward when the mouth opens? The answer is the head of the mandible, which forms the temporomandibular joint. It sits in the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone. A finger placed there feels it move as the jaw opens.

Question 9416. A described photograph of the side of the head shows a slender bony bridge from the cheek to the front of the ear, with a hollow above and below. Which structure is it? The answer is the zygomatic arch, formed by the zygomatic bone in front and the temporal bone behind. The masseter muscle passes down from it and the temporalis muscle deep to it.

Question 9417. Which pair of small bones forms the bridge of the nose? The answer is the two nasal bones. They meet in the midline between the two maxillae and are covered only by skin and a little muscle. A finger run down the bridge feels them end where the nasal cartilages begin.

Question 9418. Which midline projection can be felt under the chin at the front of the jaw? The answer is the mental protuberance, the front of the body of the mandible. It is the triangular prominence that forms the chin, covered by the mentalis muscle. Just above it on each side lies the opening for the mental nerve.

Question 9419. Which notch can be felt at the upper margin of the orbit, and which nerve passes through it? The answer is the supraorbital notch, with the supraorbital nerve. It lies at the junction of the medial and middle thirds of the upper orbital margin. In some people it is closed into a foramen instead, which is a normal variation.

Question 9420. Which pair belongs together: the inion with the external occipital protuberance, the glabella with the angle of the mandible, the nasion with the mastoid process, or the gonion with the bridge of the nose? The answer is the inion with that protuberance. The glabella lies between the eyebrows, the nasion at the root of the nose, and the gonion at the angle of the mandible.

Question 9421. A described view of the side of the skull shows a small thin area where four bones meet, bounded by a curved line. Which region is it, and what lies beneath it? The answer is the pterion, with the middle meningeal artery beneath it. The frontal, parietal, temporal and sphenoid bones meet there. A blow to that point can tear the artery.

Question 9422. Which opening is felt as a small depression on the front of the cheek, a finger width below the middle of the lower orbital margin? The answer is the infraorbital foramen. The infraorbital nerve and vessels pass through it to the cheek, the upper lip and the side of the nose. It lies roughly in line with the supraorbital notch.

Question 9423. Which pair belongs together: the hyoid bone with the third cervical vertebra, the cricoid cartilage with the second cervical vertebra, the thyroid cartilage with the seventh cervical vertebra, or the sternal angle with the second cervical vertebra? The answer is the hyoid bone with the third cervical vertebra. The cricoid lies at the sixth and the thyroid cartilage at the fourth and fifth.

Question 9424. Which structure lies at the level of the suprasternal notch: the first thoracic vertebra, the second thoracic vertebra, the fourth thoracic vertebra, or the ninth thoracic vertebra? The answer is the second thoracic vertebra. The notch is the hollow at the top of the sternum between the clavicles. The sternal angle lies lower, at the fourth thoracic vertebra.

Question 9425. True or false: the sternal angle lies at the level of the fourth thoracic vertebra. The answer is true. The sternal angle is the ridge where the manubrium meets the body of the sternum. It marks the second costal cartilage on each side and the disc between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae.

Question 9426. Which landmark on the front of the chest gives the level of the second costal cartilage, and why does that help when the ribs are counted? The answer is the sternal angle. The second costal cartilage joins the sternum at that ridge, so a finger placed there finds the second rib. The ribs are then counted downward.

Question 9427. Which small pointed bone is felt in the midline at the lower end of the sternum, and at the level of which vertebra does it lie? The answer is the xiphoid process, at the ninth thoracic vertebra. It is the lowest part of the sternum and may be forked as a normal variation. The costal margin meets on each side above it.

Question 9428. A described view of the front of the trunk shows the lower border of the chest as two curved lines meeting below in a shallow V. Which structure forms the apex of the V? The answer is the xiphoid process, with the costal margin formed by the cartilages of the seventh to the tenth ribs. The eleventh and twelfth ribs end free below it.

Question 9429. Which ribs contribute their cartilages to the costal margin, and which two end free? The answer is that the seventh to the tenth ribs join the costal margin, while the eleventh and twelfth end free. The free ribs are called floating ribs and carry no cartilage to the sternum. Their ends lie in muscle.

Question 9430. Which two ribs have no attachment to the sternum or to the costal margin? The answer is the eleventh and twelfth ribs, the floating ribs. They arise from the eleventh and twelfth thoracic vertebrae and end in the muscles of the abdominal wall. Being short and mobile, they can be broken by a blow from behind.

Question 9431. Which part of the scapula can be felt at the level of the seventh rib and the seventh thoracic vertebra? The answer is the inferior angle of the scapula, the lowest point of the triangular bone. It lies against the chest wall. When the arm is raised above the head it moves upward and outward, so the level shifts.

Question 9432. A described view of the back shows a flat triangular bone beside the spine, with a ridge across its upper part. Which ridge is it, and at which vertebra does its root lie? The answer is the spine of the scapula, whose root lies at the third thoracic vertebra. It divides the back of the scapula into parts above and below. Its outer end is the acromion.

Question 9433. Which bony hook can be felt by pressing deeply into the front of the shoulder, just below the outer third of the clavicle? The answer is the coracoid process of the scapula. The coracobrachialis, the short head of the biceps and the pectoralis minor arise from it. The front fibers of the deltoid cover it, so deep pressure is needed.

Question 9434. A constructed illustration: a person falls onto the point of the shoulder, and afterward a hard ridge is felt under the skin across the upper chest. Which bone is broken? The answer is the clavicle. It lies just under the skin along its whole length, without a cushion of muscle. It usually breaks at the junction of its middle and outer thirds.

Question 9435. Which structure lies immediately behind the medial end of the clavicle? The answer is the first rib, with the subclavian vessels and the brachial plexus passing over it. The first rib is deep to the clavicle and cannot be felt through the skin. The lower trunk of the brachial plexus crosses it.

Question 9436. Which bony point forms the tip of the shoulder and gives the level from which the arm hangs? The answer is the acromion of the scapula. It is the outer end of the spine of the scapula and lies just under the skin. The deltoid muscle arises from it and from the spine. It meets the clavicle at the acromioclavicular joint.

Question 9437. A described photograph of a bent elbow from behind shows three bony points that form a triangle when the elbow bends and a straight line when it straightens. Which points are they? The answer is the olecranon and the two epicondyles of the humerus. The medial epicondyle lies on the inner side and the lateral one on the outer side.

Question 9438. Which bony projection forms the point of the elbow and is covered only by a bursa and loose skin? The answer is the olecranon, the upper end of the ulna. It fits into the olecranon fossa of the humerus when the elbow straightens. The tendon of the triceps muscle inserts on its upper surface.

Question 9439. A constructed illustration: a blow lands on the inner side of the elbow, and afterward the little finger feels numb and the grip is weak. Which nerve is exposed there? The answer is the ulnar nerve, which runs in a groove behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus. Only skin and a thin layer of fascia cover it.

Question 9440. Which bone of the forearm turns when the palm is rotated, and where is its head felt at the elbow? The answer is the radius, whose head lies in the depression just below the lateral epicondyle of the humerus. It turns against the capitulum of the humerus. The ulna stays fixed while the radius swings around it.

Question 9441. Which of the two bony points at the wrist lies farther from the elbow: the radial styloid process or the ulnar styloid process? The answer is the radial styloid process, about one centimeter farther down. It is the lower end of the radius. The ulnar styloid lies higher and projects a little farther backward.

Question 9442. Which tendon lies immediately medial to the radial artery at the wrist? The answer is the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis. It stands out when the wrist is bent against resistance. The radial artery lies just lateral to it, resting on the lower end of the radius. The two are felt together at the pulse point.

Question 9443. Which small bone can be felt as a hard nodule at the base of the fleshy pad on the little finger side of the wrist? The answer is the pisiform bone, a pea-shaped carpal bone lying in the tendon of the flexor carpi ulnaris. The ulnar nerve passes just lateral to it. It is the most easily felt carpal bone.

Question 9444. A described view of the back of the hand shows a triangular hollow at the base of the thumb, bounded by tendons, with a pulse running through it. Which hollow is it, and which bones form its floor? The answer is the anatomical snuffbox, floored by the scaphoid and the trapezium. The abductor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis bound one side, and the extensor pollicis longus the other.

Question 9445. Which bony hook lies deep in the fleshy pad on the little finger side of the hand, just beyond the pisiform? The answer is the hook of the hamate. It projects forward from the hamate bone and is felt through the muscles of the pad. The ulnar nerve and artery pass beside it through a short tunnel on their way to the palm.

Question 9446. A constructed illustration: a person falls on the outstretched hand, and later there is tenderness in the hollow at the base of the thumb. Which bone is most likely injured, and why is the injury easily missed? The answer is the scaphoid. Its blood supply enters at the far end, so a break near the wrist can starve the bone.

Question 9447. Which tendon lies just under the skin in the middle of the front of the wrist and leads the examiner to the median nerve? The answer is the tendon of the palmaris longus. It crosses the wrist in the midline and passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum. The median nerve lies deep to it, within the carpal tunnel.

Question 9448. True or false: the tendon of the palmaris longus can be felt in every forearm. The answer is false. The muscle is absent in about one in seven people, which is a normal variation. When it is present it stands out as a fine cord if the wrist is bent while the thumb and the little finger are pressed together.

Question 9449. Name two tendons that can be felt at the front of the wrist, one on the thumb side and one in the midline. The answer is the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis and the tendon of the palmaris longus. The first lies near the thumb side, just medial to the radial pulse. The second runs down the middle of the wrist when present.

Question 9450. Which bony ridge can be felt along the top of the hip, and at the level of which vertebra does its highest point lie? The answer is the iliac crest, whose highest point lies at the fourth lumbar vertebra. The crest runs from the front to the back of the pelvis. It is the usual guide to the fourth lumbar spine.

Question 9451. Which cord-like band can be felt from the bony point at the front of the hip to the pubic region, and what lies below its midpoint? The answer is the inguinal ligament, with the femoral vessels just below its midpoint. It stretches from the anterior superior iliac spine to the pubic tubercle. It forms the fold of the groin.

Question 9452. A described photograph of the lower back shows two dimples, one on each side, at the top of the buttocks. Which bony points lie beneath them? The answer is the two posterior superior iliac spines, at the level of the second sacral segment. The dimples are made by the skin being tethered to the spines beneath. They are the best guide to the sacroiliac joints.

Question 9453. A constructed illustration: a pulse is felt in the groin, halfway between the bony point at the front of the hip and the middle of the pubis. Which vessel is it? The answer is the femoral artery. It enters the thigh beneath the inguinal ligament at that midpoint. The femoral vein lies just medial to it, and the femoral nerve lateral to it.

Question 9454. Which midline joint of the pelvis can be felt at the front, below the hair line? The answer is the pubic symphysis, where the two pubic bones meet. Its disc softens and widens in pregnancy as a normal change. In front of it lies the pubic crest, with the pubic tubercle at its outer end.

Question 9455. A horizontal plane is imagined through the highest points of the two iliac crests. Which plane is it, and at which vertebra does it cross the spine? The answer is the supracristal plane, at the fourth lumbar vertebra. It is used to identify the fourth lumbar spine. The umbilicus lies higher, at the disc between the third and fourth lumbar vertebrae.

Question 9456. Which pair belongs together: the anterior superior iliac spine with the outer end of the inguinal ligament, the pubic tubercle with the ischial spine, the greater trochanter with the sacral promontory, or the ischial tuberosity with the iliac crest? The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine with the outer end of the inguinal ligament. The pubic tubercle is the inner end of the same ligament.

Question 9457. Which bony point is the most lateral part of the hip, felt on the outer side of the upper thigh? The answer is the greater trochanter of the femur, a square projection at the junction of the neck and the shaft. The gluteal muscles insert on it. It lies at the level of the hip joint.

Question 9458. Which bony point is felt below the fold of the buttock when the hip is straight, but disappears when the hip is bent? The answer is the ischial tuberosity, the lower end of the ischium and the bone on which the body rests when sitting. When the hip is flexed the gluteus maximus slides over it.

Question 9459. A described view of the front of the knee shows a flat triangular bone, a thick band from it, and a bony bump where the band ends. Which structures are they? The answer is the patella, the patellar tendon and the tibial tuberosity. The patella is the largest sesamoid bone of the body and lies in the tendon of the quadriceps.

Question 9460. Which bone is felt as a round head on the outer side of the knee, below the outer condyle of the tibia? The answer is the head of the fibula. The tendon of the biceps femoris and the lateral collateral ligament of the knee attach to it. The common fibular nerve winds around the neck of the fibula just below.

Question 9461. A constructed illustration: a blow falls on the outer side of the knee, and afterward the foot cannot be lifted at the ankle, though the knee still straightens. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the common fibular nerve, which winds around the neck of the fibula just under the skin. Its branches supply the muscles that lift the foot.

Question 9462. Which bony point forms the inner bump of the ankle and gives attachment to the deltoid ligament? The answer is the medial malleolus of the tibia. It lies about one centimeter higher than the outer bump. The tendon of the tibialis posterior passes behind it, and the posterior tibial pulse is felt just behind it.

Question 9463. Which of the two ankle bumps lies lower and farther back: the medial malleolus or the lateral malleolus? The answer is the lateral malleolus, the lower end of the fibula. It forms the outer wall of the ankle joint and gives attachment to the lateral ligaments. The medial malleolus lies higher and farther forward.

Question 9464. A described view of the back of the heel shows a thick cord from the calf and a broad bony mass beneath it. Which structures are they? The answer is the calcaneal tendon and the calcaneus. The tendon, also called the Achilles tendon, is formed by the soleus and the two heads of the gastrocnemius. It inserts on the back of the calcaneus.

Question 9465. Which bony tuberosity is felt on the inner border of the foot, just below and in front of the medial malleolus? The answer is the tuberosity of the navicular bone, where the tendon of the tibialis posterior inserts. It is the highest point of the medial longitudinal arch.

Question 9466. Which bony point lies on the outer side of the foot, halfway along its border, and gives insertion to a muscle of the little toe? The answer is the base of the fifth metatarsal bone, where the tendon of the peroneus brevis inserts. It projects as a small knob that is easily bruised. A stumble that rolls the foot inward pulls on it.

Question 9467. A pulse is felt on the top of the foot, lateral to the tendon that stands out when the great toe is lifted. Which artery is it? The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery. It runs forward from the ankle between the tendons of the extensor muscles. The tendon beside it belongs to the extensor hallucis longus.

Question 9468. Where is the posterior tibial pulse felt, and which structures lie beside it? The answer is behind the medial malleolus, between that bone and the calcaneal tendon. The artery runs there with the tibial nerve and the deep tendons of the calf. The pulse is felt about a finger width behind the bony point.

Question 9469. Which pair belongs together: the dorsalis pedis pulse with the top of the foot, the popliteal pulse with the front of the ankle, the posterior tibial pulse with the groin, or the carotid pulse with the back of the knee? The answer is the dorsalis pedis pulse with the top of the foot. The popliteal pulse is felt behind the knee and the posterior tibial pulse behind the medial malleolus.

Question 9470. Name the two pulse points that are felt in the foot, spoken as a list. The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery and the posterior tibial artery. The first lies on the top of the foot, lateral to the tendon of the extensor hallucis longus. The second lies behind the medial malleolus.

Question 9471. Which pulse is felt in the hollow behind the knee when the knee is slightly bent? The answer is the popliteal pulse. The popliteal artery lies deepest in the hollow, against the back of the knee joint. Fascia and fat cover it, so the knee is bent to relax them. The popliteal vein lies superficial to it.

Question 9472. Which two small bones lie beneath the head of the first metatarsal bone, within the tendons that pass under the ball of the great toe? The answer is the two sesamoid bones of the flexor hallucis brevis. They lie in the tendon and glide over the metatarsal head. They take part in carrying the weight of the body.

Question 9473. A described view of the inner border of the foot shows a curved arch lifting the middle of the sole from the ground. Which arch is it, and which bones form it? The answer is the medial longitudinal arch. It is supported behind by the calcaneus and in front by the head of the first metatarsal, The tibialis posterior tendon holds it up.

Question 9474. A constructed illustration: a person lands heavily on the foot, and later the body of the ankle bone loses its blood supply although the break is thin. Which bone is involved? The answer is the talus. Vessels enter it from below and behind and run backward into the body of the bone. A break through the narrow neck can cut that supply.

Question 9475. True or false: the calcaneal tendon is the thickest and strongest tendon of the body, and it inserts on the calcaneus. The answer is true. It is formed by the gastrocnemius and the soleus and receives the plantaris tendon on its inner side. It raises the heel and lifts the body onto the toes.

Question 9476. Which point on the front of the chest gives the position of the apex of the heart, and where is it? The answer is the apex beat, felt in the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line. The apex of the heart lies there, directed downward, forward and to the left. It is the lowest and most lateral point of the heart.

Question 9477. In the examination of the heart, where is the sound of each valve best heard? The answer is that the mitral valve is heard at the apex, the tricuspid at the lower left border of the sternum, the aortic at the right second intercostal space and the pulmonary at the left second space. The sounds travel along the blood flow, not to the valve itself.

Question 9478. Which dome of the diaphragm lies higher in the chest, and why? The answer is the right dome, because the liver lies beneath it. The right dome reaches about the fourth intercostal space at rest and the left the fifth. Both descend with a deep breath. This is why the right lung is shorter than the left.

Question 9479. A described percussion map of the front of the chest shows dullness below the right costal margin, dullness beside the left border of the sternum, and a clear note elsewhere. Which organs cause the dullness? The answer is the liver on the right and the heart behind the left border of the sternum. Air-filled lung gives a clear note and solid organ dullness.

Question 9480. A constructed illustration: a hand lies flat on the right side of the abdomen, and an organ edge slips beneath it as the person breathes in. Which organ is being examined? The answer is the liver. Its lower border lies just below the right costal margin in health and moves down with the breath. It fills the right upper part of the abdomen.

Question 9481. Which ribs protect the spleen, and where does its tip lie in the healthy adult? The answer is the ninth, tenth and eleventh ribs, with the tip of the spleen just behind the left costal margin. The spleen is not felt in health. When it enlarges it moves downward and forward, and its notch can then be felt.

Question 9482. Which organ lies behind the left costal margin, extends from the ninth to the eleventh rib, and is not normally palpable? The answer is the spleen. It lies in the left upper part of the abdomen, behind the stomach and below the diaphragm. Its long axis follows the line of the tenth rib. It is the largest mass of lymphoid tissue.

Question 9483. A constructed illustration: one hand lies behind the right flank and the other below the costal margin, and they are pressed together during a deep breath. Which organ is caught between them? The answer is the right kidney. It lies lower than the left because the liver occupies the space above it. Its lower pole descends with a deep breath.

Question 9484. In the examination of the breast, into which parts is the surface divided, and what is the tail that reaches toward the armpit? The answer is the four quadrants, upper and lower, inner and outer, with the axillary tail reaching into the armpit. The tail passes through an opening in the deep fascia. The upper outer quadrant holds the largest share of the gland.

Question 9485. A described view of the groin shows a triangular opening above and medial to the pubic tubercle, with a cord passing through it. Which opening is it? The answer is the superficial inguinal ring, which transmits the spermatic cord. In the female it carries the round ligament of the uterus. The ring is a slit in the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle.

Question 9486. Which cord-like structure can be felt within the inguinal canal in the male, and which three structures pass inside it? The answer is the spermatic cord, containing the ductus deferens, the testicular artery and the plexus of veins around it. It also carries the cremaster muscle and the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve. It leaves the canal at the superficial ring.

Question 9487. Which artery is felt in the hollow at the front of the elbow, and on which side of the tendon of the biceps does it lie? The answer is the brachial artery, medial to the tendon of the biceps. The tendon forms the lateral boundary of the hollow and can be rolled between the fingers. The median nerve lies just medial to the artery.

Question 9488. Which muscle contracts at the knee jerk and which at the ankle jerk, and which nerve carries each? The answer is the quadriceps at the knee jerk, through the femoral nerve, and the muscles of the calf at the ankle jerk, through the tibial nerve. The knee jerk tests the third and fourth lumbar segments. The ankle jerk tests the first and second sacral segments.

Question 9489. Which of these structures lies most medial at the front of the elbow: the tendon of the biceps, the brachial artery, the median nerve, or the radial nerve? The answer is the median nerve, which lies medial to the brachial artery. From the medial side the order is the median nerve, the brachial artery, then the tendon of the biceps. The radial nerve lies farther out.

Question 9490. Which muscle is tested when a person stands on one leg and the pelvis on the opposite side drops? The answer is the gluteus medius of the standing leg. It arises from the outer surface of the ilium and inserts on the greater trochanter. It holds the body level over the hip. The gluteus minimus assists it, and the superior gluteal nerve supplies both.

Question 9491. A described photograph shows a tape measure from the bony point at the front of the hip to the bony point on the inner side of the ankle. Which measurement is being taken? The answer is the true length of the lower limb, from the anterior superior iliac spine to the medial malleolus. The tape follows the limb without its curves. The apparent length uses the umbilicus instead.

Question 9492. When a lower limb is measured, which two points give its true length: the anterior superior iliac spine and the medial malleolus, the umbilicus and the medial malleolus, the iliac crest and the calcaneus, or the pubic symphysis and the lateral malleolus? The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine and the medial malleolus. The umbilicus and the same ankle point give the apparent length.

Question 9493. True or false: in the examination of the abdomen, palpation is always performed before auscultation. The answer is false. Auscultation comes first, because pressing on the abdomen can change the sounds made by the bowel. Palpation and percussion follow, and any tender area is examined last.

Question 9494. Which pair belongs together: the otoscope with the tympanic membrane, the tendon hammer with the tympanic membrane, the tape measure with the reflexes, or the tuning fork with the length of a limb? The answer is the otoscope with the tympanic membrane. The tendon hammer tests the deep tendon reflexes, the tape measure the length of a limb, and the tuning fork the hearing.

Question 9495. Name two pulse points that are used when a lower limb is examined, spoken as a sentence. The answer is the femoral artery in the groin and the popliteal artery behind the knee. Two more lie below them, the posterior tibial artery behind the medial malleolus and the dorsalis pedis artery on the top of the foot.

Question 9496. Two vertical lines are imagined on the left side of the chest, one from the middle of the clavicle and one from the middle of the armpit. Which line passes through the apex beat? The answer is the midclavicular line, which passes through the apex beat in the fifth intercostal space. The midaxillary line runs through the armpit and marks the base of the lung.

Question 9497. A constructed illustration: a firm organ lies below the left costal margin, and the examiner must decide whether it is the spleen or the kidney. Which features of the spleen decide the question? The answer is that the spleen moves down and forward with the breath and lies close under the costal margin, with a notch on its upper border. The kidney lies deeper and is held between two hands.

Question 9498. Which structure lies in the transpyloric plane, at the first lumbar vertebra: the pylorus of the stomach, the umbilicus, the bifurcation of the aorta, or the pubic symphysis? The answer is the pylorus of the stomach. The same plane cuts the tips of the ninth costal cartilages and the neck of the pancreas. The umbilicus lies lower, at the third and fourth lumbar vertebrae.

Question 9499. A described computed tomography section across the upper part of the abdomen shows the liver on one side, the spleen on the other, and two kidneys behind the other organs. Which plane is it? The answer is a transverse plane at the level of the twelfth thoracic or first lumbar vertebra. At that level the kidneys lie behind the peritoneum.

Question 9500. Which surface point marks the base of the appendix: a point one third of the way from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus, the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, the transpyloric plane, or the tip of the ninth costal cartilage? The answer is the point one third of the way from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus. The position of the appendix varies, so this is only a guide.

To summarize, the surface of the body is a map of the bones beneath it, and the points that can be felt through the skin are the fixed marks on that map. The inion, the mastoid process, the hyoid bone, the laryngeal prominence and the cricoid cartilage give the levels of the neck, while the jugular notch, the sternal angle, the xiphoid process and the costal margin give the levels of the chest. The iliac crest, the anterior superior iliac spine, the pubic symphysis, the ischial tuberosity and the greater trochanter give the levels of the pelvis and the hip, and in the limbs the acromion, the epicondyles, the olecranon, the styloid processes, the head of the fibula and the two malleoli are the guides, with the pulses lying beside them. An examination is built on these marks, because the apex beat, the liver, the spleen, the kidney, the inguinal canal and the joints are all found by first finding the bone or the cartilage that lies next to them. Saying each landmark aloud, together with the structure beside it, is what fixes the map in memory.
