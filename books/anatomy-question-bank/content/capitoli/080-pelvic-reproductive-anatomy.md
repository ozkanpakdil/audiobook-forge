# Chapter 80 — Pelvic Reproductive Anatomy

This chapter examines the reproductive organs within the pelvis and the perineum, in both the female and the male, and the structures that hold them in place. It covers the uterus, the cervix, the vagina, the uterine tubes and the ovaries, the broad ligament, the perineum, the prostate, the seminal vesicles, the ductus deferens and the testis, with the vessels, the lymphatics and the nerves that serve them. It holds one hundred questions, and every question is followed immediately by its answer and then by the reason for it. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7901. Which fibromuscular canal lies between the vestibule below and the cervix above, and receives the cervix into its upper end? The answer is the vagina. It runs upward and backward, and its anterior and posterior walls lie in contact. The recess that surrounds the cervix is the vaginal fornix.

Question 7902. Which of these is the thick-walled muscular organ that lies between the urinary bladder in front and the rectum behind in the female: the ovary, the uterus, the uterine tube, or the vagina? The answer is the uterus. Its fundus lies above the openings of the tubes and its cervix projects into the vagina. The ovary lies lateral to it against the pelvic wall.

Question 7903. Which part of the uterus is the narrow lower portion that projects into the vagina and carries the external opening? The answer is the cervix. Its supravaginal part lies above the attachment of the vagina and its vaginal part lies within the canal. The internal opening lies at its junction with the body.

Question 7904. The uterine tube has four parts. Which of these is the widest and is the usual site of fertilization: the intramural part, the isthmus, the ampulla, or the infundibulum? The answer is the ampulla. It lies between the narrow isthmus and the funnel-shaped infundibulum, whose fimbriae reach toward the ovary.

Question 7905. A described radiograph of the female pelvis is taken after contrast medium fills the cavity of the uterus; the cavity appears as a narrow triangle with two fine curved lines running outward from its upper corners. Which structures do those lines represent? The answer is the uterine tubes, which carry the contrast outward toward the ovary.

Question 7906. Which almond-shaped organ lies on the lateral wall of the female pelvis, is not covered by peritoneum, and releases an oocyte in each cycle? The answer is the ovary. It sits in the ovarian fossa and is held by the mesovarium, the ovarian ligament and the suspensory ligament. Its surface is covered by germinal epithelium.

Question 7907. On the lateral wall of the female pelvis the ovary rests in a shallow depression. Which structure forms the anterior boundary of that depression, and which structures form the posterior boundary? The answer is the obliterated umbilical artery in front, with the ureter and the internal iliac artery behind. Knowing those limits keeps the ovary from being confused with the pelvic wall.

Question 7908. Which peritoneal fold drapes over the uterus, the uterine tubes and the ovaries, and is divided into the mesometrium, the mesosalpinx and the mesovarium? The answer is the broad ligament. It stretches from the sides of the uterus to the lateral pelvic wall, and the uterine artery and the ureter run in its base.

Question 7909. True or false: the round ligament of the uterus runs from the fundus through the inguinal canal and ends in the labium majus. The answer is true. It is the female remnant of the gubernaculum and helps to hold the uterus in its forward-leaning position. Unlike the ovarian ligament, it does not attach the ovary to the uterus.

Question 7910. Name the two ligaments that attach the ovary to its neighbors, one joining it to the uterus and one joining it to the pelvic wall. The answer is the ovarian ligament and the suspensory ligament of the ovary. The ovarian ligament runs from the lower pole of the ovary to the uterus, and the suspensory ligament carries the ovarian vessels to the pelvic wall.

Question 7911. Which expanded, funnel-shaped end of the uterine tube carries the finger-like processes that sweep over the ovary? The answer is the infundibulum. Its fimbriae open into the peritoneal cavity, so an ovum released at ovulation is already outside the closed lumen of the tube. The tube then narrows into the ampulla.

Question 7912. Which of these parts of the broad ligament is the mesentery of the uterine tube: the mesometrium, the mesosalpinx, the mesovarium, or the parametrium? The answer is the mesosalpinx. The mesometrium supports the uterus and the mesovarium suspends the ovary. The parametrium is the fibrous tissue beside the cervix, at the base of the ligament.

Question 7913. A described transverse section of the female pelvis is cut at the level of the cervix. The bladder lies in front, the rectum behind, and a firm mass of tissue sits on each side beside the cervix with a thin tube running through it. Which structures lie in that tissue? The answer is the ureters and the uterine arteries. The ureter passes below the artery.

Question 7914. In the base of the broad ligament the ureter and the uterine artery cross each other. Which of the two lies nearer the cervix, and which one passes above the other? The answer is that the ureter lies nearer the cervix and the uterine artery passes above it. That crossing is remembered as water running under a bridge.

Question 7915. A constructed illustration: during a pelvic operation a surgeon works close to the side of the cervix and later reports a watery discharge from the vagina. Which structure was most likely injured? The answer is the ureter, which passes about 2 centimeters lateral to the cervix below the uterine artery. This is an illustration only, and management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7916. Which vessel supplies the uterus and arises from the anterior division of the internal iliac artery: the ovarian artery, the uterine artery, the middle rectal artery, or the internal pudendal artery? The answer is the uterine artery. It reaches the uterus in the base of the broad ligament and joins the ovarian artery along its side. The ovarian artery arises from the abdominal aorta.

Question 7917. Which artery arises from the internal iliac artery, runs in the base of the broad ligament, and crosses above the ureter to reach the side of the uterus? The answer is the uterine artery. Its branches supply the uterus, the upper part of the vagina and part of the uterine tube. It meets the ovarian artery near the horn of the uterus.

Question 7918. Into which vessel does the left ovarian vein usually drain: the left renal vein, the inferior vena cava, the left internal iliac vein, or the splenic vein? The answer is the left renal vein. The right ovarian vein drains into the inferior vena cava. The same pattern holds for the testicular veins in the male.

Question 7919. Which of these pairs belongs together: the uterine artery with the internal iliac artery, the ovarian artery with the internal iliac artery, the internal pudendal artery with the abdominal aorta, or the middle rectal artery with the external iliac artery? The answer is the uterine artery with the internal iliac artery. The ovarian artery arises from the abdominal aorta.

Question 7920. Which group of lymph nodes receives the lymph from the ovary and from the testis: the superficial inguinal nodes, the deep inguinal nodes, the internal iliac nodes, or the para-aortic nodes? The answer is the para-aortic nodes. Both gonads develop high on the posterior abdominal wall and carry their lymphatics with their vessels. The scrotum drains to the superficial inguinal nodes.

Question 7921. A described lymphangiogram fills a chain of nodes beside the abdominal aorta, on both sides of the great vessels just below the renal vessels. Which organs are most likely to have contributed that lymph? The answer is the ovaries or the testes, whose lymphatics follow the gonadal vessels upward to the para-aortic nodes.

Question 7922. Which erectile structure of the female lies beneath the pubic arch, is formed of two crura and a glans, and is the homologue of the penis? The answer is the clitoris. Its crura are attached to the ischiopubic rami and its glans lies at the anterior end of the vestibule. It is supplied by the dorsal nerve of the clitoris.

Question 7923. Which of these female external structures is the homologue of the scrotum: the labium minus, the labium majus, the clitoris, or the hymen? The answer is the labium majus. It is a rounded fold of skin and fat that contains the termination of the round ligament. The labium minus is a thinner fold with no scrotal counterpart.

Question 7924. Where does the greater vestibular gland lie in relation to the vaginal opening and the bulb of the vestibule? The answer is that it lies posterolateral to the vaginal opening, in the superficial perineal pouch, behind the bulb of the vestibule. Its duct opens between the hymen and the labium minus. It is also called the Bartholin gland.

Question 7925. A constructed illustration: a woman is described with a tender, rounded swelling at the posterior part of one side of the vestibule, and the duct of a small gland is thought to be blocked. Which gland is involved? The answer is the greater vestibular gland, posterolateral to the vaginal opening. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7926. Which structure is the fibromuscular mass at the center of the perineum, between the vaginal opening in front and the anus behind, into which several muscles converge: the perineal membrane, the perineal body, the urogenital diaphragm, or the ischial spine? The answer is the perineal body. The bulbospongiosus, the external anal sphincter and the transverse perineal muscles all meet there.

Question 7927. Which midline structure lies between the posterior wall of the vaginal opening and the anterior wall of the anal canal, and gives attachment to the perineal muscles? The answer is the perineal body. It is a pyramidal fibromuscular mass in the female, and the corresponding mass in the male lies between the bulb of the penis and the anal canal.

Question 7928. The perineum is described as a diamond, and a line between the two ischial tuberosities divides it into two triangles. Which triangle lies in front of that line, and which lies behind it? The answer is the urogenital triangle in front and the anal triangle behind. The urogenital triangle contains the external genitalia.

Question 7929. Which of these structures lies in the superficial perineal pouch of the female: the urethra, the greater vestibular gland, the deep transverse perineal muscle, or the internal pudendal artery? The answer is the greater vestibular gland. The pouch also holds the crura of the clitoris, the bulb of the vestibule and the ischiocavernosus and bulbospongiosus muscles. The urethra lies in the deep pouch.

Question 7930. A described sagittal section of the male perineum shows a thick triangular mass of tissue behind the bulb of the penis and in front of the anal canal. Which structure is it, and which muscles attach to it? The answer is the perineal body, to which the bulbospongiosus, the external anal sphincter and the transverse perineal muscles are attached. The membranous urethra passes above it.

Question 7931. Which gland lies immediately below the urinary bladder, surrounds the first part of the urethra, and is described as having a base, an apex and lobes? The answer is the prostate. Its base lies against the neck of the bladder and its apex rests on the perineal membrane. The ejaculatory ducts pass through its posterior part.

Question 7932. Which of these structures lies immediately anterior to the rectum in the male and can be felt through its wall: the urinary bladder, the prostate, the seminal vesicle, or the testis? The answer is the prostate. Its posterior surface lies against the anterior wall of the rectum, separated from it by a thin layer of fascia.

Question 7933. The ejaculatory ducts and the seminal vesicles lie behind the bladder. In which direction do the ejaculatory ducts run to reach the urethra, and which gland do they pass through? The answer is that they run forward and downward through the prostate to open into the prostatic urethra. They open on the seminal colliculus, beside the prostatic utricle.

Question 7934. Which two structures join to form the ejaculatory duct: the ductus deferens and the duct of the seminal vesicle, the ductus deferens and the epididymis, the seminal vesicle and the prostate, or the epididymis and the testis? The answer is the ductus deferens and the duct of the seminal vesicle. The union takes place just behind the base of the bladder.

Question 7935. A constructed illustration: an older man is described with difficulty in starting the flow of urine, and the part of the gland that surrounds the urethra is enlarged. Which zone of the prostate is described? The answer is the transition zone, which narrows the prostatic urethra. The peripheral zone is the usual site of carcinoma. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7936. Which of these is the paired gland that lies behind the bladder, above the prostate and lateral to the ampulla of the ductus deferens: the bulbourethral gland, the seminal vesicle, the prostate, or the epididymis? The answer is the seminal vesicle. It secretes a fructose-rich fluid that forms part of the semen, and its duct joins the ductus deferens to form the ejaculatory duct.

Question 7937. Which paired sac lies between the fundus of the urinary bladder and the rectum, above the prostate, and joins its duct to the ductus deferens? The answer is the seminal vesicle. It is about 5 centimeters long and its lower end lies close to the base of the prostate. It is separated from the rectum by the rectovesical pouch and by fascia.

Question 7938. A described magnetic resonance image of the male pelvis in the sagittal plane shows the bladder above, the rectum behind, and a small rounded gland below the bladder with a tube running through it. Which structure is the gland, and what is the tube? The answer is the prostate, with the prostatic urethra running through it.

Question 7939. Which group of lymph nodes first receives lymph from the prostate: the superficial inguinal nodes, the internal iliac and sacral nodes, the para-aortic nodes, or the deep cervical nodes? The answer is the internal iliac and sacral nodes, together with the external iliac nodes. The prostate lies deep in the pelvis, unlike the testis.

Question 7940. True or false: the prostate lies within a fibrous capsule and is separated from the rectum by a layer of fascia. The answer is true. The capsule is surrounded by a venous plexus, and the fascia behind the gland separates it from the rectum. The prostate is also supported below by the perineal membrane.

Question 7941. Which firm muscular tube carries sperm from the tail of the epididymis to the ejaculatory duct, and can be felt as a cord behind the testis? The answer is the ductus deferens. It ascends behind the testis into the spermatic cord, passes through the inguinal canal and crosses above the ureter in the pelvis. Its wall is thick and its lumen is narrow.

Question 7942. Which of these describes the course of the ductus deferens after it leaves the scrotum: through the inguinal canal and then above the ureter, through the inguinal canal and then below the ureter, behind the rectum to the seminal vesicle, or through the obturator canal? The answer is through the inguinal canal and then above the ureter. It then forms the ampulla beside the seminal vesicle.

Question 7943. In the pelvis the ductus deferens and the ureter cross each other. Which of the two lies more laterally, and which one passes in front of the other? The answer is that the ureter lies more laterally and the ductus deferens crosses in front of it, running from lateral to medial. That places the ductus deferens above the ureter.

Question 7944. Which part of the ductus deferens lies immediately medial to the seminal vesicle and joins its duct to form the ejaculatory duct: the ampulla, the isthmus, the tail, or the infundibulum? The answer is the ampulla. It is the dilated terminal part of the ductus deferens. The isthmus belongs to the uterine tube.

Question 7945. A described radiograph is taken after contrast is injected into the ductus deferens. A thin line rises from the scrotum, loops over the brim of the pelvis, and ends in a small dilated sac beside the base of the bladder. Which structure is that sac, and which does it join? The answer is the ampulla, which joins the duct of the seminal vesicle.

Question 7946. A constructed illustration: a clinician describes palpating a firm, cord-like structure behind the testis in the upper part of the scrotum, separate from the softer vessels beside it. Which structure is it, and which canal does it enter? The answer is the ductus deferens, which enters the inguinal canal. Any procedure belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7947. Which of these is the coiled tube that lies on the posterior surface of the testis, where sperm complete their maturation: the ductus deferens, the epididymis, the seminal vesicle, or the rete testis? The answer is the epididymis. Its head, body and tail lie against the testis, and its tail continues as the ductus deferens. The rete testis lies inside the testis.

Question 7948. Which structure caps the upper pole of the testis and is formed by the coiled efferent ductules? The answer is the head of the epididymis. Its body and tail continue downward along the posterior border of the testis. The tail turns upward and becomes the ductus deferens.

Question 7949. Which serous sac surrounds the front and the sides of the testis and is derived from the peritoneum: the tunica albuginea, the tunica vaginalis, the tunica vasculosa, or the dartos? The answer is the tunica vaginalis. Its visceral layer covers the testis and its parietal layer lines the scrotum. Fluid collecting between the two layers is a hydrocele.

Question 7950. Which of these pairs belongs together: the head of the epididymis with the efferent ductules, the tail of the epididymis with the rete testis, the tunica albuginea with the peritoneum, or the dartos with the cremaster muscle? The answer is the head of the epididymis with the efferent ductules. The dartos is smooth muscle in the scrotal wall.

Question 7951. A described ultrasound image of the scrotum shows a rounded oval organ with a uniform gray texture and a small curved structure hugging its back. Which structures are shown? The answer is the testis with the epididymis behind it. A thin dark line around the front of the testis is the fluid in the tunica vaginalis. The spermatic cord is seen above them.

Question 7952. From which vessel does the testicular artery arise: the internal iliac artery, the external iliac artery, the abdominal aorta, or the inferior epigastric artery? The answer is the abdominal aorta. It arises at about the level of the second lumbar vertebra, below the renal arteries. It descends through the abdomen and the inguinal canal to reach the testis.

Question 7953. Which network of veins wraps around the testicular artery in the spermatic cord and cools the blood before it reaches the testis? The answer is the pampiniform plexus. It lies above and behind the testis and drains upward into the testicular vein. Its arrangement keeps the temperature of the testis below that of the body.

Question 7954. The right and the left testicular veins end differently. Into which vein does each drain, and why does the difference matter? The answer is that the right testicular vein drains into the inferior vena cava and the left into the left renal vein, which is one reason a varicocele is more common on the left.

Question 7955. A constructed illustration: a young man is described with a bag of worms felt above the left testis, worse on standing, and the veins of the cord are dilated. Which veins are described, and into which vein do they drain? The answer is the pampiniform plexus, draining into the left testicular vein. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7956. Which group receives lymph from the testis, and which from the scrotum: superficial inguinal for the testis and para-aortic for the scrotum, para-aortic for the testis and superficial inguinal for the scrotum, internal iliac for both, or para-aortic for both? The answer is para-aortic for the testis and superficial inguinal for the scrotum. The testis keeps the high lymphatics of its abdominal origin.

Question 7957. Which group of lymph nodes receives the lymph from the skin of the scrotum and the skin of the vulva? The answer is the superficial inguinal nodes. The skin of the perineum drains to the same group. The testis and the ovary, by contrast, drain to the para-aortic nodes because they began their development on the posterior abdominal wall.

Question 7958. A described cross-section of the spermatic cord shows a thick-walled tube with a tiny lumen, a plexus of thin-walled vessels around a small artery, and a thin fibrous remnant at the front. Which structures are described? The answer is the ductus deferens, the pampiniform plexus and the vestige of the processus vaginalis.

Question 7959. Which of these structures does not lie within the spermatic cord: the ductus deferens, the testicular artery, the pampiniform plexus, or the femoral nerve? The answer is the femoral nerve. It lies in the femoral triangle, well outside the cord. The cord also carries lymphatics, the artery to the ductus deferens and the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve.

Question 7960. True or false: the cremaster muscle is derived from the internal oblique muscle of the abdominal wall and raises the testis. The answer is true. Its fibers form the cremasteric fascia around the cord and the testis. The external spermatic fascia comes instead from the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle.

Question 7961. Which opening of the inguinal canal lies above and medial to the pubic tubercle, within the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle? The answer is the superficial inguinal ring. The deep ring lies lateral to the inferior epigastric artery in the transversalis fascia. The spermatic cord passes through both openings.

Question 7962. Which of these structures is derived from the paramesonephric duct: the ductus deferens, the uterine tube, the seminal vesicle, or the epididymis? The answer is the uterine tube. The paramesonephric ducts also form the uterus, the cervix and the upper part of the vagina. The other three structures come from the mesonephric duct.

Question 7963. The gubernaculum guides the descent of the gonad. Into which two adult structures does it persist in the female, and where does each attach? The answer is the ovarian ligament and the round ligament of the uterus. The ovarian ligament joins the ovary to the uterus, and the round ligament runs through the inguinal canal to the labium majus.

Question 7964. A constructed illustration: a girl is described with a small swelling in the groin, found to be a cyst of a remnant of the peritoneal process that accompanied the round ligament. Which structure is described: a cyst of the canal of Nuck, a hydrocele, a varicocele, or a cyst of the ductus deferens? The answer is a cyst of the canal of Nuck, the homologue of the persistent processus vaginalis in the male.

Question 7965. A described section of the ovary shows a surface layer of small cells, a dense fibrous coat beneath it, and, deeper, follicles at different stages of growth, one of them large and fluid-filled. Which structures are described? The answer is the germinal epithelium, the tunica albuginea and the ovarian follicles. The large follicle is a mature vesicle, which will rupture at ovulation.

Question 7966. A constructed illustration: a woman is described with sudden lower abdominal pain, and at operation a swelling is found in the widest part of one uterine tube. Which part is described? The answer is the ampulla, the widest and longest part of the tube and the usual site of fertilization. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7967. Which of these ligaments contains the ovarian vessels and must be identified before the ovary is moved: the ovarian ligament, the round ligament, the suspensory ligament of the ovary, or the cardinal ligament? The answer is the suspensory ligament of the ovary, which runs to the lateral pelvic wall and carries the ovarian artery and vein.

Question 7968. Which fold of peritoneum runs from the lateral end of the ovary to the wall of the pelvis and carries the ovarian vessels? The answer is the suspensory ligament of the ovary, also called the infundibulopelvic ligament. It lies above the ovarian fossa. The mesovarium, by contrast, attaches the ovary to the back of the broad ligament.

Question 7969. Which two arteries anastomose along the side of the uterus and the uterine tube: the uterine and the ovarian, the ovarian and the internal pudendal, the vaginal and the middle rectal, or the uterine and the obturator? The answer is the uterine and the ovarian arteries. Their anastomosis means the uterus can receive blood from either source. The ovarian artery arises from the abdominal aorta.

Question 7970. Which of these pairs belongs together: the mesosalpinx with the uterine tube, the mesovarium with the uterus, the mesometrium with the ovary, or the parametrium with the rectum? The answer is the mesosalpinx with the uterine tube. The mesovarium suspends the ovary, the mesometrium supports the uterus, and the parametrium is the tissue beside the cervix.

Question 7971. A constructed illustration: a rectal examination detects a small rounded structure in front of the rectum, below the base of the bladder, with a groove across its middle. Which structure is being described, and which structure lies immediately above it? The answer is the cervix, with the body of the uterus above it. The groove is the external opening of the cervix.

Question 7972. Which two terms describe the usual position of the uterus, one for the angle between the cervix and the vagina and one for the angle between the body and the cervix: anteversion and anteflexion, retroversion and retroflexion, anteversion and retroflexion, or retroversion and anteflexion? The answer is anteversion and anteflexion. A retroverted uterus is a common variant.

Question 7973. Name the deepest recess of the vaginal fornix and say which structure lies above it. The answer is the posterior fornix, with the rectouterine pouch above it. The anterior fornix lies in front of the cervix and is related to the base of the bladder. Two lateral fornices lie beside the uterine arteries.

Question 7974. Which structure lies immediately above the posterior fornix of the vagina and is related to it: the bladder, the rectouterine pouch, the ovary, or the ureter? The answer is the rectouterine pouch. It is the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity when the body is upright. The bladder lies above the anterior fornix instead.

Question 7975. Which peritoneal pouch lies between the uterus and the rectum in the female, and which pouch lies between the bladder and the rectum in the male? The answer is the rectouterine pouch in the female and the rectovesical pouch in the male. In the female the vesicouterine pouch lies between the bladder and the uterus.

Question 7976. A constructed illustration: fluid is described collecting in the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity of a woman, and a needle is said to have reached it through the posterior wall of the vagina. Which pouch is described, and which two organs bound it? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, bounded by the uterus and the rectum. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7977. Which peritoneal pouch lies between the uterus in front and the rectum behind, and is the lowest point of the peritoneal cavity? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, also called the pouch of Douglas. It lies behind the posterior fornix of the vagina. In the male the corresponding pouch lies between the bladder and the rectum.

Question 7978. A described sagittal section of the female pelvis shows the bladder in front, the uterus tipped forward over it, and the rectum behind, with two peritoneal recesses, one shallow in front of the uterus and one deep behind it. Which recesses are they? The answer is the vesicouterine pouch in front and the rectouterine pouch behind. The rectouterine pouch is the deeper.

Question 7979. A constructed illustration: a woman is described with a collection in the pelvis that has tracked to the lowest point of the peritoneal cavity and now lies behind the cervix. Which pouch is described, and which structure forms its floor? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, whose floor is the posterior fornix of the vagina. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7980. True or false: the ovary is covered by peritoneum in the same way as the uterus. The answer is false. The ovary is suspended within the peritoneal cavity, but its surface is covered by germinal epithelium and not by peritoneum. That is why an ovum released at ovulation passes into the peritoneal cavity before it enters the uterine tube.

Question 7981. Which ligament runs from the upper part of the uterus, through the inguinal canal, and ends in the labium majus? The answer is the round ligament of the uterus. It is the female remnant of the gubernaculum and helps to keep the uterus leaning forward. Within the canal it is accompanied by the ilioinguinal nerve and by a small artery.

Question 7982. Which structure passes through the inguinal canal in the female, and into which part of the external genitalia does it end? The answer is the round ligament, which ends in the labium majus. It leaves the pelvis at the deep ring and emerges at the superficial ring.

Question 7983. Which structure lies immediately anterior to the rectum in the female pelvis, and which lies immediately anterior to that structure? The answer is the vagina and the cervix, with the urinary bladder in front of them. The rectouterine pouch lies above, between the uterus and the rectum.

Question 7984. A described coronal magnetic resonance image of the female pelvis shows a pear-shaped organ in the midline with a narrow lower part, and two thin tubes running outward from its upper corners, each ending near a small oval structure. Which structures are shown? The answer is the uterus, the uterine tubes and the ovaries. The narrow lower part is the cervix.

Question 7985. A described specimen shows a uterus turned to one side, with a thick artery crossing a thin tube on the side of the cervix. Which two structures are shown, and which of them lies nearer the cervix? The answer is the uterine artery crossing the ureter, with the ureter nearer the cervix. The artery passes above the ureter and then turns upward along the uterus.

Question 7986. A constructed illustration: after a difficult delivery a woman is described with loss of sensation over the perineum, and the nerve that supplies it is thought to have been stretched. Which nerve is described, and where does it run? The answer is the pudendal nerve, which runs in the pudendal canal on the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7987. Which nerve supplies the muscles of the perineum and the skin of the external genitalia: the obturator nerve, the pudendal nerve, the femoral nerve, or the sciatic nerve? The answer is the pudendal nerve. It arises from the second, third and fourth sacral spinal nerves. Its branches include the inferior rectal nerve, the perineal nerve and the dorsal nerve of the penis or the clitoris.

Question 7988. A described vertical plane runs through the body from side to side and divides it into a front part and a back part. Which plane is it, and which two triangles of the perineum does it pass between? The answer is the coronal plane, between the urogenital triangle in front and the anal triangle behind.

Question 7989. Which sheet of muscle forms the pelvic diaphragm and is pierced by the urethra and the anal canal? The answer is the levator ani. With the coccygeus muscle it forms the pelvic diaphragm. Its parts are named pubococcygeus, puborectalis and iliococcygeus, and it forms the floor of the pelvic cavity.

Question 7990. A described view of the pelvic floor from above shows a funnel of muscle with a slit in front and a larger opening behind, and a small muscle behind the funnel running back to the coccyx. Which structures are shown? The answer is the levator ani, with the urogenital hiatus in front and the rectal hiatus behind, and the coccygeus muscle behind it.

Question 7991. A described coronal section of the pelvis shows a thick muscle sling passing from the pubis backward around a tube in the midline, with fat-filled spaces on each side. Which muscle is shown, and what are those spaces? The answer is the levator ani, with the ischioanal fossae on each side.

Question 7992. Which of these pairs belongs together: the bulbospongiosus with the bulb of the vestibule, the ischiocavernosus with the anal canal, the external anal sphincter with the clitoris, or the levator ani with the thigh? The answer is the bulbospongiosus with the bulb of the vestibule. The ischiocavernosus covers the crus of the clitoris, and the external anal sphincter surrounds the anal canal.

Question 7993. Which muscle forms a sling behind the anal canal, is part of the levator ani, and keeps the anorectal junction in front of the tip of the coccyx? The answer is the puborectalis. It arises from the pubis on each side and passes behind the rectum. Its tone produces the anorectal angle.

Question 7994. Which of these pairs belongs together: the pudendal nerve with the external anal sphincter, the inferior rectal nerve with the bladder, the dorsal nerve of the clitoris with the uterus, or the genitofemoral nerve with the ovary? The answer is the pudendal nerve with the external anal sphincter, whose inferior rectal branch supplies the sphincter.

Question 7995. A constructed illustration: after a delivery a woman is described with a tear that extends backward through the tissue between the vaginal opening and the anus, and the muscles that meet there are separated. Which structure is torn? The answer is the perineal body, joined by the bulbospongiosus, the external anal sphincter and the transverse perineal muscles. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7996. Which structures bound the ischioanal fossa on its medial and lateral sides: the levator ani medially and the obturator internus laterally, the obturator internus medially and the levator ani laterally, the pelvic diaphragm medially and the rectum laterally, or the perineal membrane medially and the gluteus maximus laterally? The answer is the levator ani medially and the obturator internus laterally.

Question 7997. Name the two glands of the male reproductive tract that lie behind the bladder and above the perineal membrane, and say which of them surrounds the urethra. The answer is the seminal vesicle, which lies behind the bladder, and the prostate, which surrounds the first part of the urethra. The bulbourethral glands lie in the deep perineal pouch.

Question 7998. Which small paired gland lies in the deep perineal pouch of the male and sends a duct into the bulb of the urethra? The answer is the bulbourethral gland, also called the Cowper gland. It lies beside the membranous part of the urethra and below the prostate. It is the homologue of the greater vestibular gland in the female.

Question 7999. Which zone of the prostate surrounds the urethra and is the usual site of the benign enlargement that narrows it: the peripheral zone, the central zone, the transition zone, or the anterior fibromuscular stroma? The answer is the transition zone. It surrounds the prostatic urethra above the openings of the ejaculatory ducts. The peripheral zone is the usual site of carcinoma.

Question 8000. True or false: the left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein while the left ovarian vein drains into the inferior vena cava. The answer is false. Both the left testicular vein and the left ovarian vein drain into the left renal vein, which is one reason a varicocele is more common on the left.

To summarize, the female reproductive organs are the uterus and the vagina, with the uterine tubes, the ovaries and the broad ligament around them, while the male organs are the prostate, the seminal vesicles, the ductus deferens and the testis. The ureter passes below the uterine artery about 2 centimeters lateral to the cervix. The ovary and the testis drain to the para-aortic nodes, while the vulva and the scrotum drain to the superficial inguinal nodes, and the right gonadal vein ends in the inferior vena cava while the left ends in the left renal vein. The perineal body, the perineal membrane and the two perineal pouches give the perineum its structure, and the pudendal nerve supplies its muscles and its skin. The vaginal fornices and the two peritoneal pouches fix the relations of the uterus to the bladder and the rectum.
