# Chapter 102 — MRI Anatomy

This chapter reads the magnetic resonance image as a map of the body, beginning with the signal that fat, fluid, muscle and bone each give, and then following that contrast through the brain, the head and neck, the spine, the joints, the chest, the abdomen and the pelvis. Magnetic resonance imaging, known as MRI, is described here as anatomy only, because nothing can be shown and every sequence and every section is spoken in words. The chapter holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10101. On a T2-weighted image, which tissue gives the brightest signal: subcutaneous fat, cerebrospinal fluid, cortical bone, or skeletal muscle? The answer is cerebrospinal fluid. Free water has a long relaxation time, so the fluid in the ventricles and around the cord shines. Fat is at its brightest on a T1-weighted image.

Question 10102. On a T1-weighted image, which tissue is brighter than every other soft tissue and also fills the marrow of the long bones? The answer is fat. It shortens the T1 relaxation time, so it returns the strongest signal of all. Subcutaneous fat and yellow marrow are the brightest things on the image.

Question 10103. A described image of the head shows the fluid in the lateral ventricles as dark spaces while the fat beneath the scalp is bright. Which sequence produced it? The answer is a T1-weighted sequence. On it fat is bright and water is dark, the reverse of a T2-weighted image. Gray and white matter lie between the two extremes.

Question 10104. Which tissue appears black on both a T1-weighted and a T2-weighted image and forms the hard outer shell of a long bone? The answer is cortical bone. Dense bone holds very little free water, so it returns almost no signal on either sequence. The marrow inside the bone is bright, which is why the outline of a bone is drawn by its marrow.

Question 10105. On a T1-weighted image of the chest, which structure appears as a dark circle beside the vertebral column: the aorta, the thymus, a lymph node, or the esophagus? The answer is the aorta. In a spin echo sequence the flowing blood leaves the slice before it can be imaged, so the lumen gives no signal. The wall of the vessel and the fat around it are seen instead.

Question 10106. True or false: on a T2-weighted image the edema that collects in injured tissue is brighter than the normal muscle around it. The answer is true. Edema is free water with a long relaxation time, so it shines against the darker muscle. Suppressing the fat signal makes that brightness even easier to see.

Question 10107. After a gadolinium-containing contrast agent is given, which normal structure improves in signal because it has no blood-brain barrier: the pituitary gland, the cortical bone, the subcutaneous fat, or the vitreous humor? The answer is the pituitary gland. Its capillaries are fenestrated, so contrast leaks into the gland and into the infundibulum above it. Fat is bright even without contrast.

Question 10108. On a T1-weighted image through the upper abdomen, which structure forms a bright band around the kidney: the perirenal fat, the renal cortex, the psoas muscle, or the pancreas? The answer is the perirenal fat. Fat is the brightest tissue on a T1-weighted image. The cortex is slightly brighter than the medulla inside it, and the psoas and the pancreas are intermediate gray.

Question 10109. Which of these four pairs belongs together: T1-weighted with bright fat, T1-weighted with bright cerebrospinal fluid, T2-weighted with dark fluid, or fat-suppressed T2 with bright fat? The answer is T1-weighted with bright fat. Cerebrospinal fluid is dark on a T1-weighted image, it is bright rather than dark on T2, and suppressing the fat removes the fat signal.

Question 10110. Name two materials that appear black on both a T1-weighted and a T2-weighted image because they hold almost no water. The answer is cortical bone and air. Both contain very few mobile protons, so neither returns a signal. Tendons and ligaments look much the same, though they may show a faint signal.

Question 10111. On a T2-weighted image of the brain, which tissue is brighter, the gray matter of the cortex or the white matter beneath it? The answer is the gray matter. It holds more free water than the myelin-rich white matter, so it shines more brightly on this sequence. On a T1-weighted image the two reverse.

Question 10112. On a T1-weighted image of the brain, which tissue is brightest: the white matter, the gray matter, the cerebrospinal fluid, or the cortical bone of the skull? The answer is the white matter. Its myelin and its lipids shorten T1, so it outshines the gray matter. The fluid and the cortical bone are both dark.

Question 10113. A described midsagittal image shows a thick curved band of white matter arching above the lateral ventricle, thin in the middle and swelling at both ends. Which structure is shown? The answer is the corpus callosum. Its front end is the genu and its back end the splenium, and it joins the two hemispheres across the midline.

Question 10114. Which paired tract of white matter lies beneath the corpus callosum, above the thalamus, and ends in the mammillary body behind the infundibulum? The answer is the fornix. It carries fibers from the hippocampus toward the hypothalamus. On a midline image it is the thinner band below the corpus callosum.

Question 10115. On an axial image through the frontal horns, which thin structure lies between the two lateral ventricles: the septum pellucidum, the fornix, the corpus callosum, or the third ventricle? The answer is the septum pellucidum. It is a double membrane stretched between the corpus callosum above and the fornix below.

Question 10116. On an axial image at the level of the thalamus, which band of white matter separates the thalamus from the lentiform nucleus: the internal capsule, the external capsule, the corpus callosum, or the cingulum? The answer is the internal capsule. Its posterior limb lies between the thalamus and the lentiform nucleus and carries the corticospinal fibers.

Question 10117. A constructed illustration: a person suddenly cannot move the right arm or the right side of the face, and the described image shows a small bright area in the left internal capsule on a diffusion-weighted study. Which tract is interrupted? The answer is the corticospinal tract. Its fibers cross in the medulla, so a lesion on the left weakens the right side.

Question 10118. True or false: on a T2-weighted image the gray matter of the cortex is brighter than the white matter beneath it. The answer is true. Gray matter holds more free water and so has a longer T2 relaxation time. On a T1-weighted image the white matter is the brighter of the two.

Question 10119. On an axial image, which structure lies in the depth of the sylvian fissure, covered by the frontal, parietal and temporal opercula: the insula, the amygdala, the hippocampus, or the caudate nucleus? The answer is the insula. It is hidden beneath the opercula, and the middle cerebral artery runs along its surface.

Question 10120. A described sagittal image shows a deep sulcus running downward and forward across the convexity, with a thicker gyrus in front of it and a thinner gyrus behind. Which sulcus is shown, and which gyri border it? The answer is the central sulcus, with the precentral gyrus in front and the postcentral gyrus behind. The precentral gyrus is the motor strip.

Question 10121. A described axial image shows a rounded mass of gray matter bulging into the lateral wall of the frontal horn of a lateral ventricle. Which structure is it? The answer is the head of the caudate nucleus. It lies beside the ventricle and above the anterior limb of the internal capsule.

Question 10122. On a T2-weighted image of the basal ganglia, which part is darker than the putamen beside it: the globus pallidus, the caudate nucleus, the thalamus, or the claustrum? The answer is the globus pallidus. It collects iron, which shortens the T2 relaxation time and darkens the tissue. The putamen also darkens with age.

Question 10123. A described coronal image shows two egg-shaped masses of gray matter on either side of a narrow midline slit, with a rounded mass below each one at the base of the brain. Which structures are shown? The answer is the two thalami beside the third ventricle, with the cerebral peduncles below them. The slit between the thalami is the third ventricle.

Question 10124. On an axial image at the level of the pons, which space lies immediately behind the pons and in front of the cerebellum: the fourth ventricle, the third ventricle, the interpeduncular cistern, or a lateral ventricle? The answer is the fourth ventricle. It is a diamond-shaped space behind the brainstem and below the cerebellum.

Question 10125. A described T2-weighted image at the level of the midbrain shows two thick pillars of white matter in front of a small central channel, with a dark band behind each pillar. What are the pillars, and what is the channel? The answer is the cerebral peduncles in front and the cerebral aqueduct behind. The dark band is the substantia nigra, darkened by iron.

Question 10126. A described axial image shows a broad belly of tissue in the midline of the brainstem with a small channel behind it, flanked by two wing-like masses of the cerebellum. Which level is shown? The answer is the pons. Its basis bulges forward, and the middle cerebellar peduncles run from its sides into the cerebellar hemispheres.

Question 10127. A single described section shows the corpus callosum from the rostrum in front to the splenium behind, the third ventricle below it, and the brainstem cut lengthwise. In which plane was the section taken? The answer is the midsagittal plane. Only a section in the midline shows the whole callosum from end to end and the brainstem in profile.

Question 10128. A constructed illustration: a small mass at the pineal region compresses the narrow channel between the midbrain and the cerebellum, and the described images show enlarged lateral and third ventricles with a normal fourth. Which channel is obstructed? The answer is the cerebral aqueduct. It connects the third ventricle above to the fourth below, so the fluid backs up above it.

Question 10129. On a coronal T2-weighted image, which elongated structure of gray matter lies in the medial part of the temporal lobe, below the temporal horn of the lateral ventricle? The answer is the hippocampus. It lies medial to the temporal horn and behind the amygdala, and it is best seen in a plane along its length.

Question 10130. Which of these connects the pons to the cerebellar hemisphere, is the largest of the cerebellar peduncles, and forms part of the lateral wall of the fourth ventricle: the superior, the middle, the inferior, or the cerebral peduncle? The answer is the middle cerebellar peduncle. It carries fibers from the pontine nuclei across to the opposite hemisphere.

Question 10131. On an axial image of the orbit, which structure runs in the middle of the cone of eye muscles, from the back of the eye to the apex of the orbit? The answer is the optic nerve. The medial and the lateral rectus muscles lie on either side of it, and it passes through the optic canal to reach the chiasm.

Question 10132. On a T2-weighted image of the eye, which of these gives the brightest signal: the vitreous humor, the lens, the sclera, or the eye muscle? The answer is the vitreous humor. It is almost all water, so it shines on a T2-weighted image. The lens and the sclera are fibrous and dark, and the muscles are intermediate gray.

Question 10133. A described sagittal image shows a small rounded gland sitting in a bony cup behind a sinus, joined to the brain above by a thin stalk. Which structure is shown? The answer is the pituitary gland in the sella turcica. Its posterior lobe is bright on T1-weighted images, and the stalk above it is the infundibulum.

Question 10134. On an axial image through the sella, which venous channel lies immediately lateral to the pituitary gland and beside the internal carotid artery: the cavernous sinus, the superior sagittal sinus, the straight sinus, or the transverse sinus? The answer is the cavernous sinus. The carotid artery and the sixth cranial nerve pass through it.

Question 10135. A constructed illustration: a described image shows a rounded mass rising out of the sella and pressing on the structure that crosses above it, and the person has lost the outer half of each visual field. Which structure is compressed? The answer is the optic chiasm. Its crossing fibers come from the nasal retina of each eye.

Question 10136. In the internal acoustic meatus, which nerve occupies the anterior superior quadrant: the facial nerve, the cochlear nerve, the superior vestibular nerve, or the inferior vestibular nerve? The answer is the facial nerve. The cochlear nerve lies below it in front, and the two vestibular nerves lie behind them both.

Question 10137. In a described image, the parotid gland is divided into a superficial and a deep part by a plane that contains the facial nerve. Which vein runs in that same plane and marks the division? The answer is the retromandibular vein. It lies just behind the ramus of the mandible and deep to the gland.

Question 10138. On an axial image of the neck, the internal jugular vein lies lateral to which structure: the internal carotid artery, the trachea, the thyroid gland, or the esophagus? The answer is the internal carotid artery. The vein is the more lateral of the two, and the vagus nerve lies behind and between them.

Question 10139. A described sagittal image through the side of the head shows a small fibrous disc between the head of the mandible and the articular surface above it, dividing the joint into two compartments. Which joint is shown? The answer is the temporomandibular joint. Its disc moves forward with the condyle when the mouth opens.

Question 10140. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the pituitary gland with the sella turcica, the pineal gland with the sella turcica, the pituitary gland with the posterior cranial fossa, or the pineal gland with the orbit? The answer is the pituitary gland with the sella turcica. The pineal gland lies above the midbrain, behind the third ventricle.

Question 10141. On a T2-weighted sagittal image of the spine, which part of the intervertebral disc is bright: the nucleus pulposus, the annulus fibrosus, the vertebral endplate, or the ligamentum flavum? The answer is the nucleus pulposus. It is rich in water and shines on a T2-weighted image, while the fibrous ring around it stays dark.

Question 10142. On an axial image through the first cervical vertebra, which dark band lies immediately behind the dens and holds it against the front of the bony ring? The answer is the transverse ligament of the atlas. It is the strongest part of the cruciform ligament, and it keeps the dens from pressing on the spinal cord.

Question 10143. A described sagittal image shows the lower part of the cerebellum with two rounded tonsils above a bony ring, through which the spinal cord and its coverings pass downward. Which level is shown? The answer is the foramen magnum. The tonsils lie just above it, and the medulla becomes the spinal cord as it passes through.

Question 10144. The vertebral artery ascends through a series of small bony rings beside the cervical vertebral bodies. From which vertebra upward do those rings carry the artery: the first cervical, the third cervical, the fourth cervical, or the sixth cervical? The answer is the sixth cervical vertebra. The artery enters the transverse foramen there and climbs to the atlas.

Question 10145. A constructed illustration: a described sagittal image shows disc material bulging backward and to the left at the lowest lumbar level, and the person has pain shooting down the left leg. Which structure is compressed? The answer is the traversing nerve root in the lateral recess, which the disc presses as the root travels past that level toward its foramen.

Question 10146. True or false: on a T2-weighted image the cerebrospinal fluid around the spinal cord is bright and the cord itself is darker. The answer is true. Fluid is bright on T2, while the cord is intermediate gray, so the fluid outlines it. The nerve roots cross that bright space on their way out.

Question 10147. In an adult, at which vertebral level does the tapered lower end of the spinal cord usually lie: the first or second lumbar vertebra, the fifth lumbar vertebra, the twelfth thoracic vertebra, or the first sacral vertebra? The answer is the first or second lumbar vertebra. Below it the canal holds the cauda equina, a bundle of roots.

Question 10148. On an axial T2-weighted image of the spine, which structure forms a dark band joining the laminae behind the spinal canal: the ligamentum flavum, the posterior longitudinal ligament, the anterior longitudinal ligament, or the interspinous ligament? The answer is the ligamentum flavum. It is elastic fibrous tissue, dark on every sequence, and it lies behind the canal.

Question 10149. Which statement about the intervertebral disc on a T2-weighted image is correct: the nucleus pulposus loses its bright signal with age, the nucleus pulposus becomes brighter with age, the annulus fibrosus becomes bright with age, or the disc gives no signal at any age? The answer is that the nucleus loses its bright signal with age. It dries out, and the darkening comes before any bulge appears.

Question 10150. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the nucleus pulposus with a bright signal on T2, the annulus fibrosus with a bright signal on T2, cortical bone with a bright signal on T1, or cerebrospinal fluid with a bright signal on T1? The answer is the nucleus pulposus with a bright signal on T2. The other three are dark on the sequence named.

Question 10151. On a T1-weighted sagittal image of the lumbar spine, which tissue fills the space between the thecal sac and the bony canal and appears bright? The answer is the epidural fat. Fat is bright on a T1-weighted image, so it outlines the dark thecal sac from behind. Inside the sac the cerebrospinal fluid is dark on this sequence.

Question 10152. Which structure lies within the thecal sac below the lower end of the spinal cord: the cauda equina, the conus medullaris, the ligamentum flavum, or the filum terminale alone? The answer is the cauda equina. It is a bundle of lumbar and sacral nerve roots descending to their openings, and it floats in the cerebrospinal fluid.

Question 10153. A described T2-weighted image shows a bright column of fluid in the lower canal, within which a bundle of dark rounded strands hangs downward like a horse's tail. Which level is this, and what are the strands? The answer is the lower lumbar canal, and the strands are the cauda equina, descending from the end of the cord.

Question 10154. At the level of a lumbar disc, which part of the canal does the traversing nerve root occupy before it enters its foramen: the lateral recess, the central canal, the anterior epidural space, or the posterior epidural space? The answer is the lateral recess. It lies between the disc in front and the facet joint behind.

Question 10155. A constructed illustration: a described image shows a large central disc bulge at a thoracic level pressing the cord against the bone behind it, and the person has stiffness in both legs with difficulty walking. Which structure is compressed? The answer is the spinal cord within the vertebral canal. In the thoracic region the canal is narrow, so a central bulge reaches the cord quickly.

Question 10156. True or false: the ligamentum flavum lies in front of the spinal cord and separates it from the vertebral bodies. The answer is false. The ligamentum flavum joins the laminae behind the canal, so it lies behind the cord. The posterior longitudinal ligament is the one in front of the cord.

Question 10157. Which structure lies in the canal immediately in front of the thecal sac, separating it from the back of the vertebral bodies: the posterior longitudinal ligament, the ligamentum flavum, the interspinous ligament, or the supraspinous ligament? The answer is the posterior longitudinal ligament. It runs down the back of the vertebral bodies inside the canal, and a disc meets it as it bulges backward.

Question 10158. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the conus medullaris with the tapered end of the cord, the conus medullaris with the roots below it, the filum terminale with the upper end of the cord, or the cauda equina with the tapered end of the cord? The answer is the conus medullaris with the tapered end of the cord. The roots below it are the cauda equina.

Question 10159. In a described image of the lower back, a thin strand continues downward through the fused sacral segments after the thecal sac has ended. Which structure is it? The answer is the filum terminale, a strand of pia mater that anchors the cord to the coccyx. It lies in the sacral canal and returns almost no signal.

Question 10160. Name the two spaces that surround the spinal cord within the vertebral canal, one outside the dura and one inside the arachnoid. The answer is the epidural space and the subarachnoid space. The epidural space holds fat and veins, and the subarachnoid space holds cerebrospinal fluid. The dura and the arachnoid lie between them.

Question 10161. A described coronal image of the shoulder shows a dark band running from the top of the scapula across the top of the humeral head to end on the greater tubercle. Which structure is it? The answer is the tendon of supraspinatus. It passes beneath the acromion, where the tendon is most often torn.

Question 10162. Which of these muscles forms part of the rotator cuff of the shoulder: the teres major, the supraspinatus, the deltoid, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the supraspinatus. The supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor and the subscapularis blend with the capsule, while the others do not.

Question 10163. A described axial image of the shoulder shows a dark triangular rim attached to the edge of the shallow socket of the scapula, deepening it around the head of the humerus. Which structure is shown? The answer is the glenoid labrum. It is fibrous and dark on every sequence, and a tear shows as a bright line within it.

Question 10164. On an axial image of the upper end of the humerus, which structure lies in the groove between the greater and the lesser tubercle: the tendon of the long head of the biceps, the tendon of the short head of the biceps, the axillary nerve, or the coracohumeral ligament? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps, held in the groove by the transverse humeral ligament.

Question 10165. A constructed illustration: a person cannot begin to lift the arm sideways, and the described T2-weighted image shows a bright line running through the dark tendon above the humeral head. Which tendon is torn? The answer is the tendon of supraspinatus. It begins abduction and passes beneath the acromion, where it is most often injured.

Question 10166. True or false: tendons and ligaments appear dark on both T1-weighted and T2-weighted images because they contain very little free water. The answer is true. Their collagen holds few mobile protons, so they return almost no signal. A tear brings water into the tissue and turns part of it bright.

Question 10167. At the elbow, which nerve lies in the groove behind the medial epicondyle: the median nerve, the ulnar nerve, the radial nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the ulnar nerve. It passes through the cubital tunnel behind the medial epicondyle, where it can be felt as a rounded cord.

Question 10168. On an axial image of the wrist, which structure lies in the tunnel deep to the flexor retinaculum, in the middle of the tendons that surround it? The answer is the median nerve. It lies just beneath the retinaculum, with the flexor tendons around it, while the ulnar nerve and artery lie outside the tunnel.

Question 10169. A described coronal image of the wrist shows a dark triangular plate stretched between the lower end of the ulna and the carpal bones beside it. Which structure is shown? The answer is the triangular fibrocartilage complex. It cushions the ulnar side of the wrist and stays dark on every sequence.

Question 10170. On an axial image through the wrist, which structure connects the scaphoid to the lunate and appears as a dark band between them: the scapholunate ligament, the triangular fibrocartilage, the flexor retinaculum, or the ulnar collateral ligament of the wrist? The answer is the scapholunate ligament. A tear widens the gap and changes the alignment of the carpal bones.

Question 10171. On an image of the knee, which structure lies between the femur and the tibia, is shaped like a C on the medial side, and stays dark on every sequence? The answer is the medial meniscus. The menisci are fibrocartilage, so they return almost no signal unless a tear brings fluid into them.

Question 10172. On a T2-weighted image of the knee, what does a bright line reaching the surface of the dark meniscus indicate: a tear, a normal variant, a blood vessel, or calcification? The answer is a tear. Fluid has entered the substance of the meniscus through the break. Normal meniscal signal does not reach the surface.

Question 10173. A described sagittal image of the knee shows a dark band running from the front of the tibia upward and backward to the inner surface of the lateral condyle of the femur, within the joint. Which ligament is shown? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament. It stops the tibia from sliding forward on the femur.

Question 10174. A fluid-filled swelling appears behind the knee, between the tendon of the medial head of the gastrocnemius and the tendon of the semimembranosus. Which space does it occupy? The answer is the popliteal fossa, and the swelling is a popliteal cyst. It communicates with the joint between those two tendons.

Question 10175. A constructed illustration: a described T1-weighted image shows a curved dark line running through the bright marrow of the femoral head, with a second bright line beside it on the T2-weighted image. What does this pattern indicate? The answer is avascular necrosis of the femoral head. The double line marks the boundary between dead bone and living bone.

Question 10176. Which of these tendons inserts on the back of the calcaneus and is the thickest tendon in the body: the Achilles tendon, the tibialis posterior tendon, the peroneus longus tendon, or the flexor hallucis longus tendon? The answer is the Achilles tendon, formed by the gastrocnemius and the soleus. It stays dark on every sequence.

Question 10177. In a described axial image of the ankle, which dark band runs from the front of the lateral malleolus to the neck of the talus? The answer is the anterior talofibular ligament. It is the most often injured ligament of the ankle, and it is seen best with the foot turned slightly inward.

Question 10178. A described axial image behind the medial malleolus shows a flattened nerve lying between the flexor tendons and the bone, deep to a fibrous band. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the tibial nerve. It passes through the tarsal tunnel with the tendons of tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus and flexor hallucis longus.

Question 10179. On a T1-weighted image, a child's vertebral marrow is less bright than an adult's. Which explanation is correct: red marrow holds more water and less fat than yellow marrow, it holds more fat, it contains more iron, or the two marrows look the same on T1? The answer is that red marrow holds more water and less fat, so it is darker. It is replaced by yellow marrow with age.

Question 10180. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the medial meniscus with a C shape, the lateral meniscus with a C shape, the anterior cruciate ligament with the back of the tibia, or the Achilles tendon with the tibia? The answer is the medial meniscus with a C shape. The lateral meniscus is more circular, and the Achilles tendon ends on the calcaneus.

Question 10181. On a magnetic resonance image of the chest, why do the lungs appear almost black: they contain very little water, they contain too much fat, they are filled with flowing blood, or they hold iron? The answer is that they contain very little water, and the many boundaries between air and tissue disturb the field. Only the vessels stand out.

Question 10182. A described axial image of the heart shows a thin dark line running around the myocardium, just outside the bright fat that fills the grooves of the heart. Which structure is that dark line? The answer is the pericardium. It is fibrous and returns almost no signal, so it stands out against the fat on both sides of it.

Question 10183. A described axial image through the heart shows four dark blood-filled chambers, two with thick muscular walls and two with thin walls behind them. Which chambers are shown? The answer is the two ventricles in front, with their thick walls, and the two atria behind them. The chambers look dark because the blood is flowing.

Question 10184. On an axial image at the level of the aortic arch, which structure lies to the right of the trachea and in front of the right main bronchus: the superior vena cava, the descending aorta, the esophagus, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the superior vena cava. The arch of the aorta lies to the left and crosses in front of the trachea.

Question 10185. Which structure ascends through the posterior mediastinum from the abdomen to the root of the neck, lying behind the esophagus and in front of the vertebral column: the thoracic duct, the azygos vein, the phrenic nerve, or the vagus nerve? The answer is the thoracic duct. It carries lymph upward and empties into the left venous angle.

Question 10186. On an axial image of the chest wall, which muscle lies immediately deep to the breast and forms the front wall of the axilla: the pectoralis major, the serratus anterior, the latissimus dorsi, or the trapezius? The answer is the pectoralis major. Its fibers run from the clavicle, the sternum and the ribs to the humerus.

Question 10187. A constructed illustration: a described image shows a small mass in the upper outer part of the breast, close to the fold of the armpit. Toward which group of lymph nodes does lymph from that quadrant normally drain? The answer is the axillary nodes. The upper outer quadrant is the most common site, and its lymph follows the axillary tail.

Question 10188. On an axial image at the level of the left atrium, which structure lies immediately behind the heart: the esophagus, the trachea, the thymus, or the sternum? The answer is the esophagus. It descends behind the left atrium and in front of the descending aorta, while the trachea has already divided above this level.

Question 10189. A described T1-weighted image shows the walls of the heart as intermediate gray while the blood inside the chambers is black. What accounts for the black chambers? The answer is the flow of blood, which carries it out of the slice before it can be imaged. The myocardium itself gives an intermediate signal.

Question 10190. Name the two great veins that enter the right atrium, one from above and one from below. The answer is the superior vena cava and the inferior vena cava. The superior vena cava enters from above, and the inferior vena cava enters from below, close to the interatrial septum.

Question 10191. On a T1-weighted image of the upper abdomen, which organ lies under the right dome of the diaphragm and gives a signal slightly brighter than the spleen beside it? The answer is the liver. On a T2-weighted image the relation reverses, and the spleen becomes the brighter of the two.

Question 10192. On an axial image at the level of the pancreas, which vein is formed behind the neck of the pancreas and lies in front of the inferior vena cava: the portal vein, the splenic vein, the left renal vein, or the inferior mesenteric vein? The answer is the portal vein. It is formed where the splenic vein joins the superior mesenteric vein.

Question 10193. A described T1-weighted image through a kidney shows an outer rim brighter than the inner columns of tissue, which run toward the fatty center of the organ. Which parts are shown? The answer is the renal cortex, brighter than the medulla, with the fat of the renal sinus in the middle. The cortex wraps around the pyramids.

Question 10194. On an axial image of the upper abdomen, which vessel crosses in front of the aorta and behind the superior mesenteric artery on its way to the inferior vena cava: the left renal vein, the right renal vein, the splenic vein, or the left gonadal vein? The answer is the left renal vein. It drains the left kidney, the left adrenal gland and the left gonadal vein.

Question 10195. A constructed illustration: a described image shows a small rounded mass in an adrenal gland, bright on one T1-weighted image and much darker on a second image taken at a different echo time. Which tissue does the mass contain? The answer is fat, the behavior of a benign adenoma of the adrenal cortex. Water and fat lose signal when their protons are out of phase.

Question 10196. On a T2-weighted image of the prostate, which zone lies at the back of the gland and is the brightest part: the peripheral zone, the central zone, the transition zone, or the anterior fibromuscular stroma? The answer is the peripheral zone. It wraps the gland behind, and most cancers of the prostate begin in it.

Question 10197. On a T2-weighted sagittal image of the pelvis, which organ shows three layers, a bright inner stripe, a thin dark band around it, and a thicker intermediate layer outside? The answer is the uterus. The bright stripe is the endometrium, the dark band is the junctional zone, and the outer layer is the myometrium.

Question 10198. A constructed illustration: a described axial image of the pelvis shows a pear-shaped muscle passing out of the greater sciatic foramen, with a thick nerve lying below it. Which muscle and nerve are shown? The answer is the piriformis muscle and the sciatic nerve, which leaves the pelvis below the muscle and may be pressed against it.

Question 10199. Which muscle forms the greater part of the pelvic diaphragm and separates the pelvic cavity above from the perineum below: the levator ani, the obturator internus, the piriformis, or the coccygeus? The answer is the levator ani. It slopes downward and forward from the side wall of the pelvis, and the urethra, the vagina and the rectum pass through it.

Question 10200. A single described section through the pelvis shows the bladder in front, the uterus in the middle and the rectum behind, all in one midline slice. In which plane was the section taken, and where does the bladder lie relative to the uterus? The answer is the midsagittal plane, with the bladder in front of the uterus and the rectum behind it.

To summarize, every structure on a magnetic resonance image is placed by the signal it gives and by the sequence that produced it. Fat is bright on T1-weighted images, free water is bright on T2-weighted images, and cortical bone, air and flowing blood are dark on both, while tendons, ligaments, menisci and the labrum stay dark on every sequence, so that a tear shows as a bright line. In the brain the white matter is brighter than the gray on T1 and darker than it on T2, and the corpus callosum, the fornix, the thalami, the basal ganglia, the internal capsule, the midbrain, the pons and the fourth ventricle are each recognized by shape and by their neighbors, while the pituitary gland enhances because it has no blood-brain barrier. In the spine the nucleus pulposus is bright on T2, the annulus and the ligaments are dark, the epidural fat is bright on T1, and the cord ends as the conus medullaris with the cauda equina below it. In the chest and the abdomen the dark lung, the pericardium, the flow void of the heart chambers, the bright perirenal fat and the three layers of the uterus are the landmarks.
