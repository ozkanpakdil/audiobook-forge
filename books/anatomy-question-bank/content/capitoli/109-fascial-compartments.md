# Chapter 109 — Fascial Compartments

This chapter maps the sheets of connective tissue that wrap the muscles, gather them into working groups, and carry the vessels and nerves between those groups. It covers the deep fascia of the limbs and the trunk, the intermuscular septa and interosseous membranes that divide one compartment from another, the retinacula that roof the tunnels at the wrist and the ankle, and the fascial layers of the neck and the abdominal wall. It holds one hundred questions, and every one gives its answer straight after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind. Each compartment is defined by its walls, so the questions are answered with boundaries and contents rather than with labels. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10801. Which sheet of dense connective tissue lies deep to the superficial fascia, wraps the muscles of a limb and sends septa inward to the bone? The answer is the deep fascia. It is tough and almost free of fat, and it binds the muscles of a region together. It also forms the retinacula at the wrist and the ankle.

Question 10802. Which of these muscles lies in the anterior compartment of the arm: the triceps brachii, the brachialis, the anconeus, or the brachioradialis? The answer is the brachialis. The compartment also holds the biceps brachii and the coracobrachialis, and the musculocutaneous nerve supplies all three. The triceps lies behind and the brachioradialis belongs to the forearm.

Question 10803. A described transverse section of the arm shows a single bone, two thick fascial walls running from the bone to the deep fascia, and muscle on either side of each wall. Which structures are those walls? The answer is the medial and the lateral intermuscular septa. They divide the limb into an anterior and a posterior compartment.

Question 10804. Which intermuscular septum of the arm separates the triceps brachii behind from the brachialis in front? The answer is the medial intermuscular septum. The ulnar nerve descends along it from the front of the arm to the back. The lateral septum separates the triceps from the brachioradialis.

Question 10805. What is the name of the deep fascia of the thigh, which encloses its muscles and is attached above to the inguinal ligament? The answer is the fascia lata. It is strongest on the lateral side, where it forms the iliotibial tract. It sends septa inward to the femur.

Question 10806. Which of these muscles belongs to the anterior compartment of the leg: the peroneus longus, the tibialis anterior, the gastrocnemius, or the flexor hallucis longus? The answer is the tibialis anterior. The compartment also holds the long extensors of the toes and the peroneus tertius. The peroneus longus lies laterally and the other two lie behind.

Question 10807. A constructed illustration: after a fracture of the tibia a person has severe pain in the front of the leg, worse when the toes are stretched. Which compartment is affected, and why is the pressure dangerous? The answer is the anterior compartment of the leg. Its fascial walls scarcely stretch, so swelling raises the pressure and can cut off the blood supply. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10808. True or false: in a limb the deep fascia lies between the superficial fascia and the muscles. The answer is true. The superficial fascia is the fatty layer just beneath the skin. The deep fascia lies deep to it and superficial to the muscle.

Question 10809. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lateral compartment of the leg with the superficial peroneal nerve, the anterior compartment with the tibial nerve, the posterior compartment with the deep peroneal nerve, or the anterior thigh with the obturator nerve? The answer is the lateral compartment with the superficial peroneal nerve. The anterior compartment takes the deep peroneal nerve and the posterior the tibial nerve.

Question 10810. Which strong sheet joins the shafts of the radius and the ulna, and in which direction do its fibers run? The answer is the interosseous membrane, whose fibers run downward and medially from the radius to the ulna. Force applied to the hand crosses it to the radius and then to the elbow.

Question 10811. Which muscle forms the roof of the adductor canal: the adductor magnus, the sartorius, the vastus medialis, or the adductor longus? The answer is the sartorius, with its fascia. The adductor magnus forms the posterior wall and the vastus medialis the lateral wall. The femoral vessels and the saphenous nerve pass through the canal.

Question 10812. A described transverse section of the leg shows three muscles in front of the interosseous membrane, two against the fibula, and a large mass behind. How many compartments does the section show? The answer is four. A transverse septum divides the posterior mass into a superficial and a deep part.

Question 10813. Which of these septa separates the lateral compartment of the leg from the anterior compartment: the anterior intermuscular septum, the posterior intermuscular septum, the transverse intermuscular septum, or a medial one? The answer is the anterior intermuscular septum, attached to the anterior border of the fibula. The posterior septum separates the lateral compartment from the posterior one.

Question 10814. A constructed illustration: a runner has a tight, aching outer side of the leg after a set distance, and the pressure in one compartment is raised after exercise. Which compartment and which nerve are most often involved? The answer is the lateral compartment of the leg and the superficial peroneal nerve. It is the least distensible of the four compartments. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10815. Name the two intermuscular septa of the leg that run from the fibula to the deep fascia, spoken as a list. The answer is the anterior intermuscular septum and the posterior intermuscular septum. Together with the interosseous membrane they help divide the leg into four compartments.

Question 10816. Which fascial sheath encloses the rectus abdominis muscle, and what forms it? The answer is the rectus sheath, formed by the aponeuroses of the three flat muscles of the abdominal wall. It is complete above the arcuate line and deficient behind the muscle below it.

Question 10817. Which of these layers of the abdominal wall is the deepest of the flat muscles: the external oblique, the internal oblique, the transversus abdominis, or the rectus abdominis? The answer is the transversus abdominis, whose fibers run across the abdomen. Behind it lie the extraperitoneal fat and then the peritoneum.

Question 10818. A described dissection of the front of the abdomen strips the skin and then a layer of fat, and the next sheet is thin, membranous and attached below to the fascia lata. Which layer has been reached? The answer is the membranous layer of the superficial fascia. Its attachment below the inguinal ligament keeps fluid from tracking into the thigh.

Question 10819. The linea alba is a midline band on the front of the abdomen; which structures fuse to form it? The answer is the aponeuroses of the external oblique, the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis. The band runs from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis.

Question 10820. Which large fascial sheet covers the deep muscles of the back, extends from the sacrum to the thoracic region and encloses the erector spinae? The answer is the thoracolumbar fascia. In the lumbar region it has a posterior, a middle and an anterior layer. The latissimus dorsi attaches to it.

Question 10821. Which of these relations at the thoracolumbar fascia is correct: the erector spinae lies between its posterior and middle layers, the quadratus lumborum lies behind the posterior layer, the latissimus dorsi lies deep to the anterior layer, or the fascia stops at the chest? The answer is the first. The quadratus lumborum lies between the middle and the anterior layers.

Question 10822. Which of these pairs belongs together: the clavipectoral fascia with the axilla, the crural fascia with the arm, the renal fascia with the lung, or the thoracolumbar fascia with the neck? The answer is the clavipectoral fascia with the axilla. It fills the space below the clavicle and behind the pectoralis minor, and the cephalic vein pierces it.

Question 10823. A constructed illustration: a deep infection of the perineum spreads along a fascial plane but is held below the pelvic diaphragm. Which fascia forms that floor? The answer is the pelvic diaphragm, formed by the levator ani and the coccygeus with their fasciae. It separates the perineum from the pelvic cavity above. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10824. What is the name of the deep fascia of the leg, which encloses its muscles and forms the retinacula at the ankle? The answer is the crural fascia. Above it is continuous with the fascia lata and below with the fascia of the foot. Its septa divide the leg into four compartments.

Question 10825. A described transverse section of the thigh shows three muscle groups, one in front, one medially and one behind, separated by thick fascial walls running from the outer sleeve to the femur. What are those walls? The answer is the intermuscular septa of the thigh, which run from the deep fascia to the linea aspera.

Question 10826. Which intermuscular septum of the thigh separates the anterior compartment from the posterior compartment? The answer is the lateral intermuscular septum. It is attached to the lateral lip of the linea aspera and is the strongest of the three. The medial septum separates the anterior from the medial compartment.

Question 10827. Which of these muscles lies in the posterior compartment of the thigh: the adductor magnus, the semitendinosus, the vastus medialis, or the pectineus? The answer is the semitendinosus. It is joined by the semimembranosus and the biceps femoris, and the group is supplied by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve, except for the short head of the biceps femoris. Their action is to extend the hip and flex the knee.

Question 10828. Which band of deep fascia forms the roof of the carpal tunnel and holds the flexor tendons against the wrist? The answer is the flexor retinaculum, also called the transverse carpal ligament. It is attached to the scaphoid and the trapezium laterally and to the pisiform and the hamate medially.

Question 10829. True or false: the carpal tunnel is bounded behind by the carpal bones and in front by the flexor retinaculum. The answer is true. The bones form an arch with a concave front, and the retinaculum bridges it as a roof. The tunnel therefore has almost no room to expand.

Question 10830. A described transverse section through the wrist shows a fibrous band bridging a bony arch, and inside the arch a nerve lying just under the band with tendons around it. Which nerve is described? The answer is the median nerve, within the carpal tunnel. It lies most superficially in the tunnel, immediately beneath the retinaculum.

Question 10831. Which space of the hand lies between the metacarpal bones and holds the muscles that pull the fingers together: the thenar compartment, the adductor compartment, the interosseous compartment, or the hypothenar compartment? The answer is the interosseous compartment. It contains the dorsal and the palmar interossei, supplied by the ulnar nerve.

Question 10832. A constructed illustration: a person wakes with tingling in the thumb, the index finger and the middle finger, worse at night. Which compartment is implicated, and which structure in it is compressed? The answer is the carpal tunnel, and the structure compressed is the median nerve. The tunnel is a closed compartment roofed by the flexor retinaculum. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10833. Which of these structures does not lie in the tarsal tunnel behind the medial malleolus: the tendon of the tibialis posterior, the posterior tibial artery, the tibial nerve, or the tendon of the peroneus longus? The answer is the tendon of the peroneus longus. It runs behind the lateral malleolus instead. The other three lie under the flexor retinaculum with the long flexors of the toes.

Question 10834. Which compartment of the sole lies between the medial and the lateral compartments and contains the flexor digitorum brevis? The answer is the central compartment of the sole. It lies between two septa that run from the deep fascia down to the bones. The medial compartment holds the muscles of the great toe.

Question 10835. Which layer of the deep cervical fascia encloses the thyroid gland and the trachea: the investing layer, the pretracheal layer, the prevertebral layer, or the carotid sheath? The answer is the pretracheal layer. It lies in front of the trachea and the esophagus. The investing layer encircles the neck instead.

Question 10836. A described transverse section through the neck at the level of the thyroid gland shows a midline tube wrapped by a fascial layer, a sheath around the great vessels on each side, and a strong layer in front of the spine. Which layers are shown? The answer is the pretracheal layer, the carotid sheath and the prevertebral layer. All three are parts of the deep cervical fascia.

Question 10837. Which fascial tube of the neck contains the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve? The answer is the carotid sheath. It is formed by all three layers of the deep cervical fascia. Within it the vein lies lateral to the artery and the nerve lies between them.

Question 10838. A constructed illustration: an infection behind the pharynx tracks down between two fascial layers to the diaphragm. Which space is described, and which layers bound it? The answer is the danger space, between the alar fascia in front and the prevertebral fascia behind. It lies behind the retropharyngeal space, which stops higher in the chest. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10839. Which of these pairs belongs together: the investing layer with the trapezius and the sternocleidomastoid, the pretracheal layer with the scalene muscles, the prevertebral layer with the thyroid gland, or the carotid sheath with the pharynx? The answer is the investing layer with the trapezius and the sternocleidomastoid. The pretracheal layer encloses the thyroid gland and the prevertebral layer covers the scalenes.

Question 10840. Which part of the deep cervical fascia encloses the thyroid gland and lies in front of the trachea? The answer is the pretracheal layer. It is also called the visceral layer. Below, it blends with the fibrous pericardium, and behind it lies the esophagus.

Question 10841. A described dissection opens the sheath of the rectus abdominis above the navel and finds the aponeuroses in front of the muscle, then opens it below the navel and finds them behind it. What is the name of the line at which this changes? The answer is the arcuate line. Below it only the transversalis fascia lies behind the muscle.

Question 10842. Which fascial layer of the neck lies immediately in front of the prevertebral fascia and forms the back wall of the retropharyngeal space: the buccopharyngeal fascia, the alar fascia, the investing layer, or the carotid sheath? The answer is the alar fascia. The space between it and the prevertebral fascia is the danger space.

Question 10843. Which of these statements about the parotid fascia is correct: it is part of the investing layer of the deep cervical fascia, it lies deep to the parotid duct, it encloses the submandibular gland, or it blends with the pleura? The answer is the first. It forms a strong capsule around the gland and is attached above to the zygomatic arch.

Question 10844. Which fascia extends from the clavicle into the axilla, encloses the subclavius and the pectoralis minor, and is pierced by the cephalic vein? The answer is the clavipectoral fascia. It fills the gap between the clavicle and the upper border of that muscle. Below the pectoralis minor it continues as the suspensory ligament of the axilla.

Question 10845. A described dissection of the chest wall shows a strong fascial sheet pierced by a vein running up toward the shoulder, with a small muscle above the sheet and a larger muscle below it. Which fascia is shown? The answer is the clavipectoral fascia, pierced by the cephalic vein. The thoracoacromial vessels pierce it as well.

Question 10846. Which of these is the most likely explanation when a hand becomes tense and painful after a crush injury and the fingers are held flexed: torn thenar muscles, an interosseous compartment under pressure, a divided carpal tunnel, or a cut retinaculum? The answer is an interosseous compartment under pressure. The fascia and the metacarpals leave no room for swelling, so the muscles within are compressed.

Question 10847. Which intermuscular septum does the radial nerve pierce as it passes from the back of the arm into the front, and which does the ulnar nerve pierce going the other way? The answer is that the radial nerve pierces the lateral septum and the ulnar nerve the medial septum. The radial nerve has already run in the spiral groove.

Question 10848. In a described transverse section of the leg the interosseous membrane appears as a band between two bones. Which way does the anterior compartment lie relative to the membrane? The answer is in front of it. The deep posterior compartment lies behind the membrane, which therefore separates the two.

Question 10849. Which of these statements about the compartments of the thigh is correct: the medial compartment holds the adductor group, the anterior compartment holds the hamstrings, the posterior compartment is supplied by the femoral nerve, or the lateral septum separates the anterior from the medial compartment? The answer is the first. The anterior compartment holds the quadriceps and the hamstrings lie behind.

Question 10850. Which of these statements about the femoral sheath is correct: it is formed by the transversalis fascia and the iliac fascia, it encloses the femoral nerve, it is a thickening of the deep fascia, or it wraps the hip joint? The answer is the first. It encloses the femoral artery and vein and the femoral canal, but not the nerve, which lies lateral to it.

Question 10851. Which nerve and which artery pass through the quadrangular space together and wind around the surgical neck of the humerus? The answer is the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery. The space lies between the teres minor above, the teres major below and the long head of the triceps medially. The nerve supplies the deltoid.

Question 10852. In the cubital fossa, which of these structures lies most laterally: the median nerve, the brachial artery, the tendon of the biceps brachii, or the radial nerve? The answer is the tendon of the biceps brachii. From lateral to medial the order is the tendon, the artery and the median nerve. The radial nerve lies in the floor.

Question 10853. A described radiograph shows a fracture line through the middle of the shaft of the humerus, with a spike of bone pointing upward. Which nerve is most at risk in the groove against the back of the bone? The answer is the radial nerve. It runs in the spiral groove between the origins of the triceps.

Question 10854. In the upper arm the median nerve lies lateral to the brachial artery; where do the two cross, and on which side does the nerve lie below? The answer is that they cross in the middle of the arm, and the nerve lies medial to the artery below. It then passes through the cubital fossa medial to the artery.

Question 10855. A constructed illustration: a deep cut across the front of the wrist leaves the thumb and the index finger numb, and the thumb can no longer be opposed. Which nerve has been divided? The answer is the median nerve. It lies just under the skin and the retinaculum in the middle of the wrist. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10856. Which nerve passes behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus, where it can be felt as a cord and is easily injured? The answer is the ulnar nerve. It lies in the groove between the epicondyle and the olecranon, in the cubital tunnel. It then enters the forearm between the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris.

Question 10857. Which of these is the order of the main structures in the femoral triangle from lateral to medial: vein, artery, nerve; nerve, artery, vein; artery, nerve, vein; or vein, nerve, artery? The answer is nerve, artery, vein. The nerve lies outside the femoral sheath and the vein lies most medially. Medial to the vein lies the empty femoral canal.

Question 10858. True or false: the femoral nerve lies outside the femoral sheath and lateral to the femoral artery. The answer is true. The sheath holds the artery, the vein and the femoral canal. The nerve lies lateral to the artery and outside the sheath.

Question 10859. Which of these pairs belongs together: the deep peroneal nerve with the anterior compartment of the leg, the tibial nerve with the lateral compartment, the superficial peroneal nerve with the posterior compartment, or the femoral nerve with the back of the thigh? The answer is the deep peroneal nerve with the anterior compartment. The superficial peroneal nerve supplies the lateral compartment.

Question 10860. Which nerve winds around the neck of the fibula, where it lies just beneath the skin and is easily injured by a blow or by prolonged pressure? The answer is the common peroneal nerve. It divides there into the deep and the superficial peroneal nerves. Injury weakens the muscles that lift the foot.

Question 10861. Behind the medial malleolus, which of these lies most anteriorly: the tendon of the tibialis posterior, the posterior tibial artery, the tibial nerve, or the tendon of the flexor hallucis longus? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior. From front to back the order is the tibialis posterior, the flexor digitorum longus, the posterior tibial artery, the tibial nerve and the flexor hallucis longus.

Question 10862. A described transverse section through the knee shows a diamond-shaped space behind the joint, filled with fat, with a large nerve nearest the skin, a vein beneath it and an artery deepest against the bone. Which space and which artery are shown? The answer is the popliteal fossa, with the popliteal artery deepest.

Question 10863. Which of these relations of the pudendal nerve is correct: it leaves the pelvis below the piriformis and crosses the sacrospinous ligament, it leaves above the piriformis, it passes through the obturator canal, or it lies in front of the sacrum? The answer is the first. It hooks around the ischial spine, medial to the internal pudendal artery, and enters the pudendal canal.

Question 10864. A constructed illustration: some weeks after a hip operation a person cannot abduct the hip and leans over that side when walking. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the superior gluteal nerve, which supplies the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus and the tensor fasciae latae. It leaves the pelvis above the piriformis. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10865. Which of these structures lies most superficially in the popliteal fossa: the popliteal artery, the popliteal vein, the tibial nerve, or the common peroneal nerve? The answer is the tibial nerve. It lies nearest the skin, with the vein beneath it and the artery deepest against the bone.

Question 10866. Which artery can be felt in the floor of the anatomical snuffbox, between the tendons of the extensor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis? The answer is the radial artery. It crosses the lateral side of the wrist and turns into the palm between the heads of the first dorsal interosseous.

Question 10867. Which of these structures passes through the carpal tunnel: the ulnar nerve, the radial artery, the median nerve, or the tendon of the flexor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the median nerve. It passes with the tendons of the long flexors of the fingers and the thumb. The ulnar nerve and artery pass outside the tunnel.

Question 10868. A described dissection of the palm lifts a triangular sheet of deep fascia and finds septa running from it to the third and the fifth metacarpals. Which sheet is described? The answer is the palmar aponeurosis, whose septa separate the thenar, the hypothenar and the central compartments of the palm. The interosseous compartment lies deeper.

Question 10869. At the wrist, which structure lies immediately medial to the ulnar artery as both pass into the palm? The answer is the ulnar nerve. They travel together through a canal at the medial side of the wrist. The artery then curls across the palm as the superficial palmar arch.

Question 10870. Which large nerve leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis and descends in the back of the thigh? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It is the largest nerve in the body and lies deep to the gluteus maximus at first. It divides into the tibial and the common peroneal nerves.

Question 10871. Which of these structures leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen above the piriformis: the sciatic nerve, the superior gluteal nerve, the inferior gluteal nerve, or the pudendal nerve? The answer is the superior gluteal nerve. It runs between the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus. The other three leave below the muscle.

Question 10872. A constructed illustration: after a shoulder is dislocated downward the deltoid cannot be lifted and the skin over it is numb. Which nerve has been injured, and where does it wind around the neck of the humerus? The answer is the axillary nerve, in the quadrangular space. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10873. True or false: the great saphenous vein ascends in front of the medial malleolus accompanied by the saphenous nerve. The answer is true. The nerve lies immediately beside the vein and supplies the skin of the medial side of the leg and the foot. The small saphenous vein runs behind the lateral malleolus.

Question 10874. A described dissection of the front of the thigh shows a triangle bounded above by the inguinal ligament, laterally by the sartorius and medially by the adductor longus, with a nerve lateral to an artery and a vein medial to it. Which triangle is shown? The answer is the femoral triangle. Its floor is formed by the iliacus, the psoas and the pectineus.

Question 10875. Through which opening does the obturator nerve leave the pelvis, and which compartment of the thigh does it enter? The answer is the obturator canal, and it enters the medial compartment. There it supplies the adductor group, except the hamstring part of the adductor magnus. The obturator artery and vein run with it.

Question 10876. Name two structures that pass through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis, spoken as a list. The answer is the sciatic nerve and the inferior gluteal nerve. The pudendal nerve passes below the muscle as well. Only the superior gluteal nerve passes above it.

Question 10877. Which fascial sheath of the neck contains the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve, and how do the three lie within it? The answer is the carotid sheath, with the vein lateral, the artery medial and the nerve between and behind them. It is formed by all three layers of the deep cervical fascia.

Question 10878. At the root of the neck, which relation is correct: the subclavian vein lies in front of the scalenus anterior and the subclavian artery behind it, the artery lies in front of the vein, both lie behind the muscle, or both lie in front of it? The answer is that the vein lies in front and the artery behind. The brachial plexus also passes behind the muscle.

Question 10879. A described transverse section at the level of the cricoid cartilage shows a midline tube wrapped by a gland, two thick tubes beside it, and a strong layer covering the muscles in front of the spine. Which structures are shown? The answer is the trachea with the thyroid gland, the common carotid arteries and the prevertebral layer.

Question 10880. A constructed illustration: a person has numbness and tingling down the inner side of the arm and into the little finger when the arm is held overhead. Which passage is implicated? The answer is the thoracic outlet, between the first rib, the clavicle and the scalenus anterior. The lower trunk of the brachial plexus and the subclavian artery pass through it. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10881. Which of these relations within the carotid sheath is correct: the vagus nerve lies between the internal jugular vein and the common carotid artery, the vein lies medial to the artery, the nerve lies in front of both, or the sheath contains the sympathetic trunk? The answer is the first. The vein lies lateral to the artery and the sympathetic trunk lies behind the sheath.

Question 10882. In an intercostal space, in what order do the vein, the artery and the nerve lie below a rib, and where do they run? The answer is the vein highest, then the artery, then the nerve, all in the costal groove along the lower border of the rib. The rib above them shields the bundle.

Question 10883. In the free edge of the lesser omentum, which of these structures lies most posteriorly: the common bile duct, the proper hepatic artery, the portal vein, or the cystic duct? The answer is the portal vein. It lies behind the other two, with the bile duct in front and to the right and the hepatic artery in front and to the left.

Question 10884. A described dissection of the left side of the root of the neck shows a thin-walled duct ascending behind the carotid sheath and opening at the junction of two veins. Which duct is shown? The answer is the thoracic duct, which ends at the junction of the left internal jugular and the left subclavian veins. On the right a much shorter duct ends there.

Question 10885. Which of these structures crosses the ureter in the female pelvis, a relation that matters when the ureter is found at operation: the uterine artery, the internal iliac vein, the obturator nerve, or the round ligament of the uterus? The answer is the uterine artery. It crosses above and in front of the ureter beside the cervix.

Question 10886. Which structure crosses above and medial to the ureter in the male pelvis as it descends to the prostate? The answer is the ductus deferens. The two lie close together beside the seminal vesicle, and the ureter passes beneath the duct. In the female the uterine artery takes that relation.

Question 10887. Which of these relations of the left renal vein is correct: it passes in front of the aorta and behind the superior mesenteric artery, it passes behind the aorta, it drains into the inferior vena cava without crossing, or it lies behind the left kidney? The answer is the first. It receives the left suprarenal and gonadal veins.

Question 10888. The median nerve enters the forearm between the two heads of which muscle, and which branch does it give off there? The answer is the pronator teres, and the branch is the anterior interosseous nerve. That branch then runs down the front of the interosseous membrane.

Question 10889. In a described transverse section through the middle of the forearm, which nerve lies in the midline between the superficial and the deep flexor muscles? The answer is the median nerve. It descends behind the flexor digitorum superficialis and in front of the flexor digitorum profundus. The ulnar nerve lies on the medial side.

Question 10890. A constructed illustration: a person cannot bend the tip of the thumb or of the index finger, and no feeling is lost in the hand. Which nerve is affected? The answer is the anterior interosseous nerve, which arises from the median nerve between the two heads of the pronator teres. It has no cutaneous branches, so sensation is spared. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10891. Which branch of the radial nerve winds through the supinator muscle to reach the back of the forearm, and what does it supply there? The answer is the deep branch of the radial nerve, which becomes the posterior interosseous nerve. It supplies the extensor muscles of the wrist and the fingers. Its injury causes weakness without numbness.

Question 10892. Which of these structures lies within the cavernous sinus: the internal carotid artery, the optic nerve, the facial nerve, or the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the internal carotid artery, which runs through the sinus beside the sixth cranial nerve. The third and fourth cranial nerves lie in its lateral wall.

Question 10893. True or false: the hypoglossal nerve crosses the internal and the external carotid arteries on its way to the tongue. The answer is true. It descends between the internal carotid artery and the internal jugular vein, then crosses both arteries lateral to them. It finally passes deep to the posterior belly of the digastric.

Question 10894. Which of these pairs belongs together: the axillary nerve with the quadrangular space, the radial nerve with the carpal tunnel, the median nerve with the cubital tunnel, or the ulnar nerve with the spiral groove? The answer is the axillary nerve with the quadrangular space. The radial nerve runs in the spiral groove and the median nerve through the carpal tunnel.

Question 10895. Which of these relations of the facial nerve within the parotid gland is correct: it lies superficial to the retromandibular vein, it lies deep to that vein, it lies deep to the external carotid artery, or it lies medial to the styloid process? The answer is the first. The vein separates the nerve from the external carotid artery, which lies deep to it.

Question 10896. Name two structures found within the suboccipital triangle, spoken as a list. The answer is the vertebral artery and the dorsal ramus of the first cervical nerve, also called the suboccipital nerve. The triangle is bounded by the rectus capitis posterior major and the two obliquus muscles.

Question 10897. A described transverse section of the arm shows a large nerve lying on the medial side of the brachial artery. Was the section taken above or below the point where the two cross, and which nerve is it? The answer is below the crossing, and the nerve is the median nerve. Above that point the nerve lies lateral to the artery.

Question 10898. The left recurrent laryngeal nerve loops around which structure, and why is it longer than the nerve on the right? The answer is the arch of the aorta, just beyond the ligamentum arteriosum. The left fourth and sixth aortic arches persist as the arch and the ductus, so the nerve is dragged down below them. On the right the nerve hooks the right subclavian artery.

Question 10899. Which of these statements about the vertebral artery is correct: it ascends through the transverse foramina and turns backward around the atlas, it enters the skull through the jugular foramen, it lies in front of the scalenus anterior, or it is a branch of the external carotid artery? The answer is the first. It joins its fellow to form the basilar artery.

Question 10900. Which vein runs down the side of the neck, superficial to the sternocleidomastoid muscle, and becomes prominent when a person strains? The answer is the external jugular vein. It begins near the angle of the jaw and ends in the subclavian vein. It is covered only by skin and the investing layer of the deep cervical fascia.

To summarize, the deep fascia is the tough sleeve that wraps the muscles of a limb and sends intermuscular septa inward to the bone, and those septa, with the interosseous membranes and the bones, define the compartments. Each compartment has its own muscles, its own nerve and its own vessels, so the anterior compartment of the leg lifts the foot with the deep peroneal nerve, the lateral compartment turns it outward with the superficial peroneal nerve, and the posterior compartment pushes it down with the tibial nerve. The retinacula turn bony arches into closed tunnels at the wrist and the ankle, and the median nerve suffers first in the carpal tunnel while the ulnar nerve travels outside it. In the neck the deep cervical fascia divides into an investing layer, a pretracheal layer, a prevertebral layer and the carotid sheath, and the space between the alar fascia and the prevertebral fascia carries infection down into the chest. The neurovascular relationships follow the same logic: the femoral nerve lies lateral to the artery and outside the femoral sheath, the structures of the popliteal fossa run from the skin inward as nerve, vein and artery, and those behind the medial malleolus run from the tibialis posterior in front to the flexor hallucis longus behind.
