# Chapter 70 — Liver

This chapter follows the large intestine from the cecum to the anal canal and then turns to the liver, the largest gland in the body. It describes the parts of the colon and their features, the anal canal with its two sphincters and its pectinate line, and then the lobes, ligaments, segments, blood supply and relations of the liver. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. The questions are written to be answered aloud, so no figure, table or diagram is needed anywhere. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 6901. Which part of the large intestine lies below the opening of the small intestine, is the widest part of the colon, and carries the appendix on its lower end? The answer is the cecum. It lies in the right iliac fossa, below the ileocecal junction. Because it is wide and thinly walled, it distends most when the colon is blocked.

Question 6902. Which of these features marks the base of the appendix on the cecum: the confluence of the three taeniae coli, the pectinate line, the right colic flexure, or the ileocecal valve? The answer is the confluence of the three taeniae coli. The flat bands of longitudinal muscle run along the colon and meet at the appendix, so following them down leads to it.

Question 6903. Which small blind tube opens into the cecum, contains lymphoid tissue, and has no known digestive function in the adult? The answer is the appendix, also called the vermiform appendix. It arises from the posteromedial wall of the cecum, and its position varies, the retrocecal position being the most common.

Question 6904. The ascending colon is described as a retroperitoneal structure. Which structure lies immediately behind its upper part, and which organ lies above it? The answer is the right kidney behind it, with the liver above it. The ascending colon is covered by peritoneum on its front and sides only, which keeps the gut fixed.

Question 6905. A constructed illustration: a young adult has vague pain around the navel that later settles in the right lower abdomen. Which structure lies at a surface point one third of the way from the front of the hip bone to the umbilicus? The answer is the appendix. The early pain is felt near the navel because the appendix carries visceral nerve fibers.

Question 6906. A described specimen of the colon shows three flat bands of muscle, a row of fatty tags along each band, and sacculations between them. Which three features are described? The answer is the taeniae coli, the appendices epiploicae, and the haustra. The bands are shorter than the gut itself, which is why the wall puckers into haustra.

Question 6907. Name two features of the large intestine that are not found on the small intestine, spoken as a list. The answer is the taeniae coli and the haustra, with the appendices epiploicae as a third. The small intestine has a smooth continuous muscle coat and no sacculations.

Question 6908. Which flexure of the colon lies immediately below the liver and turns the ascending colon into the transverse colon: the splenic flexure, the right colic flexure, the sigmoid flexure, or the anorectal flexure? The answer is the right colic flexure, also called the hepatic flexure. It lies in the right upper abdomen, against the lower surface of the liver.

Question 6909. Which part of the large intestine has a mesentery of its own and is therefore the most mobile part of the colon? The answer is the transverse colon. Its mesocolon suspends it from the back wall, and it hangs down across the abdomen below the stomach.

Question 6910. At the splenic flexure, which organ lies immediately above the flexure, and which structure tethers the flexure to the diaphragm? The answer is the spleen above it, with the phrenicocolic ligament tethering it. The flexure lies higher than the right colic flexure and is the most fixed part of the colon.

Question 6911. True or false: the three taeniae coli of the colon continue unchanged onto the rectum. The answer is false. The bands fan out at the lower end of the sigmoid colon, and the rectum has a complete longitudinal coat instead. The fatty tags also stop there.

Question 6912. A constructed illustration: a loop of bowel twists on its own mesentery in the left lower abdomen of an older adult, and the loop is greatly distended. Which part of the colon is described, and what makes it prone to twisting? The answer is the sigmoid colon, whose long mesocolon and narrow base allow it to rotate.

Question 6913. A described computed tomography section of the lower abdomen shows a widely dilated, gas filled loop whose two limbs converge toward the left iliac fossa. Which part of the colon is shown? The answer is the sigmoid colon, twisted on its mesentery. The two limbs meeting near the left iliac fossa are the described appearance of that twist.

Question 6914. Which artery supplies the cecum, the ascending colon, and the right half of the transverse colon: the inferior mesenteric artery, the superior mesenteric artery, the internal iliac artery, or the left colic artery? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery. The boundary between its territory and that of the inferior mesenteric artery lies near the splenic flexure.

Question 6915. Which artery runs along the inner border of the colon and joins the branches of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries into one continuous channel? The answer is the marginal artery. It lies in the mesocolon close to the bowel wall, and it lets a long segment of colon survive on one feeding vessel.

Question 6916. In the left lower abdomen, which structure lies immediately behind the sigmoid colon and is at risk when the sigmoid is diseased? The answer is the left ureter, with the left iliac vessels and the piriformis muscle behind it as well. The sigmoid colon lies in front of those structures.

Question 6917. A constructed illustration: a person has bright red bleeding from above the pectinate line and feels no pain at all. Which kind of dilated vein is described? The answer is an internal hemorrhoid. The lining above the pectinate line carries visceral nerve fibers, so it is not sensitive to pain, and its veins drain into the portal system.

Question 6918. Which statement about the pectinate line is correct: above it the lining is mucosa draining to the portal vein, above it the lining is skin draining to the inferior vena cava, below it the lining is mucosa draining to the portal vein, or below it the lining is skin draining to the portal vein? The answer is mucosa above the line draining to the portal vein.

Question 6919. What is the name of the jagged line in the anal canal that marks the change from mucosa above to skin below, and which folds hang above it? The answer is the pectinate line, with the anal columns above it. The line also marks the level of the small anal valves.

Question 6920. Which muscle of the anal canal is smooth and involuntary, and which one is skeletal and under voluntary control? The answer is the internal anal sphincter, which is smooth, and the external anal sphincter, which is skeletal. The internal sphincter is a thickening of the circular muscle coat of the gut.

Question 6921. A constructed illustration: a person reports a very tender, hard, bluish swelling at the very edge of the anus after straining, and sitting is painful. Which structure is involved, and why is this lesion so tender? The answer is an external hemorrhoid, below the pectinate line, where somatic nerves make it exquisitely sensitive.

Question 6922. About how long is the anal canal, and which way does it run: about 3 to 4 centimeters, running downward and backward, about 12 centimeters, running downward and forward, about 3 to 4 centimeters, running downward and forward, or about 8 centimeters, running straight down? The answer is about 3 to 4 centimeters, running downward and backward, from the anorectal junction to the anal margin.

Question 6923. The anal canal is described as running downward and backward from the rectum. Which muscle pulls its upper part forward and so creates the anorectal angle? The answer is the puborectalis muscle. It slings around the junction from the pubis and holds the junction forward. Losing that forward pull flattens the angle.

Question 6924. Which of these pairs belongs together: the transverse colon with a mesentery of its own, the ascending colon with a mesentery of its own, the descending colon with a mesentery of its own, or the rectum with a mesentery of its own? The answer is the transverse colon. The ascending and descending colon are retroperitoneal, and the rectum lies against the sacrum.

Question 6925. Which structures lie immediately in front of the lower rectum in the male, and which lie in front of it in the female? The answer is the seminal vesicles and the prostate in the male, and the vagina and the cervix in the female. Behind the rectum lie the sacrum and the coccyx.

Question 6926. Which statement about the liver is correct: it is the largest gland in the body and lies in the right upper abdomen, it lies mainly in the left upper abdomen, it is the smallest organ of the abdomen, or it lies behind the peritoneum on the left? The answer is that it is the largest gland in the body and lies in the right upper abdomen, weighing about 1.5 kilograms.

Question 6927. Which peritoneal fold lies on the front of the liver, runs from the diaphragm and the front abdominal wall to the liver, and separates its right and left lobes? The answer is the falciform ligament. Its free lower edge carries the round ligament of the liver, and it is the fold seen running up from the umbilicus.

Question 6928. A described section of the upper abdomen shows the liver filling the right side, with a thin band running from the front abdominal wall to its upper surface and a small rounded cord below it. Which two structures are described? The answer is the falciform ligament with the round ligament in its free edge. That cord is the closed umbilical vein.

Question 6929. The round ligament of the liver lies in the free edge of which fold, and what was it before birth? The answer is the falciform ligament, and before birth it was the umbilical vein. After birth the vessel closes and becomes a fibrous cord. Inside the liver that cord joins the left branch of the portal vein.

Question 6930. Which part of the liver is not covered by peritoneum and lies directly against the diaphragm: the bare area on the posterior surface, the whole of the left lobe, the quadrate lobe, or the gallbladder bed? The answer is the bare area on the posterior surface. The coronary ligament runs around its edge, and the triangular ligaments close it off.

Question 6931. A constructed illustration: a person is struck on the lower right ribs, and blood collects in the peritoneal cavity while one part of the liver stays fixed against the diaphragm. Which ligament marks the boundary of the part of the liver that lies outside the peritoneum? The answer is the coronary ligament. The bare area between its two layers touches the diaphragm.

Question 6932. On the visceral surface of the liver, which two smaller lobes lie between the fissure for the round ligament and the fissure for the ligamentum venosum? The answer is the quadrate lobe in front and the caudate lobe behind. Both belong to the functional left lobe, and the gallbladder lies beside the quadrate lobe.

Question 6933. A described specimen shows the liver from the front, with a large lobe on the right and a smaller one on the left, divided by a pale fold. Which lobe is the larger, and which fold divides them? The answer is the right lobe, divided from the left by the falciform ligament.

Question 6934. Which statement about the caudate lobe is correct: it lies between the inferior vena cava and the fissure for the ligamentum venosum, it lies in front of the quadrate lobe, it is part of the right lobe in every scheme, or it has no blood supply of its own? The answer is that it lies between the inferior vena cava and the fissure for the ligamentum venosum. It is segment one.

Question 6935. True or false: the whole surface of the liver is covered by peritoneum. The answer is false. The bare area on the posterior surface lies directly against the diaphragm with no peritoneal covering at all. The rest of the liver is almost entirely covered by peritoneum.

Question 6936. In the porta hepatis, which structure lies most anteriorly, which lies most posteriorly, and which lies between the two? The answer is that the hepatic ducts lie most anteriorly, the portal vein most posteriorly, and the hepatic artery proper between them. The porta is the gateway through which vessels enter and leave.

Question 6937. Which veins drain the liver into the inferior vena cava and run between the segments: the portal veins, the hepatic veins, the cystic veins, or the phrenic veins? The answer is the hepatic veins. There are usually three of them, named right, middle and left, and they leave the liver on its posterior surface.

Question 6938. A described magnetic resonance image of the liver shows a vertical plane marked by a large vein running from the upper surface down toward the inferior vena cava. Which vein is shown, and what plane does it mark? The answer is the middle hepatic vein, marking the plane between the right and left halves of the liver.

Question 6939. Which large vein lies in a groove on the posterior surface of the liver, and which part of the liver is left uncovered beside it? The answer is the inferior vena cava in the groove, with the bare area beside it. The hepatic veins open into the cava along that groove.

Question 6940. Which of these describes the fundus of the gallbladder: the rounded end that projects below the liver edge at the tip of the ninth costal cartilage, the narrow end joining the cystic duct, the part fixed to the liver, or the part in the porta hepatis? The answer is the rounded end that projects below the liver edge at the tip of the ninth costal cartilage.

Question 6941. Which structure inside the cystic duct carries a series of crescent-shaped folds that keep the duct open and help to hold bile? The answer is the spiral valves of the cystic duct. They run along its inner surface, and they are a normal feature of the duct rather than true valves.

Question 6942. A constructed illustration: a person has severe pain in the right upper abdomen after a fatty meal, and the gallbladder is described as distended with a stone lodged at its neck. Which duct leaves the gallbladder at that point, and where does it run? The answer is the cystic duct, which runs backward and downward to join the common hepatic duct.

Question 6943. Which artery usually supplies the gallbladder, and from which vessel does it arise: the cystic artery from the right hepatic artery, the cystic artery from the left hepatic artery, the left gastric artery, or the superior mesenteric artery? The answer is the cystic artery from the right hepatic artery. It runs in the triangle bounded by the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the liver.

Question 6944. A described series of images of the biliary tree shows a narrow duct leaving the gallbladder, joining another duct that comes down from the liver, and the two forming one wider channel that runs down behind the first part of the duodenum. Which three ducts are shown? The answer is the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the common bile duct.

Question 6945. Which of these pairs belongs together: the hepatic portal vein with blood from the gut, the hepatic vein with blood from the gut, the hepatic artery with blood from the gut, or the cystic vein with blood from the gut? The answer is the hepatic portal vein, which carries blood from the gut to the liver. The hepatic veins carry it away.

Question 6946. Which fold of peritoneum is the only one attached to the front of the liver, and what does it contain in its free edge? The answer is the falciform ligament, which contains the round ligament of the liver in its free edge. It runs from the umbilicus and the diaphragm to the liver and separates its two lobes.

Question 6947. Which two veins unite to form the portal vein, and where: the splenic and the superior mesenteric veins behind the neck of the pancreas, the splenic and the inferior mesenteric veins in front of the pancreas, the hepatic veins at the liver, or the gastric and cystic veins at the porta? The answer is the splenic and the superior mesenteric veins behind the neck of the pancreas.

Question 6948. A described section through the upper abdomen shows the liver on the right, with a pear-shaped sac lying against its lower surface and a thick vein running up behind the liver. Which structures are shown? The answer is the gallbladder against the liver and the inferior vena cava behind it. The gallbladder lies in a fossa on the visceral surface.

Question 6949. What is the ligamentum venosum of the liver, and between which two parts of the liver does it lie? The answer is the closed ductus venosus, and it lies in the fissure between the caudate lobe and the left lobe. Before birth that channel carried blood from the umbilical vein to the inferior vena cava.

Question 6950. In the segmental scheme of the liver, how many segments are described, and which segment is the caudate lobe: eight segments, with the caudate lobe as segment one, four segments, with the caudate lobe as segment four, eight segments, with the caudate lobe as segment eight, or six segments? The answer is eight segments, with the caudate lobe as segment one.

Question 6951. Which lobe of the liver lies on its visceral surface between the gallbladder and the fissure for the round ligament? The answer is the quadrate lobe. It lies in front of the porta hepatis. It belongs to the functional left lobe, although it lies to the right of the falciform ligament.

Question 6952. A constructed illustration: a person with long-standing liver disease has dilated veins running away from the umbilicus in the falciform ligament, and fluid in the abdomen. Which veins have opened up, and why do they meet at that point? The answer is the paraumbilical veins, which link the portal vein to the veins of the abdominal wall.

Question 6953. Which of these organs lies in contact with the visceral surface of the right lobe of the liver: the right kidney, the spleen, the pancreas, or the left lung? The answer is the right kidney. The right colic flexure and the duodenum also lie against the right lobe. The spleen lies to the left of the liver and touches the stomach.

Question 6954. A described cast of the portal system shows a trunk dividing into two branches, each of which breaks into a tree of smaller branches that do not join across the boundary. What does this casting show about the segments of the liver? The answer is that each segment has its own portal branch, and the branches do not meet across the boundaries.

Question 6955. True or false: the falciform ligament marks the boundary between the right and the left halves of the liver in the functional sense. The answer is false. It separates the anatomical right and left lobes, but the functional division runs from the gallbladder fossa to the inferior vena cava. The left half of the liver includes the quadrate lobe.

Question 6956. Which fold of peritoneum runs from the liver to the lesser curvature of the stomach and to the first part of the duodenum? The answer is the lesser omentum. Its free right edge contains the portal triad and forms the front wall of the epiploic foramen, so it joins the liver to the stomach.

Question 6957. Where do the superficial lymph vessels of the liver drain: to the celiac lymph nodes, to the inguinal lymph nodes, to the axillary lymph nodes, or to the sacral lymph nodes? The answer is the celiac lymph nodes. Deep lymph vessels follow the portal tract and the hepatic veins, and some of them end in the posterior mediastinal nodes.

Question 6958. Name two peritoneal folds that attach the liver to the diaphragm and to the front abdominal wall, spoken as a list. The answer is the falciform ligament and the coronary ligament. The right and left triangular ligaments also attach the liver to the diaphragm, and the lesser omentum joins it to the stomach.

Question 6959. A described view at operation shows a pale fold running from the umbilicus up to the liver, with a firm cord in its lower free edge that disappears into the liver substance. Which two structures are shown, and what does that cord represent? The answer is the falciform ligament and the round ligament, the closed umbilical vein.

Question 6960. Which statement is correct: the left triangular ligament is formed where the layers of the coronary ligament meet on the left, the left triangular ligament contains the portal vein, the bare area lies on the front of the liver, or the bare area is covered by the lesser omentum? The answer is that the left triangular ligament is formed where the layers of the coronary ligament meet on the left.

Question 6961. What is the name of the common variant in which a tongue-like process of the right lobe of the liver extends downward below the costal margin, and whom does it usually affect? The answer is the Riedel lobe, and it is more common in women. It is a normal variant rather than a sign of disease.

Question 6962. A constructed illustration: after an infection in the upper abdomen, pus collects between the right lobe of the liver and the diaphragm, and the person feels pain at the tip of the right shoulder. Which nerve carries that pain, and where does it arise? The answer is the phrenic nerve, which arises from the third, fourth and fifth cervical segments.

Question 6963. Which nerves supply the liver: sympathetic fibers from the celiac plexus and parasympathetic fibers from the vagus nerve, only sympathetic fibers, only parasympathetic fibers, or somatic fibers from the lower intercostal nerves? The answer is sympathetic fibers from the celiac plexus and parasympathetic fibers from the vagus nerve, running with the vessels of the portal tract.

Question 6964. A described radiograph of the abdomen shows a large soft tissue shadow filling the upper right side, with the lower ribs crossing it and the diaphragm above it. Which organ forms that shadow, and where does its upper border lie? The answer is the liver, whose upper border reaches the fifth rib, with its lower border along the costal margin.

Question 6965. Which pouch of the peritoneal cavity lies between the right lobe of the liver and the right kidney, and why does it matter in a person lying face upward? The answer is the hepatorenal pouch, which is then the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity. Fluid therefore collects there first, above the right colic flexure.

Question 6966. Which of these best describes the hepatorenal pouch: the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity when a person lies face upward, the space behind the peritoneum around the kidney, the space inside the lesser omentum, or the groove beside the inferior vena cava? The answer is the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity when a person lies face upward.

Question 6967. Which vessel carries the greater part of the blood flow to the liver, and which carries the greater part of its oxygen: the portal vein and the hepatic artery proper, the hepatic artery proper and the portal vein, the hepatic veins and the portal vein, or the inferior vena cava and the hepatic artery? The answer is the portal vein for flow and the hepatic artery proper for oxygen.

Question 6968. Which of these pairs belongs together: the right lobe with the right colic flexure, the left lobe with the right kidney, the caudate lobe with the gallbladder, or the quadrate lobe with the spleen? The answer is the right lobe with the right colic flexure. The right kidney also lies against the right lobe, while the left lobe lies against the stomach.

Question 6969. Which lobe of the liver is the largest: the right lobe, the left lobe, the caudate lobe, or the quadrate lobe? The answer is the right lobe. It forms most of the upper surface and the whole of the right side of the organ, while the left lobe crosses the midline into the epigastrium.

Question 6970. A described view of the liver from below shows the left lobe lying across the midline, with the stomach and the esophagus behind it and the lesser omentum running from its edge. Which lobe is shown, and which two organs lie against it? The answer is the left lobe, with the stomach and the esophagus against it, reaching the esophageal opening.

Question 6971. True or false: the liver is the largest gland in the body and receives blood from two sources. The answer is true. It receives blood through the portal vein and through the hepatic artery proper, and the portal vein carries the greater part of the flow while the artery carries the greater part of the oxygen.

Question 6972. Which statement about the hepatic veins is correct: they run between the segments and open into the inferior vena cava, they run inside each segment beside the portal branch, they open into the portal vein, or they drain into the stomach? The answer is that they run between the segments and open into the inferior vena cava. There are usually three of them.

Question 6973. Which segment of the liver lies between the inferior vena cava behind and the fissure for the ligamentum venosum in front, and why is it treated separately at operation? The answer is the caudate lobe, which is segment one. It receives branches from both the right and the left portal veins, and its own small veins drain straight into the inferior vena cava.

Question 6974. A constructed illustration: a fall breaks the ninth and tenth ribs on the right, and the person becomes pale with pain in the right upper abdomen. Which organ lies immediately behind those ribs and is the one most likely to be torn? The answer is the liver, whose right lobe lies under the right dome of the diaphragm behind the lower ribs.

Question 6975. Which fold carries the closed umbilical vein to the liver, and into which vessel does it run there: the falciform ligament into the left portal branch, the lesser omentum into the right portal branch, the coronary ligament into the hepatic vein, or the round ligament into the inferior vena cava? The answer is the falciform ligament, with the remnant running into the left portal branch.

Question 6976. A described magnetic resonance image of the liver shows eight territories, each outlined by the planes of the hepatic veins and supplied at its center by a branch of the portal vein. Which scheme is being shown, and what does each territory have of its own? The answer is the segmental scheme, in which each segment has its own portal branch.

Question 6977. During a deep breath in, the diaphragm descends. Which way does the liver move, and by about how much? The answer is downward, by a few centimeters. The liver is held against the diaphragm by the coronary ligament and by the pressure within the abdomen. A liver that does not move with breathing is abnormal.

Question 6978. Which space lies immediately above the right dome of the diaphragm and above the part of the liver that reaches highest, and why can a wound of the lower chest reach the liver through it? The answer is the right pleural recess, separated from the liver by the diaphragm alone. The pleura therefore rises higher than the liver does.

Question 6979. Which pear-shaped sac lies on the visceral surface of the liver, between the quadrate lobe and the right lobe, and stores bile? The answer is the gallbladder. It lies in a fossa on the visceral surface and is covered by peritoneum on its lower surface. Its fundus projects below the liver edge.

Question 6980. Which of these pairs belongs together: the bare area with the diaphragm, the bare area with the stomach, the bare area with the portal vein, or the bare area with the gallbladder? The answer is the bare area with the diaphragm. It is the part of the liver not covered by peritoneum, lying directly against the diaphragm within the coronary ligament.

Question 6981. Where does the liver lie for the most part, and where is its lower border in the midclavicular line: the right hypochondrium, with the lower border at the costal margin, the epigastrium, with the lower border at the umbilicus, the left hypochondrium, or the right lumbar region? The answer is the right hypochondrium, with the lower border at the costal margin.

Question 6982. A described computed tomography section of the upper abdomen shows a large organ on the right, with the lower ribs and the diaphragm around it and a small round sac against its inner surface. Which organ and which sac are shown? The answer is the liver and the gallbladder, the liver lying under the right dome of the diaphragm.

Question 6983. The fundus of the gallbladder is described as projecting below the lower border of the liver. At which surface point does it appear, and what lies beside it? The answer is the tip of the ninth costal cartilage, where the right linea semilunaris meets the costal margin. The fundus touches the front abdominal wall there.

Question 6984. Which of these describes the cystic duct: it runs from the neck of the gallbladder to join the common hepatic duct and contains spiral folds, it drains the liver directly, it carries bile into the duodenum on its own, or it joins the portal vein? The answer is that it runs from the neck of the gallbladder to join the common hepatic duct and contains spiral folds.

Question 6985. In which fold of peritoneum does the common bile duct run on its way to the duodenum, and against which organ does it lie? The answer is the free edge of the lesser omentum, and it lies against the back of the first part of the duodenum and then in a groove on the pancreas. It ends at the hepatopancreatic ampulla.

Question 6986. Name the three structures that make up the portal triad, spoken as a list. The answer is the portal vein, the hepatic artery proper and the bile duct. They run together in the free edge of the lesser omentum and enter the liver at the porta hepatis. The portal vein lies behind the other two.

Question 6987. Which statement about the position of the liver is correct: its upper border reaches the fifth rib in the midclavicular line and it moves with breathing, it is fixed and does not move with breathing, its upper border lies at the umbilicus, or it lies below the costal margin? The answer is that its upper border reaches the fifth rib in the midclavicular line and it moves with breathing.

Question 6988. A described section of the upper abdomen shows the liver and the gallbladder in front, a thick vein immediately behind the liver, and the right kidney below and behind. Which vein is shown, and what groove does it occupy? The answer is the inferior vena cava, in the groove on the posterior surface of the liver.

Question 6989. True or false: the gallbladder lies behind the peritoneum and is fixed to the back wall of the abdomen. The answer is false. It lies in a fossa on the visceral surface of the liver and is covered by peritoneum on its lower surface. It moves with the liver and not with the abdominal wall.

Question 6990. Which of these carries oxygen-rich blood to the liver: the hepatic artery proper, the portal vein, the hepatic vein, or the inferior vena cava? The answer is the hepatic artery proper. The portal vein carries blood that is rich in nutrients but lower in oxygen, and the hepatic veins carry blood away from the liver.

Question 6991. Which lobe of the liver lies behind the porta hepatis, between the fissure for the ligamentum venosum and the groove for the inferior vena cava, and drains into the inferior vena cava by its own small veins? The answer is the caudate lobe. It is the only lobe with that independent drainage, and it is segment one.

Question 6992. Which of these pairs belongs together: the round ligament with the closed umbilical vein, the round ligament with the closed ductus venosus, the ligamentum venosum with the closed umbilical vein, or the falciform ligament with the portal vein? The answer is the round ligament with the closed umbilical vein. The ligamentum venosum is the closed ductus venosus.

Question 6993. From which germ layer does the liver develop, and in which fold does it grow: the endoderm of the foregut in the ventral mesentery, the ectoderm of the surface in the dorsal mesentery, the mesoderm of the somites, or the endoderm of the hindgut? The answer is the endoderm of the foregut, growing in the ventral mesentery, which becomes the falciform ligament and the lesser omentum.

Question 6994. A described specimen of a liver shows a firm organ with a finely nodular surface and a shrunken right lobe. Which organ is shown, and what does the nodular surface indicate about the normal smooth architecture? The answer is the liver, and the nodules replace the normal smooth lobules. This is the described appearance of long-standing liver disease.

Question 6995. Which structures lie immediately above and immediately behind the right lobe of the liver? The answer is the diaphragm above and the right kidney behind and below it. The right colic flexure and the duodenum also lie against its visceral surface. The right lung lies above the diaphragm.

Question 6996. Which parts of the liver receive blood from the left branch of the portal vein: the left lobe along with the quadrate lobe, the whole right lobe, the gallbladder bed alone, or the bare area only? The answer is the left lobe along with the quadrate lobe. The functional left half of the liver therefore does not match the anatomical left lobe.

Question 6997. A constructed illustration: a person becomes yellow, and imaging shows a dilated duct running down from the liver and stopping in a groove on the back of the pancreas. Which duct is obstructed, and which two ducts formed it? The answer is the common bile duct, formed by the common hepatic duct and the cystic duct. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6998. Which artery supplies the gallbladder, and in which triangle does it run? The answer is the cystic artery, running in the triangle bounded by the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the lower surface of the liver. It usually arises from the right hepatic artery.

Question 6999. Which plane separates the right half of the liver from the left half in the functional sense: the plane running from the gallbladder fossa to the inferior vena cava, the plane of the falciform ligament, the plane of the lesser omentum, or the plane of the coronary ligament? The answer is the plane running from the gallbladder fossa to the inferior vena cava.

Question 7000. Which veins open into the inferior vena cava just below the diaphragm after running between the segments of the liver: the right, middle and left hepatic veins, the portal veins, the cystic veins, or the phrenic veins? The answer is the right, middle and left hepatic veins, whose openings lie on the posterior surface. The portal vein enters at the porta hepatis instead.

To summarize, the colon is marked by the taeniae coli, the haustra and the appendices epiploicae, and the transverse colon alone carries a mesentery of its own. The ascending colon lies against the right kidney and below the liver, the splenic flexure is tethered by the phrenicocolic ligament, and the marginal artery joins the territories of the two mesenteric arteries. Above the pectinate line the lining is mucosa draining to the portal vein and the internal anal sphincter is smooth, while below the line the lining is skin and the external sphincter is skeletal. The liver is the largest gland, the right lobe is by far the largest, the falciform ligament carries the round ligament, the bare area against the diaphragm is bounded by the coronary ligament, and the portal triad runs with ducts in front, artery between and portal vein behind. Its functional halves are divided by the plane from the gallbladder fossa to the inferior vena cava, its eight segments are separated by the hepatic veins and centered on portal branches, and the caudate lobe is segment one.
