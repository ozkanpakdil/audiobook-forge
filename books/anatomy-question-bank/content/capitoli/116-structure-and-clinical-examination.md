# Chapter 116 — Structure and Clinical Examination

The last chapter of this bank brings structure and the bedside together, because the anatomy a clinician can name is the anatomy a clinician can find. It covers the links between structure and symptoms, and then the examination itself, from inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation to the special tests that put a named structure under strain. It holds one hundred questions, and every one gives its answer immediately and then explains why it is right, so nothing has to be held in mind. The questions are written to be answered aloud, in the order in which a listener would work through a patient. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 11501. A constructed illustration: blood or infection under the diaphragm irritates its underside, and the person feels an ache at the tip of the shoulder. Which nerve carries that pain, and why is it felt there? The answer is the phrenic nerve, from the third, fourth and fifth cervical spinal nerves. Those segments also supply the skin over the shoulder, so the brain refers the signal there.

Question 11502. A person describes pain in the upper right abdomen that spreads to the right shoulder blade after a meal. Which of these structures lies beneath that pain: the gallbladder, the stomach, the appendix, or the left kidney? The answer is the gallbladder. It rests on the undersurface of the liver against the diaphragm, whose irritation refers pain to the shoulder.

Question 11503. A person reports a cramping pain in the calf that comes on after walking a certain distance and eases with rest. Narrowing of which artery most often explains it? The answer is the superficial femoral artery. It passes through the adductor canal, where the muscles around it compress the artery, so the calf is starved of blood only while it works.

Question 11504. A constructed illustration: a disk between the fourth and fifth lumbar vertebrae presses on a nerve root, and pain shoots down the outer side of the thigh and leg to the top of the foot. Which nerve root is compressed? The answer is the fifth lumbar root, which leaves the spine at that level and joins the sciatic nerve.

Question 11505. Early in appendicitis the pain is a dull ache around the navel rather than in the right lower abdomen. The fibers that carry it travel with which nerve, and where do they enter the spinal cord? The answer is the tenth thoracic spinal nerve. The appendix belongs to the midgut, whose pain is referred to the tenth thoracic dermatome, the band of skin around the navel.

Question 11506. A person has a sharp pain in the lower chest that worsens on a deep breath and on coughing. Irritation of which structure explains it: the lung tissue, the visceral pleura, the parietal pleura, or the diaphragm? The answer is the parietal pleura. It carries somatic pain fibers, while the visceral pleura covering the lung has none of its own.

Question 11507. A stone blocks the duct that carries bile from the liver and the gallbladder to the duodenum, and the person turns yellow. Which structure is obstructed? The answer is the common bile duct. It is formed where the common hepatic duct joins the cystic duct and opens into the duodenum beside the pancreatic duct.

Question 11508. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the outer side of the knee, the person cannot lift the front of the foot and the top of the foot is numb. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the common fibular nerve, winding around the neck of the fibula. It lies just beneath the skin there, against bone, with no muscle to shield it.

Question 11509. True or false: pain arising in the wall of the gut is poorly localized and is usually felt in the midline of the abdomen. The answer is true. The gut is supplied by autonomic nerves that carry little information about position, so the pain is referred to the midline. Pain from the peritoneum lining the wall is sharp and localizes well instead.

Question 11510. A described radiograph of the hip shows a break across the neck of the femur, and the limb lies short and turned outward. Which structure has been divided, and why does the limb shorten? The answer is the neck of the femur. The break lies inside the capsule, and the muscles around the hip draw the shaft upward while the foot rotates outward.

Question 11511. A constructed illustration: an older person becomes hoarse, and a growth is found at the top of the left lung. Which nerve is pressed, and why is the left side more often affected? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve. It hooks under the arch of the aorta before climbing to the larynx, so a growth at the left apex traps it against the aorta.

Question 11512. An enlarged left atrium can press on a muscular tube that runs down behind the heart, and the person reports that food sticks on swallowing. Which structure is compressed? The answer is the esophagus. It descends through the posterior mediastinum behind the left atrium, and its upper part lies behind the trachea.

Question 11513. After a blow to the front of the head, a person can no longer smell, although the nose is clear. Injury to which structure explains the loss? The answer is the cribriform plate of the ethmoid bone, which carries the fibers of the olfactory nerve from the nose to the brain. Those fine fibers tear when the plate fractures.

Question 11514. Which of these pairs belongs together: flank pain with a stone in the ureter, flank pain with a stone in the gallbladder, shoulder pain with a stone in the ureter, or navel pain with a stone in the gallbladder? The answer is flank pain with a stone in the ureter. A stone in the ureter blocks the urine and stretches the renal pelvis, and the pain is felt in the loin.

Question 11515. Name two structures that a growth in the head of the pancreas commonly presses on, spoken as a list. The answer is the common bile duct and the main pancreatic duct. Both run through the head of the gland and join at the ampulla before opening into the duodenum, so one is usually obstructed with the other.

Question 11516. A stone descending from the kidney lodges where the ureter is narrowed by a vessel it crosses. Which of these is that point: where it crosses the common iliac artery, where it lies against the psoas muscle, where the gonadal vein joins it, or where it passes behind the ductus deferens? The answer is where it crosses the common iliac artery at the brim of the pelvis.

Question 11517. In one illustration, blood collects above the tentorium of the cerebellum and stretches the dura there, and the person feels the pain across the forehead. Which nerve carries the pain from that part of the dura? The answer is the trigeminal nerve. Its branches supply that dura, and the pain is referred to the forehead and the eye.

Question 11518. A described photograph shows the muscles at the base of the thumb wasted, the outer three and a half fingers numb, with the little finger spared. Which nerve is compressed, and where does it lie? The answer is the median nerve in the carpal tunnel at the wrist. It lies beneath the retinaculum with the flexor tendons, while the ulnar nerve passes outside the tunnel.

Question 11519. True or false: cramping pain in the calf that appears with walking and disappears with rest points to disease of the deep veins of the leg. The answer is false. That pain comes from narrowing of the arteries, most often the superficial femoral artery in the adductor canal. Venous disease gives a heavy ache, worse at the end of the day and eased by raising the leg.

Question 11520. In one illustration, an older person describes a deep boring pain in the middle of the back for some weeks. Which of these structures can produce it when its wall stretches: the abdominal aorta, the inferior vena cava, the portal vein, or the stomach? The answer is the abdominal aorta. It lies against the vertebral column, and the stretching of its wall is felt in the back.

Question 11521. A constructed illustration: a person under strain feels a heavy pain behind the breastbone that spreads to the inner side of the left arm. The fibers of the heart enter the spinal cord at which segments, and why is the pain felt in the arm? The answer is the upper thoracic spinal nerves. Those segments also receive the skin of the inner arm, so the signal is referred there.

Question 11522. A person with a stone in the ureter feels an intense ache in the loin that moves down and forward into the groin. Which spinal segments carry that pain? The answer is the tenth thoracic to the first lumbar segments. The kidney and the ureter are supplied by the least and lumbar splanchnic nerves, whose fibers enter at those levels, so the pain follows their dermatomes.

Question 11523. A burning pain rises behind the breastbone after meals and is worse when lying flat. Which structure is being irritated? The answer is the esophagus. It runs down through the posterior mediastinum, pierces the diaphragm in front of the aorta, and its lowest part lies within the abdomen.

Question 11524. A person has pain in the right loin that travels down and forward toward the groin. Describe the direction of that spread using the words superior, inferior, lateral and anterior. The answer is that it spreads inferiorly and anteriorly, from the loin above and behind to the groin below and in front.

Question 11525. A constructed illustration: during removal of lymph nodes from the armpit a nerve on the side of the chest wall is divided, and afterwards the shoulder blade stands out from the back when the arm is pushed forward. Which nerve was divided? The answer is the long thoracic nerve, which supplies the serratus anterior. Without that muscle the medial border of the scapula lifts from the chest wall.

Question 11526. Which ridge is felt across the front of the chest at the level of the second costal cartilage, and which vertebra lies at the same level behind? The answer is the sternal angle, with the disk between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae. It marks where the manubrium joins the body of the sternum, and where the second rib is counted.

Question 11527. When the radial pulse is felt at the wrist, which of these lies immediately medial to the artery: the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis, the tendon of the palmaris longus, the ulnar artery, or the median nerve? The answer is the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis. The artery lies lateral to it, against the lower end of the radius, where it can be pressed against bone.

Question 11528. The iliac crest is used as a surface landmark for the level of which vertebra, and what is the name of the line that joins the two crests? The answer is the fourth lumbar vertebra, and the line is the supracristal plane. It is the level at which the spinal cord has ended and only the roots of the cauda equina remain.

Question 11529. Where exactly is the apex beat of the heart felt, and which chamber produces it? The answer is the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line, about 9 centimeters from the midline, produced by the left ventricle. It is the lowest and most lateral point at which the heart meets the chest wall.

Question 11530. Which of these lies at the level of the second thoracic vertebra: the suprasternal notch, the sternal angle, the xiphisternal joint, or the apex of the heart? The answer is the suprasternal notch. It is the hollow at the top of the sternum between the clavicles, and the trachea is felt just above it.

Question 11531. The xiphisternal joint, felt at the lower end of the sternum, lies at the level of which vertebra, and which two structures lie just below it? The answer is the ninth thoracic vertebra, with the central tendon of the diaphragm and the upper border of the liver below.

Question 11532. True or false: the cricoid cartilage lies at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra, where the pharynx becomes the esophagus. The answer is true. The cricoid is the only complete ring of the airway and the landmark for that level. The thyroid cartilage above it lies at the fourth and fifth cervical vertebrae.

Question 11533. Which of these pairs belongs together: the sternal angle with the second costal cartilage, the suprasternal notch with the third costal cartilage, the xiphisternal joint with the seventh cervical vertebra, or the iliac crest with the second lumbar vertebra? The answer is the sternal angle with the second costal cartilage. Counting ribs from that ridge is reliable, because the first rib lies hidden behind the clavicle.

Question 11534. Which of these points marks the surface position of the gallbladder: where the lateral border of the rectus sheath meets the costal margin, where the midline meets the costal margin, 5 centimeters below the xiphisternal joint, or halfway from the umbilicus to the anterior superior iliac spine? The answer is where the lateral border of the rectus sheath meets the costal margin, at the ninth costal cartilage.

Question 11535. A point is marked one third of the way along the line from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus. Which structure lies deep to that point, and how constant is its position? The answer is the base of the appendix, at McBurney point. The base is fairly constant there, though the tip may hang into the pelvis or lie behind the cecum.

Question 11536. In the popliteal fossa behind the knee, which structure lies deepest against the back of the joint, and which lies most superficial? The answer is the popliteal artery deepest and the tibial nerve most superficial, with the popliteal vein between them. The small saphenous vein runs on the surface above the deep fascia.

Question 11537. Which heart valve is heard best at the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line: the aortic, the pulmonary, the tricuspid, or the mitral? The answer is the mitral valve. Its sound is conducted to the apex, which points downward and to the left.

Question 11538. A described chest radiograph taken from behind shows the right border of the heart formed by one chamber and most of the left border formed by another. Which chambers are they? The answer is the right atrium on the right border and the left ventricle on the left. The left atrium contributes only a small part of the left border below the pulmonary artery.

Question 11539. A constructed illustration: the examiner presses deeply into the left lower abdomen and then lifts the hand away quickly, and the person winces. Which structure is being stretched, and what is the sign called? The answer is the parietal peritoneum, and the sign is rebound tenderness. The parietal peritoneum is supplied by somatic nerves, so it is acutely sensitive to stretch.

Question 11540. Percussion over normal lung gives a resonant note and over the liver a dull one. Which of these gives a stony dull note: normal lung, the liver, the stomach filled with gas, or fluid in the pleural cavity? The answer is fluid in the pleural cavity. Fluid transmits sound poorly, so it damps the note far more than solid liver does.

Question 11541. At the suprasternal notch the trachea is felt in the midline. Which structure lies immediately behind the trachea at that level? The answer is the esophagus. It begins at the lower border of the cricoid cartilage and descends behind the trachea in the visceral compartment of the neck. The thyroid gland wraps around the front and sides of the airway above.

Question 11542. Which pulse is felt on the top of the foot, lateral to the tendon that runs to the big toe? The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery. It lies against the bones of the foot and is often the easiest pulse to find when the ankle pulses are faint.

Question 11543. Behind the medial malleolus, which structure lies immediately against the bone, first in the line that passes beneath the flexor retinaculum? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior muscle. The tendons of the flexor digitorum longus and the flexor hallucis longus, the posterior tibial artery and the tibial nerve follow behind.

Question 11544. Name the two borders of the femoral triangle that meet at its apex, spoken as a list. The answer is the medial border, the adductor longus, and the lateral border, the sartorius. The inguinal ligament forms the base above, and the iliopsoas and the pectineus form the floor.

Question 11545. A described ultrasound image of the neck shows a gland shaped like a butterfly, wrapped around the front and the sides of the airway just below the larynx. Which gland is it, and which structure does it lie directly in front of? The answer is the thyroid gland, lying in front of the trachea and the lower larynx. Its isthmus crosses the midline over the second and third tracheal rings.

Question 11546. Which muscle is tested when a person turns the head to one side against the examiner's hand, and which cranial nerve supplies it? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid, supplied by the eleventh cranial nerve. The same nerve supplies the trapezius, tested by shrugging the shoulders against resistance.

Question 11547. Which of these muscles is tested when the examiner asks the person to screw up the eyes tightly: the orbicularis oculi, the orbicularis oris, the levator palpebrae superioris, or the frontalis? The answer is the orbicularis oculi, supplied by the seventh cranial nerve. That nerve supplies every muscle of facial expression, and its weakness leaves the eye open.

Question 11548. Which structure is tested by the anterior drawer test at the knee? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament. The examiner pulls the upper end of the tibia forward with the knee bent, and movement beyond that of the other knee means it has torn.

Question 11549. Which of these muscles is weak when the pelvis drops on the side opposite the standing leg: the gluteus medius, the gluteus maximus, the iliopsoas, or the adductor longus? The answer is the gluteus medius of the standing side, supplied by the superior gluteal nerve. It holds the pelvis level while the opposite foot is off the ground.

Question 11550. Where is the common carotid pulse felt in the neck, and which muscle forms the guide to it? The answer is at the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid at the level of the upper border of the thyroid cartilage. It lies in the carotid sheath there, with the internal jugular vein lateral and the vagus nerve behind.

Question 11551. Which cranial nerve is tested by asking the person to push the tongue against the inside of the cheek while the examiner presses on the outside? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, which supplies the muscles of the tongue. It is also tested by asking the person to protrude the tongue and move it from side to side.

Question 11552. Which muscle is tested by asking the person to press the index finger outward against the examiner's hand, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the first dorsal interosseous, supplied by the ulnar nerve. That nerve also supplies the other small muscles of the hand, so wasting between the thumb and the index finger follows its injury.

Question 11553. A described lateral radiograph of the neck shows the first cervical vertebra with no body and no spine, and a tooth-shaped process rising from the second into it. Name the two vertebrae and the process. The answer is the atlas, the axis, and the odontoid process, or dens, of the axis. The atlas is a ring, and the dens is the pivot on which the head turns.

Question 11554. Which of these muscles begins abduction of the arm at the shoulder, carrying it through the first 15 degrees: the supraspinatus, the deltoid, the teres minor, or the infraspinatus? The answer is the supraspinatus, supplied by the suprascapular nerve. It runs above the joint beneath the acromion, where its tendon wears easily.

Question 11555. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the outstretched hand there is tenderness in the hollow at the base of the thumb. Which bone is fractured, and why is the break easily missed? The answer is the scaphoid. It lies in that hollow, and its blood supply enters at the far end, so a break near the wrist can starve the fragment nearest the forearm.

Question 11556. A person is asked to stand on tiptoe on one foot. Which muscles produce the movement, and which nerve root is being tested? The answer is the gastrocnemius and the soleus, with the first sacral nerve root. They share the calcaneal tendon, and the same root carries the ankle jerk.

Question 11557. Which structure is put under strain by the McMurray test, in which the examiner rotates the bent knee while straightening it: the meniscus of the knee, the anterior cruciate ligament, the medial collateral ligament, or the patellar tendon? The answer is the meniscus of the knee. A torn meniscus clicks or traps between the bones as the joint rotates.

Question 11558. True or false: when the abdomen is examined, auscultation is performed before palpation and percussion. The answer is true. Palpation and percussion stir the bowel and change the sounds the examiner is trying to hear, and they may cause discomfort that makes the rest harder.

Question 11559. A described transverse section through the middle of the arm shows the humerus with a nerve lying in a groove on its back. Which nerve is it, and what does injury at that level cause? The answer is the radial nerve in the spiral groove. It supplies the muscles that extend the wrist and fingers, so a break of the humeral shaft there causes wrist drop.

Question 11560. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior drawer test with the anterior cruciate ligament, the McMurray test with the medial collateral ligament, the Trendelenburg sign with the gluteus maximus, or the straight leg raise with the hip flexors? The answer is the anterior drawer test with the anterior cruciate ligament. The examiner pulls the tibia forward and compares the knees.

Question 11561. A described photograph of the back shows two dimples over the lower part of the spine, one on each side. Which bony points do they mark, and at which vertebral level do they lie? The answer is the posterior superior iliac spines, at the level of the second sacral segment.

Question 11562. Which structure lies immediately in front of the right kidney, and which three lie in front of the left kidney? The answer is the liver in front of the right kidney, with the duodenum and ascending colon beside it, and the stomach, spleen and pancreas in front of the left. Both are retroperitoneal, so their anterior surfaces face the peritoneal cavity.

Question 11563. Percussion over the spleen is dull. Between which ribs does the organ lie in the midaxillary line: the fifth and seventh, the seventh and ninth, the ninth and eleventh, or the eleventh and twelfth? The answer is between the ninth and eleventh ribs. The spleen lies behind the stomach and below the diaphragm.

Question 11564. A constructed illustration: a swelling appears in the groin below the inguinal ligament and lateral to the pubic tubercle. Through which opening has it passed, and which vessel lies immediately lateral to that opening? The answer is the femoral canal, with the femoral vein lateral to it. The canal is the most medial part of the femoral sheath and the narrowest passage into the thigh.

Question 11565. Which of these glands moves upward when a person swallows and can be seen to do so: the thyroid gland, the parotid gland, the submandibular gland, or the tonsil? The answer is the thyroid gland. It is bound to the larynx by fascia and rises with it during swallowing. The other three do not move in that way.

Question 11566. Which structure is felt as a rounded cord running from the pubic tubercle toward the scrotum, and which structures does it contain? The answer is the spermatic cord, containing the ductus deferens, the testicular artery, the pampiniform plexus of veins and the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve. It carries the coverings of the abdominal wall down with it.

Question 11567. Which part of the breast extends into the armpit and must be examined as part of the breast: the axillary tail, the areola, the lobules, or the lactiferous ducts? The answer is the axillary tail. It passes through an opening in the deep fascia at the lower border of the pectoralis major, and it can be mistaken for a lump in the armpit.

Question 11568. Which of these structures lies at the level of the third cervical vertebra: the hyoid bone, the thyroid cartilage, the cricoid cartilage, or the sternal angle? The answer is the hyoid bone. It can be felt between the chin and the thyroid cartilage, and moves upward when a person swallows.

Question 11569. A described dissection of the sole of the foot shows a thick band of tough tissue running from the heel to the bases of the toes, with the short muscles lying deep to it. Which structure is it, and what does it do? The answer is the plantar aponeurosis. It holds up the arch of the foot and protects the vessels and nerves beneath it.

Question 11570. A described radiograph of the pelvis shows a large oval opening on each side, below and in front of the hip socket. Name the opening, and say which structures pass through it. The answer is the obturator foramen, closed by a membrane, through which the obturator nerve and vessels leave the pelvis. It is the largest opening in the skeleton.

Question 11571. Which reflex is tested by tapping the tendon just below the kneecap, and which two nerve roots carry it? The answer is the knee jerk, carried by the third and fourth lumbar nerve roots. The tap stretches the quadriceps muscle, and the muscle contracts in reply.

Question 11572. Which of these reflexes is lost when the first sacral nerve root is compressed: the knee jerk, the ankle jerk, the biceps jerk, or the abdominal reflex? The answer is the ankle jerk. The first sacral root supplies the calf muscles through the tibial nerve, which the calcaneal tendon tap tests.

Question 11573. In the normal plantar reflex the toes curl downward when the sole is stroked. Which of these tracts carries that response: the corticospinal tract, the spinothalamic tract, the dorsal columns, or the spinocerebellar tract? The answer is the corticospinal tract, whose fibers run from the motor cortex to the spinal cord.

Question 11574. Which muscles are tested when the examiner asks the person to spread the fingers apart against resistance, and which nerve supplies them? The answer is the dorsal interossei, supplied by the ulnar nerve. Between the metacarpal bones they pull the fingers away from the midline of the hand.

Question 11575. Which of these muscles is tested by asking the person to look outward: the lateral rectus, the medial rectus, the superior rectus, or the inferior oblique? The answer is the lateral rectus, supplied by the sixth cranial nerve. It turns the eye away from the midline, so weakness on one side leaves that eye turned inward.

Question 11576. Which muscle raises the upper eyelid, and which cranial nerve supplies it? The answer is the levator palpebrae superioris, supplied by the third cranial nerve. The same nerve supplies most of the other muscles that move the eye and carries the fibers that narrow the pupil.

Question 11577. Which two cranial nerves are tested together when the examiner strokes the back of the throat and watches the palate rise? The answer is the ninth and the tenth cranial nerves. The ninth carries sensation from the back of the throat, and the tenth supplies the palate and pharynx.

Question 11578. Which two cranial nerves take part in the corneal reflex, one carrying sensation and the other closing the eye: the fifth and the seventh, the second and the third, the ninth and the tenth, or the eleventh and the twelfth? The answer is the fifth and the seventh. The ophthalmic division of the trigeminal nerve carries sensation, and the facial nerve closes the eyelids.

Question 11579. Which muscle is tested by asking the person to turn the foot up against resistance, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the tibialis anterior, supplied by the deep fibular nerve. It arises from the upper tibia and runs down to the inner side of the foot, where its tendon can be seen and felt.

Question 11580. Loss of which movement at the elbow suggests compression of the seventh cervical nerve root: extension of the elbow, flexion of the elbow, turning the palm upward, or bending the wrist? The answer is extension of the elbow, produced by the triceps muscle. The seventh cervical root supplies it through the radial nerve and carries the triceps jerk.

Question 11581. Which of these pairs belongs together: the gag reflex with the ninth and tenth cranial nerves, the corneal reflex with the fifth and the twelfth, the knee jerk with the fifth lumbar root, or the ankle jerk with the fourth lumbar root? The answer is the gag reflex with the ninth and tenth cranial nerves. The other three pair each test with the wrong structure.

Question 11582. A described computed tomography section of the abdomen at the level of the kidneys shows both organs against the posterior wall, with the aorta and the inferior vena cava in front of the spine. Which structure lies in front of the left kidney but not of the right? The answer is the pancreas, crossing the midline in front of the left kidney and its vessels. The liver takes its place on the right.

Question 11583. A described magnetic resonance image of the knee from the side shows two ligaments crossing in the middle of the joint, one in front and one behind. Which is which? The answer is that the anterior cruciate ligament lies in front and the posterior cruciate ligament behind. They cross and stop the bones of the leg from sliding apart.

Question 11584. A described oblique radiograph of the lower back shows a break through the narrow bridge of bone in the arch of the fifth lumbar vertebra. Which structure has broken, and what is the defect called? The answer is the pars interarticularis, and the defect is spondylolysis. If both sides are affected, the body of the vertebra can slip forward.

Question 11585. A described ultrasound image of the neck shows two tubes lying side by side beneath the sternocleidomastoid, one thin walled and easily flattened, the other thick walled and pulsating. Which is which, and which lies lateral? The answer is the internal jugular vein lateral to the common carotid artery. The vein lies superficial to the artery and is easily flattened by the probe.

Question 11586. A described dissection of the upper thigh shows a triangle whose base is the inguinal ligament, with three structures lying in a row within a fibrous sheath. Which of the three lies most lateral? The answer is the femoral artery, lying most lateral within the sheath. The vein lies next to it, the femoral canal most medially, and the femoral nerve outside the sheath altogether.

Question 11587. As the subclavian artery and the subclavian vein cross the first rib, which of the two lies in front, and which structure separates them? The answer is the subclavian vein, which lies in front of the artery. The anterior scalene muscle separates them, and the brachial plexus passes behind the artery.

Question 11588. A constructed illustration: a deep cut over the inner side of the elbow just behind the bony point is followed by clawing of the hand and wasting of its small muscles. Which nerve was cut? The answer is the ulnar nerve, which lies in a groove behind the medial epicondyle. It supplies most of the small muscles of the hand, so the fingers claw and the little finger cannot be pulled inward.

Question 11589. True or false: when a person stands on the left leg and the right side of the pelvis drops, the weak gluteus medius is on the right side. The answer is false. The weak muscle is on the left, the standing side, because it should hold the opposite side of the pelvis up.

Question 11590. Which of these pairs belongs together: weakness of the supraspinatus with inability to begin abduction, weakness of the deltoid with the same inability, loss of the ankle jerk with compression of the fourth lumbar root, or loss of the knee jerk with a first sacral compression? The answer is weakness of the supraspinatus. It carries the arm through the first 15 degrees, and the deltoid then lifts it further.

Question 11591. Name the three structures that lie within the carotid sheath in the neck, spoken as a list. The answer is the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve. The sheath runs from the base of the skull to the root of the neck, and within it the artery lies medial, the vein lateral, and the nerve behind.

Question 11592. A person lying face upward is asked first to turn the head to one side and then to lift it forward. State which movement occurs about a longitudinal axis and which about a transverse axis, and name the joint that turns most. The answer is that turning occurs about a longitudinal axis, mostly at the atlantoaxial joint, and lifting about a transverse axis in the sagittal plane.

Question 11593. A fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus is followed by an inability to lift the arm and numbness over the outer shoulder. Which nerve has been injured, and which artery runs with it? The answer is the axillary nerve, with the posterior circumflex humeral artery. Both pass through the quadrangular space, bounded by the teres major and minor, the long head of the triceps and the humeral neck.

Question 11594. Which of these structures passes through the foramen spinosum and supplies the dura above the tentorium: the middle meningeal artery, the ophthalmic artery, the internal carotid artery, or the maxillary nerve? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, a branch of the maxillary artery. It grooves the inner skull, and a blow to the side of the head may tear it and strip the dura from the bone.

Question 11595. In one illustration, a person cannot close the eye on one side and has lost taste over the front two thirds of the tongue on that side. Which of these nerves is involved: the facial nerve, the glossopharyngeal nerve, the trigeminal nerve, or the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the facial nerve. It supplies the muscles of facial expression, and one branch carries taste from the front of the tongue.

Question 11596. Which of these lies immediately lateral to the pituitary gland in the sella turcica: the cavernous sinus, the sphenoid sinus, the frontal lobe, or the nasopharynx? The answer is the cavernous sinus, which contains the internal carotid artery and several cranial nerves. The sphenoid sinus lies below and in front, and the optic chiasm above.

Question 11597. Which of these findings best fits compression of the eighth cervical and first thoracic nerve roots: weakness of the triceps, weakness of the small muscles of the hand, loss of the knee jerk, or weakness of the biceps? The answer is weakness of the small muscles of the hand. Those roots form the lower trunk of the brachial plexus, whose fibers reach the muscles of the hand.

Question 11598. Which of these statements about pulse points is correct: the popliteal artery lies deepest in the popliteal fossa, the dorsalis pedis lies medial to the extensor hallucis longus tendon, the posterior tibial artery lies in front of the medial malleolus, or the femoral artery lies lateral to the femoral nerve? The answer is that the popliteal artery lies deepest there. The other three reverse the true relations.

Question 11599. As the brachial artery descends the arm, which nerve crosses it from the lateral side to the medial side, and what does that explain about the pulse at the elbow? The answer is the median nerve. It leaves the lateral cord, crosses in front of the artery, and then lies medial to it, which is why the pulse is felt just medial to the biceps tendon.

Question 11600. A described transverse section through the neck at the cricoid cartilage shows the airway in the middle. State what lies in front of it, what lies behind it, and what lies on each side. The answer is the isthmus of the thyroid gland in front, the esophagus behind, and the carotid sheath with the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve on each side.

To summarize, a symptom is the voice of a named structure, and an examiner who knows it can predict both symptom and finding. Pain from the gut is dull and midline, while pain from the peritoneum lining the wall is sharp and localized, and the phrenic nerve, the tenth thoracic segment, the upper thoracic segments and the tenth thoracic to first lumbar segments each explain a familiar pattern of referral. The surface landmarks give the examination its map, from the sternal angle at the second costal cartilage and the iliac crests at the fourth lumbar vertebra to the popliteal artery deepest behind the knee. Special tests are anatomy put under strain, so the anterior drawer test belongs to the anterior cruciate ligament, the McMurray test to the meniscus, the Trendelenburg sign to the gluteus medius, and each reflex to its own nerve root. One hundred and sixteen chapters come down to this habit of mind: name the structure, say its relations aloud, and the examination follows.
