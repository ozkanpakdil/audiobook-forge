# Chapter 88 — Head and Neck

The head and the neck hold the brain and its coverings, the organs of sight, hearing, smell and taste, the entrance to the airway and to the food passage, and the vessels and nerves that serve them all. This chapter works through the skull and its fossae, the cranial nerves, the orbit and the eye, the ear, the nose and the paranasal sinuses, the mouth, the pharynx and the larynx, and then the triangles, the fascia, the vessels and the nerves of the neck. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 8701. Which suture runs across the top of the skull and joins the frontal bone in front to the two parietal bones behind? The answer is the coronal suture. It crosses the vault from side to side and meets the sagittal suture at the bregma. The sagittal suture separates the two parietal bones behind it.

Question 8702. Which bone forms the floor of the anterior cranial fossa and the roof of the nasal cavity, pierced by many small holes for the fibers of smell? The answer is the ethmoid bone. Its cribriform plate carries the olfactory nerve fibers from the nose to the olfactory bulb. The crista galli rises from it in the midline.

Question 8703. Which of these structures passes through the foramen ovale: the maxillary nerve, the mandibular nerve, the middle meningeal artery, or the internal carotid artery? The answer is the mandibular nerve. The maxillary nerve passes through the foramen rotundum instead. The middle meningeal artery runs through the foramen spinosum, and the internal carotid artery enters by the carotid canal.

Question 8704. Which of these nerves leaves the skull through the jugular foramen: the facial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the optic nerve, or the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve. The vagus and the accessory nerves leave through the same opening. The hypoglossal nerve has a canal of its own beside the foramen magnum.

Question 8705. Which of these openings carries the maxillary nerve into the pterygopalatine fossa: the foramen rotundum, the foramen ovale, the foramen spinosum, or the foramen lacerum? The answer is the foramen rotundum. It lies in the greater wing of the sphenoid, above and in front of the foramen ovale. The foramen ovale carries the mandibular nerve.

Question 8706. A described lateral radiograph of the skull shows a saddle-shaped hollow in the middle of the base with a rounded shadow resting in it. Which structure is the hollow, and what sits in it? The answer is the sella turcica, which holds the pituitary gland. The hollow lies in the body of the sphenoid bone. The gland is joined to the brain above by a stalk.

Question 8707. A described dissection opens the dura beside the body of the sphenoid and finds a wide venous channel with one nerve lying free inside it. Which nerve is that? The answer is the sixth cranial nerve, the abducens. The third and fourth cranial nerves and the first two divisions of the trigeminal nerve lie in the lateral wall. The internal carotid artery also runs forward inside the channel.

Question 8708. The cavernous sinus lies on either side of which midline structure of the skull base? The answer is the body of the sphenoid bone. The two sinuses lie against its sides and are joined by the intercavernous sinuses. Above them lies the pituitary gland in the sella turcica.

Question 8709. A constructed illustration: a blow to the temple is followed by a short period of alertness, then deepening drowsiness and a wide pupil on the same side. Which artery has torn? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, in the extradural space. The thin bone of the pterion is the usual site of the fracture. The blood strips the dura from the inner surface of the vault.

Question 8710. Which of these pairs belongs together: the foramen spinosum with the middle meningeal artery, the foramen ovale with the maxillary nerve, the jugular foramen with the hypoglossal nerve, or the internal acoustic meatus with the twelfth cranial nerve? The answer is the foramen spinosum with the middle meningeal artery. The foramen ovale carries the mandibular nerve, and the hypoglossal nerve leaves by a canal of its own.

Question 8711. Which opening in the skull transmits the twelfth cranial nerve, the nerve that moves the tongue? The answer is the hypoglossal canal. It lies in the occipital bone beside the foramen magnum. The nerve runs from the medulla oblongata to the muscles of the tongue.

Question 8712. Through which opening do the facial nerve and the vestibulocochlear nerve leave the posterior cranial fossa? The answer is the internal acoustic meatus. It is a short canal in the petrous part of the temporal bone. The facial nerve later leaves the skull through the stylomastoid foramen, while the vestibulocochlear nerve ends in the inner ear.

Question 8713. Which of these muscles is supplied by the sixth cranial nerve: the medial rectus, the superior oblique, the lateral rectus, or the inferior oblique? The answer is the lateral rectus. It turns the eye outward, so weakness leaves the eye turned inward. The superior oblique is supplied by the fourth cranial nerve, and the other two by the third.

Question 8714. Which muscle of the eye is supplied by the fourth cranial nerve, and what movement does it produce? The answer is the superior oblique, which turns the eye downward and inward. Its tendon passes through a small pulley at the front of the orbit. A person with a weak superior oblique sees double when walking down stairs.

Question 8715. Through which opening does the facial nerve leave the skull after its long course through the temporal bone: the internal acoustic meatus, the jugular foramen, the stylomastoid foramen, or the foramen lacerum? The answer is the stylomastoid foramen. It lies just behind the base of the styloid process. The nerve then enters the parotid gland and divides into its branches.

Question 8716. A described coronal section through the orbit shows a thin muscle running forward above the eyeball to the upper eyelid, with a nerve entering it from behind. Which muscle and which nerve are shown? The answer is the levator palpebrae superioris, supplied by the third cranial nerve. The muscle raises the upper lid.

Question 8717. A described dissection of the cheek shows a large gland in front of the ear, with a nerve dividing on its surface into five groups of branches that spread across the face. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the facial nerve. It divides the gland into a superficial part and a deep part. Its branches supply the muscles of facial expression.

Question 8718. Within the parotid gland, which structure lies most superficial: the facial nerve, the retromandibular vein, the external carotid artery, or the deep part of the gland? The answer is the facial nerve. The retromandibular vein and the external carotid artery lie deeper. That is why an operation on the gland puts the nerve at risk.

Question 8719. A constructed illustration: a person cannot close the right eye, the right corner of the mouth droops, and the forehead on that side will not wrinkle. Which nerve is affected? The answer is the facial nerve, and the weak forehead places the lesion in the nerve itself rather than in the brain. Weakness from a stroke usually spares the forehead.

Question 8720. True or false: the optic nerve leaves the orbit through the superior orbital fissure. The answer is false. It leaves through the optic canal, which lies in the lesser wing of the sphenoid. The ophthalmic artery runs with it, and the superior orbital fissure carries the third, fourth and sixth cranial nerves and the first division of the trigeminal nerve.

Question 8721. Which division of the trigeminal nerve carries sensation from the cornea and from the skin of the forehead? The answer is the ophthalmic division, the first division. It reaches the orbit through the superior orbital fissure. Its branches also supply the lining of the upper part of the nasal cavity.

Question 8722. Which small ganglion lies in the orbit, lateral to the optic nerve, and sends parasympathetic fibers to the pupil? The answer is the ciliary ganglion. Its fibers come from the third cranial nerve and make the pupil constrict. The ganglion also carries sensory fibers from the eyeball.

Question 8723. Which of these bones forms the greater part of the medial wall of the orbit: the maxilla, the ethmoid, the zygomatic bone, or the frontal bone? The answer is the ethmoid. Its orbital plate is paper thin and separates the orbit from the ethmoid air cells. The maxilla and the lacrimal bone also contribute to that wall.

Question 8724. Into which part of the nasal cavity does the maxillary sinus drain: the sphenoethmoidal recess, the superior meatus, the middle meatus, or the inferior meatus? The answer is the middle meatus. Its opening lies high on the medial wall of the sinus, so the sinus drains poorly when its lining swells. The frontal and ethmoid sinuses also open into the middle meatus.

Question 8725. Which of these structures drains into the inferior meatus of the nose: the nasolacrimal duct, the frontal sinus, the maxillary sinus, or the sphenoid sinus? The answer is the nasolacrimal duct, which carries tears from the eye. It opens under the front end of the inferior concha. The sphenoid sinus opens instead into the sphenoethmoidal recess.

Question 8726. A described specimen of the nasal septum shows a thin vertical plate of bone above and behind, a second flat bone below and behind, and a plate of cartilage in front. Which two bones are shown? The answer is the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid above and the vomer below. The cartilage in front is the septal cartilage.

Question 8727. A described view through a nasal endoscope shows a small opening high on the back wall of the nasal cavity, above and behind the middle concha. Which sinus opens there? The answer is the sphenoid sinus, which opens into the sphenoethmoidal recess. The opening lies close to the pituitary gland above it. The recess is a narrow space above the superior concha.

Question 8728. Which structure lies immediately above the sphenoid sinus, separated from it by a thin plate of bone? The answer is the pituitary gland, sitting in the sella turcica. The sphenoid sinus is therefore a common route of approach to the gland. The cavernous sinuses lie on either side of it.

Question 8729. A constructed illustration: a young person has bleeding from one nostril, coming from a point on the front of the nasal septum where several arteries meet. What is that meeting place called? The answer is Kiesselbach area, fed by branches of the facial, the ophthalmic and the maxillary arteries. It is the commonest site of nosebleeds.

Question 8730. Name the two bones and the piece of cartilage that together form the nasal septum, spoken as a list. The answer is the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, the vomer, and the septal cartilage. The ethmoid plate lies above, the vomer below and behind, and the cartilage in front. A deviated septum is a common variation.

Question 8731. Which cranial nerve supplies the muscles of the tongue, and what happens when it is weak on one side? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, the hypoglossal. The tongue deviates toward the weak side when it is put out. All the tongue muscles are supplied by it except the palatoglossus.

Question 8732. Which muscle of the tongue is the exception, supplied by the vagus nerve through the pharyngeal plexus rather than by the twelfth cranial nerve? The answer is the palatoglossus. It forms the front arch of the palate and pulls the tongue up and back. Its name comes from its two attachments.

Question 8733. Which of these nerves carries the sense of taste from the front two thirds of the tongue: the lingual nerve, the glossopharyngeal nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, or the chorda tympani? The answer is the chorda tympani, a branch of the facial nerve. It joins the lingual nerve and travels with it to the tongue.

Question 8734. Which nerve carries general sensation from the front two thirds of the tongue: the chorda tympani, the glossopharyngeal nerve, the lingual nerve, or the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the lingual nerve, a branch of the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. It is the nerve at risk when a lower third molar is removed.

Question 8735. Which of these muscles of mastication opens the jaw: the masseter, the temporalis, the medial pterygoid, or the lateral pterygoid? The answer is the lateral pterygoid. It pulls the head of the mandible forward and downward. The other three close the jaw, and the medial pterygoid also helps to grind the teeth.

Question 8736. A described dissection of the floor of the mouth shows a duct running forward beside the frenulum of the tongue and opening on a small papilla. Which gland does the duct drain? The answer is the submandibular gland. The duct is joined near its opening by the duct of the sublingual gland. The gland lies below the mylohyoid muscle, curling around its posterior border.

Question 8737. Which nerve lies immediately above the deep part of the submandibular gland and loops around the submandibular duct? The answer is the lingual nerve. It descends from the infratemporal fossa and crosses the duct twice. The chorda tympani travels with it, and those fibers relay in the submandibular ganglion.

Question 8738. A constructed illustration: after the removal of a lower third molar, a person reports numbness of the front of the tongue and cannot taste sweet food there. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the lingual nerve, which carries general sensation from the tongue and also carries the chorda tympani fibers for taste. The hypoglossal nerve lies deeper and would instead weaken the tongue.

Question 8739. Which of these pairs belongs together: the parotid duct with the floor of the mouth, the submandibular duct with the sublingual papilla, the sublingual duct with the upper second molar, or the parotid duct with the sublingual papilla? The answer is the submandibular duct with the sublingual papilla, beside the frenulum. The parotid duct opens opposite the upper second molar instead.

Question 8740. A described coronal section through the mouth shows the tongue above a sheet of muscle that forms the floor of the mouth, and a duct running forward above that sheet. Is the duct above or below the mylohyoid muscle? The answer is above it. The duct belongs to the submandibular gland, which curls around the posterior border of the muscle.

Question 8741. Which cartilage of the larynx forms the prominence known as the Adam's apple? The answer is the thyroid cartilage. Its two plates meet in the midline in front at an angle. The cricoid cartilage lies below it and is the only complete ring of the airway.

Question 8742. Which is the only intrinsic muscle of the larynx that opens the vocal folds? The answer is the posterior cricoarytenoid. It pulls the muscular process of the arytenoid cartilage backward and outward. All the other intrinsic muscles close or tense the folds.

Question 8743. Which nerve supplies the cricothyroid muscle: the recurrent laryngeal nerve, the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, or the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. It runs down beside the superior thyroid artery. The internal branch carries sensation from the larynx above the vocal folds.

Question 8744. Which nerve supplies all the intrinsic muscles of the larynx except the cricothyroid: the superior laryngeal nerve, the recurrent laryngeal nerve, the glossopharyngeal nerve, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It carries both motor and sensory fibers, including sensation from below the vocal folds. On the left it has a long course around the arch of the aorta.

Question 8745. Which of these statements about the left recurrent laryngeal nerve is correct: it hooks around the subclavian artery, it hooks around the ligamentum arteriosum, it passes through the foramen ovale, or it supplies the cricothyroid muscle? The answer is that it hooks around the ligamentum arteriosum, the remnant of the ductus arteriosus. The right nerve hooks around the right subclavian artery instead, so the left nerve has the longer course.

Question 8746. A described view through a laryngoscope shows two pale folds running from front to back with a narrow gap between them. Which folds are they, and what is the gap called? The answer is the true vocal folds, and the gap is the rima glottidis. The folds above are the false vocal folds. The gap widens on inspiration and closes on swallowing.

Question 8747. A described specimen of the larynx shows a leaf-shaped plate of cartilage standing upright behind the tongue, in front of the opening into the larynx. Which cartilage is shown? The answer is the epiglottis, attached to the thyroid cartilage below and to the hyoid bone above. It folds back over the laryngeal inlet during swallowing.

Question 8748. Which nerve lies immediately posterior to the lateral lobe of the thyroid gland, in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus, and is at risk during thyroid surgery? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. The parathyroid glands also lie on the posterior surface of the lobe. Injury to the nerve weakens the vocal fold on that side.

Question 8749. A constructed illustration: after an operation on the thyroid gland, a person's voice tires quickly and the pitch can no longer be raised, though the vocal folds still move well. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, which supplies the cricothyroid muscle. Injury to the recurrent laryngeal nerve would instead weaken the fold.

Question 8750. True or false: the isthmus of the thyroid gland usually lies across the second, third and fourth tracheal rings. The answer is true. The gland moves up and down with the larynx when the person swallows. The isthmus lies in front of the trachea, below the cricoid cartilage.

Question 8751. Which nerve supplies the diaphragm, and from which cervical nerves does it arise? The answer is the phrenic nerve, from the third, fourth and fifth cervical nerves. It runs down the neck on the front of the anterior scalene muscle. It enters the chest behind the subclavian vein.

Question 8752. Which muscle divides each side of the neck into an anterior and a posterior triangle? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid. It runs from the upper part of the chest and the clavicle to the mastoid process. The anterior triangle lies in front of it and the posterior triangle behind it.

Question 8753. Which of these structures lies inside the carotid sheath: the vagus nerve, the sympathetic trunk, the phrenic nerve, or the accessory nerve? The answer is the vagus nerve. It lies behind and between the common carotid artery and the internal jugular vein. The sympathetic trunk lies behind the sheath, and the phrenic nerve lies on the anterior scalene muscle.

Question 8754. Which triangle of the neck contains the submandibular gland: the submental triangle, the submandibular triangle, the carotid triangle, or the muscular triangle? The answer is the submandibular triangle, bounded by the lower border of the mandible and the two bellies of the digastric muscle. The submental triangle lies below the chin, between the two anterior bellies. The carotid triangle contains the carotid sheath.

Question 8755. Which of these structures crosses the posterior triangle of the neck: the accessory nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the ansa cervicalis, or the superior thyroid artery? The answer is the accessory nerve. It runs downward and backward across the triangle to reach the trapezius. The hypoglossal nerve and the ansa cervicalis lie in the anterior triangle.

Question 8756. A described dissection of the back of the neck shows a nerve crossing a triangle of fascia from above downward and disappearing under the front edge of the trapezius. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the accessory nerve, the eleventh cranial nerve. It supplies the trapezius and the sternocleidomastoid. It is at risk in operations on the lymph nodes of that triangle.

Question 8757. Which space lies immediately behind the retropharyngeal space and extends down through the chest to the posterior mediastinum? The answer is the danger space, between the alar fascia and the prevertebral fascia. Infection can pass along it from the throat into the chest. The retropharyngeal space itself lies in front of the alar fascia.

Question 8758. A constructed illustration: a deep cut in the side of the neck below the angle of the jaw is repaired, and afterward the tongue deviates toward the side of the cut when it is put out. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, the hypoglossal. It supplies the tongue muscles, so the weak side cannot push the tongue forward.

Question 8759. Which of these pairs belongs together: the submental triangle with the digastric muscle, the carotid triangle with the submandibular gland, the muscular triangle with the brachial plexus, or the occipital triangle with the thyroid gland? The answer is the submental triangle with the digastric muscle, whose two anterior bellies bound its sides. The submandibular gland lies in the submandibular triangle. The brachial plexus lies in the posterior triangle instead.

Question 8760. A described transverse section of the neck shows the bodies of the vertebrae at the back, muscles in front of them, and a sheath on each side. Do the sheaths lie in front of or behind those muscles? The answer is in front of them. Each carotid sheath lies between the prevertebral muscles behind and the strap muscles in front. The sympathetic trunk stays behind, on the prevertebral fascia.

Question 8761. Which muscle runs from the transverse processes of the cervical vertebrae down to the first rib, and has the phrenic nerve lying on its anterior surface? The answer is the anterior scalene, or scalenus anterior. The subclavian artery runs behind it and the subclavian vein in front of it. It also bends the neck to the same side.

Question 8762. Which artery arises from the subclavian artery in the root of the neck and runs upward through the foramina in the transverse processes of the cervical vertebrae? The answer is the vertebral artery. It enters the skull through the foramen magnum and joins its fellow to form the basilar artery. It supplies the brain and the spinal cord.

Question 8763. Which of these arteries is a branch of the external carotid artery: the vertebral artery, the internal thoracic artery, the superior thyroid artery, or the thyrocervical trunk? The answer is the superior thyroid artery. It is the first branch of the external carotid and runs to the upper pole of the thyroid gland. The other three come from the subclavian artery.

Question 8764. Which of these statements about the internal carotid artery in the neck is correct: it gives off the superior thyroid artery, it has no branches in the neck, it lies in the posterior triangle, or it ends at the carotid bifurcation? The answer is that it has no branches in the neck. It runs up to the base of the skull, and all of its branches arise inside the skull.

Question 8765. Which of these structures lies at the bifurcation of the common carotid artery and monitors the oxygen content of the blood: the carotid body, the carotid sinus, the vagus nerve, or the sympathetic trunk? The answer is the carotid body. The carotid sinus is a slight swelling of the internal carotid above it and monitors blood pressure. Both send their signals by the glossopharyngeal nerve.

Question 8766. A described dissection of the neck shows a nerve from the cervical plexus running down in front of the internal jugular vein and dividing into branches for the strap muscles. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the ansa cervicalis, a loop of the cervical plexus. Its branches supply the sternohyoid, the sternothyroid and the omohyoid muscles. The thyrohyoid is supplied by a branch of the twelfth cranial nerve.

Question 8767. A described dissection shows a vein running down the surface of the sternocleidomastoid muscle, crossing it diagonally, and ending near the clavicle. Which vein is shown? The answer is the external jugular vein. It drains the scalp and the face and opens into the subclavian vein. Its wall is thin and it is easily cut in that region.

Question 8768. Inside the carotid sheath, which structure lies immediately medial to the internal jugular vein? The answer is the common carotid artery, with the vagus nerve lying behind and between them. The sheath is a tube of fascia that runs from the base of the skull to the root of the neck. The sympathetic trunk lies behind the sheath, not inside it.

Question 8769. A constructed illustration: a person falls on the shoulder, and afterward the arm hangs with the elbow straight and the hand turned inward, while the outer side of the shoulder is numb. Which part of the brachial plexus has been injured? The answer is the upper trunk, formed by the fifth and the sixth cervical nerves. This is the classic injury of a stretch between the neck and the shoulder.

Question 8770. True or false: the external jugular vein drains into the subclavian vein, while the internal jugular vein joins the subclavian vein to form the brachiocephalic vein. The answer is true. The internal jugular and the subclavian vein meet at the venous angle, into which the thoracic duct opens on the left side.

Question 8771. Which bone of the neck is the only bone in the body that does not articulate with another bone? The answer is the hyoid bone. It is held in place by muscles and ligaments. It lies at the level of the third cervical vertebra and gives attachment to the muscles of the tongue, of the larynx and of the floor of the mouth.

Question 8772. Which cervical vertebra has no body and no spine, and consists of an anterior and a posterior arch? The answer is the first cervical vertebra, the atlas. It supports the skull and takes part in the joint that nods the head. Its lateral masses carry the facets that receive the occipital condyles.

Question 8773. How many cervical vertebrae are there in the normal spine: 5, 6, 7, or 8? The answer is 7. They lie between the skull above and the thoracic vertebrae below. The seventh has a long spine that can be felt at the base of the neck.

Question 8774. Which cervical vertebra carries the peg-like dens, the pivot around which the head turns: the atlas, the axis, the seventh cervical vertebra, or the first thoracic vertebra? The answer is the axis, the second cervical vertebra. The dens projects upward from its body and is held against the front of the atlas by a ligament.

Question 8775. Through which part of a cervical vertebra does the vertebral artery pass: the vertebral foramen, the transverse foramen, the intervertebral foramen, or the foramen magnum? The answer is the transverse foramen, in the transverse process. The artery runs up through these foramina from the sixth vertebra to the first. The vertebral foramen carries the spinal cord.

Question 8776. A described radiograph taken through the open mouth shows two rounded masses on either side of the upper cervical spine with a small peg of bone standing between them. Which part of which bone is the peg? The answer is the dens of the axis. The two masses are the lateral masses of the atlas.

Question 8777. Which structure lies immediately behind the dens of the axis, so that it is at risk when the dens is displaced backward? The answer is the spinal cord, in the vertebral canal. The transverse ligament of the atlas holds the dens against the anterior arch of the atlas. If the dens breaks or the ligament tears, the cord can be compressed.

Question 8778. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the head, a person has pain in the neck, and the peg of the second cervical vertebra is seen to be displaced backward on a described radiograph. Which structure is in danger of compression? The answer is the spinal cord, which lies just behind the dens. The upper cervical cord also carries the fibers that supply the diaphragm.

Question 8779. Which of these pairs belongs together: the atlanto-occipital joint with rotation of the head, the atlantoaxial joint with nodding, the atlantoaxial joint with rotation, or the atlanto-occipital joint with side bending only? The answer is the atlantoaxial joint with rotation, which turns the head to look to one side. Nodding takes place mainly at the atlanto-occipital joint.

Question 8780. Name the two cervical vertebrae that are atypical in shape, and say briefly what makes each one different, spoken as a list. The answer is the atlas and the axis. The atlas has no body and no spine, and the axis carries the dens. Both take part in the movements of the head.

Question 8781. Which plexus of nerves lies in the posterior triangle of the neck and supplies the skin of the neck and of the shoulder? The answer is the cervical plexus. It is formed by the first four cervical nerves. Its branches wind around the middle of the posterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle.

Question 8782. Which structures emerge from between the anterior and the middle scalene muscles, together with the subclavian artery? The answer is the trunks of the brachial plexus. The upper and the middle trunks lie above the artery and the lower trunk behind it. The plexus then passes under the clavicle into the axilla.

Question 8783. Which of these nerves arises from the cervical plexus: the phrenic nerve, the accessory nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, or the vagus nerve? The answer is the phrenic nerve, from the third, fourth and fifth cervical nerves. The accessory nerve is a cranial nerve that enters the neck through the jugular foramen. The vagus also leaves the skull through the jugular foramen.

Question 8784. Which muscle of the shoulder is supplied by the accessory nerve: the trapezius, the levator scapulae, the rhomboid major, or the serratus anterior? The answer is the trapezius. The accessory nerve also supplies the sternocleidomastoid. The other three muscles are supplied by branches of the cervical and the brachial plexuses.

Question 8785. Into which vein does the thoracic duct usually empty: the left subclavian vein at the left venous angle, the right subclavian vein, the superior vena cava, or the azygos vein? The answer is the left venous angle. It drains the lower limbs, the abdomen and the left side of the head, the neck and the chest. The right lymphatic duct drains the rest.

Question 8786. A described dissection of the root of the neck shows a thin-walled vessel arching laterally across the front of the vertebral artery and ending at the junction of two large veins. Which vessel is shown? The answer is the thoracic duct. It ends at the left venous angle. Its thin wall and pale color distinguish it from the arteries around it.

Question 8787. The sympathetic trunk in the neck lies immediately in front of which muscles, and behind which layer of fascia? The answer is the longus colli and the longus capitis, behind the prevertebral fascia. It also lies behind the carotid sheath. Its ganglia supply the head and the neck.

Question 8788. A constructed illustration: a person has a drooping upper eyelid, a small pupil and no sweating on one side of the face, while the eye itself moves normally. Which structure is involved? The answer is the sympathetic trunk in the neck, and the eye movements are normal because the third, fourth and sixth cranial nerves are not affected. The pattern is described as Horner syndrome.

Question 8789. Which of these pairs belongs together: the internal jugular vein with the carotid sheath, the external jugular vein with the carotid sheath, the thoracic duct with the right venous angle, or the accessory nerve with the anterior triangle? The answer is the internal jugular vein with the carotid sheath. The external jugular vein lies outside the sheath, and the thoracic duct ends at the left venous angle.

Question 8790. True or false: the phrenic nerve lies on the anterior surface of the anterior scalene muscle, while the trunks of the brachial plexus lie between the anterior and the middle scalene muscles. The answer is true. The subclavian artery also passes between the two scalene muscles, behind the plexus.

Question 8791. Which layer of the scalp lies between the fibrous sheet of the galea and the periosteum of the skull, and allows infection to spread widely? The answer is the loose areolar layer, the fourth layer of the scalp. It contains the small veins that connect the scalp with the intracranial venous sinuses. Because it is loose, pus and blood spread easily through it.

Question 8792. Which layer of the scalp holds the arteries, the veins and the nerves of the scalp, and why does a cut through it gape and bleed heavily? The answer is the connective tissue layer, the second layer of the scalp. Its fibrous bands hold the walls of the vessels open when they are cut.

Question 8793. Which of these muscles closes the eye: the orbicularis oculi, the orbicularis oris, the buccinator, or the platysma? The answer is the orbicularis oculi. It encircles the orbit and is supplied by the facial nerve. The orbicularis oris closes the lips, and the buccinator presses the cheek against the teeth.

Question 8794. Which branch of the facial nerve is at greatest risk during an operation on the submandibular gland: the temporal branch, the zygomatic branch, the marginal mandibular branch, or the cervical branch? The answer is the marginal mandibular branch. It runs forward below the angle and the lower border of the mandible. Injury weakens the muscles that pull down the corner of the mouth.

Question 8795. Which of these findings points to a lesion of the facial nerve rather than to a lesion of the trigeminal nerve: loss of taste on the front two thirds of the tongue, loss of sensation over the forehead, weakness of the masseter, or numbness of the chin? The answer is loss of taste on the front two thirds of the tongue, carried by the chorda tympani. The other findings all belong to the trigeminal nerve.

Question 8796. A described dissection shows a thin sheet of muscle arising from the fascia over the chest and running up over the jaw to the corner of the mouth and the lower lip. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the platysma, supplied by the cervical branch of the facial nerve. It lies in the superficial fascia of the neck and tenses the skin of the neck.

Question 8797. Which venous plexus lies in the infratemporal fossa around the muscles of mastication and communicates with the cavernous sinus through small veins? The answer is the pterygoid venous plexus. It drains the deep parts of the face and connects with the facial vein. That connection is one route by which facial infection reaches the cavernous sinus.

Question 8798. A constructed illustration: a person is struck on the temple, recovers briefly, and then becomes deeply drowsy; a described scan shows a lens-shaped collection of blood between the skull and the dura. Which artery has bled? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, torn where the bones of the pterion meet. The sutures limit the spread of the blood, so it forms a lens.

Question 8799. True or false: the facial nerve supplies the muscles of mastication. The answer is false. The muscles of mastication, the masseter, the temporalis and the two pterygoids, are supplied by the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. The facial nerve supplies the muscles of facial expression, including the platysma and the stapedius.

Question 8800. Name the five groups of branches into which the facial nerve divides inside the parotid gland, spoken as a list. The answer is the temporal, the zygomatic, the buccal, the marginal mandibular and the cervical branches. They fan out across the side of the face. Injury to the marginal mandibular branch weakens the lower lip.

To summarize, the base of the skull is a map of openings, with the cribriform plate for the fibers of smell, the optic canal for the optic nerve, the superior orbital fissure for the third, fourth and sixth cranial nerves and the first division of the trigeminal nerve, the foramen rotundum for the maxillary nerve, the foramen ovale for the mandibular nerve, the foramen spinosum for the middle meningeal artery, the internal acoustic meatus for the facial and the vestibulocochlear nerves, the jugular foramen for the glossopharyngeal, the vagus and the accessory nerves, and the hypoglossal canal for the twelfth cranial nerve. The orbit, the nose and the paranasal sinuses drain by fixed routes, with the maxillary and the frontal sinuses opening into the middle meatus and the nasolacrimal duct into the inferior meatus, while the cavernous sinus holds the internal carotid artery and the abducens nerve within its cavity. The mouth and the larynx turn on nerves that must be named exactly, the lingual nerve for sensation and the chorda tympani for taste in the front of the tongue, the hypoglossal nerve for the tongue muscles, the recurrent laryngeal nerve for all the intrinsic muscles of the larynx except the cricothyroid, and the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve for that one exception. The neck is organized by the sternocleidomastoid muscle into an anterior and a posterior triangle, with the carotid sheath in front of the prevertebral muscles, the accessory nerve crossing the posterior triangle, and the thoracic duct ending at the left venous angle.
