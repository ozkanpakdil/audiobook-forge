# Chapter 99 — Injection and Vascular-access Anatomy

This chapter turns to the anatomy that lies under the needle, the cannula and the catheter, and then to the emergencies in which anatomy decides what is damaged and how quickly. It covers the veins and the arteries that are used for access, the landmarks that guide a needle to them, the layers each route crosses, the nerves and the vessels that lie near enough to be injured, and the anatomical emergencies of the chest, the abdomen, the head and the limbs. It holds one hundred questions, and every one of them gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. The first half follows injection and vascular access and the second half follows anatomical emergencies, and the two halves meet in the same structures. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9801. Which vein is chosen for a blood sample at the front of the elbow, where it crosses the cubital fossa just beneath the skin? The answer is the median cubital vein. It joins the cephalic vein on the thumb side to the basilic vein on the little finger side. The brachial artery and the median nerve lie deep to it, so the needle must not pass beyond the vein.

Question 9802. Which long superficial vein of the lower limb is reached just in front of the medial malleolus at the ankle? The answer is the great saphenous vein. It begins at the medial end of the dorsal venous arch of the foot and runs up the inner side of the leg. The saphenous nerve lies with it, so an incision there can leave numb skin.

Question 9803. A needle is placed into a visible vein on the front of the forearm. Which layers does it cross in order: skin and subcutaneous tissue and then the vein wall, the deep fascia and then the vein, muscle and then the vein, or the interosseous membrane and then the vein? The answer is skin and subcutaneous tissue and then the vein wall. Superficial veins lie above the deep fascia.

Question 9804. Within the cubital fossa, which structure lies immediately medial to the brachial artery: the median nerve, the radial nerve, the biceps tendon, or the ulnar artery? The answer is the median nerve. It crosses the fossa just medial to the artery, while the biceps tendon lies lateral to the artery and the radial nerve lies further lateral still.

Question 9805. A described ultrasound view of the neck shows a thin-walled vessel that collapses when the probe is pressed, lying lateral to a thick-walled vessel that keeps its round outline. Which two structures are shown? The answer is the internal jugular vein beside the common carotid artery. The vein lies lateral and is the one that flattens under pressure.

Question 9806. A constructed illustration: after several attempts at a blood sample in the cubital fossa, a person reports sharp pain down the little finger, and the needle was directed too far medially. Which nerve has most likely been touched? The answer is the medial cutaneous nerve of the forearm, which runs beside the basilic vein. The brachial artery lies at risk on the same side.

Question 9807. Which of these is the network of veins on the back of the hand, lying over the metacarpals and draining into the cephalic and basilic veins: the dorsal venous network, the deep palmar arch, the median cubital vein, or the dorsal arch of the foot? The answer is the dorsal venous network, which is the usual site for a cannula in the hand.

Question 9808. Which structure is most at risk when a cannula is placed carelessly on the radial side of the wrist: the radial artery, the ulnar nerve, the median nerve, or the tendon of the palmaris longus? The answer is the radial artery, which lies just under the skin at the wrist beside the lower end of the radius.

Question 9809. In the femoral triangle, which structure lies most lateral: the femoral vein, the femoral artery, the femoral nerve, or the lymph nodes in the femoral canal? The answer is the femoral nerve. From lateral to medial the order is nerve, artery, vein and then the canal, so the vein is the most medial of the three vessels.

Question 9810. True or false: in the femoral triangle the femoral nerve lies lateral to the femoral artery. The answer is true. The nerve lies outside the femoral sheath, while the artery and the vein lie inside it with the artery lateral to the vein. A needle aimed too far lateral may strike the nerve.

Question 9811. The femoral artery is felt as a pulse at a point halfway between the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic symphysis, just below the inguinal ligament. What is that point called? The answer is the mid-inguinal point, and the artery enters the thigh there on its way to the adductor canal.

Question 9812. Which nerve and artery lie behind the shoulder at the surgical neck of the humerus and are at risk from an injection too low in the deltoid: the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery, the radial nerve and the profunda brachii artery, the median nerve and the brachial artery, or the suprascapular nerve and artery? The answer is the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery.

Question 9813. Which nerve lies against the back of the shaft of the humerus in the spiral groove, a little below the deltoid tuberosity, and is therefore injured by a fracture there: the radial nerve, the axillary nerve, the ulnar nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the radial nerve. It is held against the bone in that groove, so the fracture damages it.

Question 9814. A described cross section behind the shoulder shows a nerve and an artery passing together through a gap bounded by the teres minor above, the teres major below, the long head of the triceps medially and the surgical neck of the humerus laterally. Which two structures pass through that gap? The answer is the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery.

Question 9815. A constructed illustration: an injection is given into the upper inner part of the buttock and the person feels a sharp shooting pain down the back of the leg. Which nerve lies in that described zone? The answer is the sciatic nerve, which runs down the middle of the buttock below the piriformis. The upper outer quadrant avoids it.

Question 9816. Through which opening does the sciatic nerve leave the pelvis, and beneath which muscle does it appear in the buttock? The answer is the greater sciatic foramen, and it appears below the piriformis muscle. It then runs downward between the ischial tuberosity and the greater trochanter on its way to the thigh.

Question 9817. Which three bony points bound the ventrogluteal injection site: the anterior superior iliac spine, the crest of the ilium and the greater trochanter; the posterior superior iliac spine, the sacrum and the ischial tuberosity; the pubic symphysis, the inguinal ligament and the femoral pulse; or the iliac crest, the twelfth rib and the first lumbar spine? The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine, the crest of the ilium and the greater trochanter.

Question 9818. As the piriformis muscle leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, which structures lie above it and which lie below it? The answer is the superior gluteal artery and nerve above, and the sciatic nerve with the inferior gluteal vessels below. The pudendal vessels also leave below the muscle.

Question 9819. A described specimen shows the sciatic nerve running down the middle of the buttock, with a safer zone of muscle lying above and outside a line drawn from the posterior superior iliac spine to the greater trochanter. Which quadrant is that safe zone? The answer is the upper outer quadrant, which is used for an injection.

Question 9820. Name the two veins of the upper limb that the median cubital vein connects, spoken as a list. The answer is the cephalic vein and the basilic vein. The cephalic vein runs up the lateral side of the limb and the basilic vein up the medial side, and the short connecting vein crosses the front of the elbow.

Question 9821. Which vein is seen crossing the surface of the sternocleidomastoid muscle in the neck, running from near the angle of the jaw down to the middle of the clavicle? The answer is the external jugular vein. It lies under the skin and the platysma, so it is easily seen and easily cut.

Question 9822. A needle is passed below the middle of the clavicle to reach the subclavian vein. Which structure lies just behind the vein and may be pierced if the needle goes too deep: the dome of the pleura, the trachea, the esophagus, or the thyroid gland? The answer is the dome of the pleura, which rises above the first rib behind the vein, so a deep pass may enter the chest.

Question 9823. During a puncture of the femoral vein at the groin, which vessel lies immediately lateral to it, and which nerve lies further lateral still? The answer is the femoral artery, with the femoral nerve beyond it. The vein is the most medial of the three, so the arterial pulse is the guide to the vein.

Question 9824. A described ultrasound view of the groin shows a thin-walled vessel that narrows when the probe is pressed, lying medial to a vessel whose walls are thick and whose lumen stays open. Which two vessels are shown? The answer is the femoral vein, which lies medial and is compressible, and the femoral artery, which lies lateral.

Question 9825. A constructed illustration: a long catheter has been passed into the femoral vein, and the described radiograph shows its tip in the midline at the level of the third lumbar vertebra. Which vessel has it reached? The answer is the inferior vena cava. A catheter passed further upward would enter the right atrium behind the sternum.

Question 9826. Which vein is formed behind the sternoclavicular joint where the internal jugular vein joins the subclavian vein, and what do the two of them form when they meet? The answer is the brachiocephalic vein. The right and the left brachiocephalic veins unite behind the manubrium to form the superior vena cava.

Question 9827. Which of these pairs belongs together: the internal jugular vein with the carotid sheath, the great saphenous vein with the popliteal fossa, the cephalic vein with the medial side of the arm, or the basilic vein with the lateral side of the arm? The answer is the internal jugular vein with the carotid sheath. The great saphenous vein runs up the medial side of the leg.

Question 9828. For access through the bone in an emergency, a needle is placed on the flat medial surface of the proximal tibia about 2 centimeters below the tibial tuberosity. Which cavity does the needle enter, and which plate must be avoided above that point in a child? The answer is the marrow cavity, and the epiphyseal plate must be avoided.

Question 9829. Which bony point is the landmark for access through the bone in the upper limb, with the needle aimed into the head of the humerus: the greater tubercle, the lesser tubercle, the acromion, or the coracoid process? The answer is the greater tubercle, the most lateral of the four. The axillary nerve lies just below it.

Question 9830. A described radiograph of the knee region of a child shows a needle entering a bone from the front, with its tip in the marrow cavity and its point well below the growth plate. Which bone and which route are shown? The answer is the proximal tibia, entered through its flat medial surface below the tibial tuberosity.

Question 9831. A constructed illustration: a fine catheter is passed through the umbilical stump of a newborn, and the described radiograph shows it running through the liver. Which channel has it followed, and which vessel does that channel join? The answer is the ductus venosus, which joins the inferior vena cava after the blood has passed through the liver.

Question 9832. Which artery is felt on the thumb side of the front of the wrist, where it is used both for a blood sample and for a cannula that measures pressure? The answer is the radial artery. Its pulse lies between the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis and the lower end of the radius.

Question 9833. Before a cannula is placed in the radial artery, the hand is clenched and opened while one artery is pressed. Which artery is pressed, and which arch of the hand does it fill: the ulnar artery and the superficial palmar arch, the radial artery and the deep palmar arch, the median artery and the palmar network, or the interosseous artery and the dorsal arch? The answer is the ulnar artery and the superficial palmar arch.

Question 9834. Which nerve runs with the brachial artery in the arm, crossing from its lateral side to its medial side on the way to the cubital fossa: the median nerve, the ulnar nerve, the radial nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the median nerve. It begins lateral to the artery and ends medial to it, which is why a brachial puncture may injure it.

Question 9835. A described arteriogram of the hand shows two arches, one deep against the metacarpals and one nearer the skin, with the ulnar artery joining the arch that lies nearer the surface. Which arch is that, and which artery completes it on the thumb side? The answer is the superficial palmar arch, completed by the radial artery.

Question 9836. After a cannula is placed in the radial artery, the described forearm becomes tense and swollen and the fingers look pale. Which structures lie in the tight space filling with blood: the flexor muscles with the median and ulnar nerves, the extensor muscles with the radial nerve, the brachialis with the musculocutaneous nerve, or the pronator quadratus with the interosseous nerve? The answer is the flexor muscles with the median and ulnar nerves.

Question 9837. Which artery is felt on the top of the foot between the tendons of the long extensor of the big toe and the long extensor of the toes? The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery, the continuation of the anterior tibial artery. Its pulse is felt there when the pulses above the ankle cannot be found.

Question 9838. Which artery and which nerve lie together behind the medial malleolus, where the artery is felt and a cannula may be placed: the posterior tibial artery and the tibial nerve, the dorsalis pedis artery and the deep fibular nerve, the anterior tibial artery and the saphenous nerve, or the small saphenous vein and the sural nerve? The answer is the posterior tibial artery and the tibial nerve.

Question 9839. In the lower part of the thigh, which large vein runs beside the femoral artery in the adductor canal, and which nerve of the skin lies in the same canal? The answer is the femoral vein, with the saphenous nerve. That nerve is the branch of the femoral nerve that supplies the skin of the medial side of the leg.

Question 9840. Which of these pairs belongs together: the brachial artery with the front of the elbow, the radial artery with the back of the knee, the femoral artery with the top of the foot, or the popliteal artery with the wrist? The answer is the brachial artery with the front of the elbow, where its pulse lies just medial to the tendon of the biceps.

Question 9841. A subcutaneous injection is placed in the fatty layer of the abdominal wall, well away from the umbilicus. What is that layer called, and what lies deep to it? The answer is the superficial fascia, with the muscles of the abdominal wall deep to it. The peritoneum lies deeper still.

Question 9842. An injection is made into the skin of the front of the forearm until a small raised bleb appears. Which layer has the needle entered: the dermis, the superficial fascia, the deep fascia, or the muscle? The answer is the dermis. The bleb forms because the needle is in the tough layer of the skin itself, and a deeper injection would raise nothing.

Question 9843. True or false: the veins used for access in the arm lie deep to the deep fascia. The answer is false. The cephalic vein, the basilic vein and the median cubital vein all lie in the subcutaneous tissue above the deep fascia, which is why they can be seen and felt.

Question 9844. Name two superficial veins of the lower limb that are used for venous access, spoken as a list. The answer is the great saphenous vein and the small saphenous vein. The first runs up the medial side of the leg to the groin, and the second runs up the back of the leg to the popliteal fossa.

Question 9845. A described transverse section of the neck shows a fibrous sheath holding three structures: a large vein with thin walls, an artery with thick walls, and a nerve lying between and behind them. Which sheath is described, and which structures does it hold? The answer is the carotid sheath, holding the internal jugular vein, the common carotid artery and the vagus nerve.

Question 9846. A constructed illustration: a catheter passed through the internal jugular vein has its tip described on a radiograph at the level of the third costal cartilage, just to the right of the midline. Which chamber has it entered? The answer is the right atrium, which lies behind the sternum at that level. The superior vena cava ends there.

Question 9847. In a described transverse section of the arm just above the elbow, the basilic vein lies in the subcutaneous tissue on the medial side and the brachial artery lies in the middle of the limb. In which direction from the artery is the vein? The answer is medial and superficial. Superficial means nearer the skin and medial means nearer the midline of the limb.

Question 9848. Which of these pairs belongs together: the deltoid site with the shoulder, the ventrogluteal site with the outer thigh, the vastus lateralis site with the buttock, or the abdominal site with the upper arm? The answer is the deltoid site with the shoulder. The vastus lateralis site lies in the middle third of the outer side of the thigh.

Question 9849. Which vein runs in the groove between the deltoid and the pectoralis major and then pierces the deep fascia to join the axillary vein? The answer is the cephalic vein. Below the elbow it continues up the lateral side of the forearm from the dorsal venous network of the hand.

Question 9850. A constructed illustration: for repeated access, an artery and a vein at the wrist are joined, and the described vein becomes thick and prominent. Which pair is joined: the radial artery and the cephalic vein, the ulnar artery and the basilic vein, the brachial artery and the median cubital vein, or the radial artery and the basilic vein? The answer is the radial artery and the cephalic vein.

Question 9851. When air enters the chest through a wound and cannot escape, it collects in which space and collapses the lung on that side? The answer is the pleural cavity. The space lies between the visceral pleura on the lung and the parietal pleura on the chest wall.

Question 9852. Which statement about the intercostal bundle is correct: the vein lies highest and the nerve lowest, the nerve lies highest and the vein lowest, the artery lies above the vein, or the bundle lies under the rib below? The answer is that the vein lies highest and the nerve lowest. The bundle runs in the costal groove of the rib above, so a needle is placed just above the rib below.

Question 9853. A described chest radiograph shows a large black area on one side with no lung markings, the edge of the lung visible near the hilum, and the mediastinum pushed to the other side. What does the described film show, and where has the air collected? The answer is a tension pneumothorax, with air in the pleural cavity. The shift of the mediastinum is what makes it dangerous.

Question 9854. A constructed illustration: a stab wound in the fourth intercostal space close to the sternum bleeds heavily into the chest. Which vessels lie in that described zone and are the likely source? The answer is the internal thoracic artery and vein, which run down behind the costal cartilages just lateral to the sternum. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9855. Blood collects between the heart and its fibrous covering, so that the heart cannot fill. What is that covering called, and what is the space in which the blood lies? The answer is the pericardium, and the blood lies in the pericardial cavity between the heart and the serous layer of that sac.

Question 9856. A needle for a pericardial tap is passed from below the xiphoid process, angled upward and to the left. Which structure does it cross last before it enters the pericardial cavity: the fibrous pericardium, the diaphragm, the parietal pleura, or the peritoneum? The answer is the fibrous pericardium. The diaphragm lies between the peritoneum and the pericardium on that route.

Question 9857. Which nerve runs down over the fibrous pericardium on each side, in front of the root of the lung, and may be irritated by blood in the pericardial sac? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It supplies the diaphragm, so irritation of it can cause hiccups or pain felt at the shoulder.

Question 9858. A described computed tomography scan of the chest shows air in the middle of the chest, outlining the trachea and the great vessels, with both lungs expanded and no air in the pleural cavities. Which space holds the air, and which structure may have been torn? The answer is the mediastinum, with the trachea or the esophagus torn.

Question 9859. Which artery lies in a groove on the inner surface of the temporal bone and is torn by a fracture at the pterion, so that blood collects outside the dura? The answer is the middle meningeal artery. It is a branch of the maxillary artery and runs upward from the infratemporal fossa.

Question 9860. A described axial CT scan of the head shows a biconvex collection of fresh blood in the temporal region that does not cross the line of the skull sutures. Which is it: an extradural hematoma, a subdural hematoma, a subarachnoid hemorrhage, or bleeding inside the brain substance? The answer is an extradural hematoma, held between the skull and the dura, and therefore stopped by the sutures.

Question 9861. Which veins cross the space between the dura and the brain to reach the superior sagittal sinus, and are torn when the head is shaken? The answer is the bridging cerebral veins. Blood from them spreads over the surface of the hemisphere and forms a subdural hematoma.

Question 9862. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the side of the head a person becomes drowsy, and the described pupil on the same side is wide and does not narrow when a light is shone into it. Which nerve is compressed, and against what? The answer is the third cranial nerve, pressed against the edge of the tentorium cerebelli by the uncus of the temporal lobe.

Question 9863. When pressure inside the skull rises, the cerebellar tonsils may be pushed down through the foramen magnum. Which structure is compressed, and why is that so dangerous: the medulla oblongata, the pons, the midbrain, or the upper cervical spinal cord? The answer is the medulla oblongata, because the centers that control breathing and the heart lie in it.

Question 9864. Blood in the subarachnoid space most often comes from a small swelling at a junction of the arteries on the base of the brain. What is the arterial ring at the base of the brain called? The answer is the circle of Willis, which joins the two internal carotid arteries to the basilar artery.

Question 9865. A described CT scan of the head shows blood filling the spaces around the base of the brain, in the channels between the midbrain and the temporal lobes, with no collection inside the brain substance. Where is the blood, and which vessels lie in that space? The answer is the subarachnoid space, and the arteries of the circle of Willis lie in it.

Question 9866. A described infection spreads from the upper lip along a vein that runs upward beside the nose, and the cavernous sinus becomes inflamed. Which vein carries the infection: the facial vein by way of the angular vein, the external jugular vein, the retromandibular vein, or the superficial temporal vein? The answer is the facial vein by way of the angular vein, because those veins have no valves.

Question 9867. A described specimen of the front of the nasal septum shows a rich network of vessels in the mucous membrane, where four arteries meet, and this is the commonest site of a nosebleed. What is that network called? The answer is Kiesselbach's plexus. The sphenopalatine artery bleeds heavily too, but further back in the nose.

Question 9868. True or false: after a fracture of the base of the skull, blood may appear as a bruise behind the ear over the mastoid process. The answer is true. The blood tracks through the fracture into the tissues behind the ear, and bruising around the eye may appear at the same time.

Question 9869. A person has bruising around both eyes and behind one ear, with clear fluid running from the nose. Which injury do these signs suggest: a fracture of the base of the skull, a fracture of the mandible, a broken nose, or a fracture of the zygomatic bone? The answer is a fracture of the base of the skull, through the anterior cranial fossa and the petrous part of the temporal bone.

Question 9870. Which four bones meet at the pterion, the H-shaped junction on the side of the skull that overlies the middle meningeal artery? The answer is the frontal bone, the parietal bone, the squamous part of the temporal bone and the greater wing of the sphenoid. A blow there may tear the artery.

Question 9871. Several ribs are broken in two places on one side, and the described segment of the chest wall moves inward as the person breathes in. What is that described chest called: a flail chest, a simple fracture of one rib, a tension pneumothorax, or a fracture of the sternum? The answer is a flail chest, in which the loose segment moves opposite to the rest of the chest wall.

Question 9872. The costodiaphragmatic recess is the lowest part of which space, and which organ lies below the diaphragm on the left and may be injured at the same time as the recess is torn? The answer is the pleural cavity, with the spleen below the diaphragm on the left. A needle placed too low may enter the recess.

Question 9873. A constructed illustration: after the head of the humerus is dislocated forward and downward, the described arm cannot be raised and the skin over the shoulder is numb. Which nerve has been stretched? The answer is the axillary nerve, which winds around the surgical neck of the humerus. The posterior circumflex humeral artery runs with it.

Question 9874. Which nerve lies in the anterior compartment of the leg, against the interosseous membrane, and is the first to be affected when that compartment swells? The answer is the deep fibular nerve. Its injury weakens the muscles that lift the foot, so the foot drops when the leg is raised.

Question 9875. Which of these statements about the compartments of the leg is correct: the deep fibular nerve lies in the anterior compartment, the tibial nerve lies in the anterior compartment, the superficial fibular nerve lies in the deep posterior compartment, or the saphenous nerve lies in the lateral compartment? The answer is that the deep fibular nerve lies in the anterior compartment, with the anterior tibial artery.

Question 9876. A described transverse section of the leg shows one compartment at the front tightly swollen, with the interosseous membrane behind it, the tibia on one side and the fibula on the other. Which structures are compressed in that described compartment? The answer is the muscles that lift the foot, with the deep fibular nerve and the anterior tibial artery.

Question 9877. A constructed illustration: after a fracture just above the elbow in a child, the described forearm is tense and the fingers cannot be straightened fully. Which artery and which nerve are involved in that described injury? The answer is the brachial artery and the median nerve, which lie in front of the lower end of the humerus.

Question 9878. In a fracture just above the elbow, which artery lies immediately in front of the bone and may be torn, and which nerve lies beside it? The answer is the brachial artery, with the median nerve just medial to it. Both lie in the cubital fossa below the level of the fracture.

Question 9879. Name two structures that lie at risk when the lower end of the humerus is fractured just above the elbow, spoken as a list. The answer is the brachial artery and the median nerve. The radial nerve may also be injured higher up, and the ulnar nerve at the elbow itself.

Question 9880. Which artery becomes blocked where the femoral artery passes through the opening in the tendon of the adductor magnus, so that the leg below becomes cold and pale? The answer is the popliteal artery. The opening is the adductor hiatus, and the artery changes its name as it passes through.

Question 9881. A described person has a fracture of the shaft of the femur and the thigh swells rapidly. Which artery is the usual source of the bleeding: the profunda femoris artery, the femoral artery in the adductor canal, the popliteal artery, or the obturator artery? The answer is the profunda femoris artery, which lies against the bone and gives off the perforating branches.

Question 9882. A described angiogram of the lower limb shows the artery of the thigh ending in a blunt point at the level of the adductor hiatus, with no vessel seen below it. Which artery has been shown to be blocked, and what is the name of that opening? The answer is the popliteal artery, blocked at the adductor hiatus.

Question 9883. After a puncture of the femoral artery, the described groin swells and the person cannot straighten the knee against resistance. Which structure has been compressed: the femoral nerve, the sciatic nerve, the obturator nerve, or the saphenous nerve? The answer is the femoral nerve, which lies lateral to the artery and supplies the muscles that straighten the knee.

Question 9884. True or false: in the anterior compartment of the leg the deep fibular nerve is more likely to be damaged than the artery when the compartment swells. The answer is true. That nerve is the most vulnerable structure in the compartment, and it supplies the muscles that lift the foot.

Question 9885. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior compartment of the leg with the deep fibular nerve, the posterior compartment with the deep fibular nerve, the lateral compartment with the tibial nerve, or the anterior compartment with the tibial nerve? The answer is the anterior compartment with the deep fibular nerve. The tibial nerve supplies the posterior compartment.

Question 9886. A described collection of blood lies behind the peritoneum around the aorta at the level of the third lumbar vertebra. Which structures share that described space: the aorta and its branches with the inferior vena cava, the spleen and the left kidney, the bladder and the rectum, or the liver and the gallbladder? The answer is the aorta and its branches with the inferior vena cava, with the duodenum and the pancreas in front.

Question 9887. A described CT scan of the abdomen shows a wide crescent of blood lying against the vertebral column behind the peritoneum, with the duodenum and the pancreas displaced forward. Which space holds the blood? The answer is the retroperitoneal space, in which the aorta, the inferior vena cava and the kidneys lie.

Question 9888. A described person with sudden back pain has a pulsating mass in the middle of the abdomen. At which level does the aorta divide into the two common iliac arteries: the second lumbar vertebra, the fourth lumbar vertebra, the fifth lumbar vertebra, or the first sacral segment? The answer is the fourth lumbar vertebra, just to the left of the midline behind the peritoneum.

Question 9889. Which great vessel lies to the right of the abdominal aorta, and which vein crosses in front of the aorta just below the origin of the superior mesenteric artery? The answer is the inferior vena cava on the right, with the left renal vein crossing in front of the aorta. The vein passes between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery.

Question 9890. Which lobe of the liver is the larger and lies against the diaphragm under the right ribs, and which ligament separates it from the smaller left lobe? The answer is the right lobe, separated by the falciform ligament. The bare area of the liver lies against the diaphragm behind.

Question 9891. A described person has fractures of the left ninth to the eleventh ribs and feels pain at the left shoulder, with the described scan showing blood around the spleen. Which organ is injured, and which nerve carries the pain? The answer is the spleen, with the phrenic nerve carrying the pain, because the same segments of the spinal cord supply the diaphragm and the skin of the shoulder.

Question 9892. A described chest radiograph shows the left half of the diaphragm standing much higher than the right, with a little fluid at the left base and the ribs of that side broken. Which organ below the diaphragm is injured? The answer is the spleen, which lies under the left dome of the diaphragm.

Question 9893. Which artery leaves the pelvis below the piriformis, hooks around the ischial spine, and may be torn by a fracture of the pelvic ring, causing bleeding deep in the perineum? The answer is the internal pudendal artery. It re-enters the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen and runs forward to the perineum.

Question 9894. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior tibial artery with the front of the leg, the posterior tibial artery with the front of the thigh, the radial artery with the back of the knee, or the popliteal artery with the wrist? The answer is the anterior tibial artery with the front of the leg, where it lies against the interosseous membrane.

Question 9895. A described transverse section of the neck at the level of the cricoid cartilage shows the trachea in the midline with the carotid sheath on each side. In which direction from the trachea does the esophagus lie, and what lies in front of the vertebral column? The answer is posterior, behind the trachea, and the esophagus and the trachea lie in front of the vertebral column.

Question 9896. A described ultrasound of the chest shows the bright line between two ribs moving with each breath on one side but standing still on the other, with no shadow behind it. What does that sign mean: air in the pleural cavity, fluid in the pleural cavity, consolidation of the lung, or a normal finding? The answer is air in the pleural cavity, because the two layers of pleura are separated.

Question 9897. A constructed illustration: a stab wound lies in the area bounded by the sternum, the clavicle above and the costal margin below, and the described person becomes pale and breathless. Which structures lie beneath that described zone and may be injured? The answer is the heart and the pericardium, with the internal thoracic vessels and the lung behind them.

Question 9898. Which duct runs upward through the posterior part of the mediastinum and the root of the neck, and may leak milky lymph into the pleural cavity after an injury? The answer is the thoracic duct, which begins in the abdomen and ends at the junction of the left internal jugular vein and the left subclavian vein.

Question 9899. True or false: the right main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical than the left, so an inhaled object is more likely to enter the right lung. The answer is true. The right main bronchus runs more steeply downward, while the left is pushed aside by the arch of the aorta.

Question 9900. A described structure in the front of the midline of the neck lies between the thyroid cartilage above and the cricoid cartilage below, and it is the place where the airway may be opened in an emergency. What is that structure: the cricothyroid membrane, the thyrohyoid membrane, the vocal ligament, or the epiglottis? The answer is the cricothyroid membrane.

To summarize, the veins used for access lie above the deep fascia, and the median cubital vein, the dorsal venous network of the hand, the great saphenous vein and the external jugular vein are the ones most often reached. In the femoral triangle the order from lateral to medial is nerve, artery, vein and canal, while in the arm the median nerve crosses the brachial artery and lies medial to it in the cubital fossa. The intercostal bundle runs in the costal groove of the rib above in the order vein, artery and nerve, which is why a needle in the chest is kept just above the rib below. An extradural hematoma comes from the middle meningeal artery at the pterion and a subdural hematoma from the bridging veins, while herniation of the uncus presses the third cranial nerve and herniation of the tonsils presses the medulla oblongata. Compartment swelling threatens the deep fibular nerve in the front of the leg and the flexor muscles with the median and ulnar nerves in the forearm, and the popliteal artery is the vessel that fails at the adductor hiatus.
