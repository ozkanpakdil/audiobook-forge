# Chapter 43 — Clinical Vascular Anatomy

This chapter turns the vessels of the body into something a hand can find and a clinician can reason about. It covers the pulse points where an artery can be pressed against bone, the places from which serious bleeding comes, the injuries that threaten named vessels, the veins used for vascular access, and the superficial veins of the leg that become varicose. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. Where a clinical correlation touches on management, the choice belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 4201. A pulse is felt at the wrist where an artery lies against the lower end of the radius, just lateral to the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis. Which artery is it? The answer is the radial artery, pressed against the bone there. The ulnar artery lies on the medial side and is harder to feel.

Question 4202. Which artery is felt in the hollow behind the knee when the joint is slightly bent: the femoral artery, the popliteal artery, the posterior tibial artery, or the dorsalis pedis artery? The answer is the popliteal artery, the deepest structure in the fossa and pressed against the back of the joint. The other three are felt in the groin, at the ankle and on the foot.

Question 4203. Which artery can be felt where it hooks around the lower border of the mandible, just in front of the masseter muscle? The answer is the facial artery. It crosses the bone of the jaw on its way to the side of the face, and its pulse is easy to feel against the mandible.

Question 4204. A described view of the front of the wrist shows a taut tendon in the middle of the wrist, with a pulse felt immediately to its outer side against the radius. Which artery is being felt? The answer is the radial artery, with the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis lying medial to it.

Question 4205. Which artery lies behind the medial malleolus, midway between that bone and the heel, with a nerve lying immediately behind it? The answer is the posterior tibial artery. It passes through a fibrous tunnel there, with the tibial nerve behind it, and its pulse is one of the two checked in the foot.

Question 4206. A constructed illustration: a clinician wants to feel the pulse in the neck. Why is the common carotid artery felt one side at a time, low in the neck beside the windpipe? The answer is that the two arteries are the main supply of the brain, so both must not be pressed at once.

Question 4207. The femoral pulse is felt below the groin at the point midway between which two landmarks: the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic symphysis, the umbilicus and the pubic symphysis, the pubic tubercle and the greater trochanter, or the anterior superior iliac spine and the umbilicus? The answer is the mid-inguinal point, between the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic symphysis. The midpoint of the ligament lies more medially, over the femoral canal.

Question 4208. Which artery is felt on the top of the foot, lateral to the tendon of the muscle that lifts the great toe? The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery. It lies against the bones of the midfoot, and in a small number of healthy people it is very small or absent.

Question 4209. True or false: the femoral pulse is best felt at the midpoint of the inguinal ligament. The answer is false. That point lies over the femoral canal and the femoral vein, the most medial structures beneath the ligament, while the artery lies at the mid-inguinal point.

Question 4210. Which artery can be felt in front of the ear, crossing the root of the zygomatic arch on the temple? The answer is the superficial temporal artery, a terminal branch of the external carotid artery. Its pulse can be pressed against the bone of the skull.

Question 4211. In the hollow at the front of the elbow, which structure lies immediately medial to the tendon of the biceps: the brachial artery, the radial nerve, the ulnar artery, or the median cubital vein? The answer is the brachial artery. The median nerve lies medial to the artery, and the tendon forms the lateral boundary of the hollow.

Question 4212. Which pair belongs together: the pulse behind the medial malleolus with the posterior tibial artery, the pulse on the top of the foot with the popliteal artery, the pulse at the wrist with the ulnar artery, or the pulse behind the knee with the dorsalis pedis artery? The answer is the first pair. The top of the foot carries the dorsalis pedis artery, and the usual wrist pulse is the radial.

Question 4213. On the top of the foot, the dorsalis pedis artery lies immediately lateral to which tendon, and over which bones does it pass? The answer is the tendon of the extensor hallucis longus, over the navicular and the cuneiform bones. Pulling the great toe upward makes the tendon stand out.

Question 4214. A described view of the ankle shows a pulse felt between the medial malleolus and the heel, with a band of fascia over the vessels. Which artery is being felt, and what lies behind it? The answer is the posterior tibial artery, with the tibial nerve behind it. Swelling in that tight tunnel can press on the nerve.

Question 4215. A constructed illustration: a cast is applied to a forearm, and the radial pulse at the wrist becomes difficult to feel. Against which bone is the artery pressed, and what lies medial to it? The answer is the lower end of the radius, with the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis medial to it.

Question 4216. In a thin person a strong pulsation can be felt in the upper middle of the abdomen. Which vessel is being felt, and where does it lie relative to the vertebral column? The answer is the abdominal aorta, in front of the vertebral column and a little to the left of the midline.

Question 4217. Which statement about the pulses of the foot is correct: the dorsalis pedis pulse lies medial to the extensor hallucis longus tendon, the posterior tibial pulse lies behind the lateral malleolus, the dorsalis pedis pulse may be absent in healthy people, or the posterior tibial pulse is felt in front of the medial malleolus? The answer is that the dorsalis pedis pulse may be absent in healthy people. The posterior tibial pulse is felt behind the medial malleolus.

Question 4218. Name the two arteries whose pulses are normally sought in the foot, spoken as a list. The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery and the posterior tibial artery. The first lies on the top of the foot and the second behind the medial malleolus, and they are the two branches into which the popliteal artery divides.

Question 4219. A constructed illustration: after a twisting injury at the ankle, the clinician feels for a pulse between the medial malleolus and the heel. Which artery is checked, and which nerve shares that tunnel? The answer is the posterior tibial artery, with the tibial nerve. An injury there can threaten the artery and the nerve together.

Question 4220. The common carotid artery divides into its two terminal branches at about which level: the upper border of the thyroid cartilage, the lower border of the cricoid cartilage, the sternal notch, or the angle of the mandible? The answer is the upper border of the thyroid cartilage. Above that point the internal carotid artery ascends in the neck without giving off branches.

Question 4221. Which layer of the scalp holds the cut vessels open, so that a scalp wound bleeds so freely? The answer is the dense layer of connective tissue, the second layer of the scalp. Its fibrous bands keep the vessels from closing, and the loose layer beneath lets fluid spread across the vault.

Question 4222. Nosebleeds most often come from a rich network of vessels on the front of the nasal septum. Which artery from the ophthalmic artery reaches it: the anterior ethmoidal, the sphenopalatine, the greater palatine, or the superior labial? The answer is the anterior ethmoidal artery. The other three come from the maxillary and facial arteries rather than from the orbit.

Question 4223. A blow to the left upper abdomen may tear the organ that lies immediately beneath the left dome of the diaphragm, behind the ninth to the eleventh ribs. Which organ is it? The answer is the spleen, a soft, blood-filled organ with a thin capsule, protected only by the lower ribs.

Question 4224. A described radiograph of the pelvis shows the ring broken at the front, with a hazy shadow spreading into the buttock. Which vessels are the usual source of that shadow? The answer is the branches of the internal iliac artery and the veins of the pelvic plexus. Much of the bleeding from a broken pelvis is venous.

Question 4225. A constructed illustration: after a blunt injury to the left lower ribs, a person has tenderness on the left of the abdomen and pain at the tip of the left shoulder. Which organ is the source, and which nerve carries the pain? The answer is the spleen, with blood irritating the diaphragm, whose nerve is the phrenic nerve.

Question 4226. A broken rib can cut an intercostal artery. Where does it run: in the groove along the lower border of the rib above, along the upper border of the rib below, through the middle of the space, or on the outer surface of the rib? The answer is the groove along the lower border of the rib above. A needle is therefore passed just above the rib below.

Question 4227. Between which two muscle layers does the intercostal nerve and vessel lie in the costal groove? The answer is the internal intercostal muscle and the innermost intercostal muscle. The bundle lies in the groove on the inner surface of the rib above.

Question 4228. A stab wound in the buttock may cut an artery that leaves the pelvis below the piriformis muscle and lies close to the sciatic nerve. Which artery is it? The answer is the inferior gluteal artery. The superior gluteal artery leaves the pelvis above the piriformis instead.

Question 4229. True or false: after a fracture of the pelvis, most of the bleeding usually comes from torn arteries rather than from veins. The answer is false. The veins of the pelvic plexus and the raw bone surfaces are the usual source, and the pelvis is a rigid ring that holds a large volume.

Question 4230. Which organ lies immediately beneath the right dome of the diaphragm and behind the lower ribs on the right, so that a broken tenth rib may tear it? The answer is the liver. Its right lobe is molded to the diaphragm, and a blunt blow to the right lower chest can split it.

Question 4231. Which pair belongs together: bleeding from far back in the nose with the sphenopalatine artery, a scalp wound with the facial artery, a tooth socket with the lingual artery, or the tongue with the inferior alveolar artery? The answer is the first pair. The tooth socket bleeds from the inferior alveolar artery and the tongue from the lingual artery.

Question 4232. A described scan of the abdomen shows fluid collecting around the spleen and tracking down the left side of the colon. Which organ has been injured, and which artery supplies it? The answer is the spleen, supplied by the splenic artery, which runs along the upper border of the pancreas.

Question 4233. A described radiograph shows a break across the shaft of the femur, and the thigh is swollen and tense. Which artery, running close to the bone, is the likely source? The answer is the profunda femoris artery, whose perforating branches wind around the shaft.

Question 4234. A described view of the inside of the nose shows blood coming from far back, above and behind the middle part of the nasal cavity, where an artery enters through the palatine bone. Which artery is bleeding? The answer is the sphenopalatine artery, a branch of the maxillary artery.

Question 4235. A constructed illustration: after a lower molar tooth is removed, bleeding continues from deep in the socket and the lower lip feels numb. Which artery runs in the canal of the lower jaw with the nerve to the teeth? The answer is the inferior alveolar artery, which runs with the inferior alveolar nerve.

Question 4236. A deep stab wound in the neck can cause profuse bleeding that fills the carotid sheath. Which vein lies in that sheath beside the common carotid artery: the internal jugular vein, the external jugular vein, the anterior jugular vein, or the vertebral vein? The answer is the internal jugular vein. The other two jugular veins lie under the skin of the neck.

Question 4237. A fracture that opens the pelvic ring at the front may tear an artery as it leaves the pelvis above the piriformis muscle. Which artery is it: the superior gluteal, the inferior gluteal, the obturator, or the internal pudendal? The answer is the superior gluteal artery, which is short and fixed at the greater sciatic foramen.

Question 4238. A stab wound in the fifth space beside the sternum may cut an artery that descends behind the costal cartilages. Which artery is it: the internal thoracic, the superior epigastric, the musculophrenic, or the posterior intercostal? The answer is the internal thoracic artery, which runs down just lateral to the sternum.

Question 4239. Which large vessel lies immediately behind the body of the stomach, along the upper border of the pancreas? The answer is the splenic artery, which runs a tortuous course there. An ulcer that penetrates the back wall of the stomach can erode it.

Question 4240. Name two organs that lie behind the peritoneum in the upper abdomen and may bleed without irritating the peritoneum, spoken as a list. The answer is the kidneys and the pancreas. The duodenum and the great vessels also lie there, so bleeding can be concealed.

Question 4241. At which landmark does the axillary artery become the brachial artery? The answer is the lower border of the teres major muscle. Above it the vessel lies in the axilla, wrapped in a sheath with the cords of the brachial plexus, and below it the artery runs down the medial side of the arm.

Question 4242. A fall on the outstretched hand pushes the head of the humerus forward out of its socket. Which artery is most at risk: the axillary artery, the brachial artery, the subclavian artery, or the radial artery? The answer is the axillary artery, which lies close to the front of the shoulder joint, and the axillary nerve is injured with it.

Question 4243. Which artery is tethered above by the opening in the adductor magnus and below by the fibrous arch of the soleus, so that a knee dislocated backward may tear it? The answer is the popliteal artery, which also lies against the back of the femur.

Question 4244. Which structure lies deepest in the popliteal fossa, directly against the back of the knee joint? The answer is the popliteal artery. The popliteal vein lies superficial to it, and the tibial nerve lies most superficial of the three. That order matters when the fossa swells, because the artery is compressed first.

Question 4245. A described radiograph of a child's elbow shows a break just above the condyles of the humerus, with the lower fragment displaced backward. Which artery and which nerve are most at risk? The answer is the brachial artery and the median nerve, both lying in front of the lower humerus.

Question 4246. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the elbow, the forearm is cool and the pulse at the wrist cannot be felt. Which artery is compressed, and what lies on each side of it? The answer is the brachial artery, with the biceps tendon lateral to it and the median nerve medial.

Question 4247. Through which opening does the femoral artery leave the front of the thigh and become the popliteal artery? The answer is the adductor hiatus, the gap in the lower part of the adductor magnus muscle. The femoral vein passes through it with the artery, and the artery then lies against the back of the knee.

Question 4248. The axillary artery is surrounded by the cords of the brachial plexus. Which muscle divides it into three parts, and where does each cord lie relative to the second part? The answer is the pectoralis minor. The lateral cord lies lateral to the artery, the posterior cord behind it, and the medial cord medial.

Question 4249. A fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus injures a nerve and an artery that pass together through a gap between muscles. Which pair is it: the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery, the radial nerve and the profunda brachii artery, the median nerve and the brachial artery, or the ulnar nerve and the superior ulnar collateral artery? The answer is the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery, which pass through the quadrangular space.

Question 4250. A break in the middle of the humeral shaft injures a nerve and an artery that run together in the groove on the back of the bone. Which pair is it: the radial nerve and the profunda brachii artery, the median nerve and the brachial artery, the ulnar nerve and the superior ulnar collateral artery, or the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery? The answer is the radial nerve and the profunda brachii artery, both in the spiral groove.

Question 4251. A fracture of the neck of the femur may tear the artery that supplies most of the blood to the head of the bone. Which artery is it: the medial circumflex femoral, the lateral circumflex femoral, the obturator, or the inferior gluteal? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral artery, which runs up behind the neck and sends branches through the capsule.

Question 4252. Which part of the colon lies at the meeting point of the territories of the superior mesenteric artery and the inferior mesenteric artery, and is most vulnerable to a fall in blood flow: the cecum, the transverse colon, the splenic flexure, or the sigmoid colon? The answer is the splenic flexure, where the marginal artery is least well developed.

Question 4253. True or false: the median nerve lies medial to the brachial artery throughout the arm. The answer is false. In the upper arm the nerve lies lateral to the artery and crosses in front of it about the middle of the arm.

Question 4254. Which pair belongs together: a break above the condyles of the humerus with the brachial artery, a dislocated knee with the brachial artery, a break of the surgical neck of the humerus with the popliteal artery, or a break of the femoral shaft with the axillary artery? The answer is the first pair. A dislocated knee threatens the popliteal artery and the surgical neck the posterior circumflex humeral artery.

Question 4255. A constructed illustration: a stab wound in the armpit produces a rapidly expanding swelling. Which artery is bleeding, and which nerves are wrapped around it? The answer is the axillary artery, with the cords of the brachial plexus around it, named lateral, posterior and medial.

Question 4256. Which artery passes forward through the opening in the upper part of the membrane between the two bones of the leg? The answer is the anterior tibial artery, the first branch of the popliteal artery. It continues down the front of the leg to become the dorsalis pedis artery.

Question 4257. A described radiograph shows a break in the upper part of the tibia, with a spike of bone pointing backward into the back of the knee. Which artery is threatened? The answer is the popliteal artery and its division into the anterior and posterior tibial arteries.

Question 4258. A described cross section of the arm is taken at right angles to its long axis, in the middle of the upper arm. Which structures lie in front of the humerus, and which behind it? The answer is the biceps, the brachial artery and the median nerve in front, and the triceps with the radial nerve behind.

Question 4259. A deep cut across the palm bleeds heavily from the arch that lies just beneath the skin. Which artery forms most of that arch: the radial artery, the ulnar artery, the median artery, or the anterior interosseous artery? The answer is the ulnar artery, which forms the superficial palmar arch, while the radial artery forms the deep arch.

Question 4260. A described radiograph shows a break in the middle of the clavicle with a spike of bone pointing downward, and a swelling spreads into the root of the neck. Which vessels lie immediately behind the bone? The answer is the subclavian artery and vein, with the vein in front of the artery and against the bone.

Question 4261. Which vein is the one most often used for taking blood at the elbow, lying just under the skin across the front of the joint? The answer is the median cubital vein, which runs from the cephalic vein on the lateral side to the basilic vein on the medial side.

Question 4262. Which structure lies between the median cubital vein and the brachial artery at the elbow, protecting the artery when the vein is used for venipuncture? The answer is the bicipital aponeurosis, a fibrous sheet from the tendon of the biceps. The median nerve lies deep to it as well.

Question 4263. Which vein runs up the lateral side of the wrist and the forearm, starting from the back of the hand: the cephalic vein, the basilic vein, the median cubital vein, or the great saphenous vein? The answer is the cephalic vein. The basilic vein runs up the medial side instead.

Question 4264. Which long vein passes in front of the medial malleolus and then runs up the medial side of the leg? The answer is the great saphenous vein, the longest vein in the body. The small saphenous vein runs behind the lateral malleolus instead.

Question 4265. When a large vein is needed in the groin, the needle is placed just medial to the point where the arterial pulse is felt. Which vein is being entered: the femoral vein, the great saphenous vein, the external iliac vein, or the profunda femoris vein? The answer is the femoral vein, which lies medial to the femoral artery below the inguinal ligament.

Question 4266. Which structure lies most medial beneath the inguinal ligament: the femoral nerve, the femoral artery, the femoral vein, or the femoral canal? The answer is the femoral canal. From lateral to medial the order is nerve, artery, vein and canal, and the canal is the small space that lets the vein swell.

Question 4267. A described view of the neck shows a needle entering at the top of the triangle formed by the two heads of the sternocleidomastoid muscle, at the level of the cricoid cartilage. Which vein is being entered, and what lies immediately medial to it? The answer is the internal jugular vein, with the common carotid artery medial to it.

Question 4268. A constructed illustration: during an attempt at a line in the neck, bright red blood is drawn back. Which structure has been entered, and how does its position explain the mistake? The answer is the common carotid artery, which lies medial to the internal jugular vein in the carotid sheath.

Question 4269. Which vein ascends in the groove between the deltoid and the pectoralis major, then pierces the fascia below the clavicle to join the axillary vein? The answer is the cephalic vein. The basilic vein runs up the medial side of the arm instead.

Question 4270. The subclavian vein is used for access below the clavicle. Which statement about it is correct: it lies in front of the scalenus anterior muscle and behind the clavicle, it lies behind the scalenus anterior, it lies below the first rib, or it opens into the superior vena cava in the neck? The answer is the first statement. It joins the internal jugular vein behind the sternoclavicular joint.

Question 4271. Which vein begins behind the lateral malleolus, runs up the back of the leg, and ends in the popliteal vein? The answer is the small saphenous vein. In the upper leg it lies between the two heads of the gastrocnemius, with the sural nerve beside it.

Question 4272. Which pair belongs together: the great saphenous vein with the front of the medial malleolus, the small saphenous vein with the front of the medial malleolus, the cephalic vein with the medial side of the forearm, or the basilic vein with the lateral side of the forearm? The answer is the first pair. The small saphenous vein lies behind the lateral malleolus.

Question 4273. A described view of the back of the hand shows a network of veins lying over the bones between the knuckles. Which two veins arise from the ends of that network? The answer is the cephalic vein from its lateral end and the basilic vein from its medial end.

Question 4274. A constructed illustration: a cut in front of the medial malleolus reaches a vein, and later the skin along the medial border of the foot feels numb. Which nerve has been injured, and which vein was sought? The answer is the saphenous nerve, with the great saphenous vein, which it accompanies in the leg.

Question 4275. True or false: the great saphenous vein ends in the femoral vein at a point below and lateral to the pubic tubercle. The answer is true. It passes through an opening in the deep fascia, the saphenous opening, about a hand's breadth below and lateral to the tubercle.

Question 4276. Which statement about the superior vena cava is correct: it is formed by the union of the two brachiocephalic veins behind the lower border of the first right costal cartilage, it is formed by the union of the internal jugular veins in the neck, it lies to the left of the ascending aorta, or it opens into the left atrium? The answer is the first statement. It enters the right atrium behind the third right costal cartilage.

Question 4277. Within the carotid sheath, which structure lies lateral to the common carotid artery, and where does the vagus nerve lie? The answer is the internal jugular vein, with the vagus nerve between and behind the two. A needle aimed too far medially therefore reaches the artery.

Question 4278. At the front of the elbow, place the tendon, the artery and the nerve in order from lateral to medial. The answer is the tendon of the biceps, then the brachial artery, then the median nerve. The radial nerve lies further lateral still.

Question 4279. Beneath the inguinal ligament, in which order do the structures lie from lateral to medial: the nerve, the artery, the vein and the canal; the artery, the nerve, the vein and the canal; the vein, the artery, the nerve and the canal; or the canal, the vein, the artery and the nerve? The answer is the nerve, the artery, the vein and the canal. The femoral nerve lies outside the sheath.

Question 4280. A described view of the groin shows a needle entering below the inguinal ligament, just medial to the point where a pulse is felt. Which vein is being entered, and why must the needle stay below the ligament? The answer is the femoral vein; above the ligament the needle would enter the abdominal cavity.

Question 4281. Which vein is the longest in the body, running from the back of the great toe up the medial side of the limb to the groin? The answer is the great saphenous vein, which passes in front of the medial malleolus and ends in the femoral vein.

Question 4282. Which vein runs up the medial side of the leg and thigh and is the one most often affected by varicose veins: the great saphenous vein, the small saphenous vein, the popliteal vein, or the femoral vein? The answer is the great saphenous vein. It lies just beneath the skin with little support.

Question 4283. What kind of vein connects the superficial veins of the leg to the deep veins through the deep fascia, and in which direction do its valves normally allow blood to flow? The answer is a perforating vein, whose valves direct blood inward from the superficial to the deep system. When they fail, blood is forced outward.

Question 4284. A person has dilated, tortuous veins running down the inner side of the leg from the groin to the ankle. Which vein and which junction are involved: the great saphenous vein and the saphenofemoral junction, the small saphenous vein and the saphenopopliteal junction, the femoral vein and the popliteal junction, or the cephalic vein and the axillary junction? The answer is the great saphenous vein with the saphenofemoral junction.

Question 4285. In the leg, which nerve runs with the great saphenous vein, and where does the vein lie relative to the medial malleolus? The answer is the saphenous nerve, and the vein passes in front of the medial malleolus. A cut beside the vein can divide the nerve.

Question 4286. A constructed illustration: a person has a shallow ulcer just above and behind the medial malleolus, with brown staining of the skin around it. Which system of veins has failed, and why does the ulcer form there? The answer is the superficial system, with failing valves in the perforating veins of the lower leg.

Question 4287. A described photograph of the leg shows veins that are dilated, elongated and twisted, standing out under the skin from the ankle to the groin. Which system is shown, and what has failed? The answer is the superficial venous system, most often the great saphenous vein.

Question 4288. What normally prevents blood from falling back down the veins of the leg: the contraction of the calf muscles with one-way valves, the elasticity of the deep fascia alone, the pressure of the arterial pulse, or the pull of the skin? The answer is the contraction of the calf muscles with one-way valves, which squeeze the deep veins and direct blood toward the heart.

Question 4289. Which superficial vein of the leg ends in the popliteal vein behind the knee? The answer is the small saphenous vein, which begins behind the lateral malleolus and runs up the back of the leg. It may end in the great saphenous vein instead, which is a normal variation.

Question 4290. Through which opening in the deep fascia does the great saphenous vein pass to reach the femoral vein, and what covers that opening? The answer is the saphenous opening, covered by the cribriform fascia. It lies below and lateral to the pubic tubercle, and small superficial veins join the great saphenous vein there.

Question 4291. A varicocele is a dilation of the veins of the pampiniform plexus. Why is it far more common on the left side: the left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein, the left testicular vein is shorter, the left testis lies lower, or the left vein has more valves? The answer is that the left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein at a right angle and over a longer course.

Question 4292. A described view of the scrotum shows a mass above the testis that feels like a bag of worms and shrinks when the person lies down. Which veins are dilated, and which side is usually affected? The answer is the veins of the pampiniform plexus, usually on the left side.

Question 4293. True or false: the valves in the deep veins of the leg let blood pass outward from the deep system into the superficial veins. The answer is false. The valves of both systems direct blood from the superficial veins into the deep veins and toward the heart.

Question 4294. Which pair belongs together: varicose veins at the lower end of the esophagus with the left gastric vein, dilated veins around the navel with the splenic vein, piles with the middle colic vein, or dilated veins of the buttock with the renal vein? The answer is the first pair. Around the navel the paraumbilical veins join the portal system.

Question 4295. Name the two superficial veins of the lower limb, spoken as a list. The answer is the great saphenous vein and the small saphenous vein. Both lie outside the deep fascia, in the fatty layer under the skin, and the first runs up the medial side of the leg.

Question 4296. Why does a venous ulcer of the leg most often sit just above the medial malleolus: because the perforating veins there carry the highest pressure when the pump fails, because the skin there is thinnest, because the great saphenous vein has no valves there, or because the tibial artery lies nearest the skin? The answer is the first reason.

Question 4297. Which statement about the deep veins of the leg is correct: they lie beneath the deep fascia within the muscle compartments, they lie in the fatty layer under the skin, they have no valves, or they drain only into the small saphenous vein? The answer is that they lie beneath the deep fascia. The perforating veins cross it to join the two systems.

Question 4298. A described venogram shows contrast flowing backward down the great saphenous vein from the groin when the person coughs. Which junction has failed, and what is the normal direction of flow there? The answer is the saphenofemoral junction. Normally a valve there stops blood passing back into the superficial vein.

Question 4299. The popliteal vein ascends through the back of the knee. Which statement is correct: it becomes the femoral vein as it passes through the adductor hiatus, it becomes the great saphenous vein at the ankle, it drains into the small saphenous vein, or it ends in the popliteal fossa? The answer is the first statement. It receives the small saphenous vein.

Question 4300. Varicose veins are more common in the left leg than in the right. Which feature best explains this: the left common iliac vein is crossed by the right common iliac artery, the left common iliac vein is longer, the left leg has fewer valves, or the left great saphenous vein has no perforating veins? The answer is the first feature.

To summarize, the pulse points are the places where an artery can be pressed against bone, among them the radial artery lateral to the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis, the brachial artery beside the biceps tendon, the femoral artery at the mid-inguinal point, the popliteal artery deepest in the popliteal fossa, and the posterior tibial and dorsalis pedis arteries at the ankle and the foot. Serious bleeding comes from the dense layer of the scalp, the plexus on the front of the nasal septum, the spleen and the liver beneath the diaphragm, the vessels in the costal groove of the rib above, and the pelvic plexus and internal iliac branches after a fracture of the pelvis. The vessels most at risk are the axillary artery in a shoulder dislocation, the brachial artery above the elbow, the popliteal artery in a dislocated knee, and the profunda femoris artery beside the shaft of the femur. Vascular access uses veins in predictable places: the median cubital vein over the bicipital aponeurosis, the cephalic and basilic veins from the back of the hand, the great saphenous vein in front of the medial malleolus, and the femoral, internal jugular and subclavian veins, each in a known relation to its artery. Varicose veins follow failure of the valves of the superficial veins and their perforators, most often in the great saphenous system, while the deep veins of the leg stay beneath the deep fascia.
