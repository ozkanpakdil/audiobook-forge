# Chapter 115 — Structure and Injury

This chapter closes the bank by joining structure to the two things a listener meets first in practice, the injury that damages a structure and the symptom that points to it. It covers bone, joint, ligament, muscle, vessel, and nerve, first as the target of a particular force and then as the source of a particular complaint. It holds one hundred questions, and every one of them gives its answer straight after the question and then explains why that answer is right. Each scenario is a constructed illustration rather than a real patient, and nothing here replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 11401. Which bone of the forearm gives way when a person falls onto the outstretched hand, breaking just above the wrist with the lower fragment tilted backward? The answer is the radius. It carries the hand and takes the force of the fall, so its lower end bends and breaks first. The wrist joint holds the lower end of the ulna in place.

Question 11402. A described radiograph shows a break across a small bone in the hollow bounded by two tendons at the base of the thumb. Which bone is broken: the scaphoid, the lunate, the triquetrum, or the pisiform? The answer is the scaphoid. It bridges the two rows of carpal bones and takes the impact, and its blood supply enters at the far end, so a break near the thumb end heals badly.

Question 11403. Which bone of the shoulder girdle is the one most often broken in the body, and at which part does it give way? The answer is the clavicle, which usually breaks through its middle third. It acts as a strut between the sternum and the shoulder blade and takes force carried up from the arm.

Question 11404. A break through the surgical neck of the humerus most threatens which nerve: the axillary nerve, the radial nerve, the median nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the axillary nerve. It passes close to the surgical neck on its way to the deltoid, so injury weakens the lifting of the arm.

Question 11405. A constructed illustration: a cyclist falls on the arm, and a described radiograph shows a break across the middle of the shaft of the humerus. The wrist droops and the fingers cannot be straightened. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the radial nerve, which lies against the bone in the spiral groove, where a midshaft break presses on it.

Question 11406. A constructed illustration: a child falls and the elbow swells, with a described radiograph showing a break just above the condyles of the humerus. Which nerve is most at risk: the median nerve, the ulnar nerve, the radial nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the median nerve, which crosses the front of the elbow close to the bone beside the brachial artery.

Question 11407. Which muscle is attached to the bony point at the back of the elbow and pulls the broken fragment upward when that point is fractured? The answer is the triceps. Its tendon inserts on the olecranon process of the ulna, so the fragment is drawn away from the shaft.

Question 11408. A constructed illustration: a described radiograph shows breaks through the pubic rami on both sides with widening of the joint between the pubic bones, and blood appears at the urinary opening. Which structure is torn? The answer is the membranous part of the urethra, which is fixed where it passes through the pelvic floor and tears there.

Question 11409. A described radiograph shows a break across the neck of the femur with the shaft riding upward. Which vessel is most likely torn, so that the head loses its supply: the obturator artery, the medial circumflex femoral artery, the lateral circumflex femoral artery, or the artery of the ligamentum teres? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral artery, whose branches climb the neck and enter the head.

Question 11410. Which nerve lies immediately posterior to the shaft of the femur and is at risk when the shaft is broken and the fragments displace backward? The answer is the sciatic nerve, which runs down the back of the thigh behind the bone. Such a break can also release a large volume of blood into the thigh.

Question 11411. A constructed illustration: a person in a vehicle collision drives the knee against the dashboard and the hip is dislocated backward. The limb is shortened, turned inward, and held against the other leg. Which nerve is most likely stretched? The answer is the sciatic nerve, which lies directly behind the hip joint.

Question 11412. Which bone lies in front of the knee within the tendon of the quadriceps and may be broken by a direct blow or by a violent contraction of that muscle? The answer is the patella. The quadriceps pulls on it from above and the patellar ligament holds it from below.

Question 11413. A described radiograph shows the tibia displaced forward on the femur after a severe twisting injury with no bone broken. Which structure is most likely torn: the popliteal artery, the great saphenous vein, the common fibular nerve, or the saphenous nerve? The answer is the popliteal artery, which lies against the back of the joint and is fixed above and below.

Question 11414. A described cross section of the leg shows the tibia and the fibula joined by a membrane, with a group of muscles in front of it. Which nerve runs in that front compartment, and what happens when the compartment swells? The answer is the deep fibular nerve, which fails first, weakening the lifting of the foot.

Question 11415. When the foot is forced into inversion, tearing the ligaments on the outer side of the ankle, which ligament is injured most often? The answer is the anterior talofibular ligament. It is the weakest of the three lateral ligaments and the first to give way as the foot rolls inward.

Question 11416. A described radiograph shows a break through the upper part of the fibula with widening of the space between the tibia and the fibula at the ankle. What has happened? The answer is that the force broke the fibula high instead of at the lateral malleolus, and the membrane joining the two bones is torn.

Question 11417. A described radiograph taken after a fall from a height shows the heel bone crushed, with the angle between its upper surface and the joint above it flattened. Which bone is injured, and which part of the spine should also be examined? The answer is the calcaneus, since the same force travels up the leg to a lumbar vertebral body.

Question 11418. A described radiograph shows a break across the base of the fifth bone of the foot, where its shaft meets its base, without any twisting of the ankle. Which fracture is this? The answer is a break at the base of the fifth metatarsal, which heals slowly because the blood supply to that part is poor.

Question 11419. Which bone of the foot most often develops a crack from repeated marching and running, felt as pain in the front of the foot that worsens with each step? The answer is the second metatarsal or the third. These bones are long and thin and take the greatest load as the foot rolls forward.

Question 11420. A described section of the side of the skull shows a thin area where four bones meet above and in front of the ear. A blow there tears an artery running in a groove on the inner surface. Which artery is it? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, and blood collects between the skull and the dura.

Question 11421. A described person who has struck the head shows bruising behind the ear and around both eyes, and clear fluid runs from the nose. Which part of the skull is fractured? The answer is the base of the skull, since blood tracks along the deep planes and cerebrospinal fluid escapes through a torn covering of the brain.

Question 11422. True or false: a described vertebral body crushed by a fall from a height can drive bone backward into the canal and injure the spinal cord. The answer is true. The body gives way and its back wall is pushed into the space that holds the cord, so that wall must be assessed.

Question 11423. A described radiograph of the neck after a fall shows a break through the peg of bone rising from the second cervical vertebra. Which joint is involved? The answer is the joint between the first and second cervical vertebrae, where that peg is the pivot for turning the head and displacement may press on the cord.

Question 11424. Which of these pairs belongs together: the scaphoid with tenderness in the hollow at the base of the thumb, the clavicle with tenderness at the elbow, the calcaneus with tenderness over the knee, or the patella with tenderness behind the heel? The answer is the scaphoid with tenderness in the hollow at the base of the thumb.

Question 11425. In the chest wall, the nerve and vessels that run below each rib lie in which position relative to that rib, and why does a break endanger them? The answer is that they lie in a groove along the lower border of the rib itself, so a break tears the bundle running in its own groove.

Question 11426. True or false: when several ribs are broken in two places on the same side, the segment between the breaks moves inward as the person breathes in. The answer is true, and that is called a flail segment. The isolated piece is drawn inward while the rest of the cage expands.

Question 11427. Which bone lies in the middle of the front of the chest and may be broken across its body by a direct blow, with the heart lying immediately behind it? The answer is the sternum. Its body is joined to the ribs by costal cartilages, and its upper end is the manubrium.

Question 11428. A constructed illustration: a person is struck on the left side over the lower ribs, and the described radiograph shows breaks of the ninth, tenth, and eleventh ribs. Which organ behind them is most exposed? The answer is the spleen, which lies under the left dome of the diaphragm and holds a rich blood supply.

Question 11429. A constructed illustration: a blow lands on the right side over the lowest ribs, and the described radiograph shows a break of the tenth rib there. Which large organ lies in that zone? The answer is the liver, whose right lobe rises high beneath the right dome of the diaphragm.

Question 11430. Which of these pairs belongs together: the eleventh and twelfth ribs with the kidney, the first rib with the stomach, the fifth rib with the bladder, or the seventh rib with the rectum? The answer is the eleventh and twelfth ribs with the kidney, for their lower ends lie over the upper pole of the kidney behind.

Question 11431. A described chest radiograph shows a lung separated from the chest wall by a band of air, with no lung markings beyond the edge of the lung. Which space is filled with air? The answer is the pleural cavity, which opened when the pleura and the chest wall were torn.

Question 11432. A described radiograph after a sudden stop in a collision shows the shadow of the aorta widened at one point. Where does that vessel tear? The answer is where the arch becomes the descending aorta, just beyond the short band joining it to the pulmonary artery.

Question 11433. A constructed illustration: a dislocated shoulder has been reduced, and afterward the person cannot lift the arm and reports numbness over the outer deltoid. Which structure was injured? The answer is the axillary nerve, which is stretched as it wraps around the neck of the humerus.

Question 11434. A person twists the knee while running and afterwards the joint fills with blood and gives way when turning. Which ligament is most likely torn: the anterior cruciate ligament, the posterior cruciate ligament, the medial collateral ligament, or the lateral collateral ligament? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament, which stops the tibia from sliding forward and rotating too far.

Question 11435. A blow lands on the outer side of the knee and forces the joint inward, opening its inner side. Which ligament is torn, and which structure of the same side is injured next? The answer is the medial collateral ligament, followed by the medial meniscus, since the three structures at that corner are linked.

Question 11436. Which structure lies between the femur and the tibia at the edges of the joint, is shaped like a letter C on one side, and tears when the knee is twisted while bearing weight? The answer is the meniscus. Its torn fragment may block straightening of the knee.

Question 11437. Which structure is torn when a sudden push-off is followed by a snap at the back of the heel, a gap that can be felt, and inability to stand on tiptoe? The answer is the Achilles tendon. It carries the force of the calf muscles to the heel bone, and squeezing the calf no longer pulls the foot down.

Question 11438. Which muscle of the rotator cuff is torn most often, and what movement does it begin? The answer is the supraspinatus, which begins abduction of the shoulder and passes beneath the arch formed by the acromion and the coracoacromial ligament. Pain in the middle of the arc of lifting the arm is the usual complaint.

Question 11439. A constructed illustration: a skier falls holding a pole, and the thumb is forced away from the hand at the joint between the thumb and the palm. Which ligament is torn? The answer is the ulnar collateral ligament of the thumb, which lies on the inner side of that joint and may fold back as it tears.

Question 11440. Which ligament runs beneath the head of the talus, from the heel bone to the boat-shaped bone, and holds up the arch of the foot? The answer is the plantar calcaneonavicular ligament, also called the spring ligament. When it gives way, the head of the talus drops and the arch flattens.

Question 11441. A described radiograph shows a break across the neck of the fibula, and the person cannot lift the foot and cannot feel a light touch over the outer side of the leg. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the common fibular nerve, which winds around the neck of the fibula just under the skin.

Question 11442. A constructed illustration: a child falls onto the elbow and a described radiograph shows the small bony prominence on the inner side broken away and displaced downward. Which nerve may be injured? The answer is the ulnar nerve, which passes in a groove behind that prominence.

Question 11443. Which nerve lies on the outer surface of the serratus anterior muscle and may be injured during removal of the breast, so that the shoulder blade stands out from the chest wall? The answer is the long thoracic nerve. When the muscle it supplies is weak, the blade wings away from the chest.

Question 11444. Which of these pairs belongs together: the eleventh cranial nerve with the trapezius muscle, the fifth cranial nerve with the diaphragm, the tenth cranial nerve with the deltoid, or the seventh cranial nerve with the tongue? The answer is the eleventh cranial nerve with the trapezius, which it supplies alongside the sternocleidomastoid.

Question 11445. A described drawing of a newborn shows the arm held against the side and turned inward, with the forearm straight and the palm facing backward. Which part of the brachial plexus was damaged? The answer is the upper trunk, formed by the fifth and sixth cervical nerves, which supply the muscles lifting the arm and bending the elbow.

Question 11446. Which of these pairs belongs together: numbness of the space between the first two toes with the deep fibular nerve, numbness of the little finger with the median nerve, numbness of the thumb with the ulnar nerve, or numbness of the sole with the radial nerve? The answer is numbness between the first two toes with the deep fibular nerve.

Question 11447. Which nerve runs with the long saphenous vein in front of the inner ankle bone and supplies the skin of the inner border of the foot? The answer is the saphenous nerve, a branch of the femoral nerve that is purely sensory below the knee. It may be injured where the vein is cut for access.

Question 11448. A constructed illustration: a cut crosses the cheek in front of the ear, and the person can no longer close the eye on that side or hold food in the corner of the mouth. Which nerve has been divided? The answer is the facial nerve, which emerges from the skull behind the ear and enters the parotid gland before dividing.

Question 11449. Which structure is avulsed when the tip of a finger is struck and the straightening of the joint nearest the nail is lost, so that the tip droops? The answer is the tendon of the extensor muscle at the last joint of the finger, torn from the base of the last bone.

Question 11450. Which of these pairs belongs together: the medial collateral ligament with a blow from outside, the posterior cruciate ligament with a blow to the front of the bent knee, the anterior cruciate ligament with a blow from behind, or the lateral collateral ligament with a blow from inside? The answer is the medial collateral ligament with a blow from outside, which opens the inner side of the knee.

Question 11451. Name two places in the knee where blood collects after a tear of the anterior cruciate ligament, spoken as a list. The answer is the joint cavity and the space beneath the quadriceps muscle. The torn ligament bleeds into the joint, and the fluid spreads upward under the muscle above the knee.

Question 11452. Which structure is injured when the back of the knee is cut and the foot cannot be pushed down or turned inward, while feeling is lost over the sole? The answer is the tibial nerve, which lies in the middle of the popliteal fossa, deep to the popliteal vein and artery.

Question 11453. True or false: a break of the medial malleolus of the tibia may occur with the fibula intact when the foot is forced outward strongly. The answer is true. The deltoid ligament on the inner side either tears or pulls the medial malleolus away with it.

Question 11454. A described radiograph shows a break of the lower end of the radius with the lower fragment displaced forward rather than backward. What force caused it? The answer is a fall onto the back of the bent wrist, and the displaced fragment may press on the median nerve in the carpal tunnel.

Question 11455. Which of these structures lies immediately behind the medial malleolus, in a tunnel that also carries the tibial artery: the tibial nerve, the saphenous nerve, the deep fibular nerve, or the sural nerve? The answer is the tibial nerve, which passes beneath the fibrous band holding the ankle tendons in place.

Question 11456. Which membrane suspends the spleen, the stomach, and the transverse colon from the structures lying behind the abdomen, and which two of them hang freely? The answer is the peritoneum, which forms their mesenteries. The stomach and the transverse colon are the mobile two.

Question 11457. A described specimen shows a long bone with a break that spirals around the shaft after a twisting force. Why does it spiral? The answer is that the crack follows the direction of the force, and the fibers of the bone run in a spiral around the shaft.

Question 11458. A described radiograph shows the head of the humerus lying below and in front of the socket of the shoulder blade after a fall. Which structure on the front of the head is at risk? The answer is the axillary artery and the nerves of the axillary region, which lie against it.

Question 11459. A described section shows a break of the shaft of the radius and the ulna at the same level, with the two bones bowed toward each other. Why do the fragments come together? The answer is that the membrane joining the bones pulls them together, and the muscles of the forearm shorten the gap.

Question 11460. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior dislocation of the hip with the limb held turned outward, the posterior dislocation of the hip with the limb held turned outward, the anterior dislocation of the shoulder with the arm held against the chest, or the posterior dislocation of the elbow with the forearm straight? The answer is the anterior dislocation of the hip with the limb turned outward.

Question 11461. A constructed illustration: a worker lifts heavy loads for years and reports pain over the front of the elbow that worsens with gripping. Which structure is strained? The answer is the common tendon of the flexor muscles, which attaches to the medial epicondyle of the humerus.

Question 11462. Which statement about a torn meniscus is correct: the torn fragment may block the knee so that it cannot straighten, the knee gives way only when standing still, the swelling appears only after several weeks, or the joint line is tender on the outer side only? The answer is that the fragment may block the knee so that it cannot straighten.

Question 11463. A described radiograph shows a break across the neck of the femur with the head staying in its socket. Which two features of the limb are seen, and why? The answer is shortening and turning outward. The muscles around the hip pull the shaft upward while the head stays in place.

Question 11464. Which statement about a sprained ankle is correct: the swelling is greatest below and in front of the outer ankle bone, the swelling is greatest above the inner ankle bone, the foot cannot be turned outward, or the heel cannot bear weight at all? The answer is that the swelling is greatest below and in front of the outer ankle bone.

Question 11465. A described cross section of the lower leg shows four compartments separated by sheets of deep fascia. Which statement is correct: the front compartment holds the muscle that lifts the foot, the outer compartment holds the calf muscles, or the back compartment holds the muscle that turns the foot outward? The answer is that the front compartment holds the muscle that lifts the foot.

Question 11466. Which structure is torn when a direct blow to the front of the thigh causes the muscle to tear within its sheath, and a firm swelling appears that does not cross the knee? The answer is the quadriceps muscle, since the sheath holds the blood inside and the knee can still be straightened.

Question 11467. Which statement about the knee is correct: the posterior cruciate ligament prevents the tibia from sliding backward, the anterior cruciate ligament prevents backward sliding, the medial collateral ligament prevents the knee from bending, or the lateral collateral ligament attaches to the medial meniscus? The answer is that the posterior cruciate ligament prevents the tibia from sliding backward.

Question 11468. What is the name given to the condition in which the pressure inside a closed compartment of the limb rises until the tissues within it are starved of blood? The answer is compartment syndrome. The fascia around the compartment cannot stretch, so the swelling raises the pressure, and pain on stretching the muscles comes first.

Question 11469. Which structure passes down the back of the thigh from the pelvis and can be pressed against the bone by a broken shaft of the femur? The answer is the sciatic nerve, which leaves the pelvis below the piriformis muscle and runs down beneath the hamstrings.

Question 11470. A described radiograph shows a break through the odontoid process. Which movement of the head becomes unsafe, and why? The answer is turning, because that peg is the pivot around which the first cervical vertebra and the head rotate on the second. Losing that pivot lets the first vertebra slide forward on the second.

Question 11471. A described radiograph shows a break of the dens with the fragment still attached to the body of the second cervical vertebra. Which statement is correct: the break lies at the base of the dens, the break lies in the arch of the first vertebra, the break lies through the third vertebra, or the break lies through the spinous process? The answer is that the break lies at the base of the dens.

Question 11472. Which statement about the ligaments of the vertebral column is correct: the ligaments joining the bodies run both in front of and behind them, the ligament joining the spines is the strongest, the ligament joining the arches is elastic, or the discs lie between the spines? The answer is that the ligaments joining the vertebral bodies run both in front of and behind them.

Question 11473. Which tissue forms the outer covering of a bone and carries the vessels that nourish its surface, and which tissue lines the marrow cavity? The answer is the periosteum outside and the endosteum within. The periosteum is stripped from the bone in a severe injury, which is why a bare piece may die.

Question 11474. Which of these bones is most often broken by a fall onto the outstretched hand in a young adult: the scaphoid, the calcaneus, the patella, or the clavicle? The answer is the scaphoid, which lies directly in the line of force from the palm and takes the weight through its narrow waist.

Question 11475. Which of these pairs belongs together: the clavicle with the middle third, the patella with the upper third of the thigh, the calcaneus with the middle of the leg, or the olecranon with the middle of the forearm? The answer is the clavicle with the middle third, while the olecranon belongs to the elbow.

Question 11476. A described section of a bone shows a dense outer layer and a spongy inner layer arranged along the lines of force. Which tissue is which, and why does the arrangement matter? The answer is compact bone outside and cancellous bone within, whose spicules lie along the directions in which the bone carries load.

Question 11477. Which statement about a break of a long bone in a child is correct: the bone may bend or crack on one side only because it is more flexible, it always breaks cleanly across, it never disrupts growth, or it never heals? The answer is that the bone may bend or crack on one side only, which is a greenstick injury.

Question 11478. A described radiograph shows a break across the shaft of the tibia that has opened through the skin. Why is that break more serious than a closed one? The answer is that the wound lets organisms reach the bone, and the bone loses the covering that carries its blood.

Question 11479. Which structure lies between the two rows of carpal bones and cushions the wrist, and may be dislocated in a fall onto the palm? The answer is the lunate, which lies in the middle of the wrist and bears the force passing from the hand to the radius.

Question 11480. A described specimen shows a joint whose surfaces are covered by smooth gristle, with a cavity between them and a capsule with a lining around the whole. Which kind of joint is described, and what does the lining secrete? The answer is a synovial joint, whose lining secretes synovial fluid to nourish and lubricate the surfaces.

Question 11481. Which of these structures is the weakest link in the shoulder and gives way most often when the arm is forced upward: the tendon of the supraspinatus, the head of the humerus, the acromion, or the clavicle? The answer is the tendon of the supraspinatus, which passes beneath the acromion through a narrow space.

Question 11482. True or false: a break of the first rib is dangerous mainly because the structures crossing it lie close by. The answer is true. The nerves and vessels of the arm pass over the first rib, and the dome of the pleura rises above it into the base of the neck.

Question 11483. Which structure forms the socket of the hip joint, and which three bones join to make it? The answer is the acetabulum, formed by the ilium, the ischium, and the pubis. The three parts fuse during the teenage years into a deep cup.

Question 11484. Which structure gives way first when the ankle is forced into eversion, so that the inner side of the joint opens: the deltoid ligament, the anterior talofibular ligament, the calcaneofibular ligament, or the posterior talofibular ligament? The answer is the deltoid ligament, which either tears or pulls the medial malleolus away.

Question 11485. Which structure joins the patella below and attaches to the tibia, completing the pull of the quadriceps? The answer is the patellar ligament. It is a continuation of the quadriceps tendon across the knee, and the patella increases the leverage of the muscle.

Question 11486. Which statement about the hip joint is correct: it is a ball and socket joint with the deepest socket in the body, it is a hinge joint, it is a saddle joint, or it is a plane joint? The answer is that it is a ball and socket joint, and its socket is deepened by a ring of fibrocartilage around the rim.

Question 11487. A blow to the upper arm just below the shoulder causes pain and swelling in the middle of the arm. Which structure does the force press onto the bone, causing numbness over the back of the hand? The answer is the radial nerve, which lies in the spiral groove against the shaft.

Question 11488. Which statement about a torn calf muscle is correct: the pain and swelling lie in the calf and worsen when the ankle is stretched upward, the pain lies behind the knee only, the swelling crosses the knee, or the foot cannot be lifted? The answer is that the pain and swelling lie in the calf and worsen when the ankle is stretched upward.

Question 11489. Name three structures that pass through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis muscle, spoken as a sentence. The answer is the sciatic nerve, the inferior gluteal artery, and the pudendal nerve. The pudendal nerve leaves the pelvis here and then re-enters through the lesser foramen.

Question 11490. Which structure lies in the floor of the front of the elbow, between the tendon of the biceps above and the muscles of the forearm below, and contains the end of the brachial artery? The answer is the cubital fossa, whose roof is the skin and fascia and whose floor is the brachialis.

Question 11491. Which statement about the elbow is correct: the joint allows bending and straightening only, it allows turning of the palm, it is a ball and socket joint, or it has no capsule? The answer is that it allows bending and straightening only, for it is a hinge between the humerus and the ulna.

Question 11492. A described cross section of the upper arm shows a bone surrounded by muscles, with a neurovascular bundle lying on its inner side. Which structures lie in that bundle? The answer is the brachial artery, the median nerve, and the veins accompanying the artery.

Question 11493. Which statement about the knee is correct: the joint depends on ligaments and muscles rather than on the shape of its surfaces for stability, it is as stable as the hip, it is held by a single ligament, or it moves only in one plane? The answer is that it depends on ligaments and muscles rather than on the shape of its surfaces.

Question 11494. True or false: a fall onto the point of the shoulder can break the clavicle even though the blow did not touch that bone. The answer is true. The force travels along the arm and the shoulder blade to the strut of the clavicle, which gives way at its thinnest part.

Question 11495. Which structure becomes inflamed where it attaches to the outer prominence of the elbow when a sport involves repeated throwing, causing pain when the wrist is lifted? The answer is the common tendon of the extensor muscles, whose pain is felt on gripping and on lifting the back of the hand.

Question 11496. Which of these pairs belongs together: a break of the femoral neck with the medial circumflex femoral artery, a break of the clavicle with the popliteal artery, a break of the tibia with the axillary nerve, or a break of the fibula with the median nerve? The answer is a break of the femoral neck with the medial circumflex femoral artery.

Question 11497. Which statement about a dislocated shoulder is correct: the head of the humerus usually leaves its socket downward and forward, it usually leaves backward, it never damages a nerve, or it always damages the radial nerve? The answer is that the head usually leaves downward and forward, where the capsule is thinnest.

Question 11498. Which statement about fractures in older people is correct: the bone loses mass with age and breaks with less force, the bones become harder, only the joints become stiffer, or the number of bone cells increases? The answer is that the bone loses mass with age, since the fine struts inside are lost first.

Question 11499. Which structure is most often injured when a heavy weight is lifted with the back bent rather than straight: the fibrous ring of the disk between two lumbar vertebrae, the vertebral body, the spinous process, or the ligament of the arches? The answer is the fibrous ring of the disk, against which the soft center is forced backward.

Question 11500. A described radiograph of a healed bone shows a thickened ridge along the line where a crack once ran. What does this thickening represent? The answer is callus, which is new bone laid down at the site of the break and later reworked until the bone regains its shape.

To summarize, the structures of the body fail in patterns that their own anatomy makes predictable. The scaphoid and the femoral head lose their blood supply because it enters at one end, while the radial nerve fails at the spiral groove, the axillary nerve at the surgical neck, the ulnar nerve behind the medial epicondyle, and the common fibular nerve at the fibular neck. The diaphragm refers pain to the shoulder through the phrenic nerve, the appendix begins with pain around the navel through the tenth thoracic segment, and the ureter sends pain from the loin to the groin. Visceral pain is dull and poorly localized, while the parietal pleura, the peritoneum, and the joint capsule give sharp pain at the site of injury. The hip refers pain to the knee through the obturator nerve. This chapter is educational material and does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, or clinical training.
