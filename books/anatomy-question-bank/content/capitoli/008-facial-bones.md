# Chapter 8 — Facial Bones

The bones of the face give the skull its shape, hold the teeth, frame the orbits and the nasal cavity, and take the brunt of most injuries to the head. This chapter works through the ethmoid bone and the paired bones of the face, the maxilla, the zygomatic, the nasal, the lacrimal, the palatine and the inferior nasal concha, together with the unpaired mandible and vomer. It holds one hundred questions, and each one states its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind. The questions move from the air cells of the ethmoid to the lower concha, and they close with a short review of the facial skeleton as a whole. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 701. Which bone of the skull contains the cribriform plate, the crista galli and the perpendicular plate of the nasal septum? The answer is the ethmoid. It is a single bone at the front of the skull base, and it also sends a thin plate into each orbit and into each side of the nasal cavity. Its many small openings give the cribriform plate its sieve-like name.

Question 702. Which structures pass through the many small openings of the cribriform plate: the optic nerves, the fibers of smell, the ophthalmic arteries, or the maxillary nerves? The answer is the fibers of smell. They run from the roof of the nasal cavity through the plate to the olfactory bulb above it. A fracture of the plate tears them and takes away the sense of smell on that side.

Question 703. A described radiograph of the sinuses shows a cluster of small air cells lying between the upper part of the nasal cavity and the medial wall of the orbit. Which bone holds that cluster? The answer is the ethmoid. The cells sit inside the ethmoid labyrinth, which fills the space between the two orbits and is separated from each orbit by a plate no thicker than heavy paper.

Question 704. Which bone lies immediately lateral to the ethmoid air cells and forms the greater part of the medial wall of the orbit? The answer is the ethmoid itself, through its orbital plate. That plate is extremely thin, and a blow to the eye can break it and push the orbital contents into the air cells.

Question 705. Which bone of the skull carries the crista galli, the bony ridge that gives attachment to the falx cerebri? The answer is the ethmoid. The ridge rises in the midline from the cribriform plate and lies inside the cranial cavity. The frontal bone and the sphenoid lie near it but do not bear it.

Question 706. A constructed illustration: after a blow across the bridge of the nose, clear fluid drips from one nostril and the sense of smell is lost on that side. Which part of which bone has most likely been broken? The answer is the cribriform plate of the ethmoid. The break opens the space around the brain into the nose and tears the fibers of smell.

Question 707. Which of these pairs belongs together: the orbital plate with the medial wall of the orbit, the perpendicular plate with the hard palate, the crista galli with the middle ear, or the vomer with the orbit? The answer is the orbital plate with the medial wall of the orbit. The perpendicular plate lies in the nasal septum, and the crista galli is a ridge inside the skull.

Question 708. Which of these are parts of the ethmoid bone: the superior and middle nasal conchae, the inferior nasal concha, the vomer, or the perpendicular plate of the palatine bone? The answer is the superior and middle nasal conchae. They are thin curled plates that project into the nasal cavity from the medial wall. The inferior concha is a separate bone of the face.

Question 709. Name two bony projections of the ethmoid bone that curl into the nasal cavity, spoken as a list. The answer is the superior nasal concha and the middle nasal concha. Each is a delicate shelf covered by mucous membrane, and the air cells of the ethmoid open beneath the middle one. The lower concha, the largest of the three, is a bone in its own right.

Question 710. A described coronal section through the orbit shows a paper-thin plate between the orbit and a cluster of air cells, and below it a second plate over an air-filled cavity. Which two bones form those walls? The answer is the ethmoid above and the maxilla below. The thin plate is the orbital plate of the ethmoid, and the orbital floor is the roof of the maxillary sinus.

Question 711. Which statement about the drainage of the ethmoid air cells is correct: the anterior and middle cells open into the middle meatus and the posterior cells into the superior meatus, all of them open into the inferior meatus, the sphenoethmoidal recess, or the maxillary sinus? The answer is the first, and the inferior meatus receives the nasolacrimal duct instead.

Question 712. Which bony plate of the ethmoid lies in the midline and forms the upper part of the nasal septum? The answer is the perpendicular plate. It hangs downward from the cribriform plate and meets the vomer below and the septal cartilage in front.

Question 713. Which bone of the skull lies between the two orbits, sends a thin plate into each orbit, a second plate into each side of the nasal cavity and a ridge upward into the floor of the cranial cavity? The answer is the ethmoid, and no other bone of the skull reaches into the orbit, the nose and the cranial cavity at once.

Question 714. True or false: the ethmoid is counted among the bones of the facial skeleton. The answer is false. It is one of the cranial bones, grouped with the frontal, the sphenoid and the occipital bones, even though it takes part in the walls of both orbits and both sides of the nasal cavity.

Question 715. Which bone forms the upper jaw and carries the sockets of the upper teeth? The answer is the maxilla. It is a paired bone, and the two maxillae meet in the midline to form the front of the hard palate and the lower part of the nasal aperture. Each one also helps to form the floor of the orbit.

Question 716. Which of these is one of the four processes of the maxilla: the frontal process, the temporal process, the mastoid process, or the styloid process? The answer is the frontal process. The other three processes of the maxilla are the zygomatic process, the alveolar process and the palatine process. The temporal, mastoid and styloid processes all belong to the temporal bone.

Question 717. Which structure lies immediately in front of the lacrimal bone on the medial wall of the orbit, and to which bone does it belong? The answer is the anterior lacrimal crest, which belongs to the maxilla. The posterior lacrimal crest belongs to the lacrimal bone. The lacrimal sac rests in the groove between the two crests.

Question 718. A described radiograph taken with the head tilted back shows two large dark cavities below the orbits, one on each side, with the teeth below them and the nasal cavity between them. Which structures are shown? The answer is the maxillary sinuses. Each is the largest of the paranasal sinuses, and its floor lies close to the roots of the upper molar teeth.

Question 719. A constructed illustration: an infection around the roots of an upper molar tooth causes pain and tenderness over the cheek. Which air-filled space lies immediately above those roots: the maxillary sinus, the frontal sinus, the ethmoid air cells, or the sphenoid sinus? The answer is the maxillary sinus. Its floor carries the sockets of the upper teeth, and its roof is the floor of the orbit.

Question 720. Which opening in the maxilla transmits the infraorbital nerve and vessels onto the face? The answer is the infraorbital foramen, which lies just below the orbital rim. The nerve within it is a branch of the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve. The mental foramen lies in the mandible and the incisive foramen lies behind the upper incisor teeth.

Question 721. Which small bony point projects forward in the midline just below the front of the nasal cavity? The answer is the anterior nasal spine, a projection of the maxilla. It gives attachment to the cartilage of the nasal septum and lies at the lower edge of the pear-shaped opening of the nose. The posterior nasal spine belongs to the palatine bone instead.

Question 722. Which of these bones does the maxilla not articulate with: the frontal bone, the zygomatic bone, the temporal bone, or the palatine bone? The answer is the temporal bone. The maxilla meets the frontal, the ethmoid, the nasal, the lacrimal, the zygomatic, the vomer, the palatine and the inferior nasal concha, and it also meets its fellow of the opposite side in the hard palate.

Question 723. Which structure lies immediately behind the palatine process of the maxilla in the roof of the mouth? The answer is the horizontal plate of the palatine bone. The two palatine processes of the maxillae form the front two thirds of the hard palate, and the horizontal plates of the palatine bones form the back third. The vomer and the ethmoid lie above them, not behind them.

Question 724. A constructed illustration: a blow to the middle of the face leaves the upper teeth and the hard palate moving together while the rest of the skull stays still. Which pattern of break is that? The answer is a break that runs above the roots of the upper teeth and across the palate, crossing the lower parts of the maxillae and the base of the nasal septum.

Question 725. Which process of the maxilla carries the sockets of the upper teeth: the alveolar process, the palatine process, the frontal process, or the zygomatic process? The answer is the alveolar process, the thick ridge along the lower border of the bone. It is gradually lost after the teeth are lost, which is why the lower face shortens with age.

Question 726. The infraorbital groove and canal, which carry the infraorbital nerve forward beneath the eye, lie within which bone? The answer is the maxilla. The groove begins at the back of the orbital floor, and the canal opens at the infraorbital foramen on the front of the face.

Question 727. True or false: the maxillary sinus opens into the inferior meatus of the nose. The answer is false. It opens into the middle meatus, where the frontal sinus and the anterior ethmoid air cells also drain. The inferior meatus receives the nasolacrimal duct, which carries tears from the eye.

Question 728. Name two bones, other than the maxilla, that form part of the medial wall of the orbit, spoken as a list. The answer is the lacrimal bone and the ethmoid. The lacrimal bone lies in front and the ethmoid behind it, and the body of the sphenoid completes the wall at the very back of the orbit.

Question 729. Which bone of the skull is the only one that is movable, being joined to the rest of the skull by a pair of synovial joints? The answer is the mandible. It meets the temporal bone on each side at the temporomandibular joint, where a disc divides the joint cavity. No other bone of the skull has a freely movable joint.

Question 730. Which of these is not a part of the mandible: the coronoid process, the condylar process, the styloid process, or the alveolar process? The answer is the styloid process, which is a projection of the temporal bone. The coronoid process gives attachment to the temporalis muscle, and the condylar process forms the joint with the skull.

Question 731. Which opening on the outer surface of the mandible transmits the nerve that supplies the skin of the chin and the lower lip? The answer is the mental foramen, which lies below the second premolar tooth. It carries the mental nerve, which is the terminal branch of the inferior alveolar nerve after its long course through the bone.

Question 732. Which small bony spine stands on the inner surface of the ramus of the mandible just above the mandibular foramen, and which ligament is attached to it? The answer is the lingula, to which the sphenomandibular ligament is attached. The lingula is a useful guide during a described dissection, because the foramen lies just behind and below it.

Question 733. A constructed illustration: some days after the removal of an impacted third molar tooth, the lower lip and the chin on that side feel numb. Which nerve has been injured, and where does it run? The answer is the inferior alveolar nerve, which runs inside the mandibular canal below the roots of the molar teeth. It leaves the canal at the mental foramen as the mental nerve.

Question 734. Which muscle pulls the mandible forward and helps to open the mouth: the masseter, the temporalis, the lateral pterygoid, or the medial pterygoid? The answer is the lateral pterygoid. The masseter, the temporalis and the medial pterygoid all pull the mandible upward, and together they close the mouth against resistance.

Question 735. A described radiograph of the lower jaw shows a dark channel running forward inside the bone from an opening on the inner surface of the ramus to a small opening below the second premolar tooth. Which structure is shown? The answer is the mandibular canal, which carries the inferior alveolar nerve and the vessels that accompany it through the body of the bone.

Question 736. On which surfaces of the angle of the mandible do the masseter and the medial pterygoid muscles attach? The answer is that the masseter attaches to the lateral surface and the medial pterygoid to the medial surface. Together the two muscles form a sling at the angle, which is why the angle is rough and turned outward in a person who grinds the teeth.

Question 737. Which of these pairs belongs together: the coronoid process with the temporalis muscle, the condylar process with the masseter, the angle with the lateral pterygoid, or the mental spine with the medial pterygoid? The answer is the coronoid process with the temporalis. The masseter and the medial pterygoid both attach at the angle, and the lateral pterygoid attaches to the neck of the mandible.

Question 738. True or false: the whole mandible is laid down in membrane bone from the mesenchyme of the first pharyngeal arch. The answer is false. The body and the ramus do form in membrane, but the condyle develops from a secondary cartilage that ossifies endochondrally, which is what allows the jaw to keep growing at the joint after birth.

Question 739. Which statement about the temporomandibular joint is correct: its articular surfaces are covered by fibrocartilage and a disc divides the joint, its surfaces are covered by hyaline cartilage, it has no disc, or it is a fibrous joint? The answer is the first. The disc divides the joint in two, and the upper compartment allows the head of the mandible to glide forward.

Question 740. A constructed illustration: a person who has yawned widely cannot close the mouth, and the chin has moved forward. In which direction has the head of the mandible left its socket relative to the articular tubercle of the temporal bone? The answer is forward, so that it now lies in front of the articular tubercle.

Question 741. Which ridge on the inner surface of the mandible marks the attachment of the mylohyoid muscle and separates the sublingual fossa above from the submandibular fossa below? The answer is the mylohyoid line. It runs obliquely down and forward on each side, and the muscle it carries forms the floor of the mouth.

Question 742. The mandibular foramen, on the inner surface of the ramus, transmits which structures: the inferior alveolar nerve and vessels, the mental nerve, the lingual nerve alone, or the maxillary artery? The answer is the inferior alveolar nerve and vessels. The lingual nerve passes close to it but does not enter it, and the mental nerve is its continuation at the front of the jaw.

Question 743. A described lateral radiograph of the jaw shows a rounded head on a narrow neck, a pointed projection in front of it and a deep notch between the two. Which bone is shown, and what are the two projections? The answer is the mandible, with the condylar process behind and the coronoid process in front, separated by the mandibular notch.

Question 744. Which statement about the mental nerve is correct: it is a branch of the inferior alveolar nerve and supplies the skin of the chin and the lower lip, it is a branch of the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve, it supplies the muscles of the tongue, or it runs the length of the mandibular canal? The answer is the first, and it belongs to the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve.

Question 745. Which bone forms the prominence of the cheek and the outer part of the orbital margin below the eye? The answer is the zygomatic bone. It is a paired bone shaped roughly like a small three-pointed star, and it joins the frontal bone above, the maxilla in front and the temporal bone behind.

Question 746. The zygomatic arch is formed by the meeting of which two processes: the temporal process of the zygomatic bone and the zygomatic process of the temporal bone, the frontal process of the maxilla and the mastoid process, the zygomatic process of the frontal bone and the styloid process, or the coronoid process and the condylar process? The answer is the first pair.

Question 747. Which muscle arises from the lower border of the zygomatic arch and runs down to the outer surface of the angle of the mandible? The answer is the masseter. The temporalis passes deep to the same arch on its way to the coronoid process, so the arch gives origin below and passage behind.

Question 748. A described radiograph of the facial bones shows a slender bar of bone running forward from the side of the skull to the front of the cheek, with a faint shadow behind and below it. Which structure is shown, and which muscle lies deep to it? The answer is the zygomatic arch, with the temporalis passing deep to it.

Question 749. A constructed illustration: a blow to the cheek leaves that side of the face flatter than the other, and the skin over the cheek and the temple feels numb. Which bone is injured, and which nerve is involved? The answer is the zygomatic bone, with branches of the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve, which pass through the small openings on its surface.

Question 750. The small zygomaticofacial and zygomaticotemporal openings on the zygomatic bone transmit branches of which nerve: the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve, the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve, the facial nerve, or the ophthalmic division of the trigeminal nerve? The answer is the maxillary division. The facial nerve supplies the muscles of the face but does not run through these bony openings.

Question 751. In a described cross section through the orbit, which two bones form the lateral wall, and where does the temporal fossa lie in relation to that wall? The answer is the zygomatic bone in front and the greater wing of the sphenoid behind. The lateral wall separates the orbit from the temporal fossa, which lies lateral to it.

Question 752. Which process of the zygomatic bone runs backward to meet the zygomatic process of the temporal bone? The answer is the temporal process. The frontal process runs upward to the frontal bone and the maxillary process runs forward to the maxilla, so the bone meets three of its neighbors by three named processes.

Question 753. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the eye, a person sees double when looking upward, and the skin of the cheek and the upper lip feels numb. Which part of which bone is most likely broken? The answer is the floor of the orbit, which is formed mostly by the maxilla. The inferior rectus muscle and the infraorbital nerve lie against it and can be caught in the break.

Question 754. True or false: the zygomatic bone forms part of the lateral wall of the orbit. The answer is true. It occupies the front of the lateral wall and, along its lower edge, part of the floor of the orbit as well. The greater wing of the sphenoid completes the lateral wall behind it.

Question 755. A described dry skull shows a small opening on the orbital surface of the zygomatic bone; which nerve passes through it: the zygomatic nerve, the frontal nerve, the lacrimal nerve, or the infraorbital nerve? The answer is the zygomatic nerve, a branch of the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve. It divides inside the bone into the zygomaticofacial and zygomaticotemporal branches.

Question 756. Which paired bones form the bridge of the nose between the two maxillae and below the frontal bone? The answer is the nasal bones. They are thick above, where they meet the frontal bone, and thin below, which is why the lower bridge breaks most easily.

Question 757. With which of these bones does a nasal bone articulate: the frontal bone, the lacrimal bone, the zygomatic bone, or the palatine bone? The answer is the frontal bone. The nasal bone also meets the ethmoid, the maxilla and its fellow of the opposite side, but it never reaches the lacrimal bone, which the frontal process of the maxilla keeps away from it.

Question 758. A described lateral radiograph of the face shows a break in the thin bone over the bridge of the nose, with the lower fragment pushed backward and the profile flattened. Which bone is fractured? The answer is the nasal bone. The break lies across the thin lower part of the bone, where the bridge is least supported.

Question 759. A constructed illustration: a person struck across the face has a swollen, tender bridge of the nose that is shifted to one side, and breathing through that nostril is difficult. Which facial bone is most commonly injured in this way? The answer is the nasal bone, which is the most frequently fractured bone of the face.

Question 760. Which structure lies immediately above the nasal bones, and which lies immediately behind them in the midline? The answer is the frontal bone above and the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid behind. The nasal bones form only the front of the bridge, while the septum behind them is part bone and part cartilage.

Question 761. A described anterior view of a dry skull shows a pear-shaped opening in the middle of the face, below the nasal bones, bounded on each side by the maxillae. What is that opening called, and what enters the nasal cavity through it? The answer is the piriform aperture, through which air passes into the nasal cavity.

Question 762. Which of these structures lies in the median plane of the face: the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, the middle nasal concha, the lacrimal bone, or the zygomatic bone? The answer is the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid. The conchae and the lacrimal and zygomatic bones are paired and lie on each side of the midline.

Question 763. Which bone separates the nasal bone from the lacrimal bone: the maxilla, the ethmoid, the frontal bone, or the vomer? The answer is the maxilla, through its frontal process. That process rises between the nasal bone in front and the lacrimal bone behind, so the two never touch each other.

Question 764. Which is the smallest and most fragile bone of the facial skeleton? The answer is the lacrimal bone. It is a thin scale of bone in the medial wall of the orbit, no larger than a fingernail, and it is easily crushed by a blow at the inner corner of the eye.

Question 765. The lacrimal sac rests in a groove on the medial wall of the orbit; which bone contains that groove? The answer is the lacrimal bone. The groove lies between the anterior lacrimal crest, which belongs to the maxilla, and the posterior lacrimal crest, which belongs to the lacrimal bone itself.

Question 766. The anterior lacrimal crest is a feature of which bone: the maxilla, the lacrimal bone, the ethmoid, or the frontal bone? The answer is the maxilla. A ridge on the frontal process of the maxilla forms the front boundary of the lacrimal fossa, and the lacrimal bone forms the back.

Question 767. A constructed illustration: an infant has a constantly watering eye, and the passage that should carry the tears into the nose is blocked. Which bone holds the upper end of that passage? The answer is the lacrimal bone, whose groove holds the lacrimal sac, and the bony canal below is formed by the lacrimal bone and the maxilla.

Question 768. A described dry skull shows a small thin plate in the medial wall of the orbit, bounded above by the frontal bone, in front by the maxilla, behind by the ethmoid and below by the inferior nasal concha. Which bone is it? The answer is the lacrimal bone, and those four are the bones that it meets.

Question 769. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lacrimal bone with the nasolacrimal duct, the vomer with the orbit, the palatine bone with the prominence of the cheek, or the inferior nasal concha with the cribriform plate? The answer is the lacrimal bone with the nasolacrimal duct. The vomer lies in the septum, the palatine bone in the palate and the concha in the nasal wall.

Question 770. Which thin bone of the facial skeleton lies between the maxilla in front and the ethmoid behind in the medial wall of the orbit, and carries a groove for the lacrimal sac? The answer is the lacrimal bone. Despite its small size it is a bone in its own right, and it also helps to form the wall of the nasal cavity.

Question 771. A described dry skull shows a delicate plate on the medial wall of the orbit carrying two faint ridges with a hollow between them, and the hollow leads downward into a canal. Which bone is shown, and what does the hollow hold? The answer is the lacrimal bone, and the hollow is the lacrimal fossa, which holds the lacrimal sac.

Question 772. Which L-shaped bone forms the back part of the hard palate and part of the lateral wall of the nasal cavity? The answer is the palatine bone. Its horizontal plate completes the hard palate behind the maxilla, and its perpendicular plate rises to help form the side wall of the nose.

Question 773. The greater palatine foramen, which lies at the back of the hard palate between the maxilla and the palatine bone, transmits which structures: the greater palatine nerve and vessels, the nasopalatine nerve, the inferior alveolar nerve, or the infraorbital nerve? The answer is the greater palatine nerve and vessels, which descend through the greater palatine canal to the palate.

Question 774. Which opening in the skull is formed by a notch in the palatine bone closed above by the body of the sphenoid, and what does it transmit? The answer is the sphenopalatine foramen, which transmits the sphenopalatine artery and the nasopalatine nerve.

Question 775. A described dry skull shows a horizontal plate at the back of the hard palate with a small spine pointing backward in the midline and two or three small openings near its outer edge. Which bone is shown? The answer is the palatine bone. The spine is the posterior nasal spine, and the openings are the greater and lesser palatine foramina.

Question 776. A constructed illustration: a person who has fallen on the face has a split across the back of the hard palate, and liquid passes from the mouth into the nose. Which bone forms the part of the palate behind that split? The answer is the palatine bone. Its horizontal plate lies behind the palatine process of the maxilla, and the split follows the suture between the two.

Question 777. Which of these pairs belongs together: the horizontal plate of the palatine bone with the hard palate, the perpendicular plate of the palatine bone with the prominence of the cheek, the pyramidal process with the cribriform plate, or the sphenoidal process with the mandible? The answer is the horizontal plate with the hard palate. The perpendicular plate belongs to the side wall of the nose.

Question 778. Which part of the palatine bone helps to form the floor of the orbit: the orbital process, the horizontal plate, the pyramidal process, or the sphenoidal process? The answer is the orbital process. It rises from the perpendicular plate at the back of the orbit and meets the maxilla and the sphenoid, forming a small part of the orbital floor.

Question 779. A described section through the hard palate shows a thick plate in front and a thinner plate behind, with a canal running upward between them near the outer edge. Which two bones are shown, and what is the canal? The answer is the maxilla in front and the palatine bone behind, with the greater palatine canal between them.

Question 780. Which bone forms the lower and back part of the nasal septum and lies in the midline like a plowshare? The answer is the vomer. It is a single unpaired bone, thin and flat, and its name is the Latin word for a plowshare.

Question 781. With how many bones does the vomer articulate: two, four, six, or eight? The answer is six. It meets the sphenoid above and behind, the ethmoid above and in front, the two maxillae and the two palatine bones. Its upper edge carries a groove that fits the rostrum of the sphenoid.

Question 782. Which bone lies immediately above the vomer in the nasal septum, and which small part of the sphenoid lies immediately above that? The answer is the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid above the vomer, with the rostrum of the sphenoid above and behind it. The three lie one above another in the midline.

Question 783. A described midline section through the skull shows a thin flat bone standing in the nasal septum below a second thin plate, with the sphenoid behind and above them. Which bone is the lower one? The answer is the vomer. The upper plate is the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, and the septal cartilage lies in front of both.

Question 784. A constructed illustration: a person breathes poorly through one side of the nose, and the partition between the two sides is bent toward the other side. Which two bones are most often involved in that bend? The answer is the vomer and the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid. The septal cartilage may be bent as well, and a slight deviation is common with no symptoms at all.

Question 785. Name the two bones that form the bony part of the nasal septum, spoken as a list. The answer is the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid and the vomer. The ethmoid forms the upper and front part of the bony septum and the vomer the lower and back part, and the septal cartilage completes the septum in front of them.

Question 786. Which statement about the vomer is correct: it is a single unpaired bone of the facial skeleton, it is a paired bone of the facial skeleton, it forms the upper part of the nasal septum, or it articulates with the mandible? The answer is the first. With the mandible it is one of the two unpaired bones of the facial skeleton.

Question 787. True or false: the vomer alone forms the whole of the nasal septum. The answer is false. The perpendicular plate of the ethmoid forms the upper and front part and the vomer the lower and back part, with the septal cartilage in front of both.

Question 788. Which bone of the facial skeleton lies lowest in the lateral wall of the nasal cavity and is not a part of the ethmoid? The answer is the inferior nasal concha. It is a separate bone, curled like a scroll, and the space beneath it is the inferior meatus.

Question 789. Which structure opens into the inferior meatus of the nose, the space below the inferior nasal concha: the nasolacrimal duct, the maxillary sinus, the frontal sinus, or the posterior ethmoid air cells? The answer is the nasolacrimal duct, which carries the tears from the eye down into the nose.

Question 790. With which group of bones does the inferior nasal concha articulate: the maxilla, the lacrimal bone, the ethmoid and the palatine bone, the vomer, the sphenoid and the frontal bone, the zygomatic bone and the mandible, or the occipital and temporal bones? The answer is the first group. Each of those four bones gives the concha a narrow surface of contact.

Question 791. A described coronal section through the nasal cavity shows a curled shelf of bone on the lateral wall with an air space below it, and a duct opening into that space. Which concha is shown? The answer is the inferior nasal concha. The space below it is the inferior meatus, and the duct that opens there is the nasolacrimal duct.

Question 792. A constructed illustration: a person has constant blockage of one side of the nose, and the curled bone on that lateral wall is enlarged and presses against the septum. Which bone is enlarged? The answer is the inferior nasal concha, whose mucous membrane swells readily and can obstruct the airway on one side.

Question 793. Which of these pairs belongs together: the inferior nasal concha with the inferior meatus, the middle nasal concha with the inferior meatus, the vomer with the orbit, or the lacrimal bone with the hard palate? The answer is the inferior nasal concha with the inferior meatus. The middle concha overhangs the middle meatus, the vomer lies in the septum and the lacrimal bone in the orbit.

Question 794. Which process of the inferior nasal concha rises to meet the lacrimal bone at the front of its upper border? The answer is the lacrimal process. It also has a maxillary process that curves down over the opening of the nasolacrimal duct, and an ethmoidal process behind that.

Question 795. Which statement about the nasal conchae is correct: the superior and middle conchae are parts of the ethmoid and the inferior concha is a separate bone, all three are separate bones, the inferior concha is part of the ethmoid, or the middle concha is part of the maxilla? The answer is the first.

Question 796. A described computed tomography section through the face at the level of the cheekbones shows the zygomatic arch on each side, an air-filled space medial to it, and the nasal cavity in the middle. Which air-filled space lies medial to the zygomatic arch at that level? The answer is the maxillary sinus, which fills the body of the maxilla beneath the orbit.

Question 797. Which bone of the facial skeleton articulates with the greatest number of other bones: the maxilla, the zygomatic bone, the palatine bone, or the lacrimal bone? The answer is the maxilla, which meets nine other bones. It shares sutures with the frontal, the ethmoid, the nasal, the lacrimal, the zygomatic, the vomer, the palatine and the inferior nasal concha, and with the maxilla of the opposite side.

Question 798. Is the facial skeleton made of fourteen bones with six of them paired, fourteen bones with seven of them paired, twelve bones with five of them paired, or sixteen bones with eight of them paired? The answer is fourteen bones with six of them paired, the mandible and the vomer being the two that stand alone.

Question 799. Which bone lies immediately behind the lacrimal bone in the medial wall of the orbit, and which lies behind that one? The answer is the ethmoid behind the lacrimal bone, and the body of the sphenoid behind the ethmoid. The maxilla stands in front of the lacrimal bone, so the four run from front to back in that order.

Question 800. Which bone of the skull forms the upper part of the nasal septum, the medial wall of the orbit and the roof of the nasal cavity, and lies between the two orbits? The answer is the ethmoid. It is the only bone that reaches into the orbit, the nasal cavity and the floor of the cranial cavity at the same time.

To summarize, the ethmoid is a cranial bone that carries the cribriform plate, the crista galli, the perpendicular plate of the nasal septum and the orbital plates, and its air cells open into the middle and superior meatuses. The maxilla forms the upper jaw, the front of the hard palate and the floor of the orbit, its infraorbital foramen carries the infraorbital nerve, and its sinus opens into the middle meatus. The mandible is the only movable bone of the skull, and the inferior alveolar nerve runs in its canal as far as the mental foramen. The zygomatic bone builds the cheek and the front of the lateral orbital wall, while the nasal, lacrimal, palatine, vomer and inferior nasal concha complete the bridge of the nose, the medial orbital wall, the back of the hard palate, the nasal septum and the lower lateral wall of the nose. Each of these bones meets its neighbors at named sutures, and knowing those meetings by heart is what makes a described fracture or a described section readable.
