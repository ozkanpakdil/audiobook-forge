# Chapter 38 — Arteries

This chapter follows the aorta from the heart to its division in the abdomen and then traces the arteries that carry blood from the arch to the arm. It covers the ascending aorta, the aortic arch and its three branches, the carotid arteries, the subclavian arteries, the axillary artery through the armpit, and the brachial artery down to the elbow. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3701. Which great vessel begins at the aortic orifice of the left ventricle and curves backward after running upward and to the right? The answer is the ascending aorta. It lies inside the pericardium and is about 5 centimeters long. It gives off the two coronary arteries before it leaves the sac.

Question 3702. From which parts of the aortic root do the coronary arteries arise: the sinuses above the aortic valve, the pulmonary trunk, the left atrium, or the arch itself? The answer is the sinuses above the aortic valve. The right coronary artery arises from the right sinus and the left artery from the left sinus. The posterior sinus gives off no artery.

Question 3703. A described microscopic section shows an arterial wall whose middle coat is packed with elastic lamellae and holds almost no smooth muscle. Which vessel was sectioned? The answer is the aorta. It is an elastic artery, built to stretch with each heartbeat and recoil again. That elastic tissue smooths the pulse into a steadier flow.

Question 3704. In the posterior mediastinum, which structure lies immediately in front of and slightly to the right of the descending thoracic aorta? The answer is the esophagus. It descends in front of the aorta and crosses towards the left near the diaphragm. The thoracic duct lies behind it.

Question 3705. True or false: the abdominal aorta ends by dividing into the two common iliac arteries at the level of the fourth lumbar vertebra. The answer is true. The division lies a little to the left of the midline. The small middle sacral artery continues downward from that point.

Question 3706. At which level does the aorta pass from the chest into the abdomen: the tenth, the eleventh or the twelfth thoracic vertebra, or the fourth lumbar vertebra? The answer is the twelfth thoracic vertebra. The aortic opening lies in the diaphragm, slightly to the left of the midline. The thoracic duct passes through the same opening.

Question 3707. Which single unpaired branch of the abdominal aorta arises below the aortic opening and supplies the upper part of the gut? The answer is the celiac trunk. It is short and wide and divides at once into the left gastric, the splenic and the common hepatic arteries. It arises at the twelfth thoracic vertebra.

Question 3708. Which of these branches of the abdominal aorta arises at about the second lumbar vertebra: the inferior mesenteric artery, the renal arteries, the celiac trunk, or the median sacral artery? The answer is the renal arteries. The right one lies a little lower than the left. The inferior mesenteric artery arises lower down, at the third lumbar vertebra.

Question 3709. A constructed illustration: a person has high blood pressure in both arms, and a described radiograph shows notches along the lower borders of several ribs. Which segment of the aorta is narrowed? The answer is the arch just distal to the left subclavian artery. The narrowing lies where the ductus arteriosus once joined, and enlarged intercostal arteries erode the ribs. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3710. Which of these pairs belongs together: the celiac trunk with the transverse colon, the superior mesenteric artery with the small intestine, the inferior mesenteric artery with the stomach, or the renal artery with the spleen? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery with the small intestine. The celiac trunk supplies the stomach, the spleen and the liver.

Question 3711. Which small unpaired artery leaves the back of the lower abdominal aorta and runs down over the front of the sacrum? The answer is the median sacral artery. It is the last small branch of the aorta. The paired lumbar arteries leave the sides of the aorta at the levels of the lumbar vertebrae.

Question 3712. A described angiogram of the abdomen shows a single wide trunk leaving the front of the aorta and dividing at once into three branches. Which trunk is shown? The answer is the celiac trunk. Its branches are the left gastric, the splenic and the common hepatic arteries.

Question 3713. Which vein ascends in the posterior mediastinum on the right of the vertebral column while the descending thoracic aorta descends on the left? The answer is the azygos vein. It lies against the right side of the vertebral bodies and arches over the root of the right lung.

Question 3714. Which of these is not a branch of the descending thoracic aorta: the bronchial artery, the esophageal artery, the posterior intercostal artery, or the superior thyroid artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery, which arises in the neck from the external carotid. The descending thoracic aorta gives bronchial, esophageal, pericardial and posterior intercostal branches.

Question 3715. Which small paired arteries arise from the lower part of the descending thoracic aorta and spread over the upper surface of the diaphragm? The answer is the superior phrenic arteries. They supply the part of the diaphragm nearest the aorta. The rest is supplied by the musculophrenic and pericardiophrenic arteries.

Question 3716. A constructed illustration: a person describes tearing chest pain that runs through between the shoulder blades, and one arm has a weaker pulse than the other. Which structure is the pain arising from? The answer is the wall of the aorta, where its layers have split. The split may extend along the arch. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3717. Which paired arteries arise from the abdominal aorta between the renal arteries and the inferior mesenteric artery and run down towards the pelvis? The answer is the gonadal arteries. In the male they are the testicular arteries and in the female the ovarian arteries. Each crosses in front of the ureter.

Question 3718. A described specimen shows the abdominal aorta opened from the front, with three unpaired openings in a row down its back wall. Which three trunks leave them, in order from above down? The answer is the celiac trunk, the superior mesenteric artery and the inferior mesenteric artery. They arise at the twelfth thoracic, the first lumbar and the third lumbar vertebrae.

Question 3719. Which of the three branches of the aortic arch is the only one that arises to the right of the midline? The answer is the brachiocephalic trunk. The other two, the left common carotid artery and the left subclavian artery, arise separately from the arch. The trunk then divides behind the right sternoclavicular joint.

Question 3720. Between which two landmarks does the aortic arch run, described in terms of the sternum and a vertebra? The answer is from the second sternocostal joint to the lower border of the fourth thoracic vertebra. The arch therefore lies in the superior mediastinum behind the manubrium. It begins and ends near the sternal angle.

Question 3721. Which of these vessels does not arise directly from the aortic arch: the brachiocephalic trunk, the left common carotid artery, the left subclavian artery, or the right subclavian artery? The answer is the right subclavian artery, which arises from the brachiocephalic trunk. Only three vessels leave the arch itself.

Question 3722. Which nerve hooks beneath the aortic arch just behind the fibrous band that joins the arch to the left pulmonary artery? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve. The band is the ligamentum arteriosum, the remains of the ductus arteriosus of the fetus. The nerve loops under the arch and climbs back up the neck.

Question 3723. A described chest radiograph shows a rounded bulge on the left border of the mediastinum, just above the shadow of the left hilum. Which structure forms that bulge? The answer is the aortic arch, whose last part is often called the aortic knuckle. The arch passes backward and to the left over the left bronchus.

Question 3724. A constructed illustration: an infant is described as having noisy breathing and difficulty swallowing, and a described study shows a ring of vessels encircling both the windpipe and the food pipe. Which developmental error accounts for the ring? The answer is persistence of both the right and the left fourth aortic arches, forming a double aortic arch.

Question 3725. Which structure lies immediately in front of the aortic arch and divides behind it into the two main bronchi: the esophagus, the trachea, the thoracic duct, or the vertebral column? The answer is the trachea. The arch then crosses over the left bronchus. The esophagus and the thoracic duct lie behind the arch.

Question 3726. Which nerve crosses the front of the aortic arch from lateral to medial on its way down to the diaphragm? The answer is the left phrenic nerve. It passes in front of the arch and then continues down in front of the root of the left lung. The left vagus nerve crosses the arch behind it.

Question 3727. True or false: the whole of the aortic arch lies within the pericardial sac. The answer is false. Only the ascending aorta lies inside the pericardium, and the arch begins where the aorta leaves it. The arch lies in the superior mediastinum behind the manubrium.

Question 3728. Which of these pairs belongs together: the brachiocephalic trunk with the left arm, the left common carotid artery with the left side of the head and neck, the left subclavian artery with the right side of the head, or the arch with the lower limb? The answer is the left common carotid artery with the left side of the head and neck.

Question 3729. Which branch of the aortic arch lies in front of the trachea before it divides, and what are its two terminal branches? The answer is the brachiocephalic trunk, which ends in the right common carotid and the right subclavian arteries. It is the first and largest branch of the arch.

Question 3730. Which of these fetal channels connects the pulmonary trunk to the aortic arch: the foramen ovale, the ductus venosus, the ductus arteriosus, or the umbilical vein? The answer is the ductus arteriosus. It joins the arch where the arch turns downward, just distal to the left subclavian artery. After birth it becomes the ligamentum arteriosum.

Question 3731. A described angiogram shows four separate vessels leaving the aortic arch, the last of them running to the right behind the esophagus. Which vessel is that last one? The answer is an aberrant right subclavian artery. It arises beyond the left subclavian artery and crosses behind the esophagus. Four branches from the arch are a common variant, not the rule.

Question 3732. A constructed illustration: during an operation near the arch in an adult with a persistent ductus arteriosus, a nerve that loops beneath the arch is said to be at risk. Which nerve is it, and why does it loop so low on the left? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, which hooks under the ligamentum arteriosum. On the right the same nerve hooks higher, under the right subclavian artery.

Question 3733. Which large vein lies in front of the three branches of the aortic arch in the superior mediastinum: the left brachiocephalic vein, the superior vena cava, the azygos vein, or the left internal jugular vein? The answer is the left brachiocephalic vein. It crosses the midline in front of those branches. The thymus lies in front of it in a child.

Question 3734. Which of these statements about the aortic arch is correct: it lies in the posterior mediastinum, it lies in the superior mediastinum, it lies inside the pericardium, or it lies below the diaphragm? The answer is that it lies in the superior mediastinum. The arch sits behind the manubrium and above the heart. The descending aorta alone continues into the posterior mediastinum.

Question 3735. At which landmark does the common carotid artery divide into the internal and the external carotid arteries? The answer is the upper border of the thyroid cartilage. The division lies at about the fourth cervical vertebra. The internal carotid then continues upward without branching in the neck.

Question 3736. Which of these structures lies within the carotid sheath: the external carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, the hypoglossal nerve, or the cervical sympathetic trunk? The answer is the internal jugular vein. The sheath also contains the common and internal carotid arteries and the vagus nerve. The sympathetic trunk lies behind the sheath.

Question 3737. Which small structure lies at the fork of the common carotid artery and acts as a chemical sensor for the blood? The answer is the carotid body. It sits in the tissue at the crotch of the division and responds to a fall in the oxygen content of the blood. The carotid sinus holds the pressure sensors instead.

Question 3738. Which is the first branch of the external carotid artery above its origin: the lingual artery, the superior thyroid artery, the facial artery, or the occipital artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery. It turns forward and downward to the upper pole of the thyroid gland. The lingual and facial arteries arise higher up.

Question 3739. In the neck, which vessel lies immediately lateral to the internal carotid artery, and which nerve lies between the two behind them? The answer is the internal jugular vein, with the vagus nerve between and behind them. The vein overlaps the artery at the side and in front. All three lie within the carotid sheath.

Question 3740. True or false: the internal carotid artery gives off no branches during its course through the neck. The answer is true. It runs straight up from the fork to the base of the skull without a branch in the neck. Its first branch, the ophthalmic artery, is given off inside the skull.

Question 3741. A described dissection of the neck shows two arteries lying one behind the other above the fork of the common carotid, the deeper one giving off nothing in the neck. Which artery is the deeper one? The answer is the internal carotid artery. It begins on the outer side of the external carotid and passes backward and medially.

Question 3742. Which of these pairs belongs together: the carotid sinus with chemical sensing, the carotid body with pressure sensing, the carotid sinus with pressure sensing, or the carotid body with temperature sensing? The answer is the carotid sinus with pressure sensing. The sinus is the swollen first part of the internal carotid artery and holds stretch receptors. The carotid body is the chemical sensor.

Question 3743. Which cranial nerve crosses the internal and the external carotid arteries in the neck, running forward above the fork? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve. It crosses the front of both vessels just above the division. It then runs forward to the tongue, deep to the digastric muscle.

Question 3744. A constructed illustration: a person has brief attacks of weakness of the right hand and difficulty finding words, and a described study shows a rough plaque at the fork of the left common carotid artery. Which vessel does the plaque narrow? The answer is the left internal carotid artery. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3745. Which branch of the internal carotid artery passes through the optic canal with the optic nerve to supply the eye: the facial artery, the ophthalmic artery, the maxillary artery, or the superficial temporal artery? The answer is the ophthalmic artery. It arises inside the cranial cavity. The other three are branches of the external carotid artery.

Question 3746. Name the two terminal branches of the external carotid artery, spoken as a list. The answer is the maxillary artery and the superficial temporal artery. The external carotid ends inside the parotid gland behind the neck of the mandible. The maxillary artery is the larger of the two.

Question 3747. A described radiograph of the base of the skull shows a vessel entering a canal in the petrous part of the temporal bone beside the jugular opening. Which vessel enters that canal? The answer is the internal carotid artery, which enters the carotid canal. It then turns forward and medially inside the skull. The external carotid stays outside.

Question 3748. Which of these arteries is not a branch of the external carotid artery: the ascending pharyngeal, the occipital, the posterior auricular, or the ophthalmic? The answer is the ophthalmic artery, which comes from the internal carotid. The external carotid gives eight branches, and the ascending pharyngeal is one of them.

Question 3749. A constructed illustration: a person has a firm swelling at the fork of the right common carotid artery, with a hoarse voice and a tongue that deviates to the right. Which two nerves near that fork account for those findings? The answer is the right vagus nerve and the right hypoglossal nerve. The vagus runs in the sheath beside the artery.

Question 3750. Which artery lies just in front of the ear and can be felt pulsing at the upper part of the temple: the facial artery, the occipital artery, the superficial temporal artery, or the maxillary artery? The answer is the superficial temporal artery, a terminal branch of the external carotid. The occipital artery runs behind the ear.

Question 3751. Which branch of the external carotid artery hooks over the lower border of the mandible just in front of the masseter muscle? The answer is the facial artery. It winds over the bone there and then runs up towards the angle of the mouth. Its pulse can be felt against the mandible.

Question 3752. A described cross section of the neck at the upper border of the thyroid cartilage shows one vessel dividing into two, with a large vein beside it and a nerve behind. Which three structures does the sheath hold there? The answer is the common carotid artery dividing, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve.

Question 3753. Which of these arteries is a true end artery, so that its blockage causes loss of the tissue it supplies with no help from neighbours: the facial artery, the central artery of the retina, the superficial temporal artery, or the maxillary artery? The answer is the central artery of the retina, a branch of the ophthalmic artery. It has no anastomosis elsewhere.

Question 3754. Which muscular band of the neck lies immediately in front of the lower part of the common carotid artery and partly covers it: the sternocleidomastoid, the omohyoid, the platysma, or the digastric? The answer is the omohyoid. Its inferior belly crosses the lower part of the carotid sheath. The sternocleidomastoid lies further lateral.

Question 3755. A constructed illustration: a person is described as having pain over the side of the head that is tender to touch, and the artery in front of the ear feels thickened. Which artery is described? The answer is the superficial temporal artery, a terminal branch of the external carotid. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3756. Which of these explains why blocking one of the arteries that reach the base of the brain may not cause a stroke: those arteries are end arteries, they join in a ring at the base of the brain, they carry no blood to the cortex, or they join only over the scalp? The answer is that they join in a ring, the circle of Willis.

Question 3757. From which vessels do the right and the left subclavian arteries arise? The answer is the brachiocephalic trunk on the right and the aortic arch on the left. The right subclavian artery begins behind the right sternoclavicular joint. The left arises from the arch as its third branch.

Question 3758. By which muscle is the subclavian artery divided into three parts, and where does the artery end? The answer is the anterior scalene muscle, and the artery ends at the outer border of the first rib. The first part lies medial to the muscle and the second part behind it.

Question 3759. Which is the first branch of the subclavian artery: the internal thoracic artery, the vertebral artery, the thyrocervical trunk, or the costocervical trunk? The answer is the vertebral artery. It arises from the upper and back part of the first part of the artery and runs up through the cervical vertebrae openings.

Question 3760. Which of these arteries arise from the thyrocervical trunk: the vertebral artery, the inferior thyroid artery, the costocervical trunk, or the deep cervical artery? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery. The trunk also gives the suprascapular and the superficial cervical arteries. The vertebral artery arises separately.

Question 3761. In the root of the neck, which vessel lies in front of the subclavian artery, with the anterior scalene muscle between them? The answer is the subclavian vein. It crosses in front of both the artery and the muscle and then joins the internal jugular vein. The brachial plexus lies behind the artery.

Question 3762. A described dissection of the root of the neck shows an artery arching upward over the dome of the pleura, marking the first rib and passing behind a muscle that runs down to that rib. Which artery is shown? The answer is the subclavian artery. It rests on the cervical pleura and the first rib.

Question 3763. True or false: the third part of the subclavian artery, beyond the anterior scalene muscle, usually gives off no named branch. The answer is true. The named branches come from the first part and from the second part, where the costocervical trunk arises. A dorsal scapular artery sometimes arises from the third part.

Question 3764. Which two arteries arise from the costocervical trunk: the inferior thyroid and the suprascapular, the superior intercostal and the deep cervical, the vertebral and the internal thoracic, or the transverse cervical and the dorsal scapular? The answer is the superior intercostal artery and the deep cervical artery. The trunk arises behind the anterior scalene muscle.

Question 3765. A constructed illustration: a person is described as having numbness along the inner side of the forearm and a weak hand, and a described radiograph shows an extra rib from the seventh cervical vertebra. Which structures are compressed at the root of the neck? The answer is the lower trunk of the brachial plexus and the subclavian artery, which pass over the first rib.

Question 3766. Which of these pairs belongs together: the internal thoracic artery with the abdominal wall above the navel, the vertebral artery with the scalp behind the ear, the costocervical trunk with the thyroid gland, or the thyrocervical trunk with the brain? The answer is the internal thoracic artery with the abdominal wall above the navel, through its superior epigastric branch.

Question 3767. Which artery runs down behind the costal cartilages about a finger's breadth from the edge of the sternum, and how does it end? The answer is the internal thoracic artery. It ends at the sixth intercostal space by dividing into the superior epigastric and the musculophrenic arteries. It lies in front of the transversus thoracis.

Question 3768. Which of these arteries ascends through the openings in the transverse processes of the cervical vertebrae and then enters the skull through the foramen magnum: the internal carotid, the vertebral, the occipital, or the ascending pharyngeal? The answer is the vertebral artery, the first branch of the first part of the subclavian artery.

Question 3769. At the root of the neck, the thoracic duct arches upward and laterally to end at which junction, and which two veins meet there? The answer is the junction of the left internal jugular and the left subclavian veins. The duct empties into the left brachiocephalic vein. On the right the right lymphatic duct ends at the matching junction.

Question 3770. Which branch of the subclavian system passes above the upper border of the scapula, through the notch beneath the transverse ligament, to reach the back of the shoulder? The answer is the suprascapular artery, from the thyrocervical trunk. It takes part in the anastomosis around the scapula.

Question 3771. A described angiogram of the neck shows the vertebral artery filling from above and carrying blood downward towards the arm, with narrowing of the subclavian artery before the vertebral artery arises. Which part of which artery is narrowed? The answer is the first part of the left subclavian artery, so the flow in the vertebral artery is reversed.

Question 3772. Into which two arteries does the internal thoracic artery divide at its lower end: the superior epigastric and the musculophrenic, the inferior epigastric and the pericardiophrenic, the superficial epigastric and the deep circumflex iliac, or the subcostal and the lumbar? The answer is the superior epigastric and the musculophrenic arteries.

Question 3773. Which artery supplies the lower part of the thyroid gland and arises from the thyrocervical trunk: the superior thyroid, the inferior thyroid, the middle thyroid, or the thyroidea ima? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery, from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery. The superior thyroid artery comes from the external carotid.

Question 3774. A described dissection of the front of the chest shows an artery running down behind the costal cartilages with two veins beside it and sending small branches forward between the cartilages. Which artery is shown? The answer is the internal thoracic artery. Its perforating branches reach the skin of the chest and the breast.

Question 3775. Where does the axillary artery begin and where does it end? The answer is at the outer border of the first rib above and the lower border of the teres major muscle below. Between those two points it crosses the armpit and lies behind the pectoralis minor. Beyond it becomes the brachial artery.

Question 3776. Which muscle divides the axillary artery into three parts: the pectoralis major, the pectoralis minor, the teres major, or the subscapularis? The answer is the pectoralis minor. The first part lies above the muscle, the second part behind it, and the third part below it. The artery then continues as the brachial artery.

Question 3777. Which is the largest branch of the axillary artery, and into which two arteries does it divide? The answer is the subscapular artery, which divides into the circumflex scapular and the thoracodorsal arteries. It arises from the third part of the axillary artery. The thoracodorsal artery runs to the latissimus dorsi.

Question 3778. Which two branches arise from the second part of the axillary artery: the superior thoracic and the lateral thoracic, the thoracoacromial trunk and the lateral thoracic, the subscapular and the anterior circumflex humeral, or the posterior circumflex humeral and the superior thoracic? The answer is the thoracoacromial trunk and the lateral thoracic artery.

Question 3779. In the armpit, which vessel lies on the medial side of the axillary artery: the axillary vein, the cephalic vein, the basilic vein, or the subscapular vein? The answer is the axillary vein. It lies medial and in front of the artery in the armpit. The two are wrapped together with the cords of the brachial plexus in the axillary sheath.

Question 3780. True or false: the axillary artery and the cords of the brachial plexus share a fibrous sheath in the armpit. The answer is true. The sheath is a continuation of the prevertebral layer of the deep fascia of the neck. It lets the vessels and nerves slide together under the shoulder.

Question 3781. A described dissection of the armpit shows a trunk behind the pectoralis minor dividing into an artery that runs between the two pectoral muscles and one that follows the lower border of the pectoralis minor. Which trunk is shown? The answer is the thoracoacromial trunk, from the second part of the axillary artery.

Question 3782. Name two branches of the third part of the axillary artery, spoken as a list. The answer is the subscapular artery and the posterior circumflex humeral artery. The third part also gives the anterior circumflex humeral artery. The posterior circumflex humeral leaves through the quadrangular space with the axillary nerve.

Question 3783. Which of these pairs belongs together: the posterior circumflex humeral artery with the triangular space, the circumflex scapular artery with the quadrangular space, the posterior circumflex humeral artery with the quadrangular space, or the circumflex scapular artery with the radial groove? The answer is the posterior circumflex humeral artery with the quadrangular space.

Question 3784. A constructed illustration: after a described fall the head of the humerus is said to lie in front of and below the socket of the shoulder, and the arm cannot be lifted. Which artery and which nerve are stretched over the displaced bone? The answer is the axillary artery and the axillary nerve, close to the surgical neck of the humerus.

Question 3785. A described fracture crosses the surgical neck of the humerus. Which artery that wraps around the bone at that level is most likely to be torn: the superior thoracic, the lateral thoracic, the anterior circumflex humeral, or the thoracodorsal? The answer is the anterior circumflex humeral artery. It runs forward around the surgical neck, where the break lies.

Question 3786. Which branch of the axillary artery runs down along the lateral border of the pectoralis minor towards the chest wall and supplies the breast? The answer is the lateral thoracic artery. It arises from the second part of the axillary artery. It continues down the side of the chest wall.

Question 3787. A described cross section through the armpit shows the artery with one cord of the brachial plexus on each side of it and a third behind it, and a large vein in front and to its medial side. In which part of the artery was the section taken? The answer is the second part, behind the pectoralis minor.

Question 3788. Which artery does the axillary artery become as it passes the lower border of the teres major: the radial artery, the brachial artery, the subscapular artery, or the profunda brachii? The answer is the brachial artery. It begins at the lower border of the teres major and runs down the medial side of the arm.

Question 3789. Where does the brachial artery begin and end? The answer is at the lower border of the teres major above and the neck of the radius below. It runs down the medial side of the arm in front of the triceps and then in front of the humerus. At the elbow it divides into the radial and the ulnar arteries.

Question 3790. Into which two arteries does the brachial artery divide in the cubital fossa: the radial and the interosseous, the radial and the ulnar, the ulnar and the interosseous, or the brachial and the profunda? The answer is the radial and the ulnar arteries. The division happens at about the neck of the radius.

Question 3791. In the upper part of the arm, on which side of the brachial artery does the median nerve lie, and what happens to that relation lower down? The answer is that the nerve begins lateral to the artery and crosses in front of it to lie medial. It crosses at about the middle of the arm. In the cubital fossa it lies medial to the artery.

Question 3792. Which is the largest branch of the brachial artery, and with which nerve does it run backward into the arm? The answer is the profunda brachii, also called the deep artery of the arm, which accompanies the radial nerve. Both pass back into the groove on the back of the humerus. It joins the network around the elbow.

Question 3793. Which branch of the brachial artery runs down with the ulnar nerve behind the medial epicondyle: the profunda brachii, the superior ulnar collateral artery, the inferior ulnar collateral artery, or the nutrient artery of the humerus? The answer is the superior ulnar collateral artery, which follows the ulnar nerve.

Question 3794. A described dissection of the front of the arm shows an artery running down the medial side of the biceps, crossed in front by a nerve at about the middle of the arm, with two veins beside it. Which artery is shown? The answer is the brachial artery. The nerve that crosses it is the median nerve.

Question 3795. A described transverse section of the arm at its middle shows a single bone with muscles around it and an artery lying in the front near the medial edge of the bone. In which compartment and at what depth does the artery lie? The answer is the front compartment, superficial to the brachialis muscle and deep to the deep fascia.

Question 3796. A described section of the limb is said to have been cut across just below the elbow. In what sideways relation do the radial and the ulnar arteries lie as they leave the cubital fossa? The answer is that the radial artery lies laterally and the ulnar artery medially. The radial artery runs down the outer side of the front of the forearm.

Question 3797. Where is the pulse of the brachial artery felt most easily: in the middle of the front of the arm, on the medial side of the arm beside the biceps, behind the medial epicondyle, or in the cubital fossa lateral to the biceps tendon? The answer is on the medial side of the arm beside the biceps, against the humerus.

Question 3798. A constructed illustration: after a described fall on the outstretched hand a child holds the elbow bent, and the hand is pale with no radial pulse felt. Which two structures cross in front of the lower end of the humerus and may be injured? The answer is the brachial artery and the median nerve, both lying close to the bone at the elbow.

Question 3799. Name two collateral branches of the brachial artery that help form the network around the elbow, spoken as a list. The answer is the superior ulnar collateral artery and the inferior ulnar collateral artery. The profunda brachii also sends a branch to that network. These vessels join the recurrent branches of the radial and ulnar arteries.

Question 3800. A described specimen of the front of the elbow shows an artery dividing into two branches at the neck of the radius, one passing deep to the pronator teres and one turning laterally under a muscle that runs to the wrist. Which artery divided? The answer is the brachial artery, dividing into the radial and the ulnar arteries.

To summarize, the aorta leaves the left ventricle as the ascending aorta, gives off the two coronary arteries from the sinuses above the valve, curves as the arch to end at the lower border of the fourth thoracic vertebra, and descends through the chest and the abdomen to divide into the two common iliac arteries at the fourth lumbar vertebra. The arch gives three branches, the brachiocephalic trunk, the left common carotid artery and the left subclavian artery, and the ligamentum arteriosum hangs from it with the left recurrent laryngeal nerve looping beneath. Each common carotid artery divides at the upper border of the thyroid cartilage into an external carotid artery with eight branches and an internal carotid artery with none in the neck, and each subclavian artery runs in three parts about the anterior scalene muscle, giving the vertebral, internal thoracic and thyrocervical vessels from its first part. The axillary artery continues from the first rib to the teres major and is named in three parts by the pectoralis minor, and the brachial artery carries on from there to divide at the neck of the radius into the radial and the ulnar arteries.
