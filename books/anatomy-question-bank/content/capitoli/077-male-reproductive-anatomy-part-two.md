# Chapter 77 — Male Reproductive Anatomy, Part Two

This chapter continues the male reproductive system and takes up three parts in turn: the prostate gland, the penis and the scrotum. It begins deep in the pelvis with the prostate, its lobes and zones, its capsule and its relations to the bladder and the rectum, and then follows the ejaculatory ducts and the prostatic urethra through it. From there it moves to the penis, its erectile bodies, its coverings and the vessels and nerves of its dorsal bundle, and it ends with the scrotum, its layers, the spermatic cord and the descent of the testis. It holds one hundred questions, and every one of them gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The structures described here lie close together in a small space, and their relations are what the questions test. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7601. Which gland of the male pelvis lies immediately below the neck of the urinary bladder and surrounds the first part of the urethra? The answer is the prostate gland. Its base faces upward against the bladder and its apex points downward onto the pelvic floor. It lies behind the pubic symphysis and in front of the rectum.

Question 7602. Which of these structures lies immediately in front of the rectum in the male: the prostate gland, the urinary bladder, the seminal vesicle, or the ductus deferens? The answer is the prostate gland. The bladder sits above it, and the seminal vesicles lie above and behind its base. A thin fascial layer separates the gland from the rectum behind.

Question 7603. Which pad of fat and which venous plexus lie between the prostate gland and the pubic symphysis? The answer is a pad of retropubic fat and the prostatic venous plexus. The fat fills the small space behind the pubic bone. The plexus lies against the front and the sides of the gland.

Question 7604. What are the two ends of the prostate gland called, and which way does each one face? The answer is the base above and the apex below. The base is broad and lies against the neck of the urinary bladder. The apex is narrow and rests on the perineal membrane, where the urethra leaves the gland.

Question 7605. Which lobe of the prostate gland lies behind the urethra and between the two ejaculatory ducts, and can project upward into the bladder? The answer is the median lobe. It is the part most often blamed for a rounded filling defect at the base of the bladder. The right and the left lobes lie on either side of the urethra.

Question 7606. A described transverse section through the base of the bladder shows a smooth rounded elevation pushing up from below, with the urethra in front of it. Which part of the prostate gland is shown? The answer is the median lobe, enlarged upward into the bladder. The urethra lies in front of it and the ejaculatory ducts pass behind and below it.

Question 7607. Which zone of the prostate gland surrounds the first part of the urethra and is the usual site of benign prostatic hyperplasia? The answer is the transition zone. It is a small zone, making up about one tenth of the glandular tissue. Because it encircles the urethra, its enlargement narrows the stream of urine.

Question 7608. Which zone of the prostate gland occupies the posterior and lateral parts of the gland and is the usual site of carcinoma: the central zone, the transition zone, the peripheral zone, or the anterior fibromuscular stroma? The answer is the peripheral zone. It holds about seventy per cent of the glandular tissue. It is the part felt through the front wall of the rectum.

Question 7609. Which paired glands lie above and behind the base of the prostate gland, one on each side of the ampulla of the ductus deferens? The answer is the seminal vesicles. Each is a coiled tube about 5 centimeters long. Its duct joins the ampulla of the ductus deferens to form the ejaculatory duct.

Question 7610. A constructed illustration: an older man reports that his stream of urine has become weak and that he must rise at night. Which zone of the prostate gland is enlarging to narrow the urethra? The answer is the transition zone, in benign prostatic hyperplasia. The enlarged tissue squeezes the prostatic urethra from all sides. The management of the condition belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7611. Which zone of the prostate gland forms a cone around the ejaculatory ducts: the anterior fibromuscular stroma, the central zone, the posterior lobe, or the median lobe? The answer is the central zone. It surrounds the ejaculatory ducts as they pass through the gland. Its ducts open beside the seminal colliculus.

Question 7612. Which part of the prostate gland is a sheet of fibromuscular tissue lying in front of the urethra, with almost no glandular tissue in it? The answer is the anterior fibromuscular stroma. It forms the front surface of the gland and blends with the capsule. Because it is not glandular, it takes little part in either hyperplasia or carcinoma.

Question 7613. True or false: the prostate gland lies behind the pubic symphysis and below the urinary bladder. The answer is true. The gland occupies the floor of the pelvis in the midline. The pubic bone lies in front of it and the bladder above it, and the rectum lies behind it.

Question 7614. Which of these pairs belongs together: the transition zone with the region around the proximal urethra, the peripheral zone with the region around the ejaculatory ducts, the central zone with the front of the gland, or the anterior fibromuscular stroma with the region behind the rectum? The answer is the transition zone with the region around the proximal urethra. The central zone surrounds the ejaculatory ducts.

Question 7615. Which fibrous coat wraps the prostate gland and sends septa into its substance? The answer is the prostatic capsule. It is a firm fibrous sheath that encloses the gland and blends with the surrounding pelvic fascia. The septa carry vessels and ducts into the glandular tissue.

Question 7616. Which nerves run in the posterolateral corner of the prostate gland, beside the capsule, and what function do they serve? The answer is the cavernous nerves, which carry the fibers for erection. They lie in the neurovascular bundles on each side. Their position is the reason surgeons try to spare that corner of the gland.

Question 7617. A constructed illustration: after an operation on the prostate gland, a man can no longer achieve an erection, though his sensation and his urine stream are normal. Which structures lying against the posterolateral surface of the gland were most likely injured? The answer is the cavernous nerves in the neurovascular bundles. They run beside the capsule toward the penis.

Question 7618. A described midline section through the prostate gland shows two slender tubes passing downward and forward through its posterior part to open beside a small midline pouch. Which tubes are shown and where do they end? The answer is the ejaculatory ducts, opening at the seminal colliculus. Each is formed by the union of the ductus deferens and the duct of the seminal vesicle.

Question 7619. What is the name of the rounded elevation on the posterior wall of the prostatic urethra, at which the ejaculatory ducts open? The answer is the seminal colliculus. It sits on a raised ridge called the urethral crest. The prostatic sinuses lie on each side of it, and the prostatic ducts open into them.

Question 7620. Which of these structures is a small blind pouch on the seminal colliculus, taken to be a remnant of the paramesonephric duct: the prostatic utricle, the prostatic sinus, the urethral crest, or the ampulla of the ductus deferens? The answer is the prostatic utricle. It opens between the two ejaculatory duct openings. In the female the same duct system forms the uterus.

Question 7621. Name two features seen on the lining of the prostatic part of the urethra, spoken as a list. The answer is the urethral crest with the seminal colliculus, and the prostatic sinuses on each side. The crest is a midline ridge on the posterior wall, and the colliculus is the rounded swelling on it.

Question 7622. Where do the ducts of the prostate gland open: on the seminal colliculus, into the prostatic sinuses, into the urinary bladder, or into the ejaculatory ducts? The answer is into the prostatic sinuses. Those are the grooves on each side of the urethral crest. About twenty small ducts open there, and none of them opens into the bladder.

Question 7623. In a described sagittal section, the ejaculatory duct enters the prostate gland at its posterior surface and ends at the seminal colliculus. In which direction does it run through the gland, and what does it pass behind? The answer is downward and forward, passing behind the median lobe. The two ducts converge as they approach the colliculus.

Question 7624. Which thin fascial layer lies between the posterior surface of the prostate gland and the front of the rectum? The answer is the rectovesical fascia. It separates the gland from the rectum and keeps the two planes distinct. In front of it runs the prostate, and behind it the rectal wall.

Question 7625. A constructed illustration: a firm nodule is felt through the front wall of the rectum on the posterior surface of an enlarged prostate gland, and the median sulcus is lost on that side. Which zone of the gland and which surface does the nodule most likely occupy? The answer is the peripheral zone of the posterior surface, where carcinoma usually begins.

Question 7626. Which artery, a branch of the internal iliac artery, is the main supply to the prostate gland? The answer is the inferior vesical artery. It reaches the gland along its posterolateral surface. The artery varies: it may arise instead from the internal pudendal artery or from the middle rectal artery.

Question 7627. The prostatic venous plexus drains into which veins: the internal iliac veins, the external iliac veins, the inferior mesenteric vein, or the superficial inguinal veins? The answer is the internal iliac veins. The plexus lies around the sides and the base of the gland. It also communicates with the internal vertebral venous plexus inside the spinal canal.

Question 7628. A described cast of the pelvic veins shows a rich network wrapped around the base and the sides of the prostate gland, receiving a midline vein from the dorsum of the penis. Which two sets of vessels are shown? The answer is the prostatic venous plexus and the deep dorsal vein of the penis.

Question 7629. Why may carcinoma of the prostate gland spread early to the bones of the vertebral column: because the prostatic veins join the internal vertebral venous plexus, because the prostate lies against the spine, because its lymphatics follow the aorta, or because the gland has no capsule? The answer is that the prostatic veins join the internal vertebral venous plexus. That valveless network lets cells reach the vertebrae directly.

Question 7630. To which groups of lymph nodes does the prostate gland drain? The answer is the internal iliac nodes and the sacral nodes. A few vessels also reach the obturator nodes along the pelvic wall. The drainage follows the internal iliac vessels rather than the inguinal nodes.

Question 7631. True or false: lymph from the prostate gland drains to the superficial inguinal nodes in the groin. The answer is false. The gland drains to the internal iliac and sacral nodes deep in the pelvis. Only the skin of the scrotum and the skin of the penis drain to the superficial inguinal nodes.

Question 7632. Which of these pairs belongs together: the inferior vesical artery with the prostate gland, the internal pudendal artery with the prostate gland, the external iliac vein with the prostate gland, or the superficial inguinal nodes with the prostate gland? The answer is the inferior vesical artery with the prostate gland, which it supplies. The internal pudendal artery supplies the penis, and the gland drains to the internal iliac nodes.

Question 7633. From which plexus do the nerves to the prostate gland reach it: the inferior hypogastric plexus, the lumbar plexus, the sacral plexus, or the celiac plexus? The answer is the inferior hypogastric plexus. It lies beside the rectum and the bladder. Its sympathetic fibers tighten the smooth muscle, and its parasympathetic fibers drive secretion.

Question 7634. Sympathetic fibers to the prostate gland arise from which segments of the spinal cord, and along which nerves do they travel? The answer is the first and second lumbar segments, travelling by way of the hypogastric nerves. They reach the inferior hypogastric plexus beside the rectum. The parasympathetic fibers come instead from the second to the fourth sacral segments.

Question 7635. A described specimen of the prostate gland is sliced across, and a firm pale nodule sits in the posterior part of the gland, well away from the urethra. Which zone is involved, and why is the urethra undistorted? The answer is the peripheral zone, which lies behind and to the sides. Carcinoma there grows away from the urethra, so it may cause no urinary symptoms until it is large.

Question 7636. Why does benign prostatic hyperplasia narrow the urine stream while an early carcinoma in the same gland often does not: the hyperplasia surrounds the urethra, the hyperplasia always blocks the bladder neck, the carcinoma never grows, or the carcinoma lies in the capsule? The answer is that the hyperplasia surrounds the urethra. It arises in the transition zone, which encircles the tube. Carcinoma begins in the peripheral zone, far away.

Question 7637. Against which structure does the base of the prostate gland rest, and on which does its apex sit: the base on the bladder and the apex on the perineal membrane, the base on the perineal membrane and the apex on the bladder, both on the bladder, or both on the rectum? The answer is the base on the bladder and the apex on the perineal membrane. The urethra leaves at the apex.

Question 7638. A constructed illustration: a man cannot pass urine, and a tender swelling is felt above the pubic symphysis. Which part of the prostate gland is most likely pressing on the urethra? The answer is the transition zone and the median lobe, enlarged in benign prostatic hyperplasia. Relief of the obstruction belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7639. A described specimen of an adult prostate gland measures about 3 centimeters long, 4 centimeters wide and 2 centimeters thick. Which organ of the male pelvis is this, and how does an enlarged gland differ from it? The answer is the prostate gland, about the size of a walnut. In hyperplasia it may grow to several times that size, mainly through the transition zone.

Question 7640. A described section shows the prostatic urethra as a wide slit running through the gland, with a ridge on its posterior wall and two slender ducts opening beside a small pouch. Which part of the male urethra is shown, and why is it the widest? The answer is the prostatic part, the widest and most dilatable part of the tube. It is about 3 centimeters long.

Question 7641. Which three masses of erectile tissue make up the body of the penis? The answer is the two corpora cavernosa and the single corpus spongiosum. The two corpora cavernosa lie side by side on the dorsal side. The corpus spongiosum lies in the midline on the ventral side and carries the urethra.

Question 7642. Which of the erectile bodies of the penis contains the spongy part of the urethra: the right corpus cavernosum, the left corpus cavernosum, the corpus spongiosum, or the glans? The answer is the corpus spongiosum. It lies below the two corpora cavernosa and widens at both ends. The urethra runs through its whole length.

Question 7643. A described transverse section of the penis shows two large rounded bodies side by side above a smaller rounded body, each wrapped in a tough white coat. Which structures are shown? The answer is the two corpora cavernosa above and the corpus spongiosum below. The tough white coat around each is the tunica albuginea.

Question 7644. Which erectile body of the penis lies in the midline, and which side of the penis does it occupy? The answer is the corpus spongiosum, and it occupies the ventral side. The two corpora cavernosa lie above it on the dorsal side. The urethra runs inside the corpus spongiosum.

Question 7645. What is the expanded lower end of the corpus spongiosum called, and what is its expanded upper end called? The answer is the bulb below and the glans above. The bulb lies in the root of the penis and receives the urethra. The glans forms the tip of the penis and covers the ends of the corpora cavernosa.

Question 7646. Which part of the penis forms the root and is fixed to the ischiopubic rami on each side: the glans, the bulb, the crura of the corpora cavernosa, or the prepuce? The answer is the crura of the corpora cavernosa, one on each side. The bulb of the corpus spongiosum lies between them in the midline. The glans and the prepuce belong to the free part.

Question 7647. A constructed illustration: a man falls astride a bar and blood collects in the penis and the perineum, while the urethra is intact. Which erectile bodies lie against the bone and were most likely injured? The answer is the crura of the corpora cavernosa, fixed to the ischiopubic rami. The bulb of the corpus spongiosum lies between them and may be spared.

Question 7648. What is the name of the fold of skin that covers the glans of the penis, and what is the small fold beneath it called? The answer is the prepuce, with the frenulum beneath the glans. The prepuce is a double layer of skin that can be drawn back over the glans. The frenulum is the midline fold that tethers it.

Question 7649. Which fascia wraps the two corpora cavernosa and the corpus spongiosum together and carries the dorsal vessels and nerves: the superficial fascia, the deep fascia of the penis, the tunica albuginea, or the dartos? The answer is the deep fascia of the penis. The tunica albuginea lies deeper still, around each erectile body separately.

Question 7650. A described cross section shows a tough fibrous envelope around each erectile body, a common envelope around all three, and a bundle of vessels and nerves lying in the groove between the two upper bodies. Which envelopes and which bundle are shown? The answer is the tunica albuginea around each body, the deep fascia around all three, and the dorsal neurovascular bundle.

Question 7651. Which vein lies in the midline of the dorsum of the penis, deep to the deep fascia and above the two dorsal arteries? The answer is the deep dorsal vein of the penis. It lies in the groove between the two corpora cavernosa. Behind the penis it passes under the pubic symphysis and drains into the prostatic venous plexus.

Question 7652. Which artery runs inside the corpus cavernosum and supplies the erectile tissue: the dorsal artery of the penis, the deep artery of the penis, the artery of the bulb, or the external pudendal artery? The answer is the deep artery of the penis, one in each corpus cavernosum. Its coiled helicine branches open into the spaces of the erectile tissue. The dorsal artery supplies the skin and the glans.

Question 7653. Which nerve carries sensation from the skin of the penis, and from which nerve does it arise? The answer is the dorsal nerve of the penis, from the pudendal nerve. It runs in the dorsal neurovascular bundle, deep to the deep fascia and lateral to the deep dorsal vein. It gives branches to the glans and the prepuce.

Question 7654. True or false: the skin of the shaft of the penis drains its lymph to the superficial inguinal nodes. The answer is true. The skin and the prepuce drain to the superficial inguinal nodes in the groin. The erectile tissue and the glans drain deeper, to the deep inguinal nodes and the internal iliac nodes.

Question 7655. Which of these pairs belongs together: the glans with the corpus spongiosum, the glans with the corpus cavernosum, the bulb with the corpus cavernosum, or the crus with the corpus spongiosum? The answer is the glans with the corpus spongiosum, whose upper end it forms. The bulb is the lower end of the same body, and each crus belongs to a corpus cavernosum.

Question 7656. Which statement about the tunica albuginea of the penis is correct: it surrounds each erectile body separately, it surrounds all three bodies together, it lies outside the deep fascia, or it contains the dorsal nerve? The answer is that it surrounds each erectile body separately. It is a tough fibrous coat that keeps the erectile tissue in shape. The deep fascia wraps all three bodies together.

Question 7657. Which suspensory structure runs from the front of the pubic symphysis to the deep fascia of the penis and holds the organ against the front of the pelvis? The answer is the suspensory ligament of the penis. It is a triangular band of fibrous tissue. A looser fold, the fundiform ligament, comes from the front of the sheath of the rectus muscle.

Question 7658. A described dissection of the free part of the penis shows the skin and the superficial fascia removed, leaving a tough sleeve that must be opened to reach a vein, two arteries and two nerves. What is that sleeve, and what lies inside it? The answer is the deep fascia of the penis, containing the deep dorsal vein, the two dorsal arteries and the two dorsal nerves.

Question 7659. Which structure lies immediately deep to the pubic symphysis as the penis passes forward and drains the blood from the erectile tissue into the prostatic venous plexus? The answer is the deep dorsal vein of the penis. It passes below the symphysis and behind the suspensory ligament. There it joins the prostatic venous plexus.

Question 7660. A constructed illustration: during erection a sudden pain is felt in the shaft, the penis swells into a dark bruise on one side, and it curves away from that side. Which structure has most likely torn? The answer is the tunica albuginea of one corpus cavernosum. Blood then escapes into the tissue around it. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7661. Which statement about the external urethral orifice is correct: it lies at the tip of the glans and is the narrowest point, it lies at the base of the glans and is the widest, it lies in the bulb, or it lies at the neck of the bladder? The answer is that it lies at the tip of the glans and is the narrowest point. The spongy part behind it is wider.

Question 7662. Which two muscles of the root of the penis cover the crura and the bulb? The answer is the ischiocavernosus over each crus and the bulbospongiosus over the bulb. They lie in the superficial perineal pouch. They help to empty the urethra and to maintain erection.

Question 7663. Name the three parts into which the penis is divided, spoken as a list. The answer is the root, the body and the glans. The root is fixed to the pelvis and holds the crura and the bulb. The body is the free shaft, and the glans is the expanded tip covered by the prepuce.

Question 7664. Which of these structures does not lie in the dorsal neurovascular bundle of the penis: the deep dorsal vein, the dorsal artery, the dorsal nerve, or the deep artery of the penis? The answer is the deep artery of the penis. It runs inside each corpus cavernosum, not on the dorsum. The other three lie in the groove between the two corpora cavernosa.

Question 7665. In a described transverse section of the penis, how do the two corpora cavernosa lie relative to the corpus spongiosum, and where does the urethra lie? The answer is that the two corpora cavernosa lie dorsally, side by side, and the corpus spongiosum lies ventrally in the midline. The urethra lies inside the corpus spongiosum. The deep dorsal vein lies in the groove between the two dorsal bodies.

Question 7666. Which ligament of the penis is the deeper of the two, and from which structure does it arise? The answer is the suspensory ligament, arising from the front of the pubic symphysis. It runs to the deep fascia of the penis. The more superficial fold is the fundiform ligament, which comes from the sheath of the rectus abdominis muscle.

Question 7667. Which nerve provides the parasympathetic fibers that produce erection of the penis, and from which plexus does it come? The answer is the cavernous nerve, from the inferior hypogastric plexus. It travels beside the prostate gland and the seminal vesicles. Its fibers relax the smooth muscle of the helicine arteries.

Question 7668. Which statement about the venous drainage of the penis is correct: the deep dorsal vein drains to the prostatic plexus, both sets of veins drain to the inferior vena cava, both drain to the internal iliac veins, or every vein lies deep to the deep fascia? The answer is that the deep dorsal vein drains to the prostatic plexus. The superficial veins drain to the external pudendal veins.

Question 7669. A described specimen shows a penis in which the urethra opens on the underside of the shaft, well behind the glans, and the prepuce hangs over the tip as a hood. Which developmental defect is shown, and which folds failed to close? The answer is hypospadias, from failure of the urogenital folds to fuse along the ventral surface.

Question 7670. Which glands open into the spongy part of the urethra within the bulb of the penis: the bulbourethral glands, the prostate gland, the seminal vesicles, or the glands of the prepuce? The answer is the bulbourethral glands, one on each side. Their long ducts open into the first part of the spongy urethra. The prostate gland opens instead into the prostatic urethra.

Question 7671. True or false: the lymphatic drainage of the glans of the penis follows the same path as that of the skin of the shaft. The answer is false. The skin and the prepuce drain to the superficial inguinal nodes. The glans and the erectile tissue drain to the deep inguinal nodes and onward to the internal iliac nodes.

Question 7672. Which of these pairs belongs together: the dorsal nerve with sensation from the glans, the cavernous nerve with sensation from the glans, the deep dorsal vein with erection, or the deep artery with the drainage of the glans? The answer is the dorsal nerve with sensation from the glans. The cavernous nerve controls erection, the deep dorsal vein drains the erectile tissue, and the deep artery supplies it.

Question 7673. A constructed illustration: a man has no sensation over the glans of the penis after a perineal injury, though he can still achieve an erection. Which nerve has been injured, and why does erection remain? The answer is the dorsal nerve, a somatic sensory branch of the pudendal nerve. The cavernous nerves that produce erection arise from the pelvic plexus and may be spared.

Question 7674. A constructed illustration: urine leaks from the underside of the penis after a straddle injury, and the perineum is swollen with dark blood above the deep fascia. Which part of the urethra is most likely torn? The answer is the spongy urethra within the corpus spongiosum. The blood and urine then follow the space deep to the deep fascia.

Question 7675. A described radiograph after a study with a contrast agent shows the urethra narrowing at the tip of the glans and widening just behind it and again within the prostate gland. Which three points of the male urethra are illustrated? The answer is the external urethral orifice, which is narrowest, the navicular fossa, which is wide, and the prostatic part, which is widest.

Question 7676. What is the name of the pouch of skin that hangs below the root of the penis and holds the two testes? The answer is the scrotum. Its skin is pigmented and wrinkled and carries a midline ridge. Each testis lies in its own compartment inside it.

Question 7677. Which structure divides the scrotum into two compartments, one for each testis: the scrotal septum, the dartos muscle, the tunica vaginalis, or the raphe? The answer is the scrotal septum. It runs in the midline between the two testes. The raphe is the ridge seen on the outside of the skin.

Question 7678. A described section across the scrotum shows two oval bodies side by side, each wrapped in a serous sac and separated by a midline wall of tissue. What is the wall, and what are the sacs? The answer is the scrotal septum and the two tunica vaginalis sacs. Each sac is a closed serous space around one testis, left behind by the peritoneum.

Question 7679. Which thin serous sac covers the front and the sides of the testis, and from which membrane is it derived? The answer is the tunica vaginalis, derived from the peritoneum. Its visceral layer covers the testis and its parietal layer lines the scrotum. Between them lies a small space holding a film of fluid.

Question 7680. Which layer of the scrotum is a network of smooth muscle in the superficial fascia, with no fat in it: the dartos muscle, the cremaster muscle, the external spermatic fascia, or the internal spermatic fascia? The answer is the dartos muscle. Its contraction wrinkles the skin and raises the testes. The absence of fat helps the scrotum lose heat.

Question 7681. Which muscle of the spermatic cord is derived from the internal oblique muscle of the abdomen and raises the testis? The answer is the cremaster muscle. Its fibers form a series of loops around the cord and the testis. The external spermatic fascia, in contrast, comes from the external oblique muscle.

Question 7682. A constructed illustration: a young man is struck on the scrotum during sport and the whole pouch fills with dark blood. Which layer, continuous with the superficial fascia of the abdomen, allows the blood to spread widely? The answer is the dartos layer of the scrotum. Its loose tissue and its lack of fat let fluid track freely.

Question 7683. Which duct leaves the back of the testis and carries sperm upward through the inguinal canal toward the prostate gland? The answer is the ductus deferens. It begins at the tail of the epididymis and runs up in the spermatic cord. Behind the bladder it widens as the ampulla and joins the duct of the seminal vesicle.

Question 7684. Which of these arteries supplies the testis: the testicular artery from the abdominal aorta, the cremasteric artery from the inferior epigastric artery, the artery to the ductus deferens, or the external pudendal artery? The answer is the testicular artery, arising from the abdominal aorta at the level of the second lumbar vertebra. The other three supply the coverings and the duct.

Question 7685. A described cast of the vessels of the spermatic cord shows a long artery from above, a coil of veins around it, and a small artery joining the duct behind. Which three sets of vessels are shown? The answer is the testicular artery, the pampiniform plexus of veins, and the artery to the ductus deferens. The vein coil lies in front of the duct.

Question 7686. Which nerve supplies the skin of the front of the scrotum: the ilioinguinal nerve, the pudendal nerve, the posterior femoral cutaneous nerve, or the obturator nerve? The answer is the ilioinguinal nerve. The genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve supplies the skin next to it. The back of the scrotum is supplied by branches of the pudendal nerve.

Question 7687. To which group of lymph nodes does the skin of the scrotum drain? The answer is the superficial inguinal nodes in the groin. They lie just below the inguinal ligament. The testis inside the scrotum drains to a different set of nodes, high in the abdomen.

Question 7688. A constructed illustration: pain from a diseased testis is felt in the skin around the navel rather than in the groin. Which segment of the spinal cord carries the sensory fibers, and why does the pain appear there? The answer is the tenth thoracic segment, because the testis develops high on the posterior abdominal wall and takes its nerve supply with it.

Question 7689. Which vein does the right testicular vein usually join: the right renal vein, the inferior vena cava, the left renal vein, or the internal iliac vein? The answer is the inferior vena cava. The left testicular vein joins the left renal vein instead. That difference is why a varicocele is far more common on the left side.

Question 7690. True or false: the testis itself drains its lymph to the superficial inguinal nodes in the groin. The answer is false. The testis follows its artery back to the para-aortic nodes beside the abdominal aorta. It is the skin of the scrotum that drains to the superficial inguinal nodes.

Question 7691. Which of these pairs belongs together: the ilioinguinal nerve with the front of the scrotum, the pudendal nerve with the front of the scrotum, the genitofemoral nerve with the back of the scrotum, or the obturator nerve with the scrotum? The answer is the ilioinguinal nerve with the front of the scrotum. The pudendal nerve supplies the back, and the genitofemoral nerve the adjoining skin.

Question 7692. Which coil of veins lies in the spermatic cord above the testis and acts as a countercurrent heat exchanger? The answer is the pampiniform plexus. The cool venous blood in the coil takes heat from the warm arterial blood on its way down. In this way the testis is kept 2 to 3 degrees Celsius below body temperature.

Question 7693. Which statement about the descent of the testis is correct: it passes through the inguinal canal and drags a fold of peritoneum with it, it passes behind the peritoneum, it crosses the front of the pubic symphysis, or it remains inside the abdomen? The answer is that it passes through the inguinal canal and drags a fold of peritoneum with it. That fold becomes the tunica vaginalis.

Question 7694. A described scan of the groin in a young man shows a bag of worms above the testis that grows larger when he stands and smaller when he lies down. Which structure is dilated, and why is the left side more often affected? The answer is the pampiniform plexus. The left testicular vein is longer and joins the left renal vein at a right angle.

Question 7695. Which nerve runs through the inguinal canal in front of the spermatic cord rather than inside it, and which nerve lies within the cord? The answer is the ilioinguinal nerve in front, with the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve inside the cord. The iliac branch of the iliohypogastric nerve may also lie in front.

Question 7696. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the groin a man has lost the cremasteric reflex but retains normal sensation of the scrotum and a normal testis. Which nerve has most likely been injured? The answer is the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve, which supplies the cremaster muscle. The reflex is mediated at the first and second lumbar segments.

Question 7697. Name two coverings of the spermatic cord that are derived from the muscles of the abdominal wall, spoken as a list. The answer is the cremaster muscle with its fascia, from the internal oblique muscle, and the external spermatic fascia, from the external oblique muscle. The internal spermatic fascia comes from the transversalis fascia.

Question 7698. A constructed illustration: a newborn boy has a swelling in the scrotum that has been present since birth and that disappears when he lies down. Which structure has failed to close, and what does it become in the adult? The answer is the processus vaginalis. In the adult it normally closes and leaves only the tunica vaginalis.

Question 7699. A described transverse section of the spermatic cord shows a firm tube behind, a long spiral artery in the middle, a mesh of thin veins around it, and a small nerve beside them. Which structure is shown, and what lies in front of it? The answer is the spermatic cord. The ilioinguinal nerve lies in front of the cord, not inside it.

Question 7700. Which of these structures lies inside the spermatic cord: the ilioinguinal nerve, the processus vaginalis, the genitofemoral nerve, or the inguinal ligament? The answer is the processus vaginalis, present as a fibrous remnant in the adult. The ilioinguinal nerve lies in front of the cord, and the inguinal ligament forms the floor of the canal beneath it.

To summarize, the prostate gland lies below the bladder, behind the pubic symphysis and in front of the rectum, its base against the bladder and its apex on the perineal membrane. Its glandular tissue is divided into a transition zone around the proximal urethra, a central zone around the ejaculatory ducts and a peripheral zone behind and to the sides; benign prostatic hyperplasia arises in the transition zone and carcinoma in the peripheral zone, and the gland drains to the internal iliac and sacral nodes. The prostatic venous plexus communicates with the internal vertebral venous plexus, and the cavernous nerves in the posterolateral neurovascular bundles carry the fibers for erection. The penis is built of two corpora cavernosa and one corpus spongiosum, wrapped in tunica albuginea and the deep fascia, with the deep dorsal vein, the two dorsal arteries and the two dorsal nerves in the dorsal bundle. The scrotum is divided by a midline septum into two compartments, each holding a tunica vaginalis sac; its dartos muscle has no fat, the cremaster muscle raises the testis, and the testis drains to the para-aortic nodes while the scrotal skin drains to the superficial inguinal nodes.
