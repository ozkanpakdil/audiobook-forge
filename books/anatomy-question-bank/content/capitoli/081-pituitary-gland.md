# Chapter 81 — Pituitary Gland

The two small glands that govern the pace and the chemistry of the whole body sit in the neck and at the base of the brain, and this chapter examines both of them. It begins with the pituitary gland, its seat in the sella turcica, its two lobes of different origin, the hormones it releases, the portal vessels that feed it, and the crowded space that surrounds it, and it then turns to the thyroid gland, its place in the front of the neck, its blood supply, the nerves that run beside it, its development, and its follicles. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The questions are gathered in the order in which the structures meet the listener, first the pituitary gland and then the thyroid gland. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 8001. In which hollow of the skull does the pituitary gland lie, and in which bone is that hollow carved? The answer is the hypophyseal fossa of the sphenoid bone. The fossa is the deepest part of the sella turcica, the saddle-shaped depression on the upper surface of the body of the sphenoid. The gland rests in it like a pea in a cup.

Question 8002. What is the name of the small circular fold of dura mater that forms the roof of the pituitary fossa? The answer is the diaphragma sellae. It is pierced in the middle by a small opening, through which the stalk of the gland passes upward to the brain. It separates the gland below from the subarachnoid space above.

Question 8003. What is the stalk that joins the pituitary gland to the floor of the brain called: the infundibulum, the habenula, the mamillary body, or the pineal stalk? The answer is the infundibulum. It carries the nerve fibers of the hypothalamus downward and the small portal blood vessels that reach the anterior lobe.

Question 8004. Which structure lies immediately above the pituitary gland and is the first to be pressed by a tumor that grows upward out of the sella? The answer is the optic chiasm. It lies in the midline just above the diaphragma sellae. The crossing fibers from the nasal half of each retina lie lowest, so they are compressed first.

Question 8005. A constructed illustration: a person describes losing the outer half of the field of vision in each eye, while the inner halves remain clear. Which structure is being compressed, and what is that pattern called? The answer is the optic chiasm, and the pattern is bitemporal hemianopia. Growth of the gland presses on the crossing fibers drawn from the nasal half of each retina.

Question 8006. Which cranial nerve runs through the interior of the cavernous sinus rather than in its lateral wall: the third, the fourth, the sixth, or the first division of the fifth? The answer is the sixth cranial nerve. It lies free in the sinus beside the internal carotid artery. The oculomotor, the trochlear and the ophthalmic nerves lie in the wall.

Question 8007. Within the cavernous sinus, on each side of the gland, which large vessel runs forward and gives small branches to the pituitary? The answer is the internal carotid artery. It grooves the side of the body of the sphenoid and lies against the medial wall of the sinus. The gland lies medial to it.

Question 8008. Which part of the pituitary gland develops from an upward pouch of the roof of the primitive mouth? The answer is the anterior lobe, also called the adenohypophysis. The pouch, named after Rathke, is a diverticulum of surface ectoderm. It loses its connection with the mouth and becomes the glandular lobe.

Question 8009. Which part of the pituitary gland develops as a downward extension of the diencephalon? The answer is the posterior lobe, also called the neurohypophysis. It is formed from the floor of the third ventricle and remains joined to the brain by the infundibulum. This is why it is nervous tissue rather than glandular tissue.

Question 8010. Which of these hormones is stored in the posterior lobe of the pituitary gland: growth hormone, oxytocin, prolactin, or thyroid stimulating hormone? The answer is oxytocin. It is made in the hypothalamus and carried down the axons of the infundibulum for storage. The other three are made and released by the anterior lobe.

Question 8011. Which two nuclei of the hypothalamus send their axons down the infundibulum to end in the posterior lobe? The answer is the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei. Their cell bodies lie in the wall of the third ventricle above the optic chiasm. Their axons form the hypothalamohypophyseal tract.

Question 8012. True or false: the posterior lobe of the pituitary gland manufactures the two hormones that it releases. The answer is false. The hormones are made in the cell bodies of the hypothalamic neurons. They travel down the axons and are stored in the posterior lobe until a nerve impulse releases them into the blood.

Question 8013. Which hormone of the anterior lobe acts on the cortex of the adrenal gland: growth hormone, adrenocorticotropic hormone, prolactin, or follicle stimulating hormone? The answer is adrenocorticotropic hormone. It drives the secretion of cortisol and of the adrenal androgens. Its release rises and falls with the demands of the day.

Question 8014. Which hormone of the anterior lobe tells the thyroid gland to make and release its own hormones? The answer is thyroid stimulating hormone. It is made by the thyrotroph cells of the anterior lobe. Its level in the blood falls when the thyroid hormones rise, a feedback loop the listener can picture.

Question 8015. Name two hormones of the anterior lobe that act on the gonads, spoken as a list. The answer is follicle stimulating hormone and luteinizing hormone. Together they are called the gonadotropins. They drive the growth of the ovarian follicle and the production of sperm.

Question 8016. Which cell of the anterior lobe secretes prolactin: the somatotroph, the lactotroph, the thyrotroph, or the corticotroph? The answer is the lactotroph. Prolactin starts and maintains the secretion of milk after birth. Dopamine from the hypothalamus holds its release in check. The name of the cell comes from the hormone that it makes.

Question 8017. A described microscopic section of the gland shows cords and clusters of cells lying between wide blood-filled sinusoids, with no follicles and no colloid anywhere. Which part of the gland is shown? The answer is the anterior lobe. Its cells are arranged in cords and nests rather than in rounded follicles. Follicles filled with colloid belong to the thyroid gland.

Question 8018. A described radiograph of the skull in profile shows a sella turcica that is deepened and ballooned, with its floor sunk into the sphenoid sinus. Which structure has enlarged to produce that appearance? The answer is the pituitary gland, usually because of a tumor within it. The gland enlarges inside the bony cup and erodes its floor. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8019. A constructed illustration: a person has a drooping eyelid, an eye that cannot look inward, and a wide pupil on one side. Which cranial nerve is compressed as the gland enlarges sideways? The answer is the oculomotor nerve, the third cranial nerve. It lies in the lateral wall of the cavernous sinus beside the gland.

Question 8020. Which of these pairs belongs together: thyrotropin releasing hormone with the thyrotroph, corticotropin releasing hormone with the lactotroph, growth hormone releasing hormone with the gonadotroph, or gonadotropin releasing hormone with the somatotroph? The answer is thyrotropin releasing hormone with the thyrotroph. Each releasing factor from the hypothalamus has its own target cell in the anterior lobe.

Question 8021. Immediately below the floor of the sella turcica lies which air-filled space, and why does that relation matter? The answer is the sphenoid sinus. The sinus lies between the pituitary fossa above and the nasopharynx below. Its position is what allows the gland to be reached through the nose.

Question 8022. A constructed illustration: a person with a known pituitary tumor suddenly has a severe headache, loses vision in both eyes, and becomes drowsy. What has most likely happened within the gland? The answer is bleeding into the tumor, a state called pituitary apoplexy. The swelling presses on the optic chiasm and the cavernous sinus at once. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8023. A described section through the head passes from front to back exactly in the midline and shows the pituitary gland in its fossa, with the sphenoid sinus below it and the third ventricle above it. In which plane is the section cut, and where does the gland lie? The answer is the median plane, with the gland above the sphenoid sinus and below the third ventricle.

Question 8024. A described magnetic resonance image of the head shows a small bean-shaped structure in the sella with a bright spot at its posterior end, next to the stalk. Which part of the gland is the bright spot, and what does it hold? The answer is the posterior lobe, which stores antidiuretic hormone and oxytocin. The stored hormones are bound to carrier proteins, and that is what gives the bright signal.

Question 8025. Which of these structures lies in the lateral wall of the cavernous sinus: the optic nerve, the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve, the facial nerve, or the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve. The optic nerve lies above and medial in the optic canal. The facial and hypoglossal nerves never enter the sinus at all.

Question 8026. What lies immediately above the diaphragma sellae, in the space that a pituitary tumor enters when it grows upward? The answer is the suprasellar cistern, with the optic chiasm in front of the infundibulum. The circle of Willis surrounds the region. Once a tumor breaks through the roof of the fossa it can press on these structures.

Question 8027. Which part of the adenohypophysis forms a thin collar that wraps around the infundibulum? The answer is the pars tuberalis. It is the upward extension of the anterior lobe along the stalk. The pars distalis is the main body of the lobe and the pars intermedia lies against the posterior lobe.

Question 8028. The secretory cells of the anterior lobe have no direct arterial supply of their own. By which arrangement are they fed: a portal venous system from the median eminence, a direct arterial plexus, lymphatic channels, or the cerebrospinal fluid? The answer is a portal venous system. Small arteries supply the median eminence, and the capillary bed there drains into veins that carry the hypothalamic factors down to the lobe.

Question 8029. Which artery supplies the posterior lobe of the pituitary gland: the inferior hypophyseal artery, the superior hypophyseal artery, the middle meningeal artery, or the ophthalmic artery? The answer is the inferior hypophyseal artery. It reaches the gland below the diaphragma sellae. The superior hypophyseal arteries supply the median eminence and the stalk instead.

Question 8030. True or false: the pituitary gland lies within the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone. The answer is true. The sella turcica is the saddle-shaped depression on the upper surface of the body of the sphenoid. Its deepest part, the hypophyseal fossa, holds the gland, and only the stalk leaves the fossa above.

Question 8031. A described dissection lays open the cavernous sinus on one side of the gland so that its contents can be seen. Which structure lies lowest in the lateral wall of the sinus? The answer is the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve. Above it lie the ophthalmic nerve, the trochlear nerve and the oculomotor nerve, in that order from below upward.

Question 8032. A pituitary tumor erodes downward through the floor of the sella. Which air-filled cavity does it enter first, and which fluid may then leak from the nose? The answer is the sphenoid sinus, with leakage of cerebrospinal fluid. The floor of the fossa is only a thin plate of bone above the sinus. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8033. A constructed illustration: after a difficult delivery with heavy bleeding, a woman cannot breastfeed, feels cold and tired, and loses her body hair. Which part of the gland has failed? The answer is the anterior lobe, a state called Sheehan syndrome. The gland, enlarged in pregnancy, is compressed when the blood pressure falls at delivery. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8034. Name the two hormones stored and released by the posterior lobe, spoken as a list. The answer is oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone. Oxytocin acts on the uterus and on the muscle of the breast. Antidiuretic hormone acts on the kidney to conserve water.

Question 8035. Which hormone, released from the posterior lobe, makes the collecting ducts of the kidney reabsorb water? The answer is antidiuretic hormone. Without it the kidney passes large volumes of dilute urine, a state called diabetes insipidus. The hormone is made in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus.

Question 8036. Which anterior lobe hormone acts on the liver and on the growth plates of the long bones: prolactin, growth hormone, thyroid stimulating hormone, or adrenocorticotropic hormone? The answer is growth hormone. It promotes growth of the skeleton in childhood and helps to regulate metabolism in adults. Its release is greatest during deep sleep.

Question 8037. A described section of the gland shows glandular tissue of one origin in front and nervous tissue of another origin behind, with a narrow cleft and scattered small cysts lying between them. Which developmental remnant is the cleft? The answer is the remnant of Rathke pouch. The pouch gives rise to the anterior lobe and normally closes off. Its remains can form a cyst.

Question 8038. Which of these pairs belongs together: the somatotroph with growth hormone, the lactotroph with thyroid stimulating hormone, the thyrotroph with prolactin, or the corticotroph with oxytocin? The answer is the somatotroph with growth hormone. Each cell type of the anterior lobe makes its own product, and the names of the cells follow the names of the hormones they make.

Question 8039. Which structure, filled with venous blood, lies on each side of the pituitary gland and receives the ophthalmic veins? The answer is the cavernous sinus. The two sinuses lie against the sides of the body of the sphenoid and connect with each other. The gland lies between them.

Question 8040. A constructed illustration: after an injury that tears the infundibulum, a person passes large volumes of dilute urine and is constantly thirsty. Which hormone has been lost, and from which part of the gland? The answer is antidiuretic hormone, from the posterior lobe. The axons that carry it are cut, so the lobe cannot release it. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8041. Which hormone causes the muscle of the breast to contract and eject milk: prolactin, oxytocin, growth hormone, or luteinizing hormone? The answer is oxytocin. Prolactin builds and maintains the secretion of milk, while oxytocin ejects it. The two hormones work together, and a listener should keep them apart.

Question 8042. What is the name of the small raised area of the hypothalamus from which the infundibulum descends and into which the superior hypophyseal arteries send their blood? The answer is the median eminence. It lies on the floor of the third ventricle behind the optic chiasm. Its capillaries are the first limb of the hypophyseal portal system.

Question 8043. A described magnetic resonance image in the midline shows a sella that is not filled by gland tissue, with the infundibulum descending into it and the gland flattened against the floor. What is this appearance called, and what does it usually mean? The answer is an empty sella. It usually means that the diaphragma sellae is incomplete and cerebrospinal fluid has pushed the gland aside.

Question 8044. A described specimen of the base of the brain shows a rounded body hanging from a stalk behind the optic chiasm and between the two halves of the circle of Willis. Which structure is shown? The answer is the pituitary gland, hanging on the infundibulum. The stalk carries the fibers and the portal vessels that connect it with the hypothalamus.

Question 8045. Which tumor of this region arises from remnants of the pouch that forms the anterior lobe and often shows flecks of calcium above the sella in a child: a craniopharyngioma, a meningioma, an acoustic neuroma, or a chordoma? The answer is a craniopharyngioma. It grows above the diaphragma sellae and presses on the optic chiasm. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8046. What general name is given to the pituitary gland together with its stalk, the name used for the whole structure at the base of the brain? The answer is the hypophysis. The glandular front part is the adenohypophysis and the nervous back part is the neurohypophysis. The name itself means the outgrowth that lies below.

Question 8047. A described cross section of the head at the level of the sella shows a small gland in the midline, a venous channel on each side of it, and a thin layer of bone below it. Which structure lies immediately on each side of the gland? The answer is the cavernous sinus. Each sinus carries the internal carotid artery and several cranial nerves in its wall and interior.

Question 8048. Which of these statements about the position of the gland is correct: it lies between the two cavernous sinuses, it lies behind the sphenoid sinus, it lies in front of the frontal sinus, or it lies within the temporal bone? The answer is that it lies between the two cavernous sinuses. The gland occupies the midline of the sella, with a sinus on either side.

Question 8049. Which thin strip of tissue lies against the back of the anterior lobe and is a remnant of the wall of the pouch that formed the gland: the pars intermedia, the pars tuberalis, the pars distalis, or the median eminence? The answer is the pars intermedia. It lies between the two lobes and is poorly developed in the adult human gland.

Question 8050. Which statement about the blood supply of the pituitary gland is correct: the anterior lobe is fed mainly by portal veins, the posterior lobe is fed mainly by portal veins, neither lobe receives blood, or both lobes are supplied only by the middle meningeal artery? The answer is that the anterior lobe is fed mainly by portal veins from the median eminence. The posterior lobe receives arterial blood directly.

Question 8051. In the front of the neck, the thyroid gland wraps around which air passage, and just below which structure does it lie? The answer is the trachea, just below the larynx. The gland sits against the front and the sides of the upper windpipe. Its two lobes lie on either side, joined across the midline.

Question 8052. What is the name of the narrow band of thyroid tissue that joins the two lobes across the midline? The answer is the isthmus. It crosses in front of the upper part of the trachea. In some glands it is absent, and in others a third lobe rises from it.

Question 8053. The isthmus of the thyroid gland usually crosses in front of which tracheal cartilages: the first and second, the second to the fourth, the fifth and sixth, or the cricoid cartilage alone? The answer is the second to the fourth. The gland is fixed at that level, which is why it rises with the larynx when a person swallows.

Question 8054. Which pair of flat muscles lies immediately in front of each lobe of the thyroid gland, covering it before the skin is reached? The answer is the sternothyroid and the sternohyoid muscles. They form the strap muscles of the neck. They must be parted to reach the gland at operation.

Question 8055. Which artery reaches the upper pole of the thyroid gland as the first branch of the external carotid artery: the superior thyroid artery, the inferior thyroid artery, the thyroidea ima artery, or the lingual artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery. It runs downward to the upper pole beside the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve.

Question 8056. Which artery reaches the lower pole of the thyroid gland, arising from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery. It approaches the gland from below and behind, and it is closely related to the recurrent laryngeal nerve along the way.

Question 8057. In the groove between the trachea and the esophagus, which nerve runs upward toward the larynx, and how does it relate to the inferior thyroid artery? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve, which passes among the branches of the artery. The nerve may run in front of, behind, or between those branches, and the variation is normal.

Question 8058. A constructed illustration: after an operation on the thyroid gland, a person's voice becomes hoarse and one vocal cord does not move. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It supplies all the muscles of the larynx except the cricothyroid. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8059. A described dissection of the lower pole of the thyroid gland shows a pale nerve crossing beneath the gland and dividing into small branches beside the artery. Which structures are shown? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve and the inferior thyroid artery. The two lie in the same small space and are easily confused with each other.

Question 8060. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery, the inferior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery, the superior thyroid artery with the subclavian artery, or the thyroidea ima artery with the facial artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery. It is its first branch.

Question 8061. What is the name of the small third lobe of thyroid tissue that rises from the isthmus in about half of all glands? The answer is the pyramidal lobe. It is the remains of the lower end of the thyroglossal duct. It usually lies just to the left of the midline.

Question 8062. Which veins of the thyroid gland drain directly into the internal jugular vein: the superior and middle thyroid veins, the inferior thyroid veins, the thyroidea ima vein, or the vertebral veins? The answer is the superior and middle thyroid veins. The inferior thyroid veins drain into the brachiocephalic veins instead.

Question 8063. Into which veins do the inferior thyroid veins empty: the internal jugular veins, the brachiocephalic veins, the superior vena cava, or the external jugular veins? The answer is the brachiocephalic veins. They leave the lower part of the gland and run downward in front of the trachea. The left one is usually the longer of the two.

Question 8064. Which sheath lies posterolateral to each lobe of the thyroid gland, carrying the common carotid artery and the internal jugular vein? The answer is the carotid sheath. The gland lies medial to it in the front of the neck. This is why an enlarging gland can press upon the vessels within it.

Question 8065. A described microscopic section of the gland shows rounded sacs lined by a single layer of cuboidal cells and filled with pink material. What are the sacs, and what is the pink material? The answer is thyroid follicles filled with colloid. The colloid holds thyroglobulin, the store from which the thyroid hormones are built.

Question 8066. What kind of epithelium lines a thyroid follicle: simple squamous, simple cuboidal, stratified squamous, or pseudostratified columnar? The answer is simple cuboidal epithelium. The cells take iodide from the blood and build the thyroid hormones. The follicle is the unit in which the gland stores them.

Question 8067. Name the two hormones that the follicles of the thyroid gland produce. The answer is thyroxine and triiodothyronine. Both contain iodine, and triiodothyronine is the more active of the two at the cell. They raise the rate at which the body uses oxygen.

Question 8068. Name two effects of thyroid hormone on the body, spoken as a list. The answer is a raised rate of metabolism and a normal growth of the brain and skeleton. Every cell responds by using more oxygen and giving off more heat. In a child the hormone is needed for normal mental development.

Question 8069. True or false: the thyroid gland is the largest endocrine gland in the body. The answer is true. It weighs about 25 grams in a normal adult. The pituitary gland weighs only about half a gram, and the adrenal glands together weigh less than the thyroid.

Question 8070. Which cells of the thyroid gland secrete calcitonin, a hormone that lowers the level of calcium in the blood: the follicular cells, the parafollicular cells, the parathyroid cells, or the cells of the parotid gland? The answer is the parafollicular cells. They lie between the follicles and are also called the clear cells of the gland.

Question 8071. From which embryonic structure do the parafollicular cells of the thyroid gland arise? The answer is the ultimobranchial body, an outgrowth of the lower pharyngeal pouches. The cells migrate into the gland from the neural crest. They are the only part of the thyroid that does not come from the thyroglossal duct.

Question 8072. On which surface of the thyroid gland do the parathyroid glands usually lie, and how many are usually present? The answer is the posterior surface, with four glands in all, two on each side. The upper pair lies near the middle of the lobe. The lower pair lies near the lower pole.

Question 8073. The inferior parathyroid gland and the thymus share which origin: the third pharyngeal pouch, the fourth pharyngeal pouch, the second pharyngeal pouch, or the thyroglossal duct? The answer is the third pharyngeal pouch. The superior parathyroid glands come from the fourth pouch. This is why the lower glands travel farther down the neck.

Question 8074. A described specimen shows the back of a thyroid lobe, where four small flattened bodies lie in the fat, two above and two below. Which structures are they, and what do they secrete? The answer is the parathyroid glands, which secrete parathyroid hormone. That hormone raises the level of calcium in the blood.

Question 8075. A constructed illustration: a person has a swelling in the front of the neck that moves up and down when swallowing and now presses on the windpipe, so that breathing is hard when lying flat. What is the swelling, and why does it move? The answer is an enlarged thyroid gland, called a goiter, which moves because the pretracheal fascia binds it to the larynx. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8076. Why does the thyroid gland rise and fall with every swallow: it is attached to the larynx by the pretracheal fascia, it lies within the carotid sheath, it is fixed to the vertebral column, or it is bound to the esophagus? The answer is that it is attached to the larynx by the pretracheal fascia.

Question 8077. Which layer of deep cervical fascia forms the false capsule that binds the thyroid gland to the larynx? The answer is the pretracheal fascia. It wraps the gland and the trachea and is attached to the thyroid cartilage. The gland's own fibrous capsule lies inside it.

Question 8078. True or false: a midline swelling of the neck that moves upward when the tongue is put out is usually a cyst of the thyroglossal duct. The answer is true. The duct once ran from the foramen cecum of the tongue down to the gland. The cyst follows the path of the duct and is pulled by the tongue.

Question 8079. A described photograph of the front of the neck shows the larynx above and, just below it, a reddish gland shaped like a butterfly whose wings curve around the sides of the windpipe. Which gland is shown? The answer is the thyroid gland. Its two lobes lie on either side of the trachea and its isthmus crosses in front.

Question 8080. Which hormone raises the rate at which the cells of the body use oxygen: insulin, thyroxine, calcitonin, or parathyroid hormone? The answer is thyroxine. It acts on almost every cell and helps to set the rate of metabolism. Calcitonin and parathyroid hormone act on calcium instead.

Question 8081. Which hormone of the thyroid gland lowers the level of calcium in the blood? The answer is calcitonin. It is made by the parafollicular cells and works against parathyroid hormone. Its effect is small in the adult but clear during the growth of a child.

Question 8082. Behind the thyroid gland lie two tubes, one directly in front of the other. Which are they, and in what order? The answer is the trachea in front and the esophagus behind it. The gland wraps the front and the sides of the airway. The tracheoesophageal groove separates the two tubes and carries the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

Question 8083. A constructed illustration: a person has a swelling of the whole thyroid gland, a rapid heart rate, weight loss, and eyes that appear to bulge. What is the usual cause of this picture? The answer is Graves disease, in which antibodies stimulate the thyroid gland. The gland makes too much hormone. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8084. Which statement about papillary carcinoma of the thyroid gland is correct: it spreads mainly through lymph vessels, it spreads mainly through the blood to the bones, it arises from the parafollicular cells, or it never involves lymph nodes? The answer is that it spreads mainly through lymph vessels. Follicular carcinoma spreads through the blood instead.

Question 8085. A described microscopic section of a thyroid tumor shows branching papillae, crowded cells with clear empty-looking nuclei, and small round calcified bodies scattered through the tissue. Which tumor is described? The answer is papillary carcinoma. The calcified bodies are called psammoma bodies. It is the commonest thyroid cancer and it spreads to lymph nodes.

Question 8086. Which of these pairs belongs together: the parafollicular cell with calcitonin, the follicular cell with calcitonin, the parathyroid cell with thyroxine, or the thyrotroph with parathyroid hormone? The answer is the parafollicular cell with calcitonin. The follicular cells make thyroxine and triiodothyronine, and the parathyroid cells make parathyroid hormone.

Question 8087. A described transverse section of the neck at the level of the thyroid shows a gland wrapping the front and the sides of a cartilaginous tube, with a muscular tube behind it and a sheath holding large vessels on either side. Which structures are the two tubes? The answer is the trachea in front and the esophagus behind. The carotid sheath lies laterally on each side.

Question 8088. Which of these arteries may reach the isthmus of the thyroid gland in the midline from below: the thyroidea ima artery, the superior thyroid artery, the lingual artery, or the occipital artery? The answer is the thyroidea ima artery. It is present in only some people and may arise from the brachiocephalic trunk or from the arch of the aorta.

Question 8089. A constructed illustration: an older person has a large thyroid swelling that extends behind the sternum and now cannot lie flat because of noisy, difficult breathing. Which structure is compressed, and why does the position matter? The answer is the trachea, which is pressed by the part of the gland lying behind the sternum. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8090. Injury to the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve during thyroid surgery weakens which function: the ability to shout loudly, the ability to make high-pitched sounds, the ability to swallow solid food, or the ability to feel the skin of the neck? The answer is the ability to make high-pitched sounds, because the nerve supplies the cricothyroid muscle.

Question 8091. A described view of the operative field at the lower pole of the thyroid shows a red artery dividing into branches that embrace the gland, with a nerve running up in the groove behind the trachea. Which two structures are shown? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery and the recurrent laryngeal nerve. Their closeness is the reason for careful dissection there.

Question 8092. In the front of the neck, between which two layers does the thyroid gland lie: between the skin and the strap muscles, between the strap muscles and the trachea, between the trachea and the esophagus, or between the esophagus and the vertebral column? The answer is between the strap muscles and the trachea, wrapped in the pretracheal fascia.

Question 8093. Which nerve lies in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus as it runs up toward the larynx? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. On the right it hooks around the subclavian artery and on the left around the arch of the aorta. It supplies almost all the muscles of the larynx.

Question 8094. A thyroglossal duct cyst is usually found where: in the midline of the neck just below the hyoid bone, at the angle of the jaw, in the floor of the mouth, or in the upper part of the chest? The answer is the midline just below the hyoid bone. It may lie anywhere along the path the duct once followed.

Question 8095. A described photograph shows a child with a smooth round swelling in the middle of the front of the neck, and the swelling rises when the child puts out the tongue. Which structure is the swelling, and from what does it come? The answer is a thyroglossal duct cyst, from a remnant of the duct by which the gland descended.

Question 8096. A constructed illustration: a young person has a lump at the base of the tongue that bleeds easily, and no thyroid tissue can be felt in the neck. What is the likely explanation? The answer is a lingual thyroid, an ectopic gland that failed to descend. The base of the tongue is the commonest ectopic site. The treating clinician decides what is done.

Question 8097. If the thyroid gland fails to descend from the floor of the pharynx during development, where does the glandular tissue remain? The answer is at the base of the tongue, which is called a lingual thyroid. The gland begins as a diverticulum at the foramen cecum. It normally travels down the front of the pharynx to the neck.

Question 8098. Which of these pairs belongs together: the thyroid gland with the pretracheal fascia, the thyroid gland with the carotid sheath, the thyroid gland with the prevertebral fascia, or the thyroid gland with the investing fascia of the neck? The answer is the thyroid gland with the pretracheal fascia, which binds it to the larynx.

Question 8099. True or false: the inferior thyroid artery is a branch of the external carotid artery. The answer is false. It arises from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery and runs upward to the lower pole. The superior thyroid artery is the one that comes from the external carotid artery.

Question 8100. Which statement about the blood supply of the thyroid gland is correct: the gland receives the superior and inferior thyroid arteries on each side, it receives only the inferior thyroid artery, it has no arteries and is fed by diffusion, or it is supplied by the facial artery? The answer is that it receives the superior and inferior thyroid arteries on each side, and sometimes a midline thyroidea ima artery as well.

To summarize, the pituitary gland lies in the hypophyseal fossa of the sphenoid bone, roofed by the diaphragma sellae and flanked by the cavernous sinuses with the internal carotid artery and cranial nerves in their walls. Its anterior lobe comes from Rathke pouch and is fed by the hypophyseal portal veins from the median eminence, while its posterior lobe comes from the diencephalon and stores oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone made in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei. The optic chiasm lies just above the gland and is the first structure compressed by a growing tumor, which is why a loss of the outer half of each visual field is such a telling sign. The thyroid gland wraps the front and sides of the trachea below the larynx, bound to the larynx by the pretracheal fascia so that it moves on swallowing, and it is supplied by the superior and inferior thyroid arteries with the recurrent laryngeal nerve running in the groove behind. Its follicles store colloid and make thyroxine and triiodothyronine, its parafollicular cells make calcitonin, and the parathyroid glands lie on its posterior surface.
