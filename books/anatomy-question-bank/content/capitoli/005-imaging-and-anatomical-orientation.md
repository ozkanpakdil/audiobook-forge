# Chapter 5 — Imaging and Anatomical Orientation

This chapter is about finding structures on the surface of the body and about reading the pictures that show them inside it. It begins with the surface markings that can be felt or seen, and then turns to X-ray films, computed tomography, known as CT, magnetic resonance imaging, known as MRI, and ultrasound. It explains what a sagittal, a coronal and an axial image each show, and it names the bony landmarks that fix the level of every picture. The chapter holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 401. Which ridge felt on the front of the chest marks the junction of the manubrium with the body of the sternum? The answer is the sternal angle, at the level of the disc between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae. The second costal cartilage joins the sternum there, so rib counting begins at that ridge.

Question 402. The transpyloric plane is a horizontal plane used to describe the upper abdomen. At which vertebral level does it lie: the twelfth thoracic, the first lumbar, the third lumbar, or the fifth lumbar? The answer is the first lumbar vertebra. The plane passes through the pylorus of the stomach and the neck of the pancreas, near the tip of the ninth costal cartilage.

Question 403. Which plane is drawn across the back through the highest points of the two iliac crests? The answer is the supracristal plane, at the level of the fourth lumbar vertebra. It is the surface guide for choosing a level to sample cerebrospinal fluid, low enough that the spinal cord has ended.

Question 404. The umbilicus is a familiar landmark in the middle of the abdominal wall. Which vertebral level does it mark in the adult? The answer is the disc between the third and fourth lumbar vertebrae. The level shifts with body build, so it is only an approximate guide.

Question 405. Which structure can be felt at the mid-inguinal point, halfway between the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic symphysis: the femoral vein, the femoral artery, the femoral nerve, or the femoral canal? The answer is the femoral artery. The vein lies at the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, medial to the artery, and the nerve lies most lateral.

Question 406. The inguinal ligament is a surface landmark at the groin. Which two bony points do its ends join? The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic tubercle. It is the folded lower border of the external oblique aponeurosis, and the femoral vessels pass beneath its midpoint.

Question 407. A described photograph taken from behind shows a bony point in the midline at the base of the neck, most obvious when the head is bent forward. Which structure is shown? The answer is the spine of the seventh cervical vertebra. Its spine is the longest of the cervical spines, with the first thoracic spine just below.

Question 408. A constructed illustration: after a heavy object falls on the top of the foot, the pulse on the instep can no longer be felt. Between which two tendons is that pulse normally found? The answer is the tendon of extensor hallucis longus and the tendon of extensor digitorum longus. The dorsalis pedis artery runs between them.

Question 409. Which artery can be felt in the hollow at the base of the thumb when the wrist is drawn back? The answer is the radial artery, in the floor of the anatomical snuffbox. The hollow is bounded by abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis on one side and extensor pollicis longus on the other.

Question 410. The surface point used to mark the appendix lies on a line between the umbilicus and the right anterior superior iliac spine. Where on that line does it lie: one third from the umbilicus, one half, two thirds, or at the iliac spine? The answer is two thirds of the way from the umbilicus, one third of the way up from the iliac spine. That point is named after McBurney.

Question 411. Which surface region lies between the twelfth rib and the outer border of the erector spinae muscle, and overlies the lower part of the kidney? The answer is the renal angle. Tenderness there points to disease of the kidney behind the peritoneum, whose lower pole lies nearest the surface at that spot.

Question 412. The line along which the pleura turns from the chest wall onto the diaphragm can be traced on the surface. Which rib does it cross in the midclavicular line, and which at the side of the chest? The answer is the eighth rib in the midclavicular line and the tenth rib in the midaxillary line. Behind, it reaches the twelfth rib.

Question 413. A constructed illustration: a clinician must choose a level to sample cerebrospinal fluid while keeping clear of the spinal cord. Which spinous process is identified by the line joining the highest points of the iliac crests? The answer is the fourth lumbar spine. The cord usually ends above it, at the first or second lumbar vertebra.

Question 414. A duct crosses the cheek and opens inside the mouth. Which tooth does it lie opposite: the upper first incisor, the upper second molar, the lower first molar, or the lower second incisor? The answer is the upper second molar. That duct is the parotid duct, and it crosses the masseter muscle before turning inward.

Question 415. Which cartilage of the larynx forms a complete ring and can be felt in the midline below the laryngeal prominence? The answer is the cricoid cartilage, at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra. The soft membrane between it and the thyroid cartilage above is the cricothyroid membrane.

Question 416. True or false: in an adult man the nipple usually lies over the fourth intercostal space. The answer is true. The position varies with body build, and greatly in women, so the sternal angle remains the more dependable guide to the fourth rib.

Question 417. Which of these pairs belongs together: the sternal angle with the eighth thoracic vertebra, the xiphisternal joint with the ninth thoracic vertebra, the highest point of the iliac crest with the second lumbar vertebra, or the pubic symphysis with the fifth lumbar vertebra? The answer is the xiphisternal joint with the ninth thoracic vertebra. The sternal angle lies at the fourth thoracic vertebra and the iliac crest at the fourth lumbar.

Question 418. A constructed illustration: a person with a fever is examined for an enlarged spleen, and percussion in the lowest part of the left axilla, below the tenth rib, becomes dull where it was resonant. Which organ has enlarged into that space? The answer is the spleen. It lies behind the stomach and the left costal margin and descends toward the left iliac fossa.

Question 419. Which rib forms the lowest point of the costal margin at the front of the abdomen: the seventh, the eighth, the ninth, or the tenth? The answer is the tenth. The margin is formed by the cartilages of the seventh to the tenth ribs, and the eleventh and twelfth ribs float free.

Question 420. Which joint at the lower end of the sternum can be felt in the midline as a small depression, and at which vertebral level does it lie? The answer is the xiphisternal joint, at the level of the ninth thoracic vertebra. It is where the xiphoid process joins the body of the sternum.

Question 421. A described photograph of a wrist held slightly bent shows a prominent tendon running to the base of the second metacarpal, with a pulse just lateral to it. Which tendon and which artery are shown? The answer is the tendon of flexor carpi radialis and the radial artery. Both cross the front of the wrist, the artery lateral to the tendon.

Question 422. The beat of the heart is sought on the chest wall. In which space does it usually appear: the third intercostal space at the sternal edge, the fourth in the midclavicular line, the fifth in the midclavicular line, or the sixth in the anterior axillary line? The answer is the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line, where the right ventricle comes closest to the chest wall.

Question 423. A described area on the back is bounded above by the trapezius, laterally by the medial border of the scapula, and below by the latissimus dorsi. Which triangle is described, and why is it useful? The answer is the triangle of auscultation. Over it the chest wall is thin and free of muscle, so breath sounds are heard there with least interference.

Question 424. The great saphenous vein can be traced on the surface from the foot upward. Which side of the medial malleolus does it pass, and where does it join the deep venous system? The answer is that it passes in front of the medial malleolus and opens into the femoral vein at the saphenous opening in the thigh.

Question 425. The oblique fissure of the lung can be marked on the surface. From which vertebral spine does it begin behind, and which costal cartilage does it reach in front? The answer is the spine of the third thoracic vertebra and the sixth costal cartilage. The horizontal fissure runs forward from it along the fourth costal cartilage.

Question 426. On a plain X-ray film, four substances are compared. Which appears whitest because it blocks the beam most completely: air, fat, muscle, or metal? The answer is metal. Bone is the whitest natural tissue and air casts almost no shadow, so air appears black.

Question 427. What name is given to a structure that appears white on a radiograph because it absorbs the beam, and what name to one that appears black? The answer is radiopaque for the white structure and radiolucent for the black one. Bone is radiopaque and air is radiolucent, with fat between them.

Question 428. A described chest radiograph shows a white area in the right lung that has erased the outline of the right border of the heart, while the right half of the diaphragm is still sharp. Which lobe is consolidated: the upper, the middle, the lower, or the whole lung? The answer is the right middle lobe, which lies against the right heart border. The lower lobe lies against the diaphragm.

Question 429. A described radiograph of the chest shows a dense white area in the right lower zone that hides the outline of the right half of the diaphragm but leaves the right heart border clear. Which lobe is affected? The answer is the right lower lobe. It rests on the diaphragm and lies behind the middle lobe.

Question 430. A described lateral radiograph of the chest shows two curved domes and a dark bubble of gas below one of them. Which dome lies higher, and which organ lies immediately beneath it? The answer is the right dome, with the liver beneath it. The left dome lies lower, with the stomach and spleen beneath.

Question 431. A constructed illustration: a person falls on the outstretched hand and is tender in the hollow at the base of the thumb. Which bone is most likely broken there, and which artery crosses the floor of that hollow? The answer is the scaphoid, with the radial artery crossing the floor of the anatomical snuffbox. A break through the waist threatens the blood supply to the proximal part.

Question 432. Which of these pairs belongs together: air with the density of water, fat with the density of bone, water with the middle of the density scale, or bone with the density of air? The answer is water with the middle of the density scale. Air, fat, water, bone and metal shade from black to white in that order, so the other pairs are reversed.

Question 433. True or false: the heart looks larger on a chest radiograph taken with the beam passing from the front to the back than on one taken from the back to the front. The answer is true. When the beam travels from front to back the heart lies far from the film, so its shadow is thrown larger.

Question 434. A described lateral radiograph of the neck shows a small peg of bone rising from the second cervical vertebra into the ring of the first. Which structure is the peg, and which vertebra carries it? The answer is the dens of the axis, carried by the second cervical vertebra. The first cervical vertebra turns around it when the head rotates.

Question 435. What name is given to the branching dark pattern of air-filled airways seen against a white area of consolidated lung on a radiograph? The answer is an air bronchogram. The bronchi still hold air while the tissue around them has taken on the density of water, as in pneumonia and pulmonary edema.

Question 436. A described chest radiograph shows a white area in the left lung that has erased the left border of the heart while leaving the left half of the diaphragm sharp. Which part of the left lung lies against that border: the apical segment, the lingula, or one of the basal segments? The answer is the lingula, a tongue-like part of the left upper lobe, while the basal segments lie against the diaphragm.

Question 437. On the scale of density used in CT, which substance is chosen as the zero point? The answer is water. Air lies far below it at the black end of the scale and dense bone far above at the white end, so the scale runs from very negative numbers to very positive ones.

Question 438. A CT scan of the body is displayed with different window settings. Which setting shows the cortex of a bone best: the soft tissue window, the lung window, the bone window, or the brain window? The answer is the bone window. It stretches the small differences between dense tissues, while the others render bone uniformly white.

Question 439. A CT scan of the head is taken soon after an injury. Fresh blood in the cranial cavity appears how: darker than brain tissue, the same shade, brighter than brain tissue, or completely black? The answer is brighter than brain tissue. Fresh clotted blood is denser than brain, so it is called hyperdense, and it darkens as the clot ages.

Question 440. A described axial CT image of the chest shows a collection of air with a straight inner edge lying against the chest wall, and the lung compressed behind it. What is the collection? The answer is air in the pleural cavity, a pneumothorax. It is black because it has the density of air, and the straight edge is the pleural surface of the lung.

Question 441. A described axial CT image of the upper abdomen shows two vessels lying immediately in front of a vertebral body, one on each side of the midline. Which vessel lies to the left, and which to the right? The answer is the aorta on the left and the inferior vena cava on the right. The vena cava lies more anteriorly, against the liver.

Question 442. A constructed illustration: an older person falls, and a CT scan shows a crescent-shaped collection hugging the curve of one cerebral hemisphere, crossing the sutures, with the midline pushed aside. Which vessels have torn, and where has the blood collected? The answer is the bridging veins, which have bled into the subdural space. A crescent crosses suture lines, while an extradural collection is lens-shaped and stops at them.

Question 443. Name the two natural substances of the body that lie at the opposite ends of the density scale used in CT, one black and one white. The answer is air and bone. Air absorbs almost none of the beam and bone absorbs a great deal, while fat and water lie between them.

Question 444. A described CT image shows bright and dark lines radiating from a small metallic object and spoiling the picture around it. What is this appearance called: a partial volume effect, a streak artifact, a window setting, or a contrast phase? The answer is a streak artifact. The dense object absorbs the beam so unevenly that the reconstruction invents lines.

Question 445. A described CT image of the upper abdomen shows a long lobulated organ lying across the midline behind the stomach, with its tail reaching toward the spleen. Which organ is described, and what lies behind its neck? The answer is the pancreas, with the formation of the portal vein behind its neck. Its head lies within the curve of the duodenum.

Question 446. A described CT image of the pelvis shows a rounded structure in the midline, a hip joint on each side, and a dark collection of gas and feces behind it. Which structure lies in the midline: the uterus, the filled urinary bladder, the rectum, or the prostate? The answer is the filled urinary bladder. Urine has the density of water and appears gray, while the gas behind it is almost black.

Question 447. What name is given to the first image of a CT examination, a single view of the whole region taken so that the technologist can choose the levels of the slices to follow? The answer is the scout image, also called the topogram. It is a plain projection rather than a cross-section, and it carries markers that give each slice its level.

Question 448. On a T1-weighted magnetic resonance image, which tissue appears bright and which fluid appears dark? The answer is that fat appears bright and water appears dark. A T1-weighted image is the one on which anatomical detail is clearest, and cerebrospinal fluid is black on it.

Question 449. Which of these appears brightest on a T2-weighted magnetic resonance image: fat, muscle, cerebrospinal fluid, or cortical bone? The answer is cerebrospinal fluid. A T2-weighted image is the one on which fluid stands out, so it shows the fluid-filled spaces, while bone stays black.

Question 450. Which of these findings matters most before a magnetic resonance examination: a healed fracture of the wrist, a cardiac pacemaker, a history of asthma, or a tattoo on the arm? The answer is a cardiac pacemaker. A strong magnetic field can move or heat metal inside the body, so metal implants and electronic devices must be identified beforehand.

Question 451. A described sagittal magnetic resonance image of the knee shows a dark band running obliquely through the joint from the front of the tibia to the back of the femur. Which structure is that band? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament. It stops the tibia from sliding forward on the femur and is dark on both weightings.

Question 452. Which tissue of the body yields almost no signal on any magnetic resonance sequence, so that it is black whatever settings are used? The answer is cortical bone. It has very few mobile protons for the machine to detect, and air behaves in the same way, while marrow is bright because it contains fat.

Question 453. A described magnetic resonance image of the shoulder shows a muscle running above the head of the humerus and passing beneath a bony arch. Which muscle is it, and through which space does its tendon pass? The answer is the supraspinatus, whose tendon passes beneath the acromion through the subacromial space. That tendon is the one most often torn.

Question 454. A magnetic resonance image is taken with a setting that removes the signal of fat, so that a structure containing water stands out against the marrow. What is that setting called: the T1 weighting, the T2 weighting, fat suppression, or the scout view? The answer is fat suppression. It shows inflammation and fluid in bone and soft tissue, where fat would otherwise hide them.

Question 455. On a described magnetic resonance image of the spine, which structure lies within the vertebral canal immediately in front of the spinal cord: the ligamentum flavum, the posterior longitudinal ligament, the interspinous ligament, or the anterior longitudinal ligament? The answer is the posterior longitudinal ligament. The ligamentum flavum lies behind the cord, and the anterior longitudinal ligament on the front of the vertebral bodies.

Question 456. Name two tissues that appear dark on both a T1-weighted and a T2-weighted magnetic resonance image. The answer is cortical bone and air. Neither contains enough mobile protons to give a signal, and flowing blood can appear dark in the same way.

Question 457. A constructed illustration: a described magnetic resonance image shows a rounded mass enlarging the pituitary fossa, and the structure above it is stretched and pushed upward. Which structure lies immediately above the pituitary gland? The answer is the optic chiasm. The gland sits in the sella turcica, and the chiasm above it is pressed on as the gland enlarges.

Question 458. What term describes a structure on an ultrasound scan that returns no echoes at all and appears completely black, such as the fluid in the gallbladder? The answer is anechoic. Simple fluid is anechoic, while solid organs such as the liver return many small echoes and appear gray.

Question 459. On a described ultrasound scan of the upper abdomen, which of these appears completely black with no internal echoes: the liver, the spleen, the pancreas, or the gallbladder full of bile? The answer is the gallbladder full of bile. Fluid returns no echoes, while the solid organs return a texture of gray echoes.

Question 460. A described ultrasound scan shows a bright curved line within the gallbladder, and behind it the echoes disappear into a dark band. What is that band: acoustic enhancement behind fluid, acoustic shadowing behind a stone, a window setting, or a streak artifact? The answer is acoustic shadowing behind a stone, because the stone reflects the sound and stops it reaching the tissues beyond.

Question 461. A described ultrasound scan shows a round dark structure within the kidney, and the echoes behind it are brighter than those beside it. What is the structure, and why are the echoes behind it brighter? The answer is a simple cyst, and the brightness is posterior acoustic enhancement. Sound passes more easily through fluid than through the tissue around it.

Question 462. A described ultrasound scan uses the Doppler effect to examine a vessel. What does the shift in frequency of the returning echoes show: the density of the tissue, the direction and speed of blood flow, the depth of the organ, or its temperature? The answer is the direction and speed of blood flow. Blood moving toward the probe shifts the echoes up in frequency, and blood moving away shifts them down.

Question 463. A constructed illustration: a described ultrasound scan of the thigh shows a dark tubular structure that does not flatten when the probe presses on it, while the artery beside it does. What has happened within that vessel? The answer is a clot within a deep vein. A normal vein collapses under gentle pressure, so a vein that stays open is the sign of thrombosis.

Question 464. On a described ultrasound scan of the neck, the lobe of the thyroid gland lies against the side of the trachea. Which large vessel lies immediately lateral to that lobe, and what lies immediately medial to it? The answer is the common carotid artery laterally and the trachea medially. The internal jugular vein lies lateral to the artery.

Question 465. A described ultrasound scan of the heart is taken with the probe on the upper abdomen and shows four chambers. Which chamber lies nearest the probe: the left atrium, the right atrium, the left ventricle, or the right ventricle? The answer is the right ventricle, the most anterior chamber of the heart. The left atrium is the most posterior.

Question 466. Which plane produces a sagittal image: one that divides the body into upper and lower parts, one that divides it into right and left parts, one that divides it into front and back parts, or one that cuts across a limb? The answer is a plane that divides the body into right and left parts. It runs from front to back, and the one through the midline is the median plane.

Question 467. Which band of white matter is seen in the midline on a sagittal image of the brain, arching above the thalamus and joining the two hemispheres? The answer is the corpus callosum. Only a section close to the midline shows it in this way, and the third ventricle lies below it.

Question 468. A described sagittal image of the head shows a bony hollow in the base of the skull, with a rounded gland inside it and an air space beneath. Which gland is shown, and in which bone does it lie? The answer is the pituitary gland, in the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone.

Question 469. On a described sagittal image of the male pelvis, which structure lies immediately in front of the rectum and immediately behind the pubic symphysis? The answer is the urinary bladder. The prostate gland lies below the bladder, and the sacrum and coccyx lie behind the rectum.

Question 470. On a described sagittal image of the ankle, which bone lies directly above the calcaneus and carries the weight of the body onto the foot: the navicular, the talus, the cuboid, or the first metatarsal? The answer is the talus. It sits between the leg bones above and the calcaneus below and forms the ankle joint with the tibia.

Question 471. A described midline sagittal image of the chest shows a long muscular tube running down behind the windpipe and in front of the vertebral column. Which structure is shown, and what lies in front of it below? The answer is the esophagus, with the trachea in front of it above and the left atrium in front of it below.

Question 472. True or false: a sagittal image taken through the middle of the head shows the two cerebral hemispheres side by side in the same picture. The answer is false. A midline section lies between the hemispheres and shows the medial surface of each, so the two are never displayed side by side.

Question 473. A described section of the trunk shows the heart and one lung, with the vertebral column behind and the front of the chest wall in front, and only one kidney is visible. Which plane produced it, and why is it not the median plane? The answer is a parasagittal plane, cut to one side of the midline. A median section would lie between the lungs and show neither of them in section.

Question 474. Which plane divides the body into an anterior part and a posterior part: the sagittal plane, the coronal plane, the transverse plane, or the oblique plane? The answer is the coronal plane. It runs from side to side and from top to bottom, so the section is viewed from the front or from behind.

Question 475. A described coronal image of the abdomen shows the two kidneys on either side of the midline and two long vessels running down in front of the vertebral column. Which vessels are they, and which lies on the right? The answer is the aorta and the inferior vena cava, with the vena cava on the right.

Question 476. A described coronal magnetic resonance image of the brain shows the two lateral ventricles above a narrow channel that joins the third ventricle to the fourth. Which structure is that channel? The answer is the cerebral aqueduct, which runs through the midbrain. It is the narrowest part of the ventricular system, so it blocks most easily.

Question 477. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the face, clear fluid drips from the nose, and a described coronal CT image shows a break in the thin roof of the nasal cavity with air inside the skull. Which bone is broken? The answer is the cribriform plate of the ethmoid bone. The fibers of smell pass through it, so a break there may cost the sense of smell.

Question 478. On a described coronal image of the chest, the trachea divides into two main bronchi below the arch of the aorta. Which main bronchus is wider and more nearly in line with the trachea, and what lies immediately above the left one? The answer is the right main bronchus, with the arch of the aorta above the left. A swallowed object therefore enters the right bronchus more often.

Question 479. A described coronal image of the knee shows the medial meniscus. What is its shape, and to what is its outer edge attached: circular and free, C-shaped and attached to the medial collateral ligament, O-shaped and attached to the tibia, or flat and attached to the patellar tendon? The answer is C-shaped and attached to the medial collateral ligament, so a tear of that ligament often tears the meniscus too.

Question 480. A described coronal image of the upper abdomen shows the stomach in the middle, with the liver on one side and the spleen on the other. Which space lies immediately behind the stomach and in front of the pancreas: the greater sac, the lesser sac, the pleural cavity, or the retroperitoneal space? The answer is the lesser sac, also called the omental bursa.

Question 481. What name is given to the plane that cuts across the body at right angles to its long axis and produces a cross-sectional image? The answer is the axial plane, also called the transverse plane. A series of such slices, one below another, builds the pictures of a CT or MRI examination.

Question 482. On a described axial CT image of the head at the level of the thalamus, which space lies in the midline between the two lateral ventricles: the fourth ventricle, the third ventricle, the subarachnoid space, or the cerebral aqueduct? The answer is the third ventricle, a narrow slit of fluid between the two halves of the thalamus. The aqueduct lies below it, in the midbrain.

Question 483. A described axial CT image of the chest shows a small rounded structure lying against the right side of a thoracic vertebral body. Which vein is it, and where does it end? The answer is the azygos vein, which arches forward over the right main bronchus to join the superior vena cava.

Question 484. A described axial magnetic resonance image of the thigh shows three groups of muscle separated by dark bands of fascia. Which group lies at the back, and which nerve runs among its muscles? The answer is the posterior compartment, with the sciatic nerve between its muscles. The anterior compartment holds the quadriceps and the medial one the adductors.

Question 485. On a described axial CT image of the neck, the lobe of the thyroid gland lies beside the trachea. Which structures lie within the sheath that runs immediately lateral to the gland? The answer is the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, and the vagus nerve. The artery lies medial to the vein.

Question 486. On a described axial image at the level of the first lumbar vertebra, two vessels lie in front of the vertebral body. Which two are they: the aorta on the right and the vena cava on the left, the aorta on the left and the vena cava on the right, the portal and splenic veins, or the common iliac arteries? The answer is the aorta on the left and the inferior vena cava on the right. The vena cava lies the more anterior of the two.

Question 487. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the temple, a person is briefly stunned and then deteriorates, and a described axial CT image shows a lens-shaped collection between the skull and the brain, with the midline pushed aside. Which vessel has torn, and where does the blood lie? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, with blood in the extradural space. A lens shape that stops at the sutures marks it.

Question 488. On a described axial image of the upper abdomen, the neck of the pancreas lies immediately in front of which vessel: the aorta, the inferior vena cava, the superior mesenteric vein, or the left renal artery? The answer is the superior mesenteric vein, where it joins the splenic vein to form the portal vein. The superior mesenteric artery arises behind the neck of the gland.

Question 489. On a described axial image of the male pelvis, a fold of peritoneum dips down between the bladder in front and the rectum behind. What is that pouch called? The answer is the rectovesical pouch. In the female the same fold dips between the bladder and the uterus and is called the rectouterine pouch.

Question 490. Which bony point in the midline of the back of the head is easily felt, and which muscle attaches to it? The answer is the external occipital protuberance, to which the trapezius attaches. It lies at the upper end of the furrow between the two sides of the back of the neck.

Question 491. A described image of the hip shows the greater trochanter of the femur. At the level of which structure of the pelvis does its tip lie: the anterior superior iliac spine, the pubic tubercle, the sacral promontory, or the fifth lumbar vertebra? The answer is the pubic tubercle. The trochanter lies about a hand's breadth below the highest point of the iliac crest.

Question 492. Which of these landmarks fixes the level of the second thoracic vertebra on the front of the chest: the jugular notch of the sternum, the sternal angle, the xiphisternal joint, or the fifth costal cartilage? The answer is the jugular notch of the sternum. The sternal angle lies two vertebrae lower, at the fourth thoracic vertebra.

Question 493. True or false: the words cephalad and caudad describe a direction, cephalad toward the head and caudad toward the feet. The answer is true. They are used to speak of the direction in which one image follows another, so a series taken from the feet upward runs cephalad.

Question 494. True or false: on an axial image displayed in the standard radiological convention, the left side of the picture is the patient's left side. The answer is false. An axial image is viewed from below, as though the observer stood at the patient's feet, so the patient's left appears on the right of the picture.

Question 495. Which of these pairs belongs together: the sagittal plane with the front and back parts, the coronal plane with the right and left parts, the axial plane with the upper and lower parts, or the oblique plane with two equal halves? The answer is the axial plane with the upper and lower parts. A sagittal plane divides the body into right and left and a coronal plane into front and back.

Question 496. Which of these pairs belongs together: CT with detailed bone structure, magnetic resonance imaging with detailed bone structure, ultrasound with the lungs beyond the chest wall, or a plain X-ray film with the spinal cord? The answer is CT with detailed bone structure. Magnetic resonance imaging shows soft tissue better, and ultrasound cannot see through bone or air.

Question 497. Which of these pairs belongs together: the coronal plane with a view from above, the axial plane with a view from below, the sagittal plane with a view from the front, or the oblique plane with a view from one side? The answer is the axial plane with a view from below, the standard convention for axial images. A coronal image is viewed from the front and a sagittal image from one side.

Question 498. A constructed illustration: a person is tender over the middle of the front of the wrist, and a described image shows the bone lying at the center of the carpal arch, between the scaphoid and the hamate. Which bone is it? The answer is the capitate, the largest bone of the wrist. Its head carries the third metacarpal bone.

Question 499. Name the two substances that lie between air and bone on the scale of density used in radiology, spoken in order from black to white. The answer is fat and water. Fat is only slightly denser than air, and water with soft tissue lies between fat and bone.

Question 500. A described image is cut in a plane that passes through the body at an angle, so that the right side of the skull and the left side of the jaw appear in the same picture. Which kind of plane produced it? The answer is an oblique plane. It cuts across all three reference planes at once, so it cannot be called sagittal, coronal or axial.

To summarize, the surface of the body carries landmarks that fix the level of everything beneath it: the sternal angle marks the second costal cartilage at the fourth thoracic vertebra, the transpyloric plane lies at the first lumbar vertebra, and the highest points of the iliac crests mark the fourth lumbar vertebra. On a radiograph the tissues shade from black to white in the order air, fat, water, bone and metal, and the silhouette sign tells which lobe of a lung lies against a lost border, while CT measures density in numbers and shows bone better than any other method. Magnetic resonance imaging uses no ionizing radiation, shows water brightly on a T2-weighted image and fat brightly on a T1-weighted one, and ultrasound returns no echoes from fluid and casts a shadow behind a stone. A sagittal image divides the body into right and left parts, a coronal image into front and back parts and an axial image into upper and lower parts, and the standard convention shows the patient's left on the right of an axial picture.
