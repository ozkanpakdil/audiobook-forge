# Chapter 31 — Abdomen

The muscles of the abdominal wall and the muscles that move the shoulder are the subject of this chapter, and both groups repay close attention because they are tested again in the clinic. The first half follows the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis, the two deep flat muscles of the abdomen, through their layers, their nerve supply and their part in the groin. The second half turns to the shoulder muscles, above all the rotator cuff and the deltoid, and to the movements they produce. The chapter holds one hundred questions, and each one states its answer in the same paragraph as the question, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3001. Of the three flat muscles of the anterior abdominal wall, which one forms the middle layer? The answer is the internal oblique muscle. The external oblique lies superficial to it and the transversus abdominis lies deep to it. Its fibers run upward and medially, almost at right angles to those of the external oblique.

Question 3002. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the external oblique muscle: the internal oblique, the rectus abdominis, the transversus abdominis, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the internal oblique. It forms the middle of the three flat muscles of the abdominal wall. The transversus abdominis lies one layer deeper still, and the latissimus dorsi belongs to the back.

Question 3003. A described dissection shows one flat layer whose fibers run upward and medially and, immediately deep to it, a second layer whose fibers run across the abdomen. Which two muscles are shown? The answer is the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis. The upward and medial direction belongs to the internal oblique, and the horizontal direction gives the transversus its name.

Question 3004. Between which two muscles of the abdominal wall do the lower thoracic nerves and the inferior epigastric vessels run? The answer is the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis. That interval is the neurovascular plane of the abdominal wall. The segmental nerves pierce the muscles in turn on their way forward to the rectus sheath.

Question 3005. True or false: the transversus abdominis lies deep to the internal oblique muscle. The answer is true. The transversus abdominis is the innermost of the three flat muscles, and the transversalis fascia lies on its deep surface. Its fibers run across the abdomen rather than upward or downward.

Question 3006. Which muscle forms the innermost of the three flat layers of the abdominal wall? The answer is the transversus abdominis. It arises from the inner surfaces of the lower six costal cartilages, the thoracolumbar fascia, the iliac crest and the lateral part of the inguinal ligament. Its aponeurosis helps to form the rectus sheath.

Question 3007. In which direction do the fibers of the transversus abdominis run: downward and medially, upward and medially, horizontally across the abdomen, or vertically from the ribs to the pubis? The answer is horizontally across the abdomen. The name of the muscle describes that direction. The external oblique and the internal oblique lie superficial to it and cross it obliquely.

Question 3008. Which nerves supply the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis muscles? The answer is the lower thoracic spinal nerves and the first lumbar nerve. The nerves of the seventh to the twelfth thoracic segments and of the first lumbar segment reach the wall in the plane between the two muscles. The same nerves supply the external oblique and the rectus abdominis.

Question 3009. A constructed illustration: inflammation begins in the appendix, and the first pain is felt around the umbilicus. Which spinal segment carries that pain, and why is it felt in the middle of the abdomen? The answer is the tenth thoracic segment. The appendix carries visceral pain fibers that enter the spinal cord at that level, and the brain refers the pain to the skin around the navel.

Question 3010. Which of these pairs belongs together: the external oblique with fibers running downward and medially, the internal oblique with fibers running horizontally, the transversus abdominis with fibers running downward and forward, or the rectus abdominis with fibers running across the body? The answer is the external oblique with fibers running downward and medially. Those fibers point toward the groin, like the fingers of a hand placed in a trouser pocket.

Question 3011. Name the two muscles whose aponeuroses join to form the conjoint tendon above the pubis. The answer is the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis. The common tendon inserts into the pubic crest and the pecten pubis. It lies behind the superficial inguinal ring and strengthens the medial part of the posterior wall of the inguinal canal.

Question 3012. Above the arcuate line, which aponeuroses form the anterior wall of the rectus sheath and which form the posterior wall? The answer is the external oblique and one lamina of the internal oblique in front, with the other lamina of the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis behind. The rectus abdominis therefore lies between two strong tendinous layers.

Question 3013. Below the arcuate line, what forms the posterior wall of the rectus sheath: the aponeurosis of the external oblique, the aponeuroses of the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis, only the transversalis fascia, or the parietal peritoneum? The answer is only the transversalis fascia. All three flat aponeuroses pass in front of the rectus abdominis below that line.

Question 3014. A described cross section through the anterior abdominal wall shows skin, two fatty layers, three flat muscles and a thin membrane on the inner surface. What is that membrane, and what lies immediately inside it? The answer is the transversalis fascia, with the parietal peritoneum inside it. A little extraperitoneal fat separates the two.

Question 3015. A described movement turns the trunk to the left about the long axis of the body. In which plane does the movement occur, and about which axis? The answer is the transverse plane and a longitudinal axis. The vertebral column acts as the pivot, and the abdominal muscles of one side produce the turn.

Question 3016. What is the name of the fibrous band in the midline of the anterior abdominal wall, formed where the aponeuroses of the flat muscles interlace? The answer is the linea alba. It runs from the xiphoid process down to the pubic symphysis. It is narrow below the umbilicus and wider above it.

Question 3017. Which muscle of the spermatic cord is derived from the lower fibers of the internal oblique: the cremaster, the dartos, the gubernaculum, or the pyramidalis? The answer is the cremaster. Its fibers accompany the spermatic cord through the inguinal canal and lift the testis in the cold. The dartos is smooth muscle in the skin of the scrotum.

Question 3018. A constructed illustration: a small hernia appears in the abdominal wall at the outer edge of the rectus sheath, below the umbilicus. Which named line has it crossed, and which muscles border that line? The answer is the linea semilunaris, where the aponeuroses of the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis meet the rectus sheath. A hernia that emerges there is called a Spigelian hernia.

Question 3019. Between which two muscles does the ilioinguinal nerve run before it enters the inguinal canal? The answer is the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis. It is a branch of the first lumbar nerve. It crosses the inguinal canal in front of the spermatic cord or the round ligament.

Question 3020. True or false: a contraction of the internal oblique muscle on the right side rotates the trunk to the left. The answer is false. The internal oblique rotates the trunk to the same side as the muscle, so the right internal oblique turns the trunk to the right. The external oblique of the opposite side assists in that movement.

Question 3021. In which direction do the fibers of the external oblique muscle run? The answer is downward and medially, toward the midline and the groin. The fibers point like the fingers of a hand placed in a trouser pocket. They run at right angles to those of the internal oblique beneath them.

Question 3022. Which of these is the chief action of the transversus abdominis: flexion of the trunk, rotation of the trunk, compression of the abdominal contents, or extension of the trunk? The answer is compression of the abdominal contents. It also steadies the trunk and takes part in forced expiration and in coughing. It is not a rotator of the trunk.

Question 3023. A described photograph of a lean athlete shows a curved line at the lateral edge of the rectus sheath, with flat muscles lying beyond it. What is that line called, and which muscles lie beyond it? The answer is the linea semilunaris, with the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis beyond it. It runs from the ninth costal cartilage to the pubic tubercle.

Question 3024. Name the three flat muscles of the abdominal wall in order from superficial to deep. The answer is the external oblique, the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis. Their aponeuroses cross the midline and interlace to form the linea alba. Two of them, the internal oblique and the transversus, form the conjoint tendon.

Question 3025. Which structure lies immediately behind the superficial inguinal ring and is formed by the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis? The answer is the conjoint tendon. It descends to the pubic crest and the pecten pubis. Behind it lies the medial part of the posterior wall of the inguinal canal, the part that gives way in a direct inguinal hernia.

Question 3026. A constructed illustration: a hernia pushes through the posterior wall of the inguinal canal, medial to the inferior epigastric artery. Which wall has given way, and which structure normally reinforces it? The answer is the posterior wall, reinforced by the conjoint tendon. A hernia that passes medial to those vessels is called direct.

Question 3027. Which small triangular muscle lies in front of the lower part of the rectus abdominis and is absent in some people? The answer is the pyramidalis. It arises from the pubis and ends in the linea alba, and it tenses that midline band. Its absence is a normal variation.

Question 3028. Which nerve is found in the plane between the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis: the ilioinguinal nerve, the femoral nerve, the lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh, or the genitofemoral nerve? The answer is the ilioinguinal nerve. The femoral nerve and the lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh lie in the thigh, and the genitofemoral nerve lies on the surface of the psoas muscle.

Question 3029. Which of these pairs belongs together: the internal oblique with the pubic crest through the conjoint tendon, the transversus abdominis with the iliac crest, the external oblique with the lower three ribs, or the rectus abdominis with the thoracolumbar fascia? The answer is the internal oblique with the pubic crest through the conjoint tendon. The other three pair a muscle with one of its origins rather than with its insertion.

Question 3030. A described computed tomography image taken across the abdomen at the level of the third lumbar vertebra shows the flat muscles as thin bands at each side and the rectus muscles as two oval masses in front. Which muscle forms the deepest band at each side? The answer is the transversus abdominis. The internal oblique lies outside it and the external oblique outside that.

Question 3031. A described cross section of the lower abdominal wall shows the rectus abdominis backed only by a thin membrane, with no tendinous layer behind the muscle. At what level relative to the arcuate line was the section taken, and what is that membrane? The answer is below the arcuate line, with only the transversalis fascia behind the muscle. Above that line the section would have shown a tendinous layer behind the rectus.

Question 3032. The posterior fibers of the internal oblique muscle arise from the thoracolumbar fascia and end where? The answer is on the lower borders of the tenth, eleventh and twelfth ribs. Those fibers lie deep to the muscles of the back. They therefore take part in both the abdominal wall and the floor of the lower part of the thorax.

Question 3033. Which of these muscles flexes the trunk and rotates it to the same side: the internal oblique, the external oblique, the transversus abdominis, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the internal oblique. The external oblique of one side rotates the trunk to the opposite side, and the transversus abdominis does not rotate it at all.

Question 3034. What is the name of the line, about halfway between the umbilicus and the pubic symphysis, below which the posterior wall of the rectus sheath is formed only by the transversalis fascia? The answer is the arcuate line. Above it the posterior wall is tendinous and strong, and below it only a thin membrane separates the rectus abdominis from the peritoneum.

Question 3035. In a dissection of the lower abdominal wall, which finding shows that the level is below the arcuate line: all three aponeuroses pass in front of the rectus, the posterior wall is tendinous, the internal oblique lies superficial to the external oblique, or the rectus muscle is absent? The answer is that all three aponeuroses pass in front of the rectus. It lies about halfway between the umbilicus and the pubis.

Question 3036. True or false: the anterior wall of the inguinal canal is formed throughout by the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle. The answer is true. The internal oblique reinforces the lateral part of that wall. The posterior wall is formed by the transversalis fascia and, medially, by the conjoint tendon.

Question 3037. A described ultrasound image taken across the abdominal wall shows three thin muscular bands separated by bright lines, with the deepest band running across the screen from side to side. Which muscle is that deepest band, and what is the bright line deep to it? The answer is the transversus abdominis, with the transversalis fascia deep to it. Bright lines between the bands are the fascial sheets that separate them.

Question 3038. Which of these statements about the rectus abdominis is correct: it lies within the rectus sheath, it lies deep to the transversalis fascia, it lies between the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis, or it lies superficial to the external oblique? The answer is that it lies within the rectus sheath. The sheath is formed by the aponeuroses of the three flat muscles and by the transversalis fascia.

Question 3039. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the lower right abdomen, a person reports burning pain and reduced sensation in the skin above the pubis and the upper part of the scrotum. Which nerve has been caught in the scar? The answer is the ilioinguinal nerve. It runs in the neurovascular plane and then through the inguinal canal, where it lies in front of the spermatic cord.

Question 3040. From which bony landmarks does the internal oblique muscle arise? The answer is the thoracolumbar fascia, the anterior two thirds of the iliac crest and the lateral half of the inguinal ligament. Its lowest fibers arch over the spermatic cord and give rise to the cremaster muscle. Its upper fibers end on the lower ribs.

Question 3041. Which strong fascia covers the deep muscles of the back and gives origin to the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis? The answer is the thoracolumbar fascia. It lies between the muscles of the back and those of the abdominal wall. It also gives attachment to the latissimus dorsi behind.

Question 3042. Which pairing of muscles rotates the trunk to the right, spoken as one answer: the right external oblique with the left internal oblique, the right external oblique with the right internal oblique, the right internal oblique with the right transversus abdominis, or the left external oblique with the left internal oblique? The answer is the right external oblique with the left internal oblique. Both of those muscles turn the trunk to the right.

Question 3043. Which muscles act in forced expiration, as in a cough: the flat muscles of the abdominal wall, the diaphragm alone, the erector spinae muscles, or the psoas major? The answer is the flat muscles of the abdominal wall. Their contraction raises the pressure inside the abdomen and pushes the diaphragm upward while the ribs are drawn down.

Question 3044. Which of these muscles lies in the posterior abdominal wall rather than in the anterior wall: the internal oblique, the transversus abdominis, the quadratus lumborum, or the rectus abdominis? The answer is the quadratus lumborum. It runs from the iliac crest to the twelfth rib and to the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae, and it bends the trunk to its own side.

Question 3045. A described image of the groin seen from behind shows a triangular area bounded below by the inguinal ligament, laterally by the inferior epigastric vessels and medially by the rectus sheath, with peritoneum bulging through it. Which region is shown, and which hernia passes through it? The answer is Hesselbach's triangle, through which a direct inguinal hernia passes. That triangle is a weak point of the posterior wall of the canal.

Question 3046. Which of these statements about the rectus abdominis is correct: it is crossed by three or four tendinous bands, it lies outside the rectus sheath, it has no attachment to the pubis, or it is supplied by the femoral nerve? The answer is that it is crossed by three or four tendinous bands. Those bands lie at the xiphoid process, at the umbilicus and between the two.

Question 3047. A constructed illustration: a person who has just lifted a heavy weight feels a sudden tearing pain low in one rectus abdominis, and a tender swelling appears within the muscle. Why does that swelling not cross the midline? The answer is that the rectus abdominis is enclosed in its own sheath on each side. The linea alba separates the two muscles and their sheaths down the middle of the wall.

Question 3048. Which of these pairs belongs together: the transversus abdominis with the lower thoracic and first lumbar nerves, the external oblique with the femoral nerve, the rectus abdominis with the obturator nerve, or the internal oblique with the ilioinguinal nerve alone? The answer is the transversus abdominis with the lower thoracic and first lumbar nerves. All three flat muscles share that same segmental supply.

Question 3049. Which of these structures passes through the deep inguinal ring: the ilioinguinal nerve, the spermatic cord, the inferior epigastric artery, or the femoral artery? The answer is the spermatic cord. The deep ring is an opening in the transversalis fascia. The inferior epigastric artery passes medial to the ring and marks its medial border.

Question 3050. A described dissection shows the posterior wall of the inguinal canal reinforced medially by a tendinous band. Which of these names that band and its wall: the conjoint tendon in the posterior wall, the conjoint tendon in the anterior wall, the inguinal ligament behind, or the transversalis fascia in front? The answer is the conjoint tendon in the posterior wall. It is formed by the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis.

Question 3051. Which muscle is the chief abductor of the arm at the shoulder joint? The answer is the deltoid. Its middle fibers lift the arm sideways, taking over from the supraspinatus after the first fifteen degrees. Its anterior fibers flex the arm and its posterior fibers extend it.

Question 3052. Which of these muscles belongs to the rotator cuff: the teres major, the teres minor, the deltoid, or the coracobrachialis? The answer is the teres minor. The cuff is made up of the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor and the subscapularis. The teres minor lies below the spine of the scapula, and its tendon joins the capsule of the joint.

Question 3053. Which muscle begins abduction of the arm during the first fifteen degrees of movement at the shoulder joint? The answer is the supraspinatus. It lies in the fossa above the spine of the scapula, and its tendon passes beneath the acromion to reach the greater tubercle. The deltoid then carries the movement on.

Question 3054. Which arch does the tendon of the supraspinatus pass beneath on its way to the greater tubercle of the humerus? The answer is the coracoacromial arch. The subacromial bursa lies between the tendon and the arch. That arch is formed by the acromion and the coracoid process with the ligament between them.

Question 3055. A constructed illustration: after a forward dislocation of the shoulder, the arm cannot be lifted sideways and the skin over the lateral part of the shoulder is numb. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the axillary nerve. It passes through the quadrangular space close to the surgical neck and supplies the deltoid and the teres minor.

Question 3056. True or false: the subscapularis muscle inserts on the greater tubercle of the humerus. The answer is false. It inserts on the lesser tubercle. The three muscles of the cuff that lie behind the joint, the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus and the teres minor, insert on the greater tubercle.

Question 3057. Which nerve supplies the infraspinatus muscle: the axillary nerve, the suprascapular nerve, the lower subscapular nerve, or the thoracodorsal nerve? The answer is the suprascapular nerve. The same nerve supplies the supraspinatus, and it passes through the suprascapular notch. The axillary nerve supplies the teres minor instead.

Question 3058. Which nerve supplies the deltoid muscle? The answer is the axillary nerve. It winds through the quadrangular space together with the posterior circumflex humeral artery. A fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus may injure it, and the skin over the muscle then loses its sensation.

Question 3059. A described radiograph of the shoulder shows a shelf of bone above the head of the humerus, with the head lying below and medial to it. Which structure is that shelf, and which tendon lies just beneath it? The answer is the acromion, with the tendon of the supraspinatus beneath it. The subacromial bursa lies between the two.

Question 3060. Which of these pairs belongs together: the subscapularis with medial rotation, the infraspinatus with medial rotation, the teres minor with abduction, or the supraspinatus with adduction? The answer is the subscapularis with medial rotation. The infraspinatus and the teres minor rotate the arm laterally, and the supraspinatus begins abduction.

Question 3061. Which two structures pass through the quadrangular space, bounded above by the teres minor, below by the teres major, medially by the long head of the triceps and laterally by the surgical neck of the humerus? The answer is the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery. Both are at risk in a fracture of the surgical neck.

Question 3062. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the axilla, the medial border of the scapula stands away from the chest wall when the arm is pushed forward against resistance. Which nerve and which muscle are involved? The answer is the long thoracic nerve and the serratus anterior. That muscle normally holds the scapula against the ribs.

Question 3063. Which muscles rotate the arm medially at the shoulder joint: the infraspinatus and the teres minor, the subscapularis and the teres major, the deltoid and the supraspinatus, or the trapezius and the rhomboid muscles? The answer is the subscapularis and the teres major. The infraspinatus and the teres minor rotate the arm laterally instead.

Question 3064. Which nerves supply the subscapularis muscle? The answer is the upper and the lower subscapular nerves. Both arise from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus. The lower subscapular nerve also supplies the teres major, and the muscle itself lies on the front of the scapula.

Question 3065. A described movement lifts the arm sideways away from the trunk. In which plane does the movement occur, and about which axis does the shoulder joint turn? The answer is the coronal plane and an anteroposterior axis. The humerus moves in that plane while the joint rotates about an axis running from front to back.

Question 3066. A described magnetic resonance image of the shoulder shows a gap in the tendon above the head of the humerus, with fluid collecting in the space between the tendon and the bone. Which tendon is torn, and which bursa lies in that space? The answer is the tendon of the supraspinatus, with the subacromial bursa. Fluid collects in that bursa because it lies between the tendon and the bone.

Question 3067. Which muscles form the force couple that keeps the head of the humerus centered while the arm is abducted: the deltoid with the trapezius, the deltoid with the rotator cuff, the pectoralis major with the latissimus dorsi, or the biceps with the triceps? The answer is the deltoid with the rotator cuff. The cuff pulls the head downward and medially against the upward pull of the deltoid.

Question 3068. From which three bony points does the deltoid muscle arise? The answer is the lateral third of the clavicle, the acromion and the spine of the scapula. The muscle converges on the deltoid tuberosity of the humerus. Its anterior part takes origin from the clavicle and its posterior part from the spine of the scapula.

Question 3069. A constructed illustration: a person feels a sharp pain in the shoulder when the arm is raised above the head, worst between seventy and one hundred and twenty degrees of abduction. Which structures are being pinched, and under which arch? The answer is the supraspinatus tendon and the subacromial bursa beneath the coracoacromial arch. The tendon is pinched against the acromion as the arm rises under that arch.

Question 3070. Name two muscles that rotate the arm laterally at the shoulder joint, spoken as a list. The answer is the infraspinatus and the teres minor. Both run from the back of the scapula to the greater tubercle of the humerus. The infraspinatus is supplied by the suprascapular nerve and the teres minor by the axillary nerve.

Question 3071. Which tendon runs in the groove between the greater and the lesser tubercles of the humerus, and where does it go? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps brachii. It leaves the groove and passes through the shoulder joint to attach to the rim of the glenoid socket, where it helps to steady the joint.

Question 3072. When the arm is lifted to the vertical, how much of the movement occurs at the shoulder joint and how much at the scapula: two thirds and one third, one third and two thirds, half and half, or all of it? The answer is two thirds at the shoulder joint and one third at the scapula. The scapula turns upward on the chest wall as the arm rises.

Question 3073. A described dissection of the axilla shows a nerve arising from the posterior cord and winding backward around the surgical neck of the humerus. Which nerve is shown, and what does it supply? The answer is the axillary nerve, which supplies the deltoid and the teres minor. It also carries sensation from the skin over the lower part of the deltoid.

Question 3074. True or false: the deltoid is the only muscle that can begin abduction of the arm from the side of the body. The answer is false. The supraspinatus begins the movement, and the deltoid takes over after the first fifteen degrees. When the supraspinatus is torn, the arm can often still be abducted once the movement has been started.

Question 3075. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supraspinatus with the suprascapular nerve, the teres minor with the suprascapular nerve, the subscapularis with the axillary nerve, or the deltoid with the radial nerve? The answer is the supraspinatus with the suprascapular nerve. The teres minor belongs to the axillary nerve, the subscapularis to the subscapular nerves and the deltoid to the axillary nerve.

Question 3076. Which nerve supplies the trapezius muscle? The answer is the spinal accessory nerve, the eleventh cranial nerve. Small branches from the third and fourth cervical nerves carry sensation from the muscle but do not move it. The trapezius elevates, retracts and rotates the scapula.

Question 3077. Which action does the serratus anterior perform on the scapula: it retracts the scapula toward the spine, it protracts the scapula around the chest wall and rotates it upward, it elevates the scapula toward the ear, or it depresses the scapula toward the hip? The answer is that it protracts the scapula and rotates it upward. Both actions are needed before the arm can be raised above the head.

Question 3078. A constructed illustration: over several months a shoulder becomes painful and then stiff, and movement is lost in every direction, while a radiograph shows no broken bone. Which structure is the site of the problem? The answer is the fibrous capsule of the shoulder joint, which becomes thickened and contracted. The joint surfaces themselves remain intact.

Question 3079. Which structure passes through the triangular space, bounded above by the teres minor, below by the teres major and laterally by the long head of the triceps? The answer is the circumflex scapular artery. It runs between those muscles to reach the back of the scapula. The axillary nerve passes through the quadrangular space instead.

Question 3080. In a complete tear of the tendon of the supraspinatus, which movement is lost first: abduction of the arm from the side, lateral rotation, medial rotation, or extension? The answer is abduction of the arm from the side. The person can still shrug the shoulder and swing the arm forward. The deltoid cannot start that movement on its own.

Question 3081. A described view of the inside of the shoulder joint shows a ring of fibrous tissue around the rim of the shallow socket, with a tendon arising just above it. What is that ring, and what does it do? The answer is the glenoid labrum, which deepens the socket and helps to hold the head in place. It is a fibrocartilaginous rim that may be torn when the joint dislocates.

Question 3082. Which nerve supplies the rhomboid major and the rhomboid minor? The answer is the dorsal scapular nerve, which carries fibers of the fifth cervical root. The same nerve also supplies the levator scapulae. Both rhomboids draw the scapula toward the spine and rotate it downward.

Question 3083. Which nerve supplies the latissimus dorsi: the thoracodorsal nerve, the long thoracic nerve, the dorsal scapular nerve, or the axillary nerve? The answer is the thoracodorsal nerve. It arises from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus. The latissimus dorsi extends, adducts and medially rotates the arm.

Question 3084. Which three muscles attach to the coracoid process of the scapula? The answer is the coracobrachialis, the short head of the biceps brachii and the pectoralis minor. The first two arise from the tip of the process. The pectoralis minor inserts on its medial border.

Question 3085. On which part of the humerus does the deltoid muscle insert? The answer is the deltoid tuberosity, a rough area on the middle of the lateral surface of the shaft. The muscle pulls on that point to raise the arm. The radial nerve runs in the groove behind the same part of the bone.

Question 3086. A constructed illustration: a person has weakness of both the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus with deep pain in the shoulder but no loss of sensation in the skin. Where is the nerve most likely compressed: at the suprascapular notch, in the quadrangular space, behind the medial epicondyle, or in the carpal tunnel? The answer is at the suprascapular notch. That nerve carries no skin fibers, so sensation is spared.

Question 3087. Which movements does the pectoralis major produce at the shoulder joint: adduction, medial rotation and flexion, abduction and lateral rotation, extension and lateral rotation, or abduction alone? The answer is adduction, medial rotation and flexion. Its clavicular head helps to flex the arm, and its sternocostal head can extend the arm from the flexed position.

Question 3088. Which small muscle lies between the clavicle and the first rib, and what does it do? The answer is the subclavius, which steadies the clavicle and draws it downward. It is supplied by a small nerve from the upper trunk of the brachial plexus. It lies in front of the vessels of the upper limb as they leave the neck.

Question 3089. A described photograph of the back shows a broad triangular muscle whose upper fibers run downward, whose middle fibers run across and whose lower fibers run upward, all converging on the shoulder. Which muscle is shown, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the trapezius, supplied by the spinal accessory nerve. Its upper fibers elevate the scapula, its middle fibers retract it and its lower fibers rotate it upward.

Question 3090. Which statement about the deltoid muscle is correct: its middle fibers abduct the arm, its anterior fibers extend the arm, its posterior fibers flex the arm, or all of its fibers adduct the arm? The answer is that its middle fibers abduct the arm. Its anterior fibers flex and medially rotate the arm, and its posterior fibers extend and laterally rotate it.

Question 3091. A described magnetic resonance image of the shoulder shows one tendon running to the greater tubercle just behind the joint, with a smaller muscle lying beneath it. Which two muscles are shown? The answer is the infraspinatus and the teres minor. Their tendons blend with the capsule of the joint and rotate the arm laterally.

Question 3092. Which structure lies immediately above the head of the humerus and separates the deltoid from the tendons of the rotator cuff? The answer is the subacromial bursa. It lets the cuff tendons glide beneath the acromion and the coracoacromial arch. It is the structure that becomes inflamed in painful abduction of the arm.

Question 3093. Which statement about the teres minor and the infraspinatus is correct: the teres minor is supplied by the axillary nerve, the infraspinatus is supplied by the axillary nerve, both are supplied by the suprascapular nerve, or neither is supplied by the suprascapular nerve? The answer is that the teres minor is supplied by the axillary nerve. The infraspinatus, which lies beside it, is supplied by the suprascapular nerve.

Question 3094. Which rotator cuff muscle lies on the anterior surface of the scapula and therefore cannot be felt from behind: the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor, or the subscapularis? The answer is the subscapularis. It fills the fossa on the front of the scapula and inserts on the lesser tubercle of the humerus.

Question 3095. A described ultrasound image of the shoulder shows a tendon crossing above the head of the humerus, with a thin dark layer between the tendon and the bone and a bright line on the deep surface of the tendon. What are those two layers? The answer is the subacromial bursa above and the surface of the head of the humerus below. The bursa lets the tendon glide as the arm moves.

Question 3096. Which nerve pierces the coracobrachialis muscle on its way down the arm? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve. It supplies the coracobrachialis, the biceps brachii and the brachialis. It then continues into the forearm as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm. It is the nerve of the front of the arm.

Question 3097. Which muscle both flexes and adducts the arm at the shoulder joint: the coracobrachialis, the deltoid, the teres minor, or the infraspinatus? The answer is the coracobrachialis. It runs from the coracoid process to the middle of the medial surface of the humerus. The other three muscles either abduct the arm or rotate it.

Question 3098. A constructed illustration: one person cannot raise the arm above the head and the shoulder droops, while another has a scapula whose medial border stands out when the arm is pushed forward. Which nerve is injured in the second person? The answer is the long thoracic nerve, which supplies the serratus anterior. The first person has a lesion of the spinal accessory nerve.

Question 3099. A described image across the shoulder shows a large muscle wrapping over the top of the joint, a group of muscles behind it and a tendon in a groove in front. Which muscle wraps over the top, and which tendon lies in the groove? The answer is the deltoid, with the tendon of the long head of the biceps in the groove. The muscles behind the joint are the rotator cuff.

Question 3100. A complete tear of the rotator cuff leaves the humeral head riding up under the acromion. Which of these explains it: the deltoid pulls up while the torn cuff no longer pulls down, the cuff pulls up while the deltoid pulls down, the biceps pulls up, or the triceps pulls up? The answer is the deltoid pulls up while the torn cuff no longer pulls down. Nothing then opposes the deltoid.

To summarize, the internal oblique is the middle of the three flat muscles of the abdominal wall, its fibers run upward and medially, and it rotates the trunk to the same side. The transversus abdominis lies deepest of the three, its fibers run across the abdomen, and its chief work is to compress the abdominal contents and to steady the trunk. Both are supplied by the lower thoracic spinal nerves and the first lumbar nerve, both take part in the conjoint tendon, and both lie in the neurovascular plane of the wall. Above the arcuate line the rectus sheath has a strong posterior wall, and below it only the transversalis fascia lies behind the muscle. At the shoulder, the supraspinatus begins abduction, the deltoid carries it on, and the four muscles of the rotator cuff hold the head of the humerus in the shallow glenoid socket. The infraspinatus and the teres minor rotate the arm laterally, the subscapularis rotates it medially, and the axillary nerve, the suprascapular nerve and the long thoracic nerve are the ones most often injured, so what this chapter offers is educational material that does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.
