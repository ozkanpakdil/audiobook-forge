# Chapter 54 — Spinal Cord

This chapter follows the spinal cord from the cervical enlargement at the top to the dorsal horns that receive the sensory roots. It covers the two enlargements that serve the limbs, the gray matter with its horns, laminae and cell columns, the white matter with its funiculi and long tracts, and the dorsal horn where the sensory fibers arrive. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 5301. Which part of the spinal cord is named the cervical enlargement, and which segments form it? The answer is the widened upper part of the cord, formed by the fifth cervical to the first thoracic segments. The extra tissue there is gray matter for the muscles of the upper limb. Those roots gather into the brachial plexus.

Question 5302. Which of these parts of the cord gives rise to the nerves of the upper limb: the cervical enlargement, the lumbar enlargement, the conus medullaris, or the filum terminale? The answer is the cervical enlargement. Its fifth cervical to first thoracic segments send their roots into the brachial plexus. The lumbar enlargement supplies the lower limb instead.

Question 5303. A described transverse section is oval, wider from side to side than from front to back, with a deep fissure in front and a large ventral horn on each side. At which level was it taken? The answer is the cervical enlargement. That outline and those bulky ventral horns belong to the lower cervical segments. A thoracic section is rounder, with slender horns.

Question 5304. The cervical enlargement lies opposite which part of the vertebral column, and does the cord fill that space? The answer is opposite the lower cervical and first thoracic vertebrae, with the cord nearly filling the canal. Little cerebrospinal fluid surrounds it there. Farther down the canal widens around the cauda equina.

Question 5305. A constructed illustration: after a fall from a motorcycle, one arm hangs limp and numb while the other limbs move normally. The injury is described as a stretch of the roots that enter the cervical enlargement. Which roots are illustrated? The answer is the fifth cervical to the first thoracic roots, which form the brachial plexus. That limb loses its power and its feeling.

Question 5306. How many pairs of cervical spinal nerves leave the cervical part of the spinal cord? The answer is 8 pairs. There are 7 cervical vertebrae but 8 cervical nerves, because the first nerve leaves above the first vertebra. The eighth nerve leaves below the seventh vertebra.

Question 5307. Why is the cervical enlargement wider than the thoracic cord: because it holds more white matter, because its gray matter is enlarged for the muscles of the upper limb, because it carries the meninges, or because the canal is narrower there? The answer is that its gray matter is enlarged for the muscles of the upper limb. More motor neurons are needed there.

Question 5308. What is the name of the deep groove that runs down the front of the spinal cord and carries the anterior spinal artery? The answer is the anterior median fissure. It divides the anterior surface into two halves and reaches almost to the gray matter. The posterior median sulcus behind it is much shallower.

Question 5309. The eighth cervical spinal nerve leaves the vertebral canal at which place: above the seventh cervical vertebra, below the seventh cervical vertebra, above the sixth cervical vertebra, or through the first thoracic vertebra? The answer is below the seventh cervical vertebra. The upper seven cervical nerves pass above their own vertebrae, and the eighth has none of its own.

Question 5310. A described section of the cervical cord shows a ventral horn whose lateral part is much bulkier than its medial part, while a thoracic section shows a slender horn of even width. What does the difference indicate? The answer is that the lateral cell column serves the limb muscles. The medial column serves the deep muscles of the back at every level.

Question 5311. True or false: a transverse section of the cervical cord holds more white matter than a section of the lumbar cord. The answer is true. Every long tract that ascends or descends must pass through the cervical cord, so fibers accumulate above. The lumbar cord has proportionally more gray matter and less white matter.

Question 5312. The denticulate ligament is a fold of which membrane, and where does it attach? The answer is the pia mater, attaching to the dura between the dorsal and the ventral roots. About 21 pairs run down the sides of the cord. They hold the cord in the middle of the canal.

Question 5313. Which of these pairs belongs together: the fifth cervical to the first thoracic segments with the brachial plexus, the cervical enlargement with the lower limb, the lumbar enlargement with the phrenic nerve, or the sacral segments with the upper limb? The answer is the fifth cervical to the first thoracic segments with the brachial plexus. Those segments supply the upper limb.

Question 5314. Ventral rootlets leave the cord along which groove, and dorsal rootlets enter along which other groove? The answer is the anterolateral sulcus for the ventral rootlets and the posterolateral sulcus for the dorsal ones. The two grooves mark the front and the back of each side.

Question 5315. Which dorsal column tract is added to the cord at about the sixth thoracic segment and lies lateral to the other in the neck: the gracile tract, the cuneate tract, the spinocerebellar tract, or the corticospinal tract? The answer is the cuneate tract. It carries fibers from the upper limb, which enter above that level. The gracile tract lies medial to it.

Question 5316. Name the two tracts that lie side by side in the dorsal column of the cervical cord and say what each carries. The answer is the gracile tract, which carries sensation from the lower half of the body, and the cuneate tract, which carries sensation from the upper limb. Both carry fine touch and the sense of position.

Question 5317. In the cervical cord, is the transverse diameter or the anteroposterior diameter the greater, and why is that so? The answer is the transverse diameter. The cervical enlargement widens from side to side as the gray matter for the upper limb grows. In the thoracic cord the two diameters are nearly equal, so the section looks round.

Question 5318. A constructed illustration: an older person has neck pain and clumsy hands, and the described radiograph shows bony spurs narrowing the cervical canal. Which part of the cord is compressed, and what is lost first? The answer is the cervical enlargement, with the hands and the arms affected before the legs. The narrow canal presses on the cord.

Question 5319. A described injury drives the cervical cord backward against a bony spur, and the person loses pain and temperature feeling in both hands while touch is preserved. Which part is damaged: the dorsal columns, the central gray matter and its crossing fibers, the ventral horns alone, or the whole cord? The answer is the central gray matter and its crossing fibers, which lie nearest the center.

Question 5320. A described magnetic resonance image of the neck shows the cord as a gray band with the nerve roots leaving at each level and fluid around it. Which part of the cord is shown? The answer is the cervical cord, where the roots leave at right angles to reach the intervertebral foramina. The fluid there is scanty, so the roots are easy to follow.

Question 5321. Which spinal segments form the lumbar enlargement of the cord, and which limb does it serve? The answer is the second lumbar to the third sacral segments, and it serves the lower limb. The extra gray matter is needed for the muscles of the thigh, the leg and the foot. Its roots gather into the lumbosacral plexus.

Question 5322. Which of these parts of the cord supplies the muscles of the lower limb: the cervical enlargement, the thoracic cord, the lumbar enlargement, or the conus medullaris? The answer is the lumbar enlargement. The second lumbar to the third sacral segments give the lower limb its nerves. The conus medullaris lies below it and serves the pelvic organs.

Question 5323. A described transverse section of the cord is round, with a great deal of gray matter and comparatively little white matter around it. Which level does this describe: the upper cervical cord, the thoracic cord, the lumbar enlargement, or the conus medullaris? The answer is the lumbar enlargement, where much gray matter is needed for the lower limb.

Question 5324. The lumbar enlargement of the cord lies opposite which part of the vertebral column? The answer is the lower thoracic and upper lumbar vertebrae, roughly the ninth to the twelfth thoracic. The segments and the vertebrae no longer match one to one there. That is why a needle is placed lower.

Question 5325. A constructed illustration: a person has numbness in the saddle area and difficulty passing urine, with the strength of the legs preserved, after a described injury at the first lumbar vertebra. Which part of the cord is injured? The answer is the conus medullaris. The lowest part of the cord serves the bladder, the bowel and the skin of the saddle area.

Question 5326. At which vertebra does the spinal cord of an adult normally end? The answer is at the lower border of the first lumbar vertebra, or the disc just below it. The tapered end there is the conus medullaris. Below it only roots and the filum terminale continue down the canal.

Question 5327. Which of these describes the filum terminale: a bundle of nerve roots, a thin strand of pia mater that anchors the cord, a fold of dura, or a ligament of the vertebral column? The answer is a thin strand of pia mater that anchors the cord. It runs from the tip of the conus medullaris to the coccyx. It carries no nerves.

Question 5328. A described specimen shows the lower end of the cord tapering to a point, with a sheaf of slender strands running down around a central thread. Which structures are shown? The answer is the conus medullaris, the cauda equina and the filum terminale. The strands are the lumbar and sacral roots on their way out.

Question 5329. True or false: in an adult the spinal cord ends above the level of the third lumbar vertebra. The answer is true. The cord ends at about the first lumbar vertebra, well above the third. That is why cerebrospinal fluid is sampled lower down, where the needle meets roots and fluid rather than cord.

Question 5330. The great anterior radicular artery reinforces the blood supply of the lower cord. From which side and at which level does it usually arise? The answer is the left side, at the lower thoracic or upper lumbar levels, most often between the ninth and the twelfth thoracic. It joins the anterior spinal artery and feeds the lumbar enlargement.

Question 5331. Which of these pairs belongs together: the conus medullaris with the tapered end of the cord, the filum terminale with the brachial plexus, the cauda equina with the upper cervical cord, or the lumbar cistern with the fourth ventricle? The answer is the conus medullaris with the tapered end of the cord, which the filum anchors.

Question 5332. Why is a lumbar puncture performed below the second lumbar vertebra in an adult: because the cord ends above that level, because the canal is wider there, because the fluid is cleaner there, or because the roots are absent there? The answer is that the cord ends above that level. The needle enters the lumbar cistern, where the roots float in cerebrospinal fluid.

Question 5333. Name two structures that hold the lower end of the spinal cord in place within the vertebral canal. The answer is the filum terminale and the denticulate ligaments. The filum runs from the tip of the conus medullaris to the coccyx, and the denticulate ligaments fix the sides of the cord to the dura.

Question 5334. Why does the distance between a spinal segment and its matching vertebra grow larger toward the lower end of the column? The answer is that the vertebral column grows faster than the cord. The cord keeps its upper attachments, so its segments are drawn upward. The lower roots must travel far before they leave.

Question 5335. A constructed illustration: after a described injury at the third lumbar vertebra, a person has severe pain down both legs, weakness of both feet and loss of the ankle reflexes, while the saddle area is spared. Which structure is injured? The answer is the cauda equina, the bundle of the lower roots. Each root serves a strip of skin, so both legs are affected.

Question 5336. In the dorsal column of the lumbar cord, which tract is present: the gracile tract alone, the cuneate tract alone, both tracts, or neither tract? The answer is the gracile tract alone. Below the sixth thoracic segment all the entering fibers join the gracile tract, so the cuneate tract has not yet begun.

Question 5337. A described radiograph after a contrast injection shows a column of fluid in the lower canal with fine parallel lines crossing it and no cord visible. Which space has been filled? The answer is the lumbar cistern. The lines are the roots of the cauda equina floating in the fluid. The cord ended above the level that is shown.

Question 5338. What is the name of the widened part of the subarachnoid space below the end of the cord, and what does it contain? The answer is the lumbar cistern, which contains cerebrospinal fluid and the roots of the cauda equina. It extends from the second lumbar vertebra to the second sacral vertebra.

Question 5339. What shape does the gray matter of the spinal cord take in a transverse section? The answer is the shape of a butterfly or a letter H. The two broad ventral horns and the two narrower dorsal horns join across the middle. The white matter surrounds the gray matter on every side.

Question 5340. Which statement about the horns of the gray matter is correct: the dorsal horns are sensory and the ventral horns motor, the dorsal horns are motor and the ventral sensory, both are motor, or both are sensory? The answer is that the dorsal horns are sensory and the ventral horns motor. The dorsal roots carry sensation in, and the ventral horns send axons out.

Question 5341. Into how many laminae did Rexed divide the gray matter of the spinal cord, and do they run its whole length? The answer is 10 laminae, running the length of the cord. They are numbered from the tip of the dorsal horn to the region around the central canal. The pattern is the same at every level.

Question 5342. Which lamina of the dorsal horn is known as the substantia gelatinosa: the first, the second, the fifth, or the seventh? The answer is the second lamina. It forms a cap over the tip of the dorsal horn and receives the finest pain fibers. The first lamina in front of it is the posteromarginal nucleus.

Question 5343. A described stained section shows the gray matter divided into bands by fine lines, with narrow bands at the tip of the dorsal horn and broad bands in the ventral horn. What do those bands represent? The answer is the laminae of Rexed. Each band holds cells of a particular kind and receives a particular set of fibers.

Question 5344. In a transverse section, where does the central canal lie in relation to the gray and white commissures? The answer is that it lies in the gray commissure, behind the anterior white commissure. The gray commissure joins the two halves of the gray matter across the midline. The canal is a narrow tube at its center.

Question 5345. A constructed illustration: a person loses pain and temperature feeling over the shoulders and the arms in a cape-like pattern, while the legs are spared. The described scan shows a cavity in the center of the cervical cord. Which fibers are interrupted first? The answer is the fibers that cross near the central canal, carrying pain and temperature from one side to the other. Both arms therefore lose that feeling.

Question 5346. Which named group of cells lies at the base of the dorsal horn in the thoracic cord and gives rise to a tract to the cerebellum? The answer is the nucleus dorsalis, also called the thoracic nucleus or the column of Clarke. It lies in the intermediate zone, medial to the entering fibers. Its axons form the dorsal spinocerebellar tract.

Question 5347. The nucleus dorsalis is found over which range of segments: the fifth cervical to the first thoracic, the eighth cervical to the third lumbar, the first thoracic to the second lumbar, or the second to the fourth sacral? The answer is the eighth cervical to the third lumbar, though it is largest in the thoracic region. Many texts give the first thoracic to the second lumbar.

Question 5348. Which column of cells lies in the lateral part of the intermediate zone between the first thoracic and the second lumbar segments? The answer is the intermediolateral cell column, the origin of the sympathetic fibers. Its axons leave by the ventral roots. It is absent above and below that range.

Question 5349. The parasympathetic cells of the spinal cord lie in which segments: the first thoracic to the second lumbar, the second to the fourth sacral, the fifth cervical to the first thoracic, or the whole length of the cord? The answer is the second to the fourth sacral segments. Their fibers supply the pelvic organs, while the sympathetic cells lie much higher.

Question 5350. A described section shows a narrow gray matter shaped like a thin H, with small horns and a small round dot of gray around the canal. Which level of the cord is this? The answer is the thoracic cord, where the horns are slender because little limb muscle is served. The white matter forms a thick ring around them.

Question 5351. True or false: the central canal of the spinal cord contains cerebrospinal fluid and is lined by ependymal cells. The answer is true. The canal is continuous above with the fourth ventricle and is filled with the same fluid. In the adult it is narrow and may be closed in places, which is a normal finding.

Question 5352. Where does the lateral horn lie in relation to the dorsal and the ventral horns, and in which segments is it found? The answer is that it lies between them, in the intermediate zone, from the first thoracic to the second or third lumbar segment. Its cells control the sweat glands and the blood vessels.

Question 5353. Which of these pairs belongs together: the second lamina with the substantia gelatinosa, the fourth lamina with the motor neurons, the ninth lamina with the central canal, or the tenth lamina with the substantia gelatinosa? The answer is the second lamina with the substantia gelatinosa. The ninth lamina holds the motor neurons.

Question 5354. Which large cell of the gray matter sends its axon out of the cord to end on the fibers of a skeletal muscle? The answer is the alpha motor neuron of the ventral horn. Its axon leaves by the ventral root and ends at the muscle. Smaller gamma motor neurons beside it supply the muscle spindles.

Question 5355. In the ventral horn, which group of motor neurons supplies the deep muscles of the back: the medial group, the lateral group, the central group, or the group around the canal? The answer is the medial group. It is present at every level of the cord and lies nearest the midline. The lateral group appears only where the limbs are served.

Question 5356. Which cell lies in the ventral horn and inhibits the motor neuron that excites it: the Renshaw cell, the alpha motor neuron, the cell of the nucleus dorsalis, or the ependymal cell? The answer is the Renshaw cell. It receives a branch of the motor axon and sends back an inhibitory signal, which helps keep a contraction steady.

Question 5357. A described section shows a round cord with a very large ventral horn cut into groups of cells and a small dorsal horn, surrounded by a thin rim of white matter. Which level is this? The answer is the lumbar enlargement, where the muscles of the lower limb are represented.

Question 5358. A constructed illustration: a person has wasting and weakness of the hands together with stiffness and exaggerated reflexes in the legs. The described disease destroys the motor neurons of the ventral horn and the fibers that descend to them. Which two sets of cells are failing? The answer is the lower motor neurons of the ventral horn and the upper motor neurons that drive them. The first loss wastes the muscles, and the second stiffens them.

Question 5359. Name the three horns or columns of gray matter in a transverse section and say what each does. The answer is the dorsal horn, which receives sensory fibers, the ventral horn, which holds motor neurons, and the intermediate zone between them, which holds the autonomic cells. The lateral horn belongs to that intermediate zone.

Question 5360. What is the name of the band of gray matter that joins the two halves of the cord across the midline? The answer is the gray commissure. It lies around the central canal. The fibers that cross from one side to the other pass through it, and through the white commissure in front of it.

Question 5361. Which group of cells supplies the muscles of the bladder and the anal sphincters and lies in the first to the third sacral segments: the intermediolateral cell column, the nucleus of Onuf, the nucleus dorsalis, or the posteromarginal nucleus? The answer is the nucleus of Onuf, a compact group of motor neurons that keeps the sphincters under voluntary control.

Question 5362. Fibers that carry pain cross to the opposite side of the cord within one or two segments of their entry. Through which commissure do they pass? The answer is the anterior white commissure, which lies in front of the gray commissure and close to the central canal. That is why a cavity in the center of the cord interrupts them on both sides.

Question 5363. A described section from the lowest part of the cord shows a small round gray matter shaped like a broad H, with thin white matter around it and a wide central canal. Which level is this? The answer is the sacral cord, near the conus medullaris, where the gray matter holds the cells for the bladder and the bowel.

Question 5364. Relative to its white matter, in which part of the cord is the gray matter most abundant: the upper cervical cord, the mid thoracic cord, the lumbar enlargement, or the sacral cord? The answer is the sacral cord. Most long tracts have already ended, so little white matter remains, while the gray matter still holds the pelvic cells.

Question 5365. A constructed illustration: a person eats a poor diet and develops numbness in the feet, unsteadiness in the dark, and stiffness of the legs. The described condition affects the dorsal columns and the lateral corticospinal tracts. Which two functions fail together? The answer is the sense of position and vibration, and the control of the legs by the brain.

Question 5366. The nucleus of which cranial nerve extends down into the upper cervical segments of the spinal cord? The answer is the trigeminal nerve, whose spinal nucleus reaches as low as the second or third cervical segment. It carries pain and temperature from the face. A lesion high in the neck can therefore disturb feeling in the face.

Question 5367. The motor neurons that supply the diaphragm lie in which segments: the first to the fourth cervical, the third to the fifth cervical, the fifth cervical to the first thoracic, or the second to the fourth sacral? The answer is the third to the fifth cervical segments. Their axons form the phrenic nerve, which runs down to the chief muscle of breathing.

Question 5368. A described map of the dorsal horn marks a wide band in its neck where cells respond both to a light touch and to a pinch of the skin. Which lamina is marked? The answer is the fifth lamina, where the cells respond more strongly as the stimulus grows. Fibers from the skin and from the organs meet there.

Question 5369. Why is the gray matter of the cervical and lumbar enlargements so much larger than that of the thoracic segments? The answer is that the enlargements serve the limbs, which need many more motor neurons and sensory cells than the trunk does. The thoracic horns are slender, and the intermediate zone is thin.

Question 5370. Where is the gray matter of the cord most abundant in absolute terms: in the cervical enlargement, in the upper thoracic cord, in the mid thoracic cord, or in the conus medullaris? The answer is the cervical enlargement, where the ventral horns reach their greatest size for the arm and the hand.

Question 5371. Which cells line the central canal of the spinal cord? The answer is the ependymal cells. They form a single layer with cilia on their free surface. They are the same cells that line the ventricles of the brain, and the canal they enclose carries cerebrospinal fluid.

Question 5372. Which small group of cells lies in the white matter of the upper cervical cord and receives fibers of the pain pathway: the nucleus dorsalis, the lateral cervical nucleus, the nucleus of Onuf, or the intermediolateral cell column? The answer is the lateral cervical nucleus, which lies beside the first two cervical segments. It is small and variable in humans.

Question 5373. Two described sections are compared. The first shows a small triangular projection between the horns of the gray matter, and the second, taken higher, shows the same region smooth and rounded. What does the comparison show? The answer is that the lateral horn is present in the first section and absent in the second. Those cells lie only between the first thoracic and the third lumbar segment.

Question 5374. A constructed illustration: a person walks with a wide-based gait and cannot tell where the toes are when the eyes are closed, though pain and temperature feeling in the feet is normal. Which part of the cord is damaged? The answer is the dorsal columns, which carry the sense of position and of vibration.

Question 5375. True or false: the gray matter of the spinal cord contains no myelinated fibers. The answer is false. Many fibers within the gray matter are myelinated, especially the axons that leave the ventral horn cells. The gray matter is named for the color it takes in a fresh section, not for the absence of myelin.

Question 5376. Which of these pairs belongs together: the intermediolateral cell column with the sympathetic fibers, the nucleus of Onuf with the upper limb, the nucleus dorsalis with the anal sphincter, or the ependymal cells with the motor neurons? The answer is the intermediolateral cell column with the sympathetic fibers. Those cells lie between the first thoracic and the second lumbar segments.

Question 5377. The cells of origin of the pain and temperature pathway lie chiefly in which laminae of the dorsal horn: the first, the fourth and the fifth, the seventh, or the tenth? The answer is the first, the fourth and the fifth. Their axons cross to the opposite side and run upward to the brain. The seventh lamina holds autonomic and cerebellar cells instead.

Question 5378. Which laminae of the dorsal horn form the nucleus proprius, and what kind of fibers end there? The answer is the third and the fourth laminae, which receive fibers carrying touch and the sense of position. The name means the proper nucleus of the dorsal horn, as distinct from the gelatinous cap.

Question 5379. Into which three columns is the white matter of the spinal cord divided on each side? The answer is the dorsal funiculus, the lateral funiculus and the ventral funiculus. They are separated by the dorsal horns and by the rootlets that enter and leave the cord. Each column carries a different set of tracts.

Question 5380. Which funiculus of the cord contains the tracts that carry the sense of position and of vibration: the dorsal, the lateral, the ventral, or the gray commissure? The answer is the dorsal funiculus, also called the posterior column. Its fibers run up to the medulla without crossing in the cord. The lateral funiculus carries the pain pathway instead.

Question 5381. What divides the dorsal funiculus into its two tracts along the midline? The answer is the posterior median septum, a thin wall of pia mater that runs into the cord from behind. The two tracts beside it are the gracile and the cuneate tracts. Higher up, a shallower groove called the posterior intermediate sulcus marks the boundary between them.

Question 5382. Within the lateral corticospinal tract, which fibers lie most laterally: those going to the cervical cord, to the thoracic cord, to the sacral cord, or to the medulla? The answer is those going to the sacral cord. The fibers are laid down from within outward as they descend, so the cervical fibers lie nearest the gray matter.

Question 5383. A described section of the mid cervical cord shows a large dorsal funiculus divided by a groove into a medial part and a lateral part. What does the groove mark? The answer is the posterior intermediate sulcus, which separates the gracile tract from the cuneate tract.

Question 5384. Most fibers of the corticospinal tract cross to the other side at which place, and in which funiculus do they then descend? The answer is at the lower end of the medulla, and they descend in the lateral funiculus. That crossing is the pyramidal decussation. The smaller uncrossed part stays in the ventral funiculus and crosses lower down.

Question 5385. A constructed illustration: after a described injury that damages the front of the cord, a person cannot move the legs and cannot feel pain or temperature below the waist, yet still feels vibration at the ankles. Which part is damaged? The answer is the anterior part, supplied by the anterior spinal artery, while the dorsal columns behind are spared.

Question 5386. The pain pathway runs in two parts within the cord. Which funiculus holds the lateral spinothalamic tract, and which holds the anterior one? The answer is the lateral funiculus for the lateral tract and the ventral funiculus for the anterior one. Both carry pain from the opposite side of the body.

Question 5387. True or false: the dorsal spinocerebellar tract arises from cells of the nucleus dorsalis on the same side of the cord. The answer is true. Those cells lie at the base of the dorsal horn, and their axons run up the lateral funiculus without crossing. The tract carries information from the muscles and the joints to the cerebellum.

Question 5388. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lateral corticospinal tract with the lateral funiculus, the gracile tract with the ventral funiculus, the spinothalamic tract with the dorsal funiculus, or the vestibulospinal tract with the dorsal funiculus? The answer is the lateral corticospinal tract with the lateral funiculus. The gracile tract lies in the dorsal funiculus.

Question 5389. What is the name of the narrow band of fine fibers at the tip of the dorsal horn, where small sensory fibers enter and run up and down for a segment before ending? The answer is the dorsolateral fasciculus, or the tract of Lissauer. Its fibers end in the first and second laminae.

Question 5390. Which fibers form the thin rim of white matter that lies immediately around the gray matter at every level: the long ascending tracts, the long descending tracts, the short intersegmental fibers, or the fibers of the dorsal columns? The answer is the short intersegmental fibers, the fasciculus proprius, which link one segment with the next.

Question 5391. The ventral corticospinal tract lies in which funiculus and beside which midline structure? The answer is the ventral funiculus, beside the anterior median fissure and the medial longitudinal fasciculus. Its fibers have not crossed in the medulla. Most of them cross in the anterior white commissure before they end.

Question 5392. A described stain of the upper thoracic cord shows a dorsal funiculus divided into two parts and a lateral column whose outer rim is packed with heavily myelinated fibers. Which tracts lie in that outer rim? The answer is the spinocerebellar tracts and the lateral corticospinal tract. The two dorsal parts are the gracile and the cuneate tracts.

Question 5393. The blood supply of the cord is poorest, and the cord most vulnerable to a fall in blood pressure, at which levels: the upper cervical, the mid thoracic, the lumbar enlargement, or the conus medullaris? The answer is the mid thoracic levels, where the anterior spinal artery is narrowest and the reinforcing arteries are few.

Question 5394. A described tumor presses on the dorsal funiculus of the cervical cord. Which fibers, those from the leg or those from the arm, lie more laterally? The answer is that the fibers from the arm lie laterally. They entered higher and were laid down outside the older ones, which are pushed medially.

Question 5395. In the lateral spinothalamic tract, which fibers lie most laterally: those from the sacral segments, the cervical segments, the thoracic segments, or the lumbar segments? The answer is those from the sacral segments. Fibers are added from within as the tract is built upward, so the earliest lie outermost.

Question 5396. Sensory fibers of the dorsal root enter the cord at the tip of which horn, and in which direction does that horn point? The answer is the dorsal horn, which points backward and a little outward. Its tip reaches the surface of the cord at the posterolateral sulcus. All the fibers of the dorsal root enter there.

Question 5397. Which group of cells caps the tip of the dorsal horn and responds to the sharpest pain: the posteromarginal nucleus, the nucleus proprius, the nucleus dorsalis, or the nucleus of Onuf? The answer is the posteromarginal nucleus in the first lamina. It receives the finest pain fibers directly from the dorsal root. The nucleus proprius behind it receives touch fibers instead.

Question 5398. Where do the fine fibers of the dorsal root end, when they enter the cord and run a short distance up and down before terminating? The answer is in the first and the second laminae of the dorsal horn. They travel in the dorsolateral fasciculus at the tip of the horn. Their endings there begin the pain pathway.

Question 5399. A described section shows the dorsal horn capped by a pale gelatinous band, with a darker band behind it and a wider region of scattered cells near its base. Which laminae are shown? The answer is the first, second and third to fifth laminae, and the pale cap is the substantia gelatinosa, where the finest pain fibers end.

Question 5400. A constructed illustration: a person with gallbladder disease feels pain near the right shoulder blade, though the gallbladder lies far from that skin. Which arrangement in the dorsal horn explains it? The answer is that fibers from the skin and fibers from the organ end on the same cells, so the brain refers the pain to the skin.

To summarize, the spinal cord has a cervical enlargement for the upper limb and a lumbar enlargement for the lower limb, and its gray matter is arranged in horns and laminae that receive sensation behind and send movement out in front. The dorsal horns are the entry point of the dorsal roots, with the posteromarginal nucleus and the substantia gelatinosa at the tip and the nucleus proprius behind them, while the fibers of the pain pathway cross close to the central canal. The white matter is divided into the dorsal, the lateral and the ventral funiculi, from the sacral fibers outermost in the lateral corticospinal tract to the gracile tract lying medial to the cuneate tract in the dorsal column. Knowing the level of each enlargement, the ending of the cord at the first lumbar vertebra, and the way the lower roots form the cauda equina makes the whole cord readable in three dimensions.
