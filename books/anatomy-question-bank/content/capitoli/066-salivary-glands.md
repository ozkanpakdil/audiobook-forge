# Chapter 66 — Salivary Glands

This chapter turns to the mouth and to the glands that keep it wet, beginning with the teeth and then taking the three pairs of major salivary glands in turn. It covers the shapes, tissues and nerve supply of the teeth, and then the position, ducts, relations and control of the parotid, the submandibular and the sublingual glands. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. The two topics belong together, because the teeth begin the mechanical breakdown of food while saliva begins its chemical digestion. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 6501. Which substance forms the hard outer covering of the crown of a tooth and is the hardest tissue in the human body? The answer is enamel. It is almost entirely mineral and covers the crown only, stopping at the neck of the tooth. Dentin lies beneath it, and the root is covered by cementum.

Question 6502. A tooth is described as having a thin layer of bone-like tissue over the dentin of its root, into which the fibers of the periodontal ligament are inserted. Which tissue is this? The answer is cementum. It covers the root from the neck to the apex and meets the enamel at the neck.

Question 6503. How many teeth are there in the primary or deciduous dentition: sixteen, twenty, twenty-four, or thirty-two? The answer is twenty. Each half of each jaw carries two incisors, one canine and two molars, and there are four such halves. The permanent dentition that follows has thirty-two teeth.

Question 6504. How many incisors are there in the permanent dentition of an adult: four, six, eight, or twelve? The answer is eight. There are two in each half of each jaw, one central and one lateral. They have chisel-shaped crowns and a single root, and their function is to cut food.

Question 6505. Which tissue lies immediately deep to the enamel and forms the bulk of a tooth, extending from the crown into the root? The answer is dentin. It is secreted by cells called odontoblasts, whose processes run in fine tubules through it. Dentin is softer than enamel but harder than cementum.

Question 6506. A described ground section of a tooth shows a thin translucent cap over a thicker yellowish layer, with a small chamber in the middle. Which tissue forms the outer cap? The answer is enamel. It is the hardest tissue of the body and covers the crown only. The yellowish layer beneath is dentin, and the chamber is the pulp cavity.

Question 6507. The periodontal ligament lies immediately between which two structures? The answer is the cementum of the root and the alveolar bone of the jaw. Its fibers run across that narrow space and hold the tooth in its socket. They also allow slight movement, which is why a tooth gives a little under pressure.

Question 6508. Which sequence gives the dental formula for one half of the adult jaw: two incisors, one canine, two premolars and three molars; two incisors, one canine, three premolars and two molars; three incisors, one canine, two premolars and two molars; or two incisors, two canines, one premolar and three molars? The answer is two incisors, one canine, two premolars and three molars. Repeated in each half of each jaw, that gives thirty-two teeth.

Question 6509. Which teeth of the deciduous dentition are replaced by the permanent premolars: the deciduous incisors, the deciduous canines, the deciduous molars, or the permanent molars? The answer is the deciduous molars. Each deciduous molar is shed and a premolar takes its place. The permanent molars have no predecessor, because they erupt behind the deciduous teeth.

Question 6510. Which nerve supplies the lower teeth and the skin of the chin, running in a canal inside the mandible? The answer is the inferior alveolar nerve. It is a branch of the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve and enters the mandibular foramen on the inner surface of the ramus. It gives branches to the teeth and ends as the mental nerve.

Question 6511. A constructed illustration: a tooth is chipped in a fall and the exposed yellowish layer inside is sensitive to cold and to sweet food. Which tissue has been exposed, and which cells formed it? The answer is dentin, formed by odontoblasts. Its fine tubules carry the stimulus toward the pulp, which is why the tooth reacts.

Question 6512. Which teeth are shaped for cutting food: the incisors, the canines, the premolars, or the molars? The answer is the incisors. Their chisel-shaped crowns and single straight roots suit them to biting. The canines are pointed for tearing, while the premolars and molars have broad cusped crowns for grinding.

Question 6513. A described dental radiograph shows a tooth at the back of the upper jaw whose crown carries several cusps and whose root divides into three, two on the outer side and one on the inner side. Which tooth is shown? The answer is a maxillary molar. This pattern of three roots is typical of the first upper molar.

Question 6514. True or false: enamel, once it has been worn away, is replaced throughout life by new enamel from the same cells that formed it. The answer is false. The cells that make enamel, the ameloblasts, are lost once the crown is complete, so enamel cannot be replaced. Dentin, by contrast, continues to be laid down slowly throughout life.

Question 6515. Which of these pairs belongs together: the incisor with grinding, the canine with tearing, the molar with cutting, or the premolar with tearing? The answer is the canine with tearing. A canine has a single pointed cusp and a long root. The incisors cut, while the premolars and molars grind.

Question 6516. Which nerve supplies the molar teeth of the upper jaw? The answer is the posterior superior alveolar nerve, a branch of the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve. It enters the back of the maxilla and runs forward above the roots of the molar teeth. The upper teeth are not supplied by one nerve.

Question 6517. Which permanent teeth are usually the first to erupt: the central incisors, the first molars, the canines, or the second molars? The answer is the first molars. They come through at about six years of age, before any deciduous tooth is shed, and are often called the six-year molars. Because they erupt behind the primary teeth, they are often mistaken for part of that set.

Question 6518. On the inner surface of the ramus of the mandible, which nerve lies just in front of and a little above the mandibular foramen, on its way to the tongue? The answer is the lingual nerve. The inferior alveolar nerve enters the foramen just behind it. The two lie so close that a needle aimed at the foramen can reach the lingual nerve.

Question 6519. A described dissection of the lower jaw shows a canal within the bone beneath the roots of the teeth, running from a foramen on the inner surface of the ramus forward to an opening on the front of the jaw. What is the canal, and what runs in it? The answer is the mandibular canal, which carries the inferior alveolar nerve with its artery and vein. The nerve leaves it as the mental nerve.

Question 6520. A constructed illustration: after a difficult removal of a lower third molar, sensation is lost over the lower lip and chin on that side, while the tongue is unaffected. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the inferior alveolar nerve, whose mental branch supplies that skin. Injury to the lingual nerve would instead dull the front of the tongue.

Question 6521. Which statement about the lower first molar is correct: it usually has two roots, one in front and one behind; it usually has three roots; it has a single conical root; or it has four roots? The answer is that it usually has two roots, both flattened and lying one behind the other. The upper first molar usually has three.

Question 6522. Name the three hard tissues that make up a tooth, spoken as a list. The answer is enamel, dentin, and cementum. Enamel covers the crown, cementum covers the root, and dentin lies beneath both of them and surrounds the pulp cavity. The pulp itself is soft tissue, carrying the vessels and nerves of the tooth.

Question 6523. True or false: a tooth is a bone, and like bone it is rebuilt throughout life by the same cells that remodel the skeleton. The answer is false. A tooth is not a bone. Its dentin is secreted by odontoblasts and its enamel by ameloblasts, and the crown is never remodeled, though the bone around it can be.

Question 6524. Which tooth is the last of the permanent teeth to erupt, usually between seventeen and twenty-one years of age, and is the one most often unable to come through? The answer is the third molar, the wisdom tooth. Lack of space at the back of the jaw often leaves it impacted, most often in the lower jaw.

Question 6525. A described section through the upper jaw shows the roots of the first and second molars projecting into the floor of a large air-filled cavity, separated from it by a thin plate of bone. Which cavity is this? The answer is the maxillary sinus. Because the separation is so thin, infection at a molar root can spread into the sinus.

Question 6526. Which is the largest of the three pairs of major salivary glands, lying in front of and below the ear? The answer is the parotid gland. It fills the hollow behind the ramus of the mandible and lies over the masseter muscle. The other two pairs are much smaller and lie in the upper neck and the floor of the mouth.

Question 6527. Which statement about salivary secretion is correct: the parotid produces a watery serous fluid rich in amylase; the parotid produces thick mucous saliva; the sublingual gland produces only serous fluid; or all three glands produce only mucous saliva? The answer is that the parotid produces a watery serous fluid rich in amylase. The submandibular gland is mixed but mainly serous, and the sublingual gland is mixed but mainly mucous.

Question 6528. The three pairs of major salivary glands all empty into the mouth, but they lie in different places. Which muscle separates the submandibular gland into a part below it in the neck and a part above it in the floor of the mouth? The answer is the mylohyoid. The gland hooks around the posterior border of that muscle, which forms the floor of the mouth.

Question 6529. A described specimen shows a gland under the mucous membrane of the floor of the mouth, above the mylohyoid muscle, with many small ducts opening along a raised fold beside the tongue. Which gland is shown? The answer is the sublingual gland. It has no single main duct and opens by a series of short ducts along the fold.

Question 6530. Which gland lies in the hollow between the ramus of the mandible and the mastoid process, in front of the ear, wrapped in a tough fascial capsule? The answer is the parotid gland. It is the largest salivary gland and is closely related to the facial nerve, which runs through it.

Question 6531. What is the name of the duct of the parotid gland, which crosses the cheek and opens into the mouth? The answer is the parotid duct, also called the duct of Stensen. It leaves the front of the gland, crosses the masseter, and turns inward to pierce the buccinator. Its opening lies opposite the upper second molar.

Question 6532. Where does the parotid duct open into the mouth: opposite the crown of the upper second molar, opposite the lower first molar, at the sublingual papilla beside the frenulum of the tongue, or in the floor of the mouth? The answer is opposite the upper second molar. The sublingual papilla is where the submandibular duct opens instead.

Question 6533. A described dissection shows a large gland over the masseter muscle, wrapped in fascia, with a duct that crosses the muscle and turns inward at its front border to pierce the cheek. Which gland is shown, and what is its duct called? The answer is the parotid gland and the parotid duct. The duct opens opposite the upper second molar.

Question 6534. Immediately superficial to which muscle does the parotid duct run as it crosses the cheek? The answer is the masseter. The duct leaves the front of the gland and runs forward across the masseter with the buccal branch of the facial nerve above it, then turns medially to pierce the buccinator.

Question 6535. Which of these structures runs through the parotid gland: the facial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the glossopharyngeal nerve, or the vagus nerve? The answer is the facial nerve. It enters the back of the gland and divides there, and the gland is described as having a superficial and a deep part on either side of it. The retromandibular vein also passes through.

Question 6536. A constructed illustration: a firm swelling in front of the ear fills the hollow behind the jaw and lifts the lobe of the ear outward. Which gland is involved, and which nerve running through it must be examined? The answer is the parotid gland, with the facial nerve. Because the nerve passes through the gland, the muscles of the face are tested on that side. Decisions about the swelling belong to the treating clinician.

Question 6537. Which artery ascends through the parotid gland and divides within it into a maxillary branch and a superficial temporal branch? The answer is the external carotid artery. It lies deep to the facial nerve, and its division marks the upper part of the parotid bed. The retromandibular vein lies between the two.

Question 6538. Which nerve provides the parasympathetic secretomotor supply to the parotid gland: the facial nerve through the chorda tympani, the glossopharyngeal nerve through the lesser petrosal nerve, the vagus nerve, or the sympathetic trunk? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve, through the lesser petrosal nerve. The fibers relay in the otic ganglion and reach the gland along the auriculotemporal nerve.

Question 6539. A described specimen shows the facial nerve dividing within a gland into a larger part near the surface and a smaller part lying deeply, with a vein passing between the two. Which gland is shown, and which vein lies between its parts? The answer is the parotid gland, with the retromandibular vein between its superficial and deep parts.

Question 6540. Which branch of the facial nerve runs just above the parotid duct as the duct crosses the masseter muscle? The answer is the buccal branch. It travels forward with the duct and supplies the muscles of the cheek. Because of this close relation, a wound of the cheek can injure the duct and the branch together.

Question 6541. True or false: the parotid gland is a serous gland, and its secretion contains amylase but no mucus. The answer is true. All of its acini are serous, and its watery secretion is rich in amylase, which begins the digestion of starch. The submandibular and sublingual glands are mixed glands and add mucus to the mouth.

Question 6542. Which statement about the facial nerve within the parotid gland is correct: it divides the gland into a superficial and a deep part; it supplies the secretion of the gland; it lies deep to the gland and never enters it; or it passes behind the gland? The answer is that it divides the gland into a superficial and a deep part. Its branches supply the muscles of the face, not the gland.

Question 6543. Which nerve carries the secretomotor fibers from the otic ganglion to the parotid gland, and also supplies sensation to the skin in front of the ear? The answer is the auriculotemporal nerve. It is a branch of the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve and passes through the parotid gland on its way to the temple.

Question 6544. A described operative field near the ear shows the facial nerve dividing within a gland, and one branch running upward across the arch of the cheekbone to the forehead. Which branch is shown, and which movement would be weakened if it were injured? The answer is the temporal branch, and wrinkling the forehead and raising the eyebrow would be weakened.

Question 6545. A constructed illustration: after an operation near the parotid gland, a person's face flushes and beads with sweat on that side whenever a meal is eaten. Which nerve carries the fibers responsible? The answer is the auriculotemporal nerve. The fibers that once reached the parotid gland now reach the sweat glands of the skin.

Question 6546. Which statement about the parotid duct is correct: it is about five centimeters long and opens opposite the upper second molar; it opens at the sublingual papilla; it runs deep to the masseter; or it opens into the floor of the mouth? The answer is that it is about five centimeters long and opens opposite the upper second molar. It runs superficial to the masseter.

Question 6547. Name four structures other than the gland itself that lie within the parotid gland, spoken as a list. The answer is the facial nerve, the retromandibular vein, the external carotid artery, and the auriculotemporal nerve. The facial nerve divides the gland into a superficial and a deep part.

Question 6548. Which vein, formed by the union of the superficial temporal vein and the maxillary vein, runs through the parotid gland? The answer is the retromandibular vein. It is formed within or just below the gland and descends behind the ramus of the mandible. It lies between the facial nerve and the external carotid artery.

Question 6549. In which ganglion do the parasympathetic fibers destined for the parotid gland relay: the otic ganglion, the submandibular ganglion, the ciliary ganglion, or the pterygopalatine ganglion? The answer is the otic ganglion. The preganglionic fibers arrive by the lesser petrosal nerve, and the postganglionic fibers travel with the auriculotemporal nerve.

Question 6550. A constructed illustration: a deep wound in front of the ear leaves a person unable to close the eye or wrinkle the forehead on that side, yet the gland in that region still produces saliva when food is tasted. How can both be true? The answer is that the facial nerve has been divided while the secretomotor supply is intact. Salivary fibers reach the gland with the auriculotemporal nerve, not with the facial nerve.

Question 6551. A described scan shows a mass in the parotid region that passes deeply between the ramus of the mandible and the mastoid process and lies medial to the ramus. Which part of the gland does it occupy? The answer is the deep part of the parotid gland. It lies against the styloid process and its muscles and can extend into the pharynx.

Question 6552. Which statement explains why a sudden swelling of the parotid gland is painful while a swelling of the sublingual gland of the same size is not: the parotid lies in a tight fascial capsule; the parotid has a longer duct; the skin over it is more sensitive; or it has no lymph drainage? The answer is that the parotid lies in a tight unyielding fascial capsule, so swelling raises the pressure within it.

Question 6553. Which thickened band of fascia runs from the styloid process to the angle of the mandible and separates the parotid gland from the submandibular gland? The answer is the stylomandibular ligament. It is part of the deep cervical fascia and forms the medial boundary of the parotid bed. The submandibular gland lies in front and below it.

Question 6554. Which slender bony projection, with the three muscles attached to it, lies immediately medial to the parotid gland and separates the gland from the internal jugular vein? The answer is the styloid process. The stylohyoid, the styloglossus and the stylopharyngeus muscles arise from it. The parotid bed is built around this projection.

Question 6555. True or false: all three pairs of major salivary glands receive their parasympathetic secretomotor fibers from the facial nerve. The answer is false. The parotid gland receives its fibers from the glossopharyngeal nerve by way of the lesser petrosal nerve and the otic ganglion. Only the submandibular and sublingual glands are supplied through the facial nerve.

Question 6556. A deep cut across the cheek just in front of the masseter muscle divides a structure that lies there. Which structure is most likely to be divided: the parotid duct, the submandibular duct, the lingual nerve, or the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the parotid duct, which crosses the masseter there with the buccal branch of the facial nerve. The others lie elsewhere.

Question 6557. Which gland lies in the triangle below the body of the mandible, between the two bellies of the digastric muscle, and molds itself around the posterior border of the mylohyoid? The answer is the submandibular gland. Its superficial part lies in the neck below the mylohyoid, and its deep part lies in the floor of the mouth.

Question 6558. What kind of secretion does the submandibular gland produce: serous only, mucous only, mixed but mainly serous, or mixed but mainly mucous? The answer is mixed but mainly serous. It contains both serous and mucous acini, but the serous acini predominate. The parotid gland is purely serous, and the sublingual gland is mixed but mainly mucous.

Question 6559. A described dissection shows a gland molded around the posterior border of a muscle in the floor of the mouth, with one part below the muscle in the neck and another above it in the mouth. Which gland is shown? The answer is the submandibular gland, wrapping around the mylohyoid, which separates its superficial part from its deep part.

Question 6560. Which muscle lies immediately between the two parts of the submandibular gland, so that one part lies in the neck and the other in the floor of the mouth? The answer is the mylohyoid. The larger superficial part lies below it in the digastric triangle, and the smaller deep part lies above it. When the muscle contracts it presses the gland and helps to empty it.

Question 6561. Where does the submandibular duct open into the mouth: at the sublingual papilla beside the frenulum of the tongue, opposite the upper second molar, along the sublingual fold, or at the back of the tongue? The answer is at the sublingual papilla. The parotid duct opens opposite the upper second molar, and the small sublingual ducts open along the fold.

Question 6562. What is the name of the duct of the submandibular gland, which runs forward in the floor of the mouth to open beside the frenulum of the tongue? The answer is the submandibular duct, also called the duct of Wharton. It leaves the deep part of the gland and runs forward above the mylohyoid, with the lingual nerve above it.

Question 6563. A constructed illustration: a person has a hard swelling in the floor of the mouth that aches and grows larger at mealtimes, and the gland in the upper neck is tender. Which gland is involved, and what does this suggest? The answer is the submandibular gland, and the story suggests a stone in its duct. The duct runs upward and forward, so saliva must travel against gravity. Decisions belong to the treating clinician.

Question 6564. In the floor of the mouth the submandibular duct runs forward between which two muscles: the mylohyoid and the hyoglossus, the masseter and the buccinator, the genioglossus and the geniohyoid, or the two bellies of the digastric? The answer is the mylohyoid and the hyoglossus. The duct lies above the mylohyoid and lateral to the hyoglossus, where it can be felt through the mucous membrane. The lingual nerve crosses above it and the hypoglossal nerve below.

Question 6565. A described dissection of the floor of the mouth shows a duct running forward with a nerve hooking beneath it, and a small ganglion hanging from that nerve. What are the duct and the nerve? The answer is the submandibular duct and the lingual nerve. The ganglion is the submandibular ganglion, where the secretomotor fibers relay.

Question 6566. Which of these pairs belongs together: the parotid duct with the duct of Wharton, the submandibular duct with the duct of Stensen, the sublingual ducts with the ducts of Rivinus, or the parotid duct with the sublingual papilla? The answer is the sublingual ducts with the ducts of Rivinus. The parotid duct is the duct of Stensen and the submandibular duct that of Wharton.

Question 6567. True or false: the submandibular gland lies partly above and partly below the mylohyoid muscle, and contraction of that muscle helps to express its secretion. The answer is true. The superficial part lies below the muscle in the digastric triangle and the deep part above it. As the muscle lifts the floor of the mouth during swallowing, it presses the gland.

Question 6568. Which nerve lies in the floor of the mouth, takes a spiral course around the submandibular duct, and carries general sensation from the front of the tongue: the lingual nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the glossopharyngeal nerve, or the chorda tympani? The answer is the lingual nerve, a branch of the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. It crosses the duct and then ascends into the tongue.

Question 6569. Which artery grooves the posterior surface of the submandibular gland on its way to the face? The answer is the facial artery. It hooks over the lower border of the mandible just in front of the masseter and leaves its mark on the gland. The gland also receives branches from the lingual artery.

Question 6570. Which nerve supplies the submandibular and sublingual glands with their secretomotor fibers? The answer is the facial nerve, through its chorda tympani branch. The chorda tympani joins the lingual nerve, and the fibers relay in the submandibular ganglion. They then pass to the glands along the blood vessels.

Question 6571. A constructed illustration: during an operation in the floor of the mouth, a structure lying immediately below the submandibular duct is divided, and afterward the tongue deviates to that side when it is put out. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the hypoglossal nerve, which lies below the duct on the hyoglossus. Weakness on one side makes the tongue point toward that side.

Question 6572. By which route do sympathetic fibers reach the submandibular gland, and from which ganglion do they arise? The answer is along the arteries, from the superior cervical ganglion. They travel with the facial and lingual arteries to the gland. Sympathetic stimulation produces a small volume of thicker saliva, while parasympathetic stimulation produces a copious watery secretion.

Question 6573. Which statement about the two parts of the submandibular gland is correct: the superficial part is the larger and lies below the mylohyoid; the deep part is the larger; the superficial part lies inside the mandible; or the two parts lie on opposite sides of the hyoid bone? The answer is that the superficial part is the larger and lies below the mylohyoid. The deep part is a small extension above the muscle.

Question 6574. Which list correctly names the structures that run forward in the floor of the mouth between the mylohyoid and the hyoglossus: the submandibular duct, the lingual nerve and the hypoglossal nerve; the parotid duct and the facial nerve; the glossopharyngeal nerve and the stylohyoid; or the maxillary artery and the auriculotemporal nerve? The answer is the submandibular duct, the lingual nerve and the hypoglossal nerve.

Question 6575. Which statement best explains why stones form more often in the submandibular duct than in the parotid duct: its secretion is richer in mucus, its duct is longer and runs upward, and its secretion is less watery; the parotid duct is too short; the parotid gland has a larger blood supply; or parotid saliva contains no calcium? The answer is the first. A mucinous, less watery secretion traveling up a longer duct favors stasis.

Question 6576. A constructed illustration: during removal of a submandibular gland, a nerve branch running forward near the lower border of the mandible is stretched, and afterward the lower lip on that side no longer moves downward when the person smiles. Which branch has been injured? The answer is the marginal mandibular branch of the facial nerve, which is at risk in this region.

Question 6577. A described specimen shows a mass in the floor of the mouth on one side that lifts the tongue and sends a narrow tail of glandular tissue backward around the posterior border of the mylohyoid. Which structure is enlarged? The answer is the deep part of the submandibular gland, which passes above the mylohyoid.

Question 6578. The submandibular ganglion hangs from which nerve, and where does it lie? The answer is the lingual nerve, and it lies above the deep part of the submandibular gland. The parasympathetic fibers of the facial nerve relay in it before passing to the submandibular and sublingual glands.

Question 6579. Which of these pairs belongs together: the submandibular gland with the chorda tympani, the parotid gland with the chorda tympani, the sublingual gland with the glossopharyngeal nerve, or the parotid gland with the facial nerve? The answer is the submandibular gland with the chorda tympani. The chorda tympani carries facial nerve fibers to the submandibular and sublingual glands.

Question 6580. Which statement about the relations of the submandibular gland is correct: its superficial part lies on the mylohyoid in the digastric triangle; it lies against the hyoglossus; it lies within the parotid bed; or it lies above the mylohyoid in the floor of the mouth? The answer is that the superficial part lies on the mylohyoid in the digastric triangle. The deep part lies above the mylohyoid.

Question 6581. Which is the smallest of the three pairs of major salivary glands, lying in the floor of the mouth beneath the mucous membrane? The answer is the sublingual gland. It lies above the mylohyoid muscle and against the sublingual fossa of the mandible. It has no single main duct, and its secretion is mainly mucous.

Question 6582. What kind of secretion does the sublingual gland produce: serous only, mucous only, mixed but mainly mucous, or mixed but mainly serous? The answer is mixed but mainly mucous. Its acini are predominantly mucous, so its secretion is thick and stringy. The parotid gland is serous, and the submandibular gland is mixed but mainly serous.

Question 6583. A described dissection shows a collection of small glands in the floor of the mouth that raise a ridge of mucous membrane beside the tongue, with many short ducts opening along it. Which gland is shown? The answer is the sublingual gland. The ridge is the sublingual fold, and the short ducts are the ducts of Rivinus.

Question 6584. Which of these pairs belongs together: the sublingual gland with the sublingual fold, the sublingual gland with the sublingual papilla, the parotid gland with the sublingual fold, or the submandibular gland with the parotid duct? The answer is the sublingual gland with the sublingual fold. It is the submandibular duct that opens at the sublingual papilla.

Question 6585. Where does the sublingual gland lie: above the mylohyoid muscle in the floor of the mouth, below the mylohyoid in the neck, in the parotid bed in front of the ear, or behind the angle of the mandible? The answer is above the mylohyoid in the floor of the mouth. It rests in the sublingual fossa of the mandible, covered only by mucous membrane.

Question 6586. Which structures lie immediately medial to the sublingual gland in the floor of the mouth? The answer is the submandibular duct and the lingual nerve. They run forward on the hyoglossus, separated from the sublingual gland by the deep part of the submandibular gland. The genioglossus muscle lies nearer the midline.

Question 6587. A constructed illustration: a person complains of a dry mouth and finds dry food hard to swallow. Which three pairs of glands moisten the mouth, and which produces most of the saliva at rest? The answer is the parotid, the submandibular and the sublingual glands, and the submandibular glands produce most of the resting saliva. The parotid glands contribute most during a meal.

Question 6588. The small ducts of the sublingual gland are known by whose name, and where do they open? The answer is the ducts of Rivinus, and they open along the sublingual fold. A larger duct, the duct of Bartholin, may join the submandibular duct. The gland has no single main duct of its own.

Question 6589. Which statement about the ducts of the sublingual gland is correct: they are numerous small ducts opening along the sublingual fold, and one larger duct may join the submandibular duct; the gland has a single duct at the sublingual papilla; it opens into the parotid duct; or it has no ducts? The answer is the first.

Question 6590. A described specimen shows a small gland in the floor of the mouth with many short ducts opening along a ridge beside the tongue, and one longer duct running forward to join the duct that opens beside the frenulum. Which gland is shown? The answer is the sublingual gland, with its ducts of Rivinus and its duct of Bartholin.

Question 6591. Besides the three pairs of major salivary glands, where else in the mouth are salivary glands found: in the lips, the cheeks, the palate and the tongue; only in the palate; only inside the parotid capsule; or only in the pharynx? The answer is in the lips, the cheeks, the palate and the tongue. These minor glands are numerous and mostly mucous, and they are scattered through the submucosa.

Question 6592. Which nerve supplies the secretomotor fibers to the sublingual gland, and in which ganglion do those fibers relay: the facial nerve and the submandibular ganglion, the glossopharyngeal nerve and the otic ganglion, the facial nerve and the otic ganglion, or the vagus nerve and the ciliary ganglion? The answer is the facial nerve and the submandibular ganglion. The fibers arrive by way of the chorda tympani and pass with the blood vessels to the gland.

Question 6593. A constructed illustration: a soft, bluish, fluctuant swelling appears in the floor of the mouth on one side, lifts the tongue, and grows larger at mealtimes. Which gland has leaked, and what is the collection called? The answer is the sublingual gland, and the collection is a ranula. Mucus escapes from the gland and collects above the mylohyoid. Its management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6594. A described specimen shows a gland in the floor of the mouth with no single main duct, whose secretion is thick and mainly mucous, and whose medial relations are the submandibular duct and the lingual nerve. Which gland is shown? The answer is the sublingual gland, lying above the mylohyoid against the sublingual fossa.

Question 6595. On which surface of the mandible does the sublingual gland rest, and above which structure does it lie? The answer is the sublingual fossa of the mandible, above the mylohyoid muscle. The fossa lies above the mylohyoid line on the inner surface of the body of the mandible, so the gland lies between bone and mucous membrane.

Question 6596. Which statement about the control of salivation is correct: parasympathetic stimulation produces a copious watery saliva and sympathetic stimulation a small volume of thicker saliva; sympathetic stimulation produces watery saliva; both produce identical saliva; or salivation is voluntary? The answer is the first. Salivation is a reflex, not a voluntary act.

Question 6597. Which of these pairs belongs together: the sublingual gland with the duct of Bartholin, the parotid gland with the ducts of Rivinus, the submandibular gland with the duct of Stensen, or the sublingual gland with the sublingual papilla? The answer is the sublingual gland with the duct of Bartholin. That larger duct may join the submandibular duct.

Question 6598. In a horizontal section through the floor of the mouth on one side, place the sublingual gland, the submandibular duct and the genioglossus muscle in order from the lateral side to the midline. The answer is the sublingual gland, then the submandibular duct with the lingual nerve, then the genioglossus. The gland lies laterally against the mandible.

Question 6599. A coronal section through the floor of the mouth on one side is described from below upward. Which structures does it cross between the mylohyoid muscle below and the tongue above? The answer is the sublingual gland and the mucous membrane of the floor of the mouth. The gland lies above the mylohyoid in the sublingual fossa and is covered only by mucous membrane.

Question 6600. Name the three pairs of major salivary glands, and state which of them has no single main duct. The answer is the parotid, the submandibular and the sublingual glands, and the sublingual gland has no single main duct. It opens by many small ducts along the sublingual fold, with one larger duct that may join the submandibular duct.

To summarize, a tooth is built of enamel over the crown, cementum over the root and dentin beneath both, with a pulp cavity at the center, and the adult has thirty-two permanent teeth. The lower teeth are supplied by the inferior alveolar nerve in the mandibular canal, the upper molars by the posterior superior alveolar nerve, and the roots of the upper molars lie close to the floor of the maxillary sinus. The parotid gland is the largest and purely serous, its duct crosses the masseter and opens opposite the upper second molar, and the facial nerve, the retromandibular vein and the external carotid artery pass through it. The submandibular gland hooks around the mylohyoid and its duct opens at the sublingual papilla, while the sublingual gland lies above the mylohyoid and opens by many small ducts along the sublingual fold. The submandibular and sublingual glands take their secretomotor fibers from the facial nerve through the chorda tympani, and the parotid gland takes its from the glossopharyngeal nerve through the otic ganglion.
