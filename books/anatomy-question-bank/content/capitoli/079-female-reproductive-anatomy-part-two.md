# Chapter 79 — Female Reproductive Anatomy, Part Two

This chapter takes up the lower part of the female reproductive system, working from the wall of the uterus outward to the surface of the body. It covers the myometrium, the cervix, the vagina, and the external genitalia, with their glands, their blood supply, and their nerves. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer in the same breath and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7801. Which layer of the uterine wall is the thick muscular coat that contracts during labor: the endometrium, the myometrium, the perimetrium, or the parametrium? The answer is the myometrium. It is the smooth muscle layer between the endometrium inside and the perimetrium outside. The parametrium is the fatty tissue beside the uterus and is not part of its wall.

Question 7802. What name is given to the smooth muscle layer that forms most of the wall of the uterus and enlarges greatly during pregnancy? The answer is the myometrium. It is built of bundles of smooth muscle separated by connective tissue. During pregnancy the fibers grow longer and thicker, and the whole organ becomes a large muscular sac.

Question 7803. Which layer of the uterine wall lies immediately deep to the perimetrium and immediately superficial to the endometrium? The answer is the myometrium. The wall has three coats: the serous perimetrium outside, the muscular myometrium in the middle, and the glandular endometrium inside. The myometrium is much the thickest of the three.

Question 7804. Which part of the uterus has the thickest myometrium: the fundus and body, the isthmus, the supravaginal cervix, or the vaginal cervix? The answer is the fundus and body. The muscle grows thinner toward the isthmus and is thinnest in the cervix. That is why the upper uterus contracts strongly in labor while the cervix must soften and open.

Question 7805. A described magnetic resonance image of the pelvis shows the uterus in section, with a thick band of intermediate signal around a thin bright central stripe. Which layer forms the thick band, and what is the bright stripe? The answer is the myometrium, with the endometrium as the bright stripe. The inner part of the muscle beside the lining is the junctional zone and looks darker.

Question 7806. Which muscle layer of the uterus is described as having three ill-defined coats, an outer longitudinal coat, a middle interlacing coat, and an inner coat around the openings of the uterine tubes? The answer is the myometrium. The three coats blend into one another rather than forming sharp planes.

Question 7807. True or false: the smooth muscle of the myometrium is arranged as a single flat sheet with all its fibers running in one direction. The answer is false. The fibers run in several directions and interlace, especially in the middle coat. That arrangement allows the whole wall to shorten in every direction at the same time.

Question 7808. During a described delivery the placenta separates, and bleeding from a wide placental site stops quickly. Which property of the myometrium explains this: secretion of mucus, interlacing fibers that compress the vessels, a ciliated lining, or elastic cartilage in the wall? The answer is its interlacing fibers, which compress the vessels. The middle coat crosses in a figure of eight around the arteries.

Question 7809. Name the two layers of the uterine wall that lie on either side of the myometrium, spoken as a pair. The answer is the endometrium inside and the perimetrium outside. The endometrium is the glandular lining that is shed at menstruation. The perimetrium is the serous coat reflected over the outer surface of the organ.

Question 7810. A described section cuts the uterus across its long axis, so that the cavity appears as a narrow slit and the wall as a ring around it. In which plane was the section cut, and which coat forms the outer edge of the ring? The answer is a transverse plane, with the perimetrium at the outer edge. A section across the long axis shows the cavity surrounded by the wall.

Question 7811. A described ultrasound image of a pregnant uterus shows a thick muscular wall with the placenta attached over the fundus. Which coat lies directly under the decidua at that site, and what must it do after delivery? The answer is the myometrium, which must contract and then retract to close the vessels of the placental site. It stretches and thins as pregnancy advances.

Question 7812. A constructed illustration: after a delivery the uterus stays soft and bleeding continues from the placental site. Which layer is failing to close the vessels, and by what action does it normally do so? The answer is the myometrium, which normally contracts and then retracts around the open arteries. Its interlacing fibers act as a living ligature. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7813. In which coat of the uterine wall do the arcuate arteries lie before their branches turn inward toward the lining: the endometrium, the perimetrium, the middle coat of the myometrium, or the parametrium? The answer is the middle coat of the myometrium. The arcuate arteries lie in the outer part of the muscle.

Question 7814. Which band of tissue lies immediately lateral to the body and the cervix of the uterus and carries the uterine artery across to the organ? The answer is the parametrium, the connective tissue of the broad ligament beside the uterus. The uterine artery crosses it to reach the uterus.

Question 7815. Which part of the myometrium lies next to the endometrium and appears as a narrow dark band on magnetic resonance imaging of the uterus? The answer is the junctional zone, the inner part of the myometrium. It shows slow waves of contraction during the cycle. The outer muscle gives a lighter signal and is not sharply separated from it.

Question 7816. Which statement about the myometrium is correct: it is skeletal muscle under voluntary control, it is smooth muscle under involuntary control, it is cardiac muscle, or it is a layer of dense bone? The answer is that it is smooth muscle under involuntary control. Its contractions are not governed by the will, and they continue after the nerves to the uterus are cut.

Question 7817. Which of these pairs belongs together: the myometrium with the glandular lining of the uterus, the myometrium with the muscular coat of the uterus, the perimetrium with the muscle, or the endometrium with the serous coat? The answer is the myometrium with the muscular coat of the uterus. The endometrium is the lining and the perimetrium is the serous coat.

Question 7818. A described specimen shows the uterus opened from the front, with a thick pale wall and a thin lining, and a probe passing through the wall into the cavity. Which coat does the probe cross last before it enters the cavity: the perimetrium, the myometrium, the parametrium, or the broad ligament? The answer is the myometrium. The wall is built as serosa, muscle, and lining, in that order.

Question 7819. What is the name of the outer serous coat of the uterus, formed by the peritoneum that covers the organ? The answer is the perimetrium. It covers the fundus and the body and is reflected onto the bladder in front. The cervix has no peritoneal covering.

Question 7820. The muscle of the myometrium is continuous with which of these structures: the skeletal muscle of the abdominal wall, the smooth muscle of the uterine tube, the cartilage of the pubic symphysis, or the fibrous tissue of the ovary? The answer is the smooth muscle of the uterine tube. Its fibers run into the wall of the uterus without a break.

Question 7821. A constructed illustration: a woman has heavy bleeding each month, and a scan shows a rounded mass within the uterine wall. Which layer has given rise to the mass, and why does the bleeding increase? The answer is the myometrium, whose smooth muscle cells form such masses. A mass near the lining distorts it and enlarges the surface shed each month. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7822. An operation removes the body of the uterus and leaves the cervix in place. Which part of the myometrium is left behind: the muscle of the fundus, the muscle of the isthmus, the small amount of muscle in the cervix, or no muscle at all? The answer is the small amount of muscle in the cervix. The cervix is mostly fibrous tissue with a little muscle.

Question 7823. Which term names the condition in which glandular tissue of the lining lies within the myometrium, with thickening of the muscle around it? The answer is adenomyosis. The islands of glandular tissue and their supporting stroma lie among the muscle fibers. The uterus is enlarged, and the wall feels firm and tender.

Question 7824. Which statement best explains why the wall of the uterus becomes firm and hard after the placenta separates: the muscle fibers stretch and lengthen, the muscle fibers retract and stay shorter, the lining swells, or the vessels fill with blood? The answer is that the muscle fibers retract and stay shorter. Contraction is followed by retraction, in which the fibers keep their shortened length.

Question 7825. A described magnetic resonance image of the uterus shows a bulge on the outer surface with the same signal as the myometrium, while the lining above it is smooth. Which layer has produced the bulge, and why is the lining undisturbed? The answer is the myometrium, whose cells form the mass. Because it grows outward near the serous surface, it does not distort the cavity.

Question 7826. Which part of the uterus is the narrow lower portion that projects into the vagina: the fundus, the body, the isthmus, or the cervix? The answer is the cervix. It lies below the isthmus and opens into the vagina at the external os. The fundus is the rounded upper part above the openings of the uterine tubes.

Question 7827. What is the name of the opening of the cervix into the vagina, seen at the center of the part that projects into the canal? The answer is the external os. In a woman who has not borne a child it is usually small and round.

Question 7828. Which band of tissue lies immediately lateral to the cervix and the upper vagina and carries the uterine vessels toward the uterus? The answer is the cardinal ligament, also called the transverse cervical ligament. It runs from the cervix and the upper vagina to the wall of the pelvis.

Question 7829. A described ultrasound image of the pelvis shows the uterus in the midline, with a narrow lower segment lying between the bladder and the rectum. Which part of the uterus is shown, and which peritoneal pouch lies behind it? The answer is the cervix, with the rectouterine pouch behind it. That pouch dips lower behind the cervix than the vesicouterine pouch does in front.

Question 7830. Which of these is the ordinary length of the adult cervix from the external os to the internal os: about 2.5 centimeters, about 8 centimeters, about 15 centimeters, or about 20 centimeters? The answer is about 2.5 centimeters. The cervix forms the lowest part of the uterus.

Question 7831. Which junction marks the place where the columnar epithelium of the cervical canal meets the stratified squamous epithelium of the outer surface? The answer is the squamocolumnar junction. The zone of change around it is called the transformation zone. That zone shifts with age and with pregnancy, moving out onto the vaginal surface and back again.

Question 7832. True or false: the canal of the cervix is lined by the same many-layered squamous epithelium as its outer surface. The answer is false. The canal carries a single layer of mucus-secreting columnar cells. The outer surface carries many layers of flat cells, which resist friction and stretch.

Question 7833. A constructed illustration: after a long labor the cervix is swollen and the base of the bladder is bruised. Which structure lies directly in front of the supravaginal cervix in that described region? The answer is the base of the bladder, separated from the cervix by loose connective tissue. Pressure of the presenting part can involve the bladder base and the urethra. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7834. Which statement about the mucus of the cervical canal is correct: it is thick and acid throughout the cycle, it becomes thin and plentiful near the middle of the cycle, it is made by the squamous cells, or it is absent during pregnancy? The answer is that it becomes thin and plentiful near the middle of the cycle. That change helps sperm pass through the canal.

Question 7835. A described view of the cervix shows a red, velvety ring of tissue around the external os, with paler tissue beyond it. Which epithelium covers the red ring, and why does it look different? The answer is columnar epithelium, which is a single layer and shows the vessels beneath. It has moved out from the canal, a change called eversion.

Question 7836. Name the two parts of the cervix described by their relation to the vagina, spoken as a pair. The answer is the supravaginal part and the vaginal part. The supravaginal part lies above the attachment of the vagina. The vaginal part, also called the portio vaginalis, projects into the canal and carries the external os.

Question 7837. Which ligament runs from the cervix backward around the sides of the rectum to the sacrum and helps to hold the uterus in place: the inguinal ligament, the uterosacral ligament, the suspensory ligament of the ovary, or the median umbilical ligament? The answer is the uterosacral ligament. It forms a fold on each side of the rectum.

Question 7838. Which peritoneal pouch lies immediately behind the cervix, and which part of the vagina is related to its lowest point? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, with the posterior fornix of the vagina. The pouch dips down between the cervix and the rectum. Fluid in the abdomen therefore collects low in that pouch when the woman is upright.

Question 7839. What is the name of the opening at the upper end of the cervical canal, where the canal joins the cavity of the uterus? The answer is the internal os. It lies at the level of the isthmus. The external os lies at the other end of the canal and opens into the vagina.

Question 7840. In the standing woman the cervix rests against the posterior wall of the vagina and points downward and backward. Which recess of the vagina therefore lies below the cervix: the anterior fornix, the posterior fornix, the urethral opening, or the hymen? The answer is the posterior fornix, the deepest recess of the vagina. It lies between the cervix and the posterior wall.

Question 7841. Which artery supplies the cervix, running down beside it from above: the ovarian artery, the descending branch of the uterine artery, the inferior epigastric artery, or the obturator artery? The answer is the descending branch of the uterine artery. It runs down beside the cervix and the upper vagina.

Question 7842. Which of these pairs belongs together: the external os with the cavity of the uterus, the internal os with the vaginal canal, the external os with the vaginal canal, or the internal os with the vulva? The answer is the external os with the vaginal canal. The canal runs between the internal os above and the external os below.

Question 7843. What is the name of the rounded part of the cervix that can be seen and felt projecting into the upper vagina? The answer is the portio vaginalis, the vaginal part of the cervix. It is covered by stratified squamous epithelium. Its central opening is the external os, and the recesses around it are the fornices.

Question 7844. A described specimen shows a cervix cut open from front to back, with a spindle-shaped canal and a ring of firm, pale tissue around it. Which tissue makes up most of that ring, and how does it differ from the wall above? The answer is dense fibrous tissue with only a little smooth muscle. The canal runs between the internal os and the external os.

Question 7845. Which statement about the lymphatic drainage of the cervix is correct: it drains only to the superficial inguinal nodes, it drains to the nodes along the internal and external iliac vessels and the obturator nerve, it drains to the axillary nodes, or it has no lymphatic drainage? The answer is that it drains to the iliac and obturator nodes. Some channels also run to the sacral nodes.

Question 7846. A constructed illustration: during labor the cervix is examined and feels soft, stretching rather than shortening. Which feature of its structure explains this behavior? The answer is that it is mostly fibrous tissue with little muscle, so it softens and opens instead of contracting. Its collagen and elastic fibers let it stretch. The muscular fundus above contracts and pulls the cervix upward. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7847. Which term names the shift of the squamocolumnar junction onto the outer surface of the cervix, seen as a red ring around the external os? The answer is eversion, also called ectropion. Columnar epithelium of the canal comes to lie on the vaginal surface. It is a normal finding in adolescence and during pregnancy.

Question 7848. The external os is a transverse slit in a woman who has borne children and round in one who has not. What does this difference depend on: the amount of muscle in the wall, the fibrous tissue and the changes of childbirth, the length of the canal, or the position of the uterus? The answer is the fibrous tissue and the changes of childbirth. Childbirth tears it, and it heals as a slit.

Question 7849. A described magnetic resonance image of the pelvis shows a spindle-shaped structure between the bladder and the rectum, with a narrow segment directly above it. Which part of the uterus is the spindle-shaped structure, and what is the narrow segment above it? The answer is the cervix, with the isthmus above it. The isthmus is about 1 centimeter long and joins the cervix to the body.

Question 7850. Why can the cervix be felt through the wall of the upper vagina on every side: it projects into the vagina with recesses around it, it lies behind the peritoneum, it is suspended by the round ligament, or it is fixed to the pubic bone? The answer is that it projects into the vagina with recesses around it. Those recesses are the fornices, deepest behind the cervix and shallowest in front.

Question 7851. Which of these is the ordinary length of the adult vagina, from the vestibule to the cervix: about 2 centimeters, about 7 to 9 centimeters, about 15 centimeters, or about 20 centimeters? The answer is about 7 to 9 centimeters. The canal runs upward and backward from the vestibule.

Question 7852. What name is given to a recess of the vagina that surrounds the cervix, deepest behind it and shallowest in front? The answer is a fornix. There are four of them: one in front, one behind, and one on each side. The posterior fornix is the deepest and lies next to the rectouterine pouch.

Question 7853. Which structure lies immediately in front of the lower part of the vagina, separated from it by connective tissue: the rectum, the anal canal, the urethra, or the sacrum? The answer is the urethra. It runs downward and forward in front of the vagina and opens into the vestibule.

Question 7854. A described cross section of the pelvis shows a flattened canal whose walls are in contact, with the urethra in front of it and the rectum behind it. Which canal is shown, and what holds its walls together at rest? The answer is the vagina, whose walls are in contact at rest. The canal therefore appears as a slit in section.

Question 7855. A constructed illustration: a pregnant woman has a bulge of the lower vagina in front, and the base of the bladder descends with it. Which wall of the vagina has lost its support? The answer is the anterior wall, which lies against the bladder and the urethra. The pubocervical tissue and the muscles of the pelvic floor support it. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7856. Which kind of epithelium lines the vagina: a single layer of columnar cells, many layers of stratified squamous cells, ciliated columnar cells, or transitional epithelium? The answer is many layers of stratified squamous cells. The surface is thick and moist and carries no keratin. The wall has no glands of its own.

Question 7857. What are the transverse ridges on the inner surface of the vaginal wall called? The answer is the rugae. They are most marked in the lower part of the canal and in women who have not borne children. They allow the canal to stretch.

Question 7858. True or false: the vagina has its own glands that produce the fluid which moistens it. The answer is false. The wall of the vagina has no glands at all. The moisture comes from mucus of the cervix and from fluid that seeps through the wall.

Question 7859. The upper part of the vagina is separated from the rectum behind it by which structure: the perineal body, the rectouterine pouch, the anal canal, or the levator ani? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, which lies between the upper vagina and the rectum. Lower down, the rectum and the anal canal lie behind the vagina.

Question 7860. Which two structures lie close to the lateral wall of the upper vagina and cross near its side? The answer is the ureter and the uterine artery. The artery crosses above the ureter close to the cervix. The levator ani lies lower down and farther lateral, beside the middle and lower canal.

Question 7861. Name the two layers of smooth muscle in the wall of the vagina, spoken as a pair. The answer is an inner circular layer and an outer longitudinal layer. They lie inside a coat of connective tissue rich in elastic fibers. Those fibers and the folds of the lining allow the canal to stretch.

Question 7862. A described specimen shows a canal with a pale ridged lining, a layer of muscle outside it, and a rich network of veins beyond that. Which structure is shown, and which feature explains how it can stretch so greatly? The answer is the vagina, whose lining is folded into transverse ridges. The ridges and the elastic fibers allow the canal to widen.

Question 7863. Which statement about the nerve supply of the vagina is correct: the whole canal is supplied by the pudendal nerve, the upper part by autonomic nerves and the lower part by the pudendal nerve, the whole canal by the sciatic nerve, or the canal has none? The answer is the upper part supplied by autonomic nerves and the lower part by the pudendal nerve. The lower third is the sensitive part.

Question 7864. What is the name of the thin fold of membrane at the entrance of the vagina, which may be ring-shaped or have a fringed edge? The answer is the hymen. It lies at the junction of the vagina and the vestibule. Its remains form small tags around the opening in the adult.

Question 7865. A constructed illustration: a woman has an opening in the front wall of the vagina that lies just behind the base of the bladder. Which wall is involved, and which two structures lie in front of it? The answer is the anterior wall, with the bladder base and the urethra in front of it. That wall is closely related to both. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7866. Which of these pairs belongs together: the upper vagina with the superficial inguinal nodes, the lower vagina with the superficial inguinal nodes, the upper vagina with the axillary nodes, or the lower vagina with the sacral nodes? The answer is the lower vagina with the superficial inguinal nodes. Lymph from the lower third passes to the groin.

Question 7867. Which structure lies behind the lowest part of the vagina, separated from it by the perineal body: the rectum, the anal canal, the sacrum, or the coccyx? The answer is the anal canal. The perineal body lies between the lower vagina and the anal canal. Higher up, the rectum lies behind the vagina with only a thin septum between them.

Question 7868. Which structure lies immediately behind the middle part of the vagina, with only a thin sheet of connective tissue between the two? The answer is the rectum. The rectovaginal septum separates them. Above it the rectouterine pouch lies behind the upper vagina, and below it the anal canal lies behind the lowest part.

Question 7869. What is the name of the fluid found in the vagina of a woman of reproductive age, which is acidic? The answer is the vaginal fluid. It comes from the wall and from the cervix, because the wall has no glands of its own. Its acidity discourages the growth of many organisms.

Question 7870. A described sagittal section of the female pelvis shows a canal running upward and backward from the outside to a rounded structure that projects into its upper end. At what angle does the canal lie, and which structure projects into it? The answer is about 45 degrees, with the cervix projecting into the upper end. The canal is the vagina.

Question 7871. Which statement about the support of the vagina is correct: it hangs from the cervix alone, it is supported by the levator ani, the perineal body, and the connective tissue of the pelvis, it is supported by the round ligament, or it rests on the pubic bone? The answer is the levator ani, the perineal body, and the connective tissue of the pelvis. The cardinal and uterosacral ligaments also hold it.

Question 7872. A constructed illustration: a woman who has borne several children reports a feeling of something coming down, and the lower vagina and the cervix lie outside the body. Which supports have failed? The answer is the levator ani, the perineal body, and the connective tissue of the pelvis, with the cardinal and uterosacral ligaments above. The vaginal wall loses its folds and descends. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7873. Which part of the vaginal wall receives its nerve supply from the pudendal nerve, and what follows from that arrangement? The answer is the lower third, which is the part sensitive to pain and to touch. The upper two thirds are supplied by autonomic nerves. A cut or a stretch high in the canal is felt far less than one low down.

Question 7874. The posterior fornix of the vagina lies next to which structure, and why does that relation matter: the bladder, the rectouterine pouch, the urethra, or the symphysis pubis? The answer is the rectouterine pouch. Its lowest part lies against the posterior fornix. Fluid in the abdominal cavity gathers there and can be felt through the vaginal wall.

Question 7875. A described magnetic resonance image of the pelvis shows fluid filling a pouch that lies between the uterus above and the rectum below and bulging into a recess of the vagina. Which recess is that, and which wall of the vagina is nearest the fluid? The answer is the posterior fornix, with the posterior wall of the vagina nearest. The pouch is the rectouterine pouch, the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity.

Question 7876. What is the name of the rounded pad of fat and skin covered with hair that lies over the pubic symphysis in the female? The answer is the mons pubis. It lies in front of the pubic bones. The labia majora lie on either side below it, and the pubic hair in the female forms a triangle with a flat upper border.

Question 7877. Which of these structures is homologous to the scrotum of the male: the mons pubis, the labia majora, the labia minora, or the clitoris? The answer is the labia majora. They are the two folds of skin and fat that bound the vulva on either side.

Question 7878. A described photograph shows two outer folds of skin bearing hair and two inner folds without hair, meeting in front around a small rounded structure. Which are the folds, and what is the rounded structure? The answer is the labia majora outside and the labia minora inside, with the clitoris in front. The labia minora split around the clitoris to form its prepuce and frenulum.

Question 7879. Which structure lies immediately beneath the skin on either side of the vaginal opening and deep to the bulbospongiosus muscle? The answer is the bulb of the vestibule, a mass of erectile tissue. One lies on each side of the orifice. It corresponds to the bulb of the penis in the male.

Question 7880. A constructed illustration: a woman has a painful swelling in the lower part of one labium majus, behind the vaginal opening, at the groove between the hymen and the labium. Which gland is involved, and where does its duct open? The answer is the greater vestibular gland, whose duct opens in that groove. The gland lies deep to the bulbospongiosus muscle. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7881. What is the name of the small erectile organ of the female that lies in front of the vestibule and is covered by a prepuce? The answer is the clitoris. It has a body and two crura that are attached to the pubic bone. Its glans is the most sensitive part of the external genitalia.

Question 7882. True or false: the clitoris is made of erectile tissue and corresponds to the penis. The answer is true. Its crura and its body are erectile, and its glans corresponds to the glans of the penis. The prepuce formed by the labia minora corresponds to the prepuce of the penis.

Question 7883. Which of these pairs belongs together: the labia majora with the scrotum, the labia majora with the penis, the clitoris with the scrotum, or the mons pubis with the testis? The answer is the labia majora with the scrotum. Both arise from the same pair of folds in the embryo.

Question 7884. Which of these structures opens into the vestibule: the glans of the clitoris, the external urethral opening, the mons pubis, or the anus? The answer is the external urethral opening. The vestibule is the space between the labia minora. The vaginal opening and the ducts of the greater vestibular glands also open into it.

Question 7885. A described dissection of the perineum shows a triangular space bounded behind by a line between the two ischial tuberosities, with two openings within it. Which triangle is shown, and which openings lie in it? The answer is the urogenital triangle, which contains the urethral and vaginal openings. The anal triangle lies behind that line and contains the anus.

Question 7886. A constructed illustration: after a delivery a described tear runs from the vaginal opening backward toward the anus and involves the tissue between them. Which structure is torn, and which muscles meet there? The answer is the perineal body, where the bulbospongiosus, the transverse perineal muscles, the external anal sphincter, and the levator ani meet. Damage there can weaken the pelvic floor. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7887. Which nerve supplies most of the external genitalia, including the labia and the clitoris: the femoral nerve, the pudendal nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the obturator nerve? The answer is the pudendal nerve. It arises from the second, third, and fourth sacral segments of the spinal cord.

Question 7888. Which nerves supply the front of the labium majus, while branches of the pudendal nerve supply the rest of the vulva? The answer is the ilioinguinal nerve and the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve. They reach the front of the vulva from the abdomen. The pudendal nerve supplies the posterior parts, including the clitoris.

Question 7889. What is the name of the paired pea-sized glands that lie on either side of the vaginal opening and produce a mucus-like fluid? The answer is the greater vestibular glands. Each lies deep to the bulbospongiosus muscle, behind the bulb of the vestibule. The corresponding gland in the male is the bulbourethral gland.

Question 7890. True or false: the labia minora carry hair on their outer surface. The answer is false. The labia minora have no hair and almost no fat beneath the skin. They carry many sebaceous glands on both surfaces, and those glands keep the skin moist. Hair grows only on the labia majora and the mons pubis.

Question 7891. Which artery is the chief supply of the external genitalia: the femoral artery directly, the internal pudendal artery, the obturator artery, or the inferior epigastric artery? The answer is the internal pudendal artery. It arises from the internal iliac artery and runs in the pudendal canal.

Question 7892. Which of these pairs belongs together: the labia minora with the deep inguinal nodes, the clitoris with the superficial inguinal nodes, the labia majora with the superficial inguinal nodes, or the vestibule with the para-aortic nodes? The answer is the labia majora with the superficial inguinal nodes. The external genitalia drain mainly to the groin.

Question 7893. A described specimen of the female perineum shows a thin triangular sheet stretched between the two sides of the pubic arch, with the vaginal opening below it. What is this sheet, and which pouch lies just beneath it? The answer is the perineal membrane, with the superficial perineal pouch beneath it. That pouch contains the crura of the clitoris and the bulbs of the vestibule.

Question 7894. What is the name of the opening of the female urethra, which lies in the vestibule? The answer is the external urethral orifice. It lies in front of the vaginal opening and behind the clitoris. The urethra behind it is short and runs downward and forward.

Question 7895. A constructed illustration: after a fall onto a bar, a woman has a large, dark, tender swelling in one labium majus. Which tissue has bled, and which vessels supply it? The answer is the loose tissue of the labium and the bulb of the vestibule, supplied by branches of the internal pudendal artery. That loose tissue holds a large collection of blood. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7896. Which statement about the venous drainage of the clitoris is correct: it drains only to the superficial inguinal veins, it drains by a deep dorsal vein that joins the vesical plexus, it drains to the portal vein, or it has no venous drainage? The answer is that a deep dorsal vein drains the clitoris into the vesical plexus. The veins of the labia drain to the internal pudendal vein.

Question 7897. Which muscle lies immediately over the bulb of the vestibule and compresses it? The answer is the bulbospongiosus. It covers the bulb on each side of the vaginal opening and lies in the superficial perineal pouch. The ischiocavernosus covers the crus of the clitoris instead.

Question 7898. During development the labia majora and the scrotum arise from the same pair of folds. Which adult structure of the female is therefore homologous to the scrotum: the mons pubis, the labia majora, the labia minora, or the perineal body? The answer is the labia majora. Both arise from the labioscrotal folds.

Question 7899. What name is given to the small fold formed where the two labia minora meet behind the vaginal opening? The answer is the frenulum of the labia, also called the fourchette. It lies in the midline behind the vaginal orifice. It is often torn during a first childbirth.

Question 7900. Which of these statements about the nerve supply of the clitoris is correct: its glans is supplied by the ilioinguinal nerve, its glans is supplied by the dorsal nerve of the clitoris, its glans is supplied by the obturator nerve, or its glans has no nerve supply? The answer is that its glans is supplied by the dorsal nerve of the clitoris. That nerve is a branch of the pudendal nerve.

To summarize, the myometrium is the thick smooth muscle coat of the uterus, built in interlacing bundles that retract after delivery and act as a living ligature on the vessels, and it is thickest in the fundus and thinnest in the cervix. The cervix is a mostly fibrous organ about 2.5 centimeters long, with a columnar-lined canal that meets squamous epithelium at the squamocolumnar junction. The vagina is a fibromuscular canal about 7 to 9 centimeters long, lined by stratified squamous epithelium without glands, with four fornices around the cervix, the posterior one lying against the rectouterine pouch, and the lower third draining to the superficial inguinal nodes. The external genitalia comprise the mons pubis, the labia majora and minora, the clitoris, the vestibule with the greater vestibular glands, and the bulbs of the vestibule, supplied mainly by the internal pudendal artery and the pudendal nerve.
