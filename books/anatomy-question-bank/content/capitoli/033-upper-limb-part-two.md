# Chapter 33 — Upper Limb, Part Two

This chapter takes the muscles of the hand and then the muscles of the pelvis and the hip. The first fifty questions belong to the hand, from the thenar and hypothenar groups through the lumbricals and the interossei to the tendons, the fibrous bands and the nerves that reach them. The second fifty belong to the pelvis and the hip, where the gluteal muscles, the short lateral rotators, the adductors and the muscles of the pelvic floor are set out in turn. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind until the end. The chapter is educational material and does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3201. Which muscle of the hand draws the thumb across the palm so that its pad meets the pads of the fingers: the abductor pollicis brevis, the opponens pollicis, the adductor pollicis, or the flexor pollicis brevis? The answer is the opponens pollicis. It lies deep to the short muscles of the thumb and rolls the first metacarpal toward the palm.

Question 3202. Which small muscle forms the rounded pad at the base of the little finger and pulls that finger away from the others? The answer is the abductor digiti minimi. It lies most superficially in the hypothenar eminence and abducts the fifth finger. The flexor digiti minimi brevis lies beside it, and the opponens digiti minimi lies deepest.

Question 3203. Which muscle lies in the skin of the palm and wrinkles it when the hand grips: the palmaris brevis, the palmaris longus, the first lumbrical, or the first dorsal interosseous? The answer is the palmaris brevis. It is a thin sheet just beneath the skin over the hypothenar eminence, the only muscle of the hand that moves the skin rather than a joint.

Question 3204. Which intrinsic muscles of the hand can flex the knuckles and straighten the joints between the knuckles and the fingertips at the same time? The answer is the lumbrical muscles. Four of them arise from the tendons of the flexor digitorum profundus and pass to the extensor expansions. The interossei share this action but also move the fingers sideways.

Question 3205. At the wrist, which structure passes through the carpal tunnel: the ulnar nerve, the tendon of the flexor carpi ulnaris, the median nerve, or the radial artery? The answer is the median nerve. It runs beneath the flexor retinaculum with the tendons of the deep and superficial flexors of the fingers.

Question 3206. Which muscle forms the fleshy mass at the base of the thumb and lies closest to the skin of the three short muscles there? The answer is the abductor pollicis brevis. It lies immediately deep to the skin and the palmar aponeurosis. The opponens pollicis and the flexor pollicis brevis lie deep to the abductor.

Question 3207. Which muscle lies in the first web space and is tested by pinching the thumb and the index finger together: the first dorsal interosseous, the adductor pollicis, the second palmar interosseous, or the first lumbrical? The answer is the first dorsal interosseous. It arises from the sides of the first two metacarpals that face each other and abducts the index finger. The adductor pollicis lies deeper in the same space.

Question 3208. A described dissection of the palm lifts the skin and the palmar aponeurosis and turns aside the tendons of the superficial flexor. A muscle with two bellies lies in the first web space. Which muscle is shown, and what does it do? The answer is the first dorsal interosseous, which abducts the index finger. The second dorsal interosseous lies in the second space and abducts the middle finger.

Question 3209. Into which structure do the tendons of the extensor digitorum, the lumbricals and the interossei all insert at the fingers: the palmar aponeurosis, the extensor expansion, the flexor retinaculum, or the deep transverse metacarpal ligament? The answer is the extensor expansion. It is a triangular sheet on the back of each finger, and its central slip reaches the base of the middle phalanx.

Question 3210. Which short muscle of the hand arises from the hook of the hamate and the flexor retinaculum and draws the fifth metacarpal forward? The answer is the opponens digiti minimi. It rotates the fifth metacarpal toward the palm, so the little finger can be cupped against the ring finger. The abductor digiti minimi arises partly from the pisiform instead.

Question 3211. Which muscle of the hand adducts the thumb toward the index finger and lies in the deepest plane of the first web space: the adductor pollicis, the abductor pollicis brevis, the opponens pollicis, or the first lumbrical? The answer is the adductor pollicis. It has an oblique head from the capitate and a transverse head from the third metacarpal, and it is supplied by the ulnar nerve.

Question 3212. A described radiograph of a hand shows two small rounded bones lying side by side in front of the joint at the base of the thumb. Which structures are shown, and in which tendons do they lie? The answer is the sesamoid bones of the thumb, which lie in the tendons of the flexor pollicis brevis and the adductor pollicis. They cushion the tendon as it crosses the joint.

Question 3213. Which nerve supplies all of the thenar muscles except one: the ulnar nerve, the radial nerve, the median nerve, or the posterior interosseous nerve? The answer is the median nerve. Its recurrent branch runs to the abductor pollicis brevis, the flexor pollicis brevis and the opponens pollicis. The deep head of the flexor pollicis brevis usually follows the ulnar nerve.

Question 3214. Which muscle of the forearm sends tendons to the middle phalanges of the four fingers and bends the fingers at the joint just above the tip? The answer is the flexor digitorum superficialis. Each of its tendons splits to let the deeper tendon pass through. Its partner, the flexor digitorum profundus, bends the joint at the tip itself.

Question 3215. Which muscles abduct the fingers away from the middle finger: the palmar interossei, the dorsal interossei, the lumbricals, or the hypothenar muscles? The answer is the dorsal interossei, of which there are four. The palmar interossei adduct the fingers toward the middle finger instead.

Question 3216. A described transverse section through the palm shows a flat muscle lying just beneath the skin on the little finger side, with a deeper muscle beneath it. Which two muscles are shown, and what does the deeper one do? The answer is the abductor digiti minimi and the opponens digiti minimi, which draws the fifth metacarpal forward and rotates it toward the palm.

Question 3217. Which muscle gives origin to the lumbrical muscles: the flexor digitorum superficialis, the flexor digitorum profundus, the extensor digitorum, or the interossei? The answer is the flexor digitorum profundus. The four lumbricals arise from its tendons in the palm and pass to the extensor expansions.

Question 3218. Which structure lies immediately superficial to the flexor retinaculum at the wrist: the ulnar artery, the median nerve, the ulnar nerve, or the tendon of the flexor digitorum profundus? The answer is the ulnar nerve. It crosses the front of the retinaculum on the little finger side, together with the ulnar artery, and divides there into its superficial and deep branches.

Question 3219. Which tendon forms the medial border of the hollow on the back of the hand at the base of the thumb: the extensor pollicis brevis, the abductor pollicis longus, the extensor pollicis longus, or the extensor indicis? The answer is the extensor pollicis longus. The abductor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis form the lateral border.

Question 3220. Which band of fibrous tissue holds the tendons of the long flexor muscles against the front of the wrist bones and is divided in the operation for carpal tunnel syndrome? The answer is the flexor retinaculum. It stretches between the tubercle of the scaphoid and the trapezium on the outer side and the pisiform and the hook of the hamate on the inner side.

Question 3221. Which structure separates the tendon of the extensor digitorum from that of the extensor carpi ulnaris on the back of the wrist: the extensor expansion, the flexor retinaculum, the extensor retinaculum with its septa, or the interosseous membrane? The answer is the extensor retinaculum, whose septa divide the back of the wrist into six compartments. Each tendon passes under its own sheath.

Question 3222. A constructed illustration: after several weeks of walking with crutches under the arms, a person cannot straighten the wrist and the back of the hand between the thumb and the index finger feels numb. Which nerve has been compressed? The answer is the radial nerve, which lies close to the bone in the armpit and then in the spiral groove. Its branches there straighten the wrist and the fingers.

Question 3223. A described specimen of the palm shows a thick triangular sheet spreading from the wrist to the bases of the four fingers. Which structure is shown, and which condition draws the ring and little fingers into the palm when it thickens? The answer is the palmar aponeurosis, whose thickening lies behind the contracture described by Dupuytren. Its cords tether the skin and draw in the ring finger and the little finger.

Question 3224. Which structures lie immediately deep to the palmar aponeurosis in the middle of the palm: the lumbrical muscles, the tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis, the palmar interossei, or the median nerve? The answer is the tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis. They lie directly beneath the aponeurosis, and the deep palmar arch and the deep branch of the ulnar nerve lie deeper still, against the metacarpals.

Question 3225. Which muscles are paralysed when the ulnar nerve is divided at the wrist: the thenar muscles, the first and second lumbricals, the interossei and the medial two lumbricals, or the long flexors of the fingers? The answer is the interossei and the medial two lumbricals. The little finger and the ring finger are then pulled straight at the knuckles and bent beyond them, which is the claw hand.

Question 3226. Which hand muscle arises only from the radial side of the tendon to the index finger, has a single belly, and straightens that finger at its two joints beyond the knuckle? The answer is the first lumbrical. The second lumbrical is also unipennate, while the third and the fourth take origin from two adjacent tendons.

Question 3227. A constructed illustration: a person wakes at night with tingling in the thumb, the index finger and the middle finger, and the fleshy pad at the base of the thumb is wasted. Which structure is compressed, and where? The answer is the median nerve, beneath the flexor retinaculum at the wrist. The ulnar nerve lies outside that tunnel, so the little finger is spared.

Question 3228. A described radiograph shows a break through the neck of the fifth metacarpal with the head of the bone tipped toward the palm. Which muscles produced that tilt? The answer is the short muscles of the hand that cross the joint, together with the flexor digiti minimi brevis and the interossei.

Question 3229. True or false: the lumbrical muscles of the hand have no bony attachment at all. The answer is true. They arise from the tendons of the flexor digitorum profundus and insert into the extensor expansions, so each one runs from tendon to tendon. The interossei, by contrast, arise from the metacarpal bones.

Question 3230. Which structure runs in the shallow groove between the pisiform bone and the hook of the hamate at the wrist: the median nerve, the ulnar nerve, the radial artery, or the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis? The answer is the ulnar nerve, with the ulnar artery beside it. The groove is roofed by a band of fibrous tissue, and the nerve divides inside it.

Question 3231. Which structure passes through the split in the tendon of the flexor digitorum superficialis: the median nerve, the tendon of the flexor digitorum profundus, the first lumbrical, or the palmar aponeurosis? The answer is the tendon of the flexor digitorum profundus. The split lets the deeper tendon reach the base of the distal phalanx.

Question 3232. Which muscle of the thenar eminence has two heads, one from the flexor retinaculum and the trapezium and one from the capitate, and bends the thumb at its first joint? The answer is the flexor pollicis brevis. Its superficial head is supplied by the median nerve and its deep head usually by the ulnar nerve.

Question 3233. A constructed illustration: after a cut on the front of the forearm, a person cannot bend the joint at the tip of the thumb or of the index finger, and the skin feels normal. Which branch has been injured? The answer is the anterior interosseous branch of the median nerve. It supplies the flexor pollicis longus and the lateral half of the flexor digitorum profundus, and it carries no skin fibers.

Question 3234. A described dissection of a finger shows a triangular sheet on its back whose central part reaches the middle phalanx and whose side bands pass to the distal phalanx. Which structure is shown, and which muscles pull on it? The answer is the extensor expansion, pulled by the extensor digitorum, the lumbricals and the interossei.

Question 3235. True or false: the thenar muscles and the hypothenar muscles are supplied by the same nerve. The answer is false. The thenar muscles are supplied mainly by the median nerve and the hypothenar muscles by the ulnar nerve. The two groups therefore fail separately after a nerve injury.

Question 3236. Name two muscles of the hand that are supplied by the ulnar nerve and that act on the thumb, spoken as a list. The answer is the adductor pollicis and the deep head of the flexor pollicis brevis. The superficial head of the flexor pollicis brevis belongs to the median nerve instead, and the deep head varies.

Question 3237. Which structure lies immediately deep to the palmar aponeurosis in the middle of the palm: the palmaris brevis, the flexor retinaculum, the tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis, or the median nerve? The answer is the tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis. They lie directly beneath the aponeurosis, and the deep palmar arch and the deep branch of the ulnar nerve lie against the metacarpals below them.

Question 3238. Which movement of the thumb is lost when the median nerve is cut at the wrist: pressing the thumb toward the index finger, drawing the thumb across the palm to meet the fingers, straightening the thumb at its last joint, or bending the thumb at the joint nearest its tip? The answer is drawing the thumb across the palm, which is opposition. The opponens pollicis is a thenar muscle, so it is lost.

Question 3239. Which of these pairs belongs together: the abductor pollicis brevis with the ulnar nerve, the first dorsal interosseous with the median nerve, the opponens pollicis with the median nerve, or the adductor pollicis with the radial nerve? The answer is the opponens pollicis with the median nerve, through its recurrent branch. The first dorsal interosseous and the adductor pollicis are supplied by the ulnar nerve.

Question 3240. Which muscle of the forearm sends its tendon to the index finger alone and helps to straighten that finger? The answer is the extensor indicis. It lies deep to the extensor digitorum, and its tendon runs beside that of the extensor digitorum on the back of the index finger. It occupies its own compartment beneath the extensor retinaculum.

Question 3241. A constructed illustration: a person is asked to hold a sheet of paper between the thumb and the side of the index finger, and the tip of the thumb curls instead of pressing flat. Which muscle is weak, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the adductor pollicis, supplied by the ulnar nerve. The flexor pollicis longus takes over and bends the tip of the thumb.

Question 3242. A described specimen shows two fibrous tunnels, one on the little finger and one on the thumb, each running without a break from the wrist into the finger. Which sheaths are shown, and where does infection from them spread? The answer is the synovial sheaths of the little finger and the thumb, which are continuous with the bursae of the palm.

Question 3243. True or false: the palmar interossei and the dorsal interossei are both supplied by the median nerve. The answer is false. All of the interossei are supplied by the deep branch of the ulnar nerve. They adduct and abduct the fingers, and they also help the lumbricals to straighten the joints near the fingertips.

Question 3244. In a hand held with the palm facing forward, does the thumb lie in the plane of the palm or in a plane at right angles to it? The answer is that the thumb lies in a plane at right angles to the palm. The first metacarpal is rotated in that way, so the pad of the thumb faces the pads of the other fingers and can meet them.

Question 3245. Name two of the three short muscles that form the fleshy pad at the base of the little finger, spoken as a list. The answer is the abductor digiti minimi and the flexor digiti minimi brevis, with the opponens digiti minimi as the third. All three are supplied by the ulnar nerve, and the abductor is the most superficial of them.

Question 3246. Which structure lies immediately in front of the tendons of the flexor digitorum profundus within the carpal tunnel: the median nerve, the ulnar artery, the radial artery, or the tendon of the flexor pollicis longus? The answer is the median nerve. It lies against the deep surface of the flexor retinaculum, in front of the flexor tendons.

Question 3247. Which movement do the palmar interossei produce: abduction of the fingers away from the middle finger, adduction of the fingers toward the middle finger, rotation of the thumb, or extension of the wrist? The answer is adduction of the fingers toward the middle finger.

Question 3248. Which of these pairs belongs together: the flexor retinaculum with the ulnar nerve, the extensor retinaculum with the flexor tendons, the flexor retinaculum with the median nerve, or the extensor expansion with the lumbrical muscles? The answer is the flexor retinaculum with the median nerve, which passes beneath it in the carpal tunnel.

Question 3249. Which muscle sends its tendon to the little finger alone, lies in its own compartment beneath the extensor retinaculum, and straightens that finger? The answer is the extensor digiti minimi. It runs beside the extensor digitorum and is supplied by the deep branch of the radial nerve.

Question 3250. A constructed illustration: a cut across the front of the wrist on the little finger side opens the groove between the pisiform and the hook of the hamate, and the ring finger and the little finger later become clawed. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the ulnar nerve. Its branches to the interossei and the medial two lumbricals are lost, while the long flexors still bend the fingers.

Question 3251. Which muscle extends the hip and turns the thigh outward and is the strongest extensor of that joint: the gluteus medius, the gluteus maximus, the piriformis, or the iliopsoas? The answer is the gluteus maximus. It acts most strongly when the hip is already bent, as in climbing stairs or rising from a low chair.

Question 3252. Which muscle arises from the outer surface of the ilium between the front and the back gluteal lines and is the chief abductor of the hip? The answer is the gluteus medius. When it is paralysed the pelvis drops on the opposite side as the person stands on the affected leg. It also turns the thigh inward.

Question 3253. Which structure leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis muscle: the superior gluteal nerve, the sciatic nerve, the obturator nerve, or the femoral nerve? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It passes out beneath the piriformis with the inferior gluteal vessels. The superior gluteal nerve leaves above that muscle.

Question 3254. Which muscle arises from the front of the sacrum, leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, and turns the thigh outward? The answer is the piriformis. It crosses the back of the hip joint to reach the upper border of the greater trochanter, and the sciatic nerve emerges below its lower border.

Question 3255. Which muscle arises from the front of the iliac crest, tightens the iliotibial band, and helps to steady the pelvis on the hip: the sartorius, the tensor fasciae latae, the gluteus minimus, or the adductor longus? The answer is the tensor fasciae latae. It abducts and turns the thigh inward, and it works with the gluteus medius.

Question 3256. A described dissection of the buttock shows a large muscle rising from the back of the ilium, the sacrum and the sacrotuberous ligament and inserting into the ridge on the back of the femur and into the iliotibial band. Which muscle is shown, and which movement does it produce? The answer is the gluteus maximus, which extends the hip and turns the thigh outward. Its lower fibers reach the iliotibial band.

Question 3257. Which nerve supplies the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus: the inferior gluteal nerve, the superior gluteal nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the obturator nerve? The answer is the superior gluteal nerve. It enters the buttock above the piriformis, so it survives an injury to the sciatic nerve below.

Question 3258. Which muscle is the chief flexor of the hip and is formed by the union of two muscles from the lumbar vertebrae and the inner surface of the ilium? The answer is the iliopsoas. It passes beneath the inguinal ligament to reach the lesser trochanter of the femur.

Question 3259. Which muscle of the medial side of the thigh is the most superficial there and is the only one that crosses both the hip and the knee: the adductor longus, the gracilis, the pectineus, or the adductor magnus? The answer is the gracilis. It arises from the pubic bone, runs straight down the inner thigh, and inserts on the inner surface of the upper tibia.

Question 3260. A described dissection of the buttock reflects the gluteus maximus and shows a pair of small muscles lying above and below the tendon of a muscle that turns around the edge of the lesser sciatic foramen. Which muscles are shown? The answer is the superior and the inferior gemellus, with the obturator internus between them. All three turn the thigh outward.

Question 3261. Which nerve supplies most of the muscles that adduct the thigh: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the superior gluteal nerve? The answer is the obturator nerve. It leaves the pelvis through the obturator foramen and enters the medial compartment of the thigh. The pectineus usually takes its supply from the femoral nerve instead.

Question 3262. Which muscle arises from the outer surface of the obturator membrane and the bone around it, passes behind the neck of the femur, and turns the thigh outward? The answer is the obturator externus. It inserts into the pit at the base of the greater trochanter and is supplied by the obturator nerve.

Question 3263. Which muscle lies deepest of the three gluteal muscles and both abducts and turns the thigh inward: the gluteus maximus, the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus, or the piriformis? The answer is the gluteus minimus. It is covered by the gluteus medius and shares its nerve. The two together hold the pelvis level when the opposite foot is lifted.

Question 3264. A described cross section through the middle of the thigh shows three compartments separated by sheets of deep fascia, with a large nerve and vessel in the middle of the back compartment. Which compartment is shown, and which muscles lie in it? The answer is the posterior compartment, which holds the hamstring muscles.

Question 3265. Which group of muscles lies in the back of the thigh, arises from the ischial tuberosity, and both extends the hip and bends the knee: the quadriceps, the adductors, the hamstrings, or the gluteal muscles? The answer is the hamstrings. The group is made up of the semitendinosus, the semimembranosus and the two heads of the biceps femoris.

Question 3266. Which sheet of muscle forms the floor of the pelvis, arising from the back of the pubis and from the fascia over the obturator internus and meeting its fellow in the midline? The answer is the levator ani. It supports the pelvic organs and resists any rise in the pressure inside the abdomen.

Question 3267. Which fibrous node lies in the midline of the perineum between the urogenital triangle and the anal canal and gives attachment to several muscles: the perineal body, the ischial spine, the sacrotuberous ligament, or the arcuate line? The answer is the perineal body. The bulbospongiosus, the external anal sphincter and the deep transverse perineal muscles all meet there.

Question 3268. A described dissection from inside the pelvis shows a broad muscular sheet sloping downward and backward to the coccyx, with an opening in front through which the urethra passes. Which muscle is shown, and which structures pass through the opening? The answer is the levator ani, and the opening carries the urethra and, in the female, the vagina.

Question 3269. Which structure runs down the outer side of the thigh from the iliac crest to the outer side of the tibia and receives the pull of two muscles: the inguinal ligament, the iliotibial band, the sacrotuberous ligament, or the linea aspera? The answer is the iliotibial band. The tensor fasciae latae and the gluteus maximus both pull on it.

Question 3270. Which muscle lies immediately behind the hip joint and is pierced by the sciatic nerve in some people: the gluteus minimus, the piriformis, the quadratus femoris, or the obturator internus? The answer is the piriformis. The nerve usually passes below it, but in a minority of people the nerve divides and one part runs through the muscle, which is a normal variation.

Question 3271. Which muscle lies lowest of the short lateral rotators of the hip, running from the outer edge of the ischial tuberosity to the ridge between the trochanters: the piriformis, the quadratus femoris, the obturator externus, or the gluteus minimus? The answer is the quadratus femoris. It turns the thigh outward and is supplied by a small branch of the sacral plexus.

Question 3272. Which long ribbon-shaped muscle arises from the front of the upper iliac spine and runs down and inward across the thigh to the inner side of the knee? The answer is the sartorius, the longest muscle in the body. It flexes and abducts the hip and turns the thigh outward, and it also bends the knee.

Question 3273. A constructed illustration: a person stands on the right leg and the left side of the pelvis drops, while the trunk leans to the right. Which muscle group is weak, and which nerve has failed? The answer is the abductors of the right hip, chiefly the gluteus medius, with the superior gluteal nerve. This is the sign described by Trendelenburg.

Question 3274. A described radiograph of the upper end of the femur shows a rounded head on a narrow neck, with a large blunt prominence on the outer side where the neck meets the shaft. Which structure is shown, and which muscles are attached to it? The answer is the greater trochanter, where the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus and the piriformis are attached.

Question 3275. Which muscle of the front of the thigh is the only one of the quadriceps group that also crosses the hip joint: the vastus lateralis, the rectus femoris, the vastus medialis, or the vastus intermedius? The answer is the rectus femoris. It arises from the front of the lower iliac spine and bends the hip as well as straightening the knee.

Question 3276. Which muscle arises from the inner surface of the obturator membrane and the bone around it, leaves the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen, and turns the thigh outward: the piriformis, the obturator internus, the quadratus femoris, or the gluteus maximus? The answer is the obturator internus. Its tendon crosses the back of the hip joint between the two gemelli.

Question 3277. Which nerve leaves the pelvis below the piriformis and supplies the largest muscle of the buttock? The answer is the inferior gluteal nerve. Loss of it weakens the power to rise from a low chair or to climb stairs. The muscle it supplies is the gluteus maximus.

Question 3278. A constructed illustration: after a fall onto the buttocks a person has tenderness over the lowest part of the spine behind the anal canal and pain on sitting. Which structure is injured, and which muscles are attached to it? The answer is the coccyx, to which the coccygeus and the levator ani are attached.

Question 3279. A described dissection shows a large opening in the back of the pelvis through which a thick nerve, an artery and a vein pass below a small muscle, while another nerve passes above it. Which opening is shown? The answer is the greater sciatic foramen, which the piriformis divides.

Question 3280. Which muscle lies immediately behind the neck of the femur and separates it from the obturator membrane: the pectineus, the obturator externus, the quadratus femoris, or the iliacus? The answer is the obturator externus. It runs from the outer surface of the obturator membrane to the pit at the base of the greater trochanter.

Question 3281. Which muscle of the medial thigh is the largest and has two parts with different nerve supplies: the adductor longus, the adductor brevis, the adductor magnus, or the gracilis? The answer is the adductor magnus. Its adductor part is supplied by the obturator nerve, and its hamstring part by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve.

Question 3282. Which of these pairs belongs together: the gluteus medius with the inferior gluteal nerve, the piriformis with the obturator nerve, the gluteus maximus with the inferior gluteal nerve, or the tensor fasciae latae with the femoral nerve? The answer is the gluteus maximus with the inferior gluteal nerve. The gluteus medius and the tensor fasciae latae are supplied by the superior gluteal nerve.

Question 3283. Which flat quadrangular muscle lies in the floor of the femoral triangle, arises from the front of the pubis, and inserts on the back of the femur just below the lesser trochanter? The answer is the pectineus. It flexes and adducts the hip, and it is usually supplied by the femoral nerve.

Question 3284. A constructed illustration: after a deep wound in the buttock below the middle of the gluteal fold, a person cannot bend the knee and the foot hangs down. Which nerve is injured, and which two movements are lost? The answer is the sciatic nerve, with loss of bending the knee and of all movement at the ankle and the toes.

Question 3285. A described dissection of the perineum shows a triangular muscle running from the inner surface of the ischial spine to the side of the coccyx and the lower sacrum. Which muscle is shown, and what does it form with its fellow? The answer is the coccygeus, which forms the back part of the pelvic diaphragm behind the levator ani.

Question 3286. True or false: the gluteus maximus is the chief abductor of the hip. The answer is false. The gluteus maximus extends the hip and turns the thigh outward, while the abductors are the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus. Those two are the muscles that keep the pelvis level in walking.

Question 3287. Which structure lies immediately superficial to the gluteus medius in the buttock: the gluteus maximus, the gluteus minimus, the piriformis, or the tensor fasciae latae? The answer is the gluteus maximus, which covers the greater part of the medius. The gluteus minimus lies deep to the medius.

Question 3288. Which group of small muscles turns the thigh outward and steadies the head of the femur in its socket: the adductors, the lateral rotators, the quadriceps, or the hamstrings? The answer is the lateral rotators, which include the piriformis, the obturator internus, the gemelli and the quadratus femoris. They balance the muscles that turn the thigh inward.

Question 3289. Which of these pairs belongs together: the sartorius with the femoral nerve, the gracilis with the femoral nerve, the adductor longus with the femoral nerve, or the pectineus with the obturator nerve? The answer is the sartorius with the femoral nerve. The gracilis and the adductor longus are supplied by the obturator nerve, and the pectineus usually by the femoral nerve.

Question 3290. Which muscle of the medial thigh arises from the front of the pubis just below the pubic crest and inserts into the middle third of the rough line on the back of the femur? The answer is the adductor longus. It is the most anterior of the adductor group and forms the medial border of the femoral triangle.

Question 3291. A constructed illustration: a person who receives a blow to the outer side of the hip cannot push the leg outward against resistance, and the pelvis drops when the weight is taken on that leg. Which muscle and nerve are involved? The answer is the gluteus medius and the superior gluteal nerve. The nerve reaches the muscle above the piriformis.

Question 3292. A described dissection of the front of the upper thigh shows a triangle bounded above by a band running from the front of the hip bone to the pubic bone, laterally by a long muscle from the front of the iliac spine and medially by an adductor. Which muscles form the lateral and the medial borders? The answer is the sartorius laterally and the adductor longus medially, with the inguinal ligament above them.

Question 3293. True or false: the obturator nerve carries fibers to the skin of the medial side of the thigh. The answer is true. Its anterior branch gives a small branch to the skin there. Its main work, however, is to supply the adductor muscles.

Question 3294. In the standing position a person turns the right foot outward so that the toes point away from the midline. In which plane and about which axis does the hip move, and which muscles produce the movement? The answer is the transverse plane about a longitudinal axis, and the short lateral rotators produce it. The same muscles steady the head of the femur in its socket.

Question 3295. Name two muscles of the buttock that are supplied by the superior gluteal nerve, spoken as a list. The answer is the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus, with the tensor fasciae latae as a third. All three help to keep the pelvis level while walking.

Question 3296. Which structure passes immediately in front of the hip joint and is separated from it by a large bursa: the psoas major tendon, the sciatic nerve, the gluteus minimus, or the quadratus femoris? The answer is the psoas major tendon, with the iliacus beside it. The bursa beneath that tendon often communicates with the cavity of the hip joint.

Question 3297. Which nerves supply the levator ani and the coccygeus, the muscles of the pelvic floor: the pudendal nerve, the nerve to the obturator internus, the sacral nerves, or the inferior gluteal nerve? The answer is the sacral nerves, from the third and the fourth sacral segments. The pudendal nerve supplies the muscles of the perineum and the external anal sphincter instead.

Question 3298. Which of these pairs belongs together: the iliotibial band with the gluteus maximus, the inguinal ligament with the sacrotuberous ligament, the sacrotuberous ligament with the levator ani, or the obturator membrane with the piriformis? The answer is the iliotibial band with the gluteus maximus, whose lower fibers insert into it. The tensor fasciae latae also pulls on the band.

Question 3299. Which muscle of the medial thigh is the smallest and most deeply placed, arising from the body and the lower ramus of the pubis and inserting into the upper part of the rough line on the back of the femur? The answer is the adductor brevis. The obturator nerve divides around it, one branch passing in front and one behind.

Question 3300. A constructed illustration: a person who plays a sport with sudden changes of direction feels a tearing pain in the upper inner thigh, and the pain returns when the thigh is pulled toward the midline against resistance. Which muscle group is injured, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the adductor group, chiefly the adductor longus, supplied by the obturator nerve.

To summarize, the intrinsic muscles of the hand fall into four groups: the thenar muscles that move the thumb, the hypothenar muscles that move the little finger, the lumbricals that flex the knuckles and straighten the finger joints, and the interossei that move the fingers sideways. The median nerve supplies the thenar muscles and the lateral two lumbricals, while the ulnar nerve supplies everything else in the hand. In the pelvis and the hip, the gluteus maximus extends the hip, the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus abduct it and keep the pelvis level, and the short lateral rotators steady the head of the femur in its socket. The adductors fill the medial thigh, the iliopsoas is the chief flexor of the hip, and the levator ani and the coccygeus form the floor of the pelvis.
