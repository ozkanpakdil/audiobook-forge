# Chapter 101 — CT Anatomy

This chapter listens to the body the way a computed tomography scanner sees it: in section, by density, and at the scale of the organs and the spaces between them. It covers the densities that make up an image, the windows and the planes in which images are shown, and the appearance of the head, the neck, the chest, the abdomen and the pelvis, together with the artifacts that imitate disease. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right. Everything is described in words, because a listener cannot see a monitor, so each image is spoken as the section it would show. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10001. On a computed tomography image, which of these appears brightest: air in the bowel, fat around a kidney, water in the bladder, or the dense cortical bone of the femur? The answer is the dense cortical bone of the femur. The image shows density on a scale that runs from air at the black end to bone at the white end. Fat and water lie between them, and fat is the darker of the two.

Question 10002. Which normal tissue fills the cavity of a long bone and appears on a computed tomography image as a gray close to that of muscle? The answer is bone marrow. In an adult most of the marrow of the long bones is fat and appears darker than muscle. In a child the marrow is more cellular and so comes closer to muscle in density.

Question 10003. A scan of the chest is shown with settings that make the fine vessels of the lung crisp and leave the mediastinum in a narrow band of gray. Which display setting was used: a lung window, a bone window, a soft tissue window, or a liver window? The answer is a lung window. It spreads the display across the very low densities of air, so the small vessels stand out. Soft tissue is then squeezed into a few shades of gray.

Question 10004. A described section through the upper abdomen shows a large rounded organ on the right, dark branching vessels within it, and short bright arcs of bone where the ribs are cut across. Which organ is described? The answer is the liver. It fills the right upper abdomen and receives the portal vein, whose branches are darker than the liver tissue when no contrast has been given. The ribs appear as arcs because the plane cuts across them.

Question 10005. Which structure descends in the abdomen in front of the vertebral bodies and slightly to the left of the midline? The answer is the abdominal aorta. It lies against the front of the spine, with the inferior vena cava to its right. Both appear as rounded gray structures of soft tissue density when no contrast has been given.

Question 10006. On a section at the level of the kidneys, which statement is correct: the right kidney lies lower than the left, the left kidney lies lower than the right, the two lie at the same level, or the kidneys lie behind the vertebral column? The answer is that the right kidney lies lower than the left. The liver occupies the space above the right kidney and presses it downward. Both kidneys lie behind the peritoneum, against the posterior wall.

Question 10007. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the head, a scan shows a collection of blood between the skull and the outer surface of the dura mater. Which space is described, and what shape does the collection take? The answer is the extradural space. Blood there forms a lens-shaped mass that pushes the dura and the brain inward. Blood beneath the dura instead spreads along the surface of the brain and follows its curves.

Question 10008. Which structure is described as the dome of bone above the brain, made of two plates with spongy bone between them and seen as a bright band of varying thickness? The answer is the vault of the skull. Its outer and inner tables are compact bone, and the spongy diploe lies between them. The inner table is thinner, so it may break while the outer one holds.

Question 10009. True or false: on a computed tomography image, air within the bowel is darker than fat and fat is darker than water. The answer is true. The scale of density places air at the black end, fat below water, and water below soft tissue. That order is what lets a listener tell gas from fat from fluid when a section is described.

Question 10010. Which statement describes an axial section of the trunk: a section across it that shows the right and the left sides together, a section from front to back, a section taken vertically from side to side, or a section along the length of the spine? The answer is a section across the trunk that shows both sides together. It is taken at right angles to the long axis of the body. A section from front to back through the midline shows the two sides apart.

Question 10011. Which large vein is seen as a rounded gray structure beside the aorta on a section of the abdomen, lying to the right of the midline? The answer is the inferior vena cava. It runs upward in front of the vertebral column and to the right of the aorta. At the level of the kidneys it receives the renal veins from both sides.

Question 10012. Which structure lies immediately behind the body of the stomach and immediately in front of the aorta: the pancreas, the spleen, the left kidney, or the transverse colon? The answer is the pancreas. Its body crosses the midline in front of the great vessels and behind the stomach. The spleen lies further to the left, and the left kidney lies behind the tail of the pancreas.

Question 10013. A described image of the chest shows two large black spaces outlined by thin white lines, each holding smaller branching white structures, with a gray mass between them. Which structures are described? The answer is the right and the left lungs, with the mediastinum between them. Air fills the lungs, so they appear black. The vessels inside them are soft tissue surrounded by air, so they appear white.

Question 10014. Which structure lies in the midline of the upper neck at the level of the thyroid gland: the trachea, the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, or the sternocleidomastoid muscle? The answer is the trachea. The thyroid gland wraps around its front and its sides. The carotid artery and the jugular vein travel together in the carotid sheath on each side, and the muscle lies further out and nearer the skin.

Question 10015. Which structure is described as a butterfly-shaped mass of gray matter in the center of the brain, with a thin dark slit between its two halves and the third ventricle behind them? The answer is the thalamus. Its two halves lie on either side of the third ventricle and are joined across the midline. Around them run the internal capsule and the fibers joining the cortex with the rest of the nervous system.

Question 10016. Which of these pairs belongs together: cortical bone with the highest density on the image, the air of the lung with the density of water, fat beneath the skin with the density of muscle, or cerebrospinal fluid with the density of bone? The answer is cortical bone with the highest density. Dense bone is the brightest natural tissue shown, air is the least dense, and fat is darker than water and muscle.

Question 10017. On a section of the head at the level of the eyes, which of these lies within the bony orbit: the lens of the eye, the pituitary gland, the maxillary sinus, or the mastoid air cells? The answer is the lens of the eye. It sits at the front of the globe and appears as a small disc of soft tissue density. The pituitary gland lies in the sella turcica, and the maxillary sinus lies below the orbit.

Question 10018. A constructed illustration: after a fracture through the petrous part of the temporal bone, a scan shows blood in an air-filled chamber of the bone with the small bones of hearing outlined within it. Which chamber is described, and which structures does the blood outline? The answer is the middle ear. It is an air-filled space in the petrous bone, and blood there outlines the auditory ossicles. The inner ear lies medial to it.

Question 10019. On a section of the abdomen at the level of the second lumbar vertebra, which structure lies immediately medial to the kidney: the psoas major muscle, the liver, the spleen, or the twelfth rib? The answer is the psoas major muscle. It lies against the side of the vertebral column, and the kidney lies lateral to it. In front of the kidney lies the peritoneum, and behind it lie the twelfth rib and the muscles of the posterior wall.

Question 10020. Which statement best describes a coronal reconstruction of an abdominal scan: a view from the front that shows both sides at once with the diaphragm above, a view from the side, a view across the body at the level of the umbilicus, or a view along the aorta? The answer is a view from the front that shows both sides at once. The plane runs from side to side and from head to foot, at right angles to a section across the body.

Question 10021. A section shows two structures lying on opposite sides of the midline at the same level. Which plane separates the right side of the body from the left, and in which direction must the listener move to pass from one structure to the other? The answer is the median plane, and the listener moves sideways to the right or to the left. Movement along that line crosses the median plane at right angles.

Question 10022. A described image shows a rounded organ on the left, behind the stomach and the left ribs, with a scalloped inner border where the kidney presses against it. Which organ is described? The answer is the spleen. It lies behind the stomach and under the left dome of the diaphragm, and the left kidney and the splenic flexure of the colon lie against it. Its density is close to that of the liver.

Question 10023. Which vessel is described as a long gray tube crossing in front of the pancreas from left to right and joining the superior mesenteric vein behind the neck of the pancreas? The answer is the splenic vein. It runs along the back of the pancreas and unites with the superior mesenteric vein to form the portal vein. That junction lies behind the neck of the pancreas.

Question 10024. Name the two large vessels that lie in front of the vertebral column at the level of the fourth lumbar vertebra, spoken as a list. The answer is the aorta and the inferior vena cava. The aorta lies slightly to the left of the midline and the inferior vena cava to the right. Both rest against the front of the vertebral bodies.

Question 10025. Which statement explains why a small stone in the ureter can be seen as a bright dot: it is denser than the urine and the fat around it, it is less dense than air, it has the density of muscle, or it is filled with contrast? The answer is that it is denser than the urine and the fat around it. A calcified stone stands out against those softer tissues. Whether it is seen at all depends on its size and on the width of the section.

Question 10026. Which space is described as a thin dark crescent of fluid lying against the outer surface of the brain, deep to the skull? The answer is the subarachnoid space. It holds cerebrospinal fluid, which appears dark. The arachnoid lies against the inner surface of the dura and the pia against the brain, so the fluid lies between them.

Question 10027. A constructed illustration: an older person is scanned after a fall, and a crescent of blood is described along the inner surface of the skull, spreading over one hemisphere and following its curves. Which collection is described, and which vessels usually bleed? The answer is a subdural hematoma, usually from the bridging veins that cross from the brain to the venous sinuses. Blood there spreads widely because the space has no fixed edge.

Question 10028. Which muscle is seen on each side of the midline in the back of the neck, running from the occiput down over the cervical vertebrae? The answer is the trapezius. It forms the rounded contour of the back of the neck and lies over the splenius and the semispinalis muscles. The sternocleidomastoid runs down the side of the neck instead.

Question 10029. On a section of the neck, which structure lies immediately medial to the internal jugular vein and lateral to the trachea: the common carotid artery, the thyroid gland, the esophagus, or a cervical vertebra? The answer is the common carotid artery. The artery and the vein travel together in the carotid sheath, and the artery lies medial to the vein. The vagus nerve lies behind and between them.

Question 10030. Which structure is described as a bony ring without a body, with a front and a back arch and two wide lateral masses, at the top of the spine? The answer is the first cervical vertebra, the atlas. It supports the skull and turns about the second vertebra below it. Its lateral masses carry the surfaces that meet the occipital condyles.

Question 10031. A described image of the skull base shows a pair of air-filled spaces divided by a thin bony septum, with a small rounded gland in a hollow just above and behind them. Which structures are described? The answer is the sphenoid sinuses with the pituitary gland in the sella turcica behind and above them. The sinuses are air filled and appear black, and the gland lies in the bony hollow.

Question 10032. Which statement about the spinal cord on a scan of the spine is correct: it ends at the level of the first or second lumbar vertebra, it reaches the sacrum, it lies outside the canal, or it fills the whole canal? The answer is that it ends at the level of the first or second lumbar vertebra. Below that level the canal holds the cauda equina, a bundle of nerve roots, with cerebrospinal fluid around them.

Question 10033. True or false: on a computed tomography image, the gray matter of the brain appears darker than the white matter because it holds more water. The answer is true. Gray matter contains more water and so is slightly darker, which is what allows the two to be told apart. Contrast given into a vein can blur that difference.

Question 10034. Which cleft is described on a section of the brain as a deep dark line running outward and backward from the front of the skull, separating the temporal lobe below from the frontal and parietal lobes above? The answer is the lateral sulcus. It carries the middle cerebral artery deep within it. The fluid in the sulcus makes it appear dark.

Question 10035. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the temple, a scan shows a lens-shaped collection of blood against the inside of the skull over the temporal lobe, and the bone above the ear is cracked. Which vessel has bled, and into which space? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, which bleeds into the extradural space. The lens shape comes from the dura being stripped from the bone.

Question 10036. In a normal brain scan made after contrast has been given into a vein, which structures become brighter: the blood vessels, the cerebrospinal fluid, the white matter, or the air in the sinuses? The answer is the blood vessels. Vessels and the dura take up the contrast, and so does the choroid plexus within the ventricles. Normal brain tissue and cerebrospinal fluid do not.

Question 10037. Which space lies between the back of the pharynx and the muscles that cover the cervical vertebrae? The answer is the retropharyngeal space. It lies behind the pharynx and in front of the prevertebral muscles and runs down into the upper chest. An infection in it can therefore track downward along that plane.

Question 10038. Which sinus is described as a pair of air-filled spaces in the cheek, below the orbit and above the roots of the upper teeth, divided by a thin bony wall? The answer is the maxillary sinus. It is the largest of the paranasal sinuses and drains into the nasal cavity below the middle concha. Its roof is the floor of the orbit.

Question 10039. A described image of the pelvis shows a rounded fluid-filled organ in the midline behind the pubic symphysis and, behind it, a gas-filled organ with a folded wall. Which two organs are described? The answer is the urinary bladder in front and the rectum behind. In the female the uterus and the vagina lie between them, and in the male the seminal vesicles lie against the back of the bladder above the rectum.

Question 10040. In the male pelvis, which structure lies behind the pubic symphysis and below the bladder: the prostate gland, the rectum, the seminal vesicle, or the ascending colon? The answer is the prostate gland. It lies below the bladder and in front of the rectum, and the urethra passes through it. The seminal vesicles lie above it and behind the bladder.

Question 10041. Which of these pairs belongs together: a density below that of water with fat, a density below that of water with bone, a density close to that of air with water, or a density above that of bone with urine? The answer is a density below that of water with fat. Fat lies just below water on the scale, while air lies far below and bone far above. Urine is close to water.

Question 10042. Which order of tissues goes from the least dense to the most dense: air, fat, water, muscle, bone; water, air, fat, muscle, bone; air, muscle, fat, water, bone; or bone, muscle, water, fat, air? The answer is air, fat, water, muscle, bone. Air is least dense and bone most dense, and fat is less dense than water. This order underlies every description of a scan.

Question 10043. A described section of the upper chest shows a wide dark tube in front of the spine, divided along its length by a thin gray band, with air in front and soft tissue behind. Which structures are described? The answer is the trachea in front and the esophagus behind it. The trachea holds air and appears black, and the empty esophagus has the density of soft tissue.

Question 10044. Which chamber of the heart is described as the most anterior one, lying just behind the sternum? The answer is the right ventricle. It forms the front of the heart and reaches almost to the chest wall. The right atrium lies behind it and to the right, and the left ventricle lies behind and to the left.

Question 10045. A constructed illustration: after an injury to the chest, a scan shows air in the tissues of the chest wall and a collapsed lung with air in the space around it. Which layer has been breached, and what is the result called? The answer is the pleura, and the result is a pneumothorax. Air between the two layers separates the lung from the chest wall, and the lung falls back toward the hilum.

Question 10046. Which structure lies immediately in front of the vertebral column in the lower chest: the descending aorta, the left atrium, the trachea, or the pulmonary trunk? The answer is the descending aorta. It runs downward in front of the spine and slightly to the left of the midline. The esophagus lies in front of it, and the left atrium lies further forward still.

Question 10047. Which structure lies immediately to the right of the trachea in the upper chest, arching forward over the root of the right lung: the azygos vein, the thoracic duct, the left common carotid artery, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the azygos vein. It ascends in front of the vertebral column on the right and joins the superior vena cava.

Question 10048. Which space on a scan of the chest lies between the arch of the aorta above and the left pulmonary artery below? The answer is the aortopulmonary window. It contains fat and lymph nodes, and nodes there may enlarge. It lies just below the aortic arch and above the left pulmonary artery.

Question 10049. A described image of the lung shows a network of thin white lines spreading out from the hilum toward the edges of the lung against a black background. Which structures are the white lines? The answer is the pulmonary vessels, which branch alongside the bronchi. A vessel cut across appears as a white dot, and a bronchus cut across appears as a ring with a dark center.

Question 10050. Which statement about the pleura on a computed tomography image is correct: the normal pleura is too thin to be seen as a separate line, it appears as a bright band 5 millimeters thick, the pleural space normally holds air, or it normally holds fluid in everyone? The answer is that the normal pleura is too thin to be seen as a separate line. Fluid or air in the space changes that appearance.

Question 10051. Why is a scan of the abdomen often repeated some minutes after the first one: to outline the collecting systems with contrast that has reached the urine, to make the bones brighter, to make the fat darker, or to reduce the number of sections? The answer is to outline the collecting systems. The kidneys, the ureters and the bladder then appear bright, while the earlier scan shows the vessels and the organs.

Question 10052. Name the three arteries that arise from the celiac trunk, spoken as a list. The answer is the left gastric artery, the splenic artery, and the common hepatic artery. They supply the stomach, the spleen, and the liver with the gallbladder. The trunk arises from the front of the aorta just below the aortic opening of the diaphragm.

Question 10053. Which small gland is seen as a thin triangular or Y-shaped structure sitting on the upper pole of each kidney? The answer is the adrenal gland. The right one lies between the liver and the upper pole of the right kidney, and the left one lies medial to the upper pole of the left kidney. Both lie within the renal fascia.

Question 10054. A constructed illustration: a scan shows a small bright stone in the line of the ureter just below the kidney, with the tract above it wider than on the other side. Which structure is obstructed, and what does the appearance above it show? The answer is the ureter, and the widening shows that urine is being held back. The collecting system of that kidney is fuller than normal.

Question 10055. Which statement about the space behind the peritoneum is correct: the kidneys, the adrenal glands, the aorta and the inferior vena cava lie behind it, the stomach and the spleen lie behind it, the small bowel lies behind it, or the bladder lies behind it? The answer is that the kidneys, the adrenal glands and the great vessels lie behind the peritoneum, as do the pancreas, the duodenum and the ascending and descending colon.

Question 10056. Which structure lies immediately in front of the right kidney at the level of the second lumbar vertebra: the duodenum, the spleen, the pancreas, or the sigmoid colon? The answer is the duodenum. It lies against the medial part of the right kidney, and the right colic flexure lies in front of the lower lateral part.

Question 10057. A described section of the upper abdomen shows a pear-shaped sac of fluid with nearly the density of water, tucked under the right lobe of the liver. Which organ is described? The answer is the gallbladder. It lies against the under-surface of the liver and holds bile, which is close to water in density. Concentrated bile looks denser and more like soft tissue.

Question 10058. True or false: without contrast, the wall of the aorta and the blood inside it cannot be told apart, because both have the density of soft tissue. The answer is true. Blood and the wall of the vessel have almost the same density, so they appear as one gray ring. After contrast the column of blood becomes bright.

Question 10059. Which structure lies most anteriorly in the female pelvis: the urinary bladder, the uterus, the rectum, or the sacrum? The answer is the urinary bladder. It lies behind the pubic symphysis, with the uterus and the vagina behind it and the rectum behind those. The sacrum forms the back wall of the pelvis.

Question 10060. Which bone forms the roof of the socket of the hip joint, seen as a thick arch of bone over the head of the femur? The answer is the ilium. All three bones join in the socket, but the ilium forms its roof and the ischium its back. The pubis forms the front and the lower part.

Question 10061. A constructed illustration: a scan shows the aorta and the inferior vena cava lying together in front of the spine with a large collection of blood around them, and the organs in front are pushed forward. Which space is described, and what else lies in it? The answer is the space behind the peritoneum. It also holds the kidneys, the adrenal glands, the pancreas, the duodenum and the ascending and descending colon.

Question 10062. Which statement about the psoas major muscle is correct: it runs from the sides of the lumbar vertebrae downward and outward to the lesser trochanter, it lies behind the vertebral bodies, it lies medial to the aorta at every level, or it lies inside the peritoneal cavity? The answer is that it runs to the lesser trochanter. It is a flexor of the hip, and it lies lateral to the great vessels.

Question 10063. Which two veins join behind the neck of the pancreas, and which vessel do they form? The answer is the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein, which form the portal vein. The portal vein then runs upward and to the right behind the first part of the duodenum to reach the liver.

Question 10064. A described image shows a part of the bowel crossing in front of the great vessels from the left side toward the right, with sacculations along its wall and a wider caliber than the rest of the bowel. Which part is described? The answer is the transverse colon. Its haustral folds give it a segmented outline. The small bowel has more numerous and thinner folds.

Question 10065. Which pair of muscles is seen on a section of the abdomen as two flat bands on either side of the midline just beneath the skin, divided by a dark line? The answer is the rectus abdominis muscles, separated by the linea alba. They lie within the rectus sheath, in front of the peritoneum, with fat of the abdominal wall in front of them.

Question 10066. Which of these pairs belongs together: the liver with the right upper quadrant, the spleen with the right upper quadrant, the appendix with the left upper quadrant, or the stomach with the right iliac fossa? The answer is the liver with the right upper quadrant. The spleen lies in the left upper quadrant, and the appendix lies in the right iliac fossa.

Question 10067. In the midline of the abdomen, which order of structures runs from the front to the back: skin, rectus abdominis, small bowel, aorta, vertebral body; skin, aorta, small bowel, vertebral body, rectus abdominis; skin, small bowel, rectus abdominis, aorta, vertebral body; or skin, vertebral body, aorta, small bowel, rectus abdominis? The answer is skin, rectus abdominis, small bowel, aorta, vertebral body. The muscles lie in front and the great vessels against the spine.

Question 10068. A described image shows a long vein crossing in front of the aorta from left to right to reach the inferior vena cava, with an artery passing in front of that vein. Which vein and which artery are described? The answer is the left renal vein and the superior mesenteric artery. The vein drains the left kidney across the midline, and the artery crosses in front of it.

Question 10069. A constructed illustration: after a fall, a scan shows a break through the neck of the femur with the head held in the socket. Which structure within the bone is at risk, and what does its loss threaten? The answer is the blood supply running along the neck in the fold of the capsule. If it is torn, the head may lose its blood supply and die.

Question 10070. Below the inguinal ligament, which structure lies immediately lateral to the femoral vein: the femoral artery, the femoral nerve, the pubic tubercle, or the great saphenous vein? The answer is the femoral artery. From lateral to medial the order is the nerve, the artery, the vein, and the lymph nodes. The nerve lies outside the sheath.

Question 10071. Which tendon lies closest to the bone behind the medial malleolus of the ankle, with the artery and the nerve behind it? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior. From the bone outward the order is the tibialis posterior, the flexor digitorum longus, the artery, the nerve, and the flexor hallucis longus. The tendon supports the arch of the foot.

Question 10072. A section of the thigh shows the skin in front, the femur in the middle, and a large mass of muscle behind it. In which direction must the listener move to pass from the skin to the femur, and which plane lies at right angles to that direction? The answer is backward, that is posteriorly, and the coronal plane lies at right angles to it.

Question 10073. Why are the bones of the skull base hard to examine on a plain radiograph but easy to see on a computed tomography scan? The answer is that the scan shows a section in which the structures above and below it do not overlap. On a radiograph all the layers lie one over another, so the base is hidden by everything in front of it.

Question 10074. A described image of the hand shows a row of five long bones between the small bones of the wrist and the bones of the fingers. Which bones are described? The answer is the metacarpal bones. They form the framework of the palm, and each one carries a finger. The thumb has two phalanges and the other fingers have three.

Question 10075. Which statement about the appearance of normal fat is correct: it is darker than muscle and brighter than air, it is brighter than bone, it has the same density as water, or it has the same density as muscle? The answer is that it is darker than muscle and brighter than air. Fat lies just below water on the scale, which is why it outlines organs so clearly.

Question 10076. Which part of a vertebra is the bony arch behind the body that encloses the spinal canal? The answer is the vertebral arch, whose pedicles and laminae form it.

Question 10077. Which structure is a cushion between two vertebral bodies, with a darker center and an outer ring of fibers? The answer is the intervertebral disc, whose center is the nucleus pulposus.

Question 10078. In a growing child, which structure is a dark band across the end of a long bone, between the shaft and the bony cap? The answer is the growth plate, which is cartilage and closes with age.

Question 10079. Which structure in the lower neck is a complete ring of cartilage, with a narrow front and a broad back, below the thyroid cartilage? The answer is the cricoid cartilage.

Question 10080. Which muscle forms a sling from the pubis to the coccyx and separates the pelvic cavity from the perineum: the levator ani, the obturator internus, the piriformis, or the gluteus maximus? The answer is the levator ani.

Question 10081. About a minute after contrast is given into a vein, which structures are brightest: the aorta, the portal vein and the liver, the ureters and the bladder, or the bones? The answer is the portal vein and the liver.

Question 10082. Which substance is the zero point of the density scale of a computed tomography image: air, fat, water, or cortical bone? The answer is water. Air lies far below it, fat just below.

Question 10083. Which of these lies in the posterior mediastinum, in front of the vertebral column: the thymus, the esophagus, the ascending aorta, or the pulmonary trunk? The answer is the esophagus. The others lie in the middle mediastinum.

Question 10084. A small nodule is seen on one section but not above or below it. Which effect explains this: partial volume averaging, contrast, beam hardening, or motion? The answer is partial volume averaging, because the nodule fills only part of that section.

Question 10085. A scan of a restless person shows a doubled outline of the diaphragm and the aorta. Which artifact is this: motion, beam hardening, partial volume averaging, or metal streak? The answer is motion, because the structure moved during the scan.

Question 10086. Which structure is a small joint between the articular processes of two adjacent vertebrae, behind the spinal canal? The answer is the facet joint, whose enlargement can narrow the canal.

Question 10087. On a computed tomography image, which statement about muscle and tendon is correct: both look the same, muscle is much brighter, tendon is as dense as bone, or both are darker than fat? The answer is that both look the same.

Question 10088. On a section through the larynx, which structures lie on either side of the airway: the vocal cords, the tonsils, the parotid glands, or the longus colli muscles? The answer is the vocal cords, with the muscles that move them.

Question 10089. Which structure is a fan of fat and vessels that suspends the small bowel from the posterior abdominal wall? The answer is the mesentery, which carries the vessels of the small bowel.

Question 10090. Why do the bones of a newborn look less bright than those of an adult: they hold less mineral, they hold more air, thicker muscle covers them, or they lie nearer the scanner? The answer is that they hold less mineral.

Question 10091. A described image of a young child shows a soft tissue mass in the upper chest, with the trachea and the great vessels behind it. Which structure is described? The answer is the thymus, which shrinks and is replaced by fat with age.

Question 10092. A described section through the neck shows a ring of bone around a dark canal, a gray cord within it, and small joints behind. Which structures are described? The answer is a cervical vertebra, the spinal cord, and the facet joints.

Question 10093. A described image shows curved lines crossing the abdomen that follow no organ boundary and change position from one section to the next. What do they represent? The answer is a reconstruction artifact.

Question 10094. True or false: the bright and dark streaks that radiate from a metal implant are an artifact of the scan rather than a structure of the body. The answer is true, because dense metal absorbs the beam unevenly.

Question 10095. True or false: the open sutures of a young child's skull appear as dark lines on a computed tomography scan. The answer is true, because they are fibrous and not yet ossified.

Question 10096. Which of these pairs belongs together: the arterial phase with the aorta, the portal venous phase with the gallbladder, the delayed phase with the lung, or the unenhanced phase with the great vessels? The answer is the arterial phase with the aorta.

Question 10097. Which of these pairs belongs together: beam hardening with dark bands between two dense bones, motion with a doubled edge of a still organ, partial volume with a sharp edge, or metal with a smooth density? The answer is beam hardening with dark bands between two dense bones.

Question 10098. Which canal lies on the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa, medial to the obturator internus muscle, and carries the internal pudendal vessels and the pudendal nerve? The answer is the pudendal canal.

Question 10099. A constructed illustration: a child swallows a coin, and on the frontal image it lies flat, high in the chest. In which passage does it lie? The answer is the esophagus. A coin in the trachea stands on edge.

Question 10100. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the left lower ribs, a scan shows free fluid around the spleen and a broken tenth rib. Which organ is the source of the fluid? The answer is the spleen, with blood in the peritoneal cavity.

To summarize, a computed tomography image is a map of density, running from air at the black end, through fat and water, to muscle and bone at the white end. Window settings decide which tissues spread across the gray shades, and the plane of the section decides which structures appear together. Each region has its landmarks: the liver above the lower right kidney, the pancreas in front of the great vessels, the aorta to the left of the midline with the inferior vena cava to its right, and the bladder in front of the rectum. Contrast brightens the vessels, then the liver and the portal vein, and last the collecting systems, while artifacts from metal, motion, beam hardening, and partial volume can imitate disease.
