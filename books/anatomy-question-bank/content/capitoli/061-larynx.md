# Chapter 61 — Larynx

This chapter follows the airway and the food passage from the back of the nose and the mouth down to the entrance of the windpipe. Its first part covers the pharynx, the three regions into which it is divided, the openings that pierce its walls and the spaces that surround it. The larger part then takes the larynx in turn, through its cartilages, its vocal folds and the small muscles that move them. It holds one hundred questions, running from question 6001 to question 6100, and every one of them gives its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 6001. Which muscular tube lies behind the nasal cavity, behind the mouth and behind the larynx, and becomes continuous below with the esophagus? The answer is the pharynx. It hangs from the base of the skull as a half-tube that is open in front, where it communicates with each of those three cavities in turn.

Question 6002. Which is the correct order of the parts of the pharynx from above downward: the nasopharynx, the oropharynx and the laryngopharynx; the laryngopharynx, the oropharynx and the nasopharynx; the oropharynx, the nasopharynx and the laryngopharynx; or the laryngopharynx, the nasopharynx and the oropharynx? The answer is the nasopharynx, the oropharynx and the laryngopharynx. Each part is named for the cavity behind it: the nose, the mouth and the larynx.

Question 6003. Which part of the pharynx lies directly behind the nasal cavity and above the soft palate? The answer is the nasopharynx. It is continuous in front with the nasal cavity through the two posterior openings called the choanae. The auditory tube opens on its lateral wall.

Question 6004. A described sagittal section through the head shows a mass of soft tissue filling the roof and the back wall of the space behind the nose. Which structure occupies that position? The answer is the pharyngeal tonsil. It is a collection of lymphoid tissue under the mucosa of the roof and the posterior wall. When it enlarges in a child it can block the nasal airway.

Question 6005. Which opening pierces the lateral wall of the nasopharynx, about a finger's breadth behind the back end of the inferior nasal concha: the opening of the maxillary sinus, the opening of the auditory tube, the opening of the frontal sinus, or the opening of the sphenoid sinus? The answer is the opening of the auditory tube. It leads back to the middle ear cavity.

Question 6006. Which fold runs downward from the cartilage of the auditory tube and forms a ridge behind its pharyngeal opening: the salpingopharyngeal fold, the palatopharyngeal arch, the palatoglossal arch, or the median glossoepiglottic fold? The answer is the salpingopharyngeal fold. It covers the salpingopharyngeus muscle, which helps to lift the pharynx during swallowing.

Question 6007. A described sagittal section through the head shows the nasal cavity above, the mouth in front and a wide muscular tube behind them, divided across by the soft palate. Which region is shown, and what does the soft palate separate? The answer is the pharynx, and the soft palate separates the nasopharynx above from the oropharynx below.

Question 6008. A constructed illustration: a child breathes through the mouth at night and snores, and the described tissue in the roof of the nasopharynx is enlarged. Which structure is enlarged, and why does it cause these symptoms? The answer is the pharyngeal tonsil. It sits in the roof and the back wall of the nasopharynx, close to the opening of the auditory tube. The assessment belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6009. True or false: the oropharynx carries both air and food. The answer is true. It lies behind the mouth, between the soft palate above and the upper border of the epiglottis below. Air passes through it in breathing and a bolus passes through it in swallowing, and the two uses share the same space.

Question 6010. Which structure lies in the fossa between the palatoglossal arch in front and the palatopharyngeal arch behind? The answer is the palatine tonsil. The fossa is the interval between the two arches, and the tonsil is a mass of lymphoid tissue that fills it.

Question 6011. Which large artery lies lateral to the palatine tonsil, separated from it only by the wall of the pharynx: the facial artery, the lingual artery, the internal carotid artery, or the ascending pharyngeal artery? The answer is the internal carotid artery. It lies behind the superior constrictor, so it is a feared structure in that region.

Question 6012. Which muscle forms the thickest part of the circular coat of the pharynx and arises from the thyroid and cricoid cartilages: the superior constrictor, the middle constrictor, the inferior constrictor, or the stylopharyngeus? The answer is the inferior constrictor. Its upper fibers are the thyropharyngeal part and its lower fibers the cricopharyngeal part.

Question 6013. Which of these pairs belongs together: the nasopharynx with the pharyngeal tonsil, the oropharynx with the pharyngeal tonsil, the laryngopharynx with the palatine tonsil, or the nasopharynx with the lingual tonsil? The answer is the nasopharynx with the pharyngeal tonsil. The palatine tonsils lie in the oropharynx, one on each side.

Question 6014. Which part of the pharynx runs from the upper border of the epiglottis down to the lower border of the cricoid cartilage, behind the larynx: the nasopharynx, the oropharynx, the laryngopharynx, or the cervical esophagus? The answer is the laryngopharynx. It opens forward into the larynx through the laryngeal inlet.

Question 6015. Name, spoken as a list, the two arches that bound the fossa of the palatine tonsil. The answer is the palatoglossal arch in front and the palatopharyngeal arch behind. Each arch is a fold of mucosa over a muscle. The palatoglossus runs to the tongue and the palatopharyngeus into the pharyngeal wall.

Question 6016. Which statement describes the piriform fossa: a recess beside the laryngeal inlet, a recess in the roof of the nasopharynx, a pouch between the hyoid bone and the thyroid cartilage, or a space behind the cricoid cartilage? The answer is a recess beside the laryngeal inlet. It lies between the aryepiglottic fold medially and the thyroid cartilage laterally. The internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve lies under its mucosa.

Question 6017. A constructed illustration: a swallowed fish bone lodges in the recess beside the laryngeal inlet, and the described mucosa there is scratched. Which nerve lies immediately beneath that mucosa and may be injured? The answer is the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. It carries sensation from the larynx above the vocal folds, so its injury dulls the protective cough. The removal of the bone belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6018. Which bony process of the skull lies lateral to the pharynx and gives origin to the stylopharyngeus muscle? The answer is the styloid process of the temporal bone. Three muscles arise from it and pass downward into the tongue, the pharynx and the hyoid region.

Question 6019. Which tough fibrous layer lies deep to the pharyngeal muscles and is attached above to the base of the skull? The answer is the pharyngobasilar fascia. It forms the inner coat of the pharyngeal wall and is thickest in the upper part, where the muscle coat is incomplete.

Question 6020. Where is the weak point in the wall of the pharynx: between the thyropharyngeal and cricopharyngeal parts of the inferior constrictor, between the superior and middle constrictors, in the roof of the nasopharynx, or in the posterior wall of the oropharynx? The answer is between the thyropharyngeal and the cricopharyngeal parts of the inferior constrictor. The circular fibers are absent in that midline gap behind. A pouch of mucosa can protrude through it.

Question 6021. A constructed illustration: a person reports that food sticks high in the neck and a soft swelling gurgles when pressed. The pouch protrudes backward through a gap in the lower pharyngeal wall. Which gap is described? The answer is the interval between the thyropharyngeal and the cricopharyngeal parts of the inferior constrictor. The pouch lies above the cricopharyngeus, the sphincter at the top of the esophagus. The assessment belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6022. True or false: at the level of the fifth cervical vertebra the pharynx lies behind the larynx and in front of the bodies of the cervical vertebrae. The answer is true. The laryngopharynx occupies that interval, and the laryngeal inlet opens forward from it.

Question 6023. True or false: the wall of the pharynx is fixed to the base of the skull and cannot be moved. The answer is false. Only the upper attachment is fixed, while the muscular wall is raised and shortened during swallowing. The constrictors and the stylopharyngeus lift the pharynx over the bolus.

Question 6024. A described sagittal section shows a space filled with loose tissue lying between the back of the pharynx and the front of the vertebral column, running from the base of the skull downward. Which space is shown, and where does it lead? The answer is the retropharyngeal space, and it leads down into the chest behind the esophagus. Because the tissue in it is loose, infection can track along it into the mediastinum.

Question 6025. Which nerve plexus lies on the surface of the middle constrictor and supplies the muscles and the mucosa of the pharynx? The answer is the pharyngeal plexus. It is formed by pharyngeal branches of the vagus nerve and of the glossopharyngeal nerve, together with sympathetic fibers. The vagus supplies the muscles and the glossopharyngeal nerve carries most of the sensation.

Question 6026. Which cartilage of the larynx is the largest and forms the prominence in the front of the neck? The answer is the thyroid cartilage. It is made of two flat plates, called laminae, that meet in the midline at an angle. That angle is sharper in the male, so the prominence is more obvious there.

Question 6027. Which cartilage of the larynx has the shape of a signet ring, narrow in front and broad behind: the thyroid cartilage, the cricoid cartilage, the arytenoid cartilage, or the epiglottis? The answer is the cricoid cartilage. It is the only cartilage of the airway that forms a complete ring, and it is narrow in front and tall behind. The arytenoid cartilages sit on its broad posterior part.

Question 6028. Which leaf-shaped cartilage stands in front of the laryngeal inlet and is attached below to the back of the thyroid cartilage? The answer is the epiglottis. Its lower end is narrowed into a stalk, and its broad upper end projects upward behind the tongue.

Question 6029. Which pair of small cartilages sits on the upper border of the cricoid cartilage and moves the vocal folds: the arytenoid cartilages, the corniculate cartilages, the cuneiform cartilages, or the triticeal cartilages? The answer is the arytenoid cartilages. Each is shaped like a three-sided pyramid and carries a vocal process in front and a muscular process at its side.

Question 6030. Which two small cartilages lie within the aryepiglottic fold: the thyroid and the cricoid cartilages, the arytenoid and the triticeal cartilages, the corniculate and the cuneiform cartilages, or the epiglottis and the cricoid cartilage? The answer is the corniculate and the cuneiform cartilages. The corniculate cartilages sit on the tops of the arytenoid cartilages.

Question 6031. Which joint of the larynx lets the thyroid cartilage tilt forward on the cricoid cartilage and so lengthen the vocal fold? The answer is the cricothyroid joint. It lies between the inferior horn of the thyroid cartilage and the side of the cricoid cartilage. The movement changes the tension of the fold and therefore the pitch of the voice.

Question 6032. Which ligament joins the lower border of the cricoid cartilage to the first ring of the trachea: the thyrohyoid membrane, the cricotracheal ligament, the cricothyroid membrane, or the lateral thyrohyoid ligament? The answer is the cricotracheal ligament. It is a fibrous ring that continues the wall of the airway downward.

Question 6033. Which membrane lies deep to the laryngeal mucosa and stretches from the vocal ligament down to the upper border of the cricoid cartilage? The answer is the cricothyroid membrane, also called the conus elasticus. Its thickened upper free edge is the vocal ligament, which forms the core of the vocal fold.

Question 6034. Which membrane forms the framework of the aryepiglottic fold and gives its lower free edge to the vestibular ligament: the quadrangular membrane, the cricothyroid membrane, the thyrohyoid membrane, or the cricotracheal ligament? The answer is the quadrangular membrane. It runs from the side of the epiglottis back to the arytenoid cartilage. Its lower edge is free and forms the vestibular ligament above the vocal fold.

Question 6035. A described frontal section through the larynx shows a shield of cartilage in front whose two plates meet at an angle, and below it a narrow ring. Which two cartilages are shown, and which of them is a complete ring? The answer is the thyroid cartilage above and the cricoid cartilage below, and the cricoid is the complete ring.

Question 6036. A described transverse section through the neck shows a gland wrapped around the front and the sides of the airway below the larynx, with its two lobes joined across the midline. Which gland is shown? The answer is the thyroid gland. Its isthmus crosses in front of the second and third rings of the trachea.

Question 6037. Which ridge on the outer surface of the thyroid cartilage runs downward and forward and gives attachment to the sternothyroid and thyrohyoid muscles? The answer is the oblique line. The inferior constrictor of the pharynx also takes origin from the area behind it.

Question 6038. The superior horns of the thyroid cartilage reach which structure, and the inferior horns which: the hyoid bone and the cricoid cartilage, the cricoid and the hyoid, the mandible and the cricoid, or the hyoid and the trachea? The answer is the hyoid bone above and the cricoid cartilage below. The inferior horn forms the cricothyroid joint.

Question 6039. True or false: the epiglottis lies above the level of the vocal folds and is covered by mucous membrane on both of its surfaces. The answer is true. Its anterior surface faces the tongue and forms the valleculae on each side of the midline fold. Its posterior surface faces the laryngeal inlet.

Question 6040. Which of these pairs belongs together: the arytenoid cartilage with the vocal process, the corniculate cartilage with the vocal process, the cuneiform cartilage with the muscular process, or the thyroid cartilage with the muscular process? The answer is the arytenoid cartilage with the vocal process. The vocal ligament attaches to the vocal process at the front of the cartilage.

Question 6041. A described specimen shows a small rounded elevation at the top of each arytenoid cartilage, lying in the back part of the aryepiglottic fold. Which cartilage forms that elevation? The answer is the corniculate cartilage. It caps the apex of the arytenoid cartilage and is covered by the fold.

Question 6042. Which artery enters the larynx through the thyrohyoid membrane, accompanied by the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve: the superior thyroid artery, the superior laryngeal artery, the inferior laryngeal artery, or the ascending pharyngeal artery? The answer is the superior laryngeal artery. It is a branch of the superior thyroid artery and supplies the larynx above the vocal folds.

Question 6043. The laryngeal prominence in the front of the neck is formed by the meeting of which two structures: the two laminae of the thyroid cartilage, the two horns of the thyroid cartilage, the cricoid and the thyroid cartilages, or the epiglottis and the thyroid cartilage? The answer is the two laminae of the thyroid cartilage. They meet in the midline at an angle that projects forward.

Question 6044. A described lateral radiograph of the neck shows a curved soft tissue shadow standing up in front of the airway between the hyoid bone and the thyroid cartilage. Which structure casts that shadow? The answer is the epiglottis. Its broad upper end lies behind the tongue and its stalk is fixed to the back of the thyroid cartilage.

Question 6045. A described transverse section is cut through the larynx at the level of the vocal folds. Where does the airway lie in that section, and what lies on each side of it? The answer is that the airway lies in the middle, between the two vocal folds, with the thyroid cartilage in front and the arytenoid cartilages behind.

Question 6046. Which part of the cricoid cartilage carries the arytenoid cartilages and slopes downward and forward on each side? The answer is the lamina, the tall part behind. It is broad in the midline and carries a facet on each side for the arytenoid cartilage.

Question 6047. A described dissection of the front of the neck shows a broad membrane stretched between the hyoid bone above and the thyroid cartilage below, with a small nodule of cartilage in its thickened lateral edge. Which membrane is shown, and what is the nodule? The answer is the thyrohyoid membrane, and the nodule is the triticeal cartilage. The membrane is pierced by the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve.

Question 6048. Which of these laryngeal cartilages is unpaired: the thyroid cartilage, the arytenoid cartilage, the corniculate cartilage, or the cuneiform cartilage? The answer is the thyroid cartilage. The cricoid cartilage and the epiglottis are also unpaired, so three of the nine cartilages are single.

Question 6049. Name, spoken as a list, the three unpaired cartilages of the larynx. The answer is the thyroid cartilage, the cricoid cartilage and the epiglottis. Together they form the front wall and the roof of the laryngeal framework. The paired cartilages, the arytenoids with the corniculate and the cuneiform above them, lie behind.

Question 6050. A constructed illustration: a blow to the front of the neck is followed by noisy breathing, and the described break is in the only cartilage of the airway that forms a complete ring. Which cartilage is broken? The answer is the cricoid cartilage, whose arch in front is the part usually fractured. The folds above it may swell and narrow the airway. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6051. Which U-shaped bone lies above the larynx and is the only bone of the body that does not articulate with another bone? The answer is the hyoid bone. It is held in place by muscles and ligaments, with its body in front and its greater horns projecting backward.

Question 6052. Which statement about the attachment of the epiglottis is correct: its stalk is fixed to the back of the thyroid cartilage, it is fixed to the front of the thyroid cartilage, it is fixed to the cricoid cartilage, or it is attached to no cartilage at all? The answer is that its stalk is fixed to the back of the thyroid cartilage. The thyroepiglottic ligament holds the stalk in place.

Question 6053. A described radiograph of an older adult shows plates of bone laid down within the thyroid cartilage. Which cartilages of the larynx commonly ossify with age? The answer is the thyroid and the cricoid cartilages, and the arytenoid cartilages less often. This change begins in adult life and is a normal part of aging rather than a disease.

Question 6054. A described specimen of the larynx is opened from behind, showing the epiglottis above, then a pair of thick folds, and below them a pair of thinner folds with sharp free edges and a gap between them. Which pair are the vocal folds? The answer is the lower pair, with the sharp free edges. The thicker pair above are the vestibular folds, and the recess between them is the laryngeal ventricle.

Question 6055. Counting both sides, how many cartilages make up the larynx: three, six, nine, or twelve? The answer is nine. Three are unpaired, the thyroid, the cricoid and the epiglottis, and three are paired, the arytenoid, the corniculate and the cuneiform. The cuneiform cartilages are small and may be absent, which is a normal variation.

Question 6056. Which pair of folds stretches from the back of the thyroid cartilage to the arytenoid cartilages and produces the voice? The answer is the vocal folds. Each fold contains the vocal ligament, which is its core, and the vocalis muscle beside it. The gap between the two folds is the rima glottidis.

Question 6057. Which two of the folds in the larynx are the true vocal cords: the vocal folds, the vestibular folds, both pairs, or neither pair? The answer is the vocal folds, which are the true cords. The vestibular folds are the false cords and take no part in producing sound. The vocal folds contain the vocal ligament and are covered by stratified squamous epithelium.

Question 6058. What is the name of the narrow gap between the two vocal folds through which air passes? The answer is the rima glottidis. It has a front part between the membranous folds and a back part between the arytenoid cartilages. In the adult it is the narrowest part of the airway above the trachea.

Question 6059. Which fold lies immediately above the vocal fold and forms the upper lip of the laryngeal ventricle? The answer is the vestibular fold. It contains the vestibular ligament, which is the free lower edge of the quadrangular membrane. It takes no part in producing sound.

Question 6060. What is the name of the recess between the vestibular fold above and the vocal fold below, from which a small pouch runs upward? The answer is the laryngeal ventricle, and the pouch is the laryngeal saccule. The saccule lies between the vestibular fold and the thyroid cartilage and is rich in mucous glands.

Question 6061. A described view of the open larynx from above shows two pale folds meeting in front at a sharp point, with a dark gap between them. What is the point called, and what is the gap? The answer is the anterior commissure, and the gap is the rima glottidis.

Question 6062. Which statement about the rima glottidis is correct: it is a narrow wedge during quiet breathing and a narrow slit during speech, it is wide open during speech, it keeps the same shape at all times, or it is closed during quiet breathing? The answer is that it is a narrow wedge during quiet breathing and a narrow slit during speech. In a deep breath it opens into a wide diamond.

Question 6063. Which part of the laryngeal cavity lies below the vocal folds and above the lower border of the cricoid cartilage? The answer is the subglottic space. It is lined by respiratory epithelium and is circular in section. In the infant it is the narrowest part of the whole airway, which is why swelling there is dangerous.

Question 6064. Which artery supplies the larynx below the vocal folds, and with which nerve does it run? The answer is the inferior laryngeal artery, and it runs with the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It is a branch of the inferior thyroid artery. It enters the larynx behind the cricothyroid joint, below the lower border of the inferior constrictor.

Question 6065. Which artery supplies the lining of the larynx below the vocal folds: the superior laryngeal artery, the inferior laryngeal artery, the superior thyroid artery, or the lingual artery? The answer is the inferior laryngeal artery, a branch of the inferior thyroid artery. It ascends with the recurrent laryngeal nerve and enters the larynx behind the cricothyroid joint.

Question 6066. Lymph from the part of the larynx above the vocal folds drains to which group of nodes, and why does a growth on the vocal fold itself spread late? The answer is the upper deep cervical nodes, and the vocal folds have almost no lymph vessels. The part below the folds drains to the lower deep cervical nodes and to the nodes in front of the trachea.

Question 6067. A constructed illustration: a person who has been hoarse for several weeks is found to have a growth on the sharp-edged fold inside the larynx. Which fold is described, and which kind of epithelium covers it? The answer is the vocal fold, covered by stratified squamous epithelium. It is the only part of the larynx lined that way. The diagnosis belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6068. True or false: the vestibular folds take part in the production of the voice. The answer is false. They contain the vestibular ligament and many mucous glands, and they help to keep the vocal folds moist. Sound is produced by the vocal folds below them, not by the vestibular folds.

Question 6069. Which of these pairs belongs together: the vocal fold with the vocal ligament, the vestibular fold with the vocal ligament, the vocal fold with the vestibular ligament, or the aryepiglottic fold with the cricoid cartilage? The answer is the vocal fold with the vocal ligament. The vestibular fold contains the vestibular ligament.

Question 6070. What is the name of the small pouch that runs upward from the front of the laryngeal ventricle, between the vestibular fold and the thyroid cartilage? The answer is the laryngeal saccule. Its wall is packed with mucous glands, and their secretion lubricates the vocal folds.

Question 6071. Which muscle lies immediately lateral to the vocal ligament and forms the bulk of the vocal fold? The answer is the vocalis muscle, the medial part of the thyroarytenoid. When it contracts it shortens and thickens the fold and raises the pitch of the voice.

Question 6072. Which statement about the vocal ligament is correct: it is the thickened upper edge of the cricothyroid membrane, the lower edge of the quadrangular membrane, part of the thyrohyoid membrane, or part of the epiglottis? The answer is the thickened upper edge of the cricothyroid membrane, which runs from the thyroid cartilage to the vocal process. The lower edge of the quadrangular membrane is the vestibular ligament.

Question 6073. A described view of the larynx during quiet breathing shows two pale folds separated in front by a dark triangle, and behind them a wider gap between two small cartilages. What are the two parts of the rima glottidis shown? The answer is the intermembranous part in front and the intercartilaginous part behind. The front part lies between the vocal folds and the back part between the arytenoid cartilages.

Question 6074. Which nerve carries sensation from the lining of the larynx above the vocal folds: the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the recurrent laryngeal nerve, or the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. It pierces the thyrohyoid membrane with the superior laryngeal artery. The recurrent laryngeal nerve carries sensation from below the folds.

Question 6075. A constructed illustration: after a described operation on one side of the neck, the voice is hoarse and the fold on that side lies motionless near the midline. Which nerve has most likely been injured? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. Its fibers supply every intrinsic muscle of the larynx except the cricothyroid, so the adductors act unopposed and the fold comes to rest near the midline.

Question 6076. Name, spoken as a list, the two structures attached to the vocal process of the arytenoid cartilage. The answer is the vocal ligament and the vocalis muscle. The process is the small forward-pointing tip of the cartilage. The vocal fold is formed over these two structures.

Question 6077. A described frontal section through the larynx is cut first just above the vocal folds and then just below them. How do the two sections differ? The answer is that above the folds the cavity is wider and leads up into the vestibule, while below them it narrows into the subglottic space.

Question 6078. Which muscle is the only one that can open the rima glottidis and so widen the airway? The answer is the posterior cricoarytenoid. It arises from the back of the lamina of the cricoid cartilage and pulls the muscular process of the arytenoid backward and medially. That movement swings the vocal process outward.

Question 6079. Which muscle pulls the vocal fold inward toward the midline: the posterior cricoarytenoid, the lateral cricoarytenoid, the cricothyroid, or the thyroepiglottic? The answer is the lateral cricoarytenoid. It runs from the upper border of the arch of the cricoid cartilage to the muscular process of the arytenoid. Pulling that process forward rotates the vocal process inward and closes the gap.

Question 6080. Which muscle runs from the front of the cricoid cartilage up to the lower border of the thyroid cartilage and stretches the vocal fold? The answer is the cricothyroid. When it contracts it tilts the thyroid cartilage forward and so lengthens the fold, raising the pitch. It is the only intrinsic muscle supplied by the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve.

Question 6081. Which nerve supplies the cricothyroid muscle: the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the recurrent laryngeal nerve, or the pharyngeal plexus? The answer is the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. It runs down on the cricothyroid muscle beside the superior thyroid artery. Injury to it during an operation on the neck weakens the upper range of the voice.

Question 6082. A constructed illustration: a person chokes on a large piece of food, and the described reflex closes the entrance of the larynx. Which muscle closes that entrance? The answer is the aryepiglottic muscle, together with the oblique arytenoid muscles. The fibers run in the aryepiglottic fold from the side of the epiglottis to the arytenoid cartilage and draw the fold inward.

Question 6083. Which muscle lies across the back of the larynx between the two arytenoid cartilages and pulls them together? The answer is the transverse arytenoid. It is a single muscle that spans the gap between the two cartilages. Its action closes the back part of the rima glottidis.

Question 6084. Which muscle shortens and slackens the vocal fold when it acts alone: the cricothyroid, the vocalis, the posterior cricoarytenoid, or the transverse arytenoid? The answer is the vocalis. It pulls the arytenoid cartilage forward toward the thyroid cartilage and so shortens the fold. The cricothyroid does the opposite, lengthening the fold and raising the pitch.

Question 6085. A described dissection of the back of the larynx shows two muscles crossing each other over the back of the cricoid cartilage. Which muscles are they, and what do they do? The answer is the transverse arytenoid and the oblique arytenoid muscles. The transverse fibers run straight across and the oblique fibers cross to the opposite side.

Question 6086. Which structure does the recurrent laryngeal nerve pass beneath as it enters the larynx, and behind which joint does it lie? The answer is the lower border of the inferior constrictor muscle, and it enters behind the cricothyroid joint. It runs up in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus with the inferior laryngeal artery.

Question 6087. A constructed illustration: a person reports that the voice tires and that the upper notes of a song are lost, while the rest of the voice is normal. Which muscle is weak? The answer is the cricothyroid, supplied by the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. Without it the vocal fold cannot be lengthened and the higher notes cannot be reached. The assessment belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6088. Which of these pairs belongs together: the cricothyroid with the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the cricothyroid with the recurrent laryngeal nerve, the posterior cricoarytenoid with the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, or the vocalis with the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the cricothyroid with the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. All the other intrinsic muscles are supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

Question 6089. Which of these muscles is not an intrinsic muscle of the larynx: the cricothyroid, the thyroarytenoid, the sternothyroid, or the aryepiglottic? The answer is the sternothyroid. An intrinsic muscle has both of its attachments on the cartilages of the larynx, while the sternothyroid runs from the sternum to the thyroid cartilage and moves the whole larynx.

Question 6090. Which extrinsic muscle runs from the sternum up to the oblique line of the thyroid cartilage and steadies the larynx while the cricothyroid tilts it? The answer is the sternothyroid. Without it the pull of the cricothyroid would drag the whole larynx instead of tilting the thyroid cartilage forward.

Question 6091. A constructed illustration: after a described operation on both sides of the neck, breathing becomes noisy and both folds lie close to the midline. Which nerve has been injured, and which muscle has been lost? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve, and the loss is of the posterior cricoarytenoid, the only abductor. With both folds fixed near the midline the airway is narrow. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6092. Which muscle spans the gap between the two arytenoid cartilages at the back of the larynx? The answer is the transverse arytenoid. It is a single, unpaired muscle that lies behind the mucous membrane of the back wall. Its fibers run from one cartilage across to the other.

Question 6093. Which statement about the thyroarytenoid muscle is correct: it adducts the vocal fold and shortens it, it abducts the fold and lengthens it, it has no effect on the fold, or it acts only on the epiglottis? The answer is that it adducts and shortens the fold. Its lateral part pulls the arytenoid forward and inward, and its medial part is the vocalis.

Question 6094. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the lamina of the thyroid cartilage, with its medial edge projecting into the laryngeal cavity as a ridge covered by mucosa? The answer is the thyroarytenoid muscle, whose medial edge is the vocalis. That ridge, covered by mucosa, forms the vocal fold. The lateral part of the muscle lies in the wall of the larynx beside the thyroid cartilage.

Question 6095. A described dissection shows muscle fibers running from the back of the cricoid lamina upward and laterally to the muscular process of the arytenoid cartilage. Which muscle is shown, and what happens when it contracts? The answer is the posterior cricoarytenoid. Pulling the muscular process backward and medially swings the vocal process outward and opens the rima glottidis.

Question 6096. Which of these pairs belongs together: the cricothyroid with tension of the vocal fold, the posterior cricoarytenoid with tension of the vocal fold, the vocalis with abduction of the vocal fold, or the lateral cricoarytenoid with abduction? The answer is the cricothyroid with tension of the vocal fold. It lengthens and tightens the fold.

Question 6097. Which statement about the aryepiglottic muscle is correct: it narrows the entrance of the larynx during swallowing, it opens the rima glottidis, it lengthens the vocal fold, or it has no action on the larynx? The answer is that it narrows the entrance of the larynx during swallowing. It lies in the aryepiglottic fold and continues the oblique arytenoid muscle forward to the epiglottis.

Question 6098. A constructed illustration: after a described operation high in the neck, a person has lost the sensation that protects the larynx and coughs after drinking. Which nerve is injured, and which part of the lining has lost its sensation? The answer is the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, and the lining above the vocal folds has lost its sensation. The protective reflex that closes the inlet is therefore weakened.

Question 6099. A described cross-sectional image taken at the level of the vocal folds shows a small muscle within each fold, just lateral to the sharp edge, and a larger muscle outside it in the wall of the larynx. Which muscles are shown? The answer is the vocalis within the fold and the thyroarytenoid, of which the vocalis is the medial part.

Question 6100. A described larynx is watched from above during quiet breathing and then during speech. Which muscles are chiefly active in each state? The answer is the posterior cricoarytenoid during quiet breathing, which opens the folds, and the lateral cricoarytenoid with the arytenoid muscles during speech, which bring the folds together.

To summarize, the pharynx is a fibromuscular half-tube that hangs from the base of the skull and is divided into the nasopharynx, the oropharynx and the laryngopharynx, each named for the cavity behind it. The auditory tube opens into its lateral wall, the palatine tonsils lie between the two arches, and a weak point between the thyropharyngeal and cricopharyngeal parts of the inferior constrictor is where a pouch of mucosa may protrude. The larynx is built from nine cartilages, three unpaired and three paired, and its cavity is narrowed at the vocal folds, which contain the vocal ligament and the vocalis and are covered by stratified squamous epithelium. The cricothyroid lengthens and tightens the fold and is the only intrinsic muscle supplied by the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, while the posterior cricoarytenoid is the only abductor and every other intrinsic muscle is supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve. Lymph above the folds drains to the upper deep cervical nodes and lymph below them to the lower deep cervical nodes, and the folds themselves have almost no lymph vessels. Naming each structure together with its nerve and its neighbor is what fixes this region in memory, and this chapter is educational material that does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program or the judgment of a teacher.
