# Chapter 53 — Cranial Nerves, Part Two

This chapter continues the survey of the twelve cranial nerves and takes up the six that serve the face, the ear, the throat and the tongue: the seventh, the eighth, the ninth, the tenth, the eleventh and the twelfth. It holds one hundred questions, and each one states its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind from one item to the next. The nerves are followed from the brainstem to the openings they use in the skull, through the structures they supply, and on to the way a lesion of each one shows itself. Every full name is spelled out, so the chapter can be listened to on its own. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 5201. Which cranial nerve supplies the muscles of facial expression, the stapedius and the posterior belly of the digastric? The answer is the seventh cranial nerve, the facial nerve. It leaves the brainstem between the pons and the medulla, passes through the internal acoustic meatus, and leaves the skull through the stylomastoid foramen.

Question 5202. Which opening does the facial nerve use to leave the skull: the jugular foramen, the foramen ovale, the stylomastoid foramen, or the hypoglossal canal? The answer is the stylomastoid foramen. It lies at the base of the skull just behind the styloid process. The jugular foramen carries the ninth, tenth and eleventh cranial nerves, and the hypoglossal canal carries the twelfth.

Question 5203. In which swelling along the facial canal do the sensory cell bodies of the facial nerve lie? The answer is the geniculate ganglion. The canal runs sideways, turns sharply backward at that swelling, and then descends to the stylomastoid foramen. The greater petrosal nerve is given off there.

Question 5204. Which nerve carries taste from the anterior two thirds of the tongue: the lingual nerve, the chorda tympani, the hypoglossal nerve, or the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the chorda tympani, a branch of the facial nerve. It joins the lingual nerve, which belongs to the trigeminal nerve, and travels with it to the tongue.

Question 5205. Which cranial nerve carries the parasympathetic fibers that make the submandibular and sublingual glands secrete? The answer is the seventh cranial nerve. The fibers leave the facial nerve as the chorda tympani and end in the submandibular ganglion. From there short fibers pass to the two glands in the floor of the mouth.

Question 5206. Which tiny muscle of the middle ear is supplied by the facial nerve, and what happens when that supply is lost? The answer is the stapedius. It attaches to the stapes and stiffens the chain of ossicles when a sound is loud. A person with a damaged facial nerve may find ordinary sounds uncomfortably loud on that side.

Question 5207. The chorda tympani, a branch of the facial nerve, joins which nerve in the infratemporal region, and what does that nerve carry? The answer is the lingual nerve, which carries general sensation from the anterior two thirds of the tongue. The taste fibers travel with it but belong to the facial nerve.

Question 5208. The greater petrosal nerve carries parasympathetic fibers to which ganglion, and which gland does that ganglion then supply? The answer is the pterygopalatine ganglion, which supplies the lacrimal gland. The same pathway reaches the glands of the nose and the palate. A lesion before the geniculate ganglion therefore dries the eye as well as weakening the face.

Question 5209. Which nerve arises at the geniculate ganglion and runs forward through the skull to reach the pterygopalatine ganglion? The answer is the greater petrosal nerve. It carries the parasympathetic fibers of the facial nerve. The lesser petrosal nerve, which runs close to it, belongs to the ninth cranial nerve instead.

Question 5210. A described dissection shows a nerve leaving the skull behind the ear and dividing into branches inside a gland in front of the ear. Which gland is that, and does the nerve supply it? The answer is the parotid gland, and the facial nerve does not supply it. The nerve only passes through the gland on its way to the face.

Question 5211. A described view of the face shows five branches fanning out from in front of the ear. Which branch runs forward below the angle of the jaw and supplies the muscles of the lower lip? The answer is the marginal mandibular branch. Injury to it leaves the lower lip weak while the rest of the face still moves.

Question 5212. True or false: in a lesion of the fibers that reach the facial nucleus from the motor cortex, the forehead is spared. The answer is true. The motor neurons for the upper face receive fibers from both sides of the cortex, while those for the lower face receive fibers from the opposite side only.

Question 5213. A constructed illustration: during an operation on the parotid gland a branch running along the lower border of the jaw is divided, and later the person cannot pull down the corner of the mouth. Which branch was divided? The answer is the marginal mandibular branch of the facial nerve. It runs just below the angle of the mandible.

Question 5214. Name two muscles supplied by the facial nerve that are not muscles of facial expression, spoken as a list. The answer is the stapedius and the posterior belly of the digastric. The stylohyoid muscle is a third. All three lie away from the face, which is why they are often forgotten.

Question 5215. Which of these pairs belongs together: the facial nerve with the stylomastoid foramen, the facial nerve with the jugular foramen, the facial nerve with the foramen ovale, or the facial nerve with the hypoglossal canal? The answer is the facial nerve with the stylomastoid foramen. The jugular foramen carries the ninth, tenth and eleventh nerves.

Question 5216. A described series of sections through a temporal bone follows a narrow canal that runs outward, turns sharply backward at a swelling, and then descends to open at the base of the skull. Which nerve runs in that canal, and what is the swelling? The answer is the facial nerve, and the swelling is the geniculate ganglion.

Question 5217. A lesion in the facial canal just before the geniculate ganglion would interrupt which functions: only the movement of the face, only the sense of taste, only the dampening of loud sounds, or the movement of the face together with tearing and taste? The answer is the movement of the face together with tearing and taste. The branches that carry those fibers leave the trunk distal to that point.

Question 5218. Which brainstem nucleus gives the facial nerve its motor fibers, and where does the nerve emerge from the brainstem? The answer is the facial nucleus in the pons, and the nerve emerges at the junction of the pons and the medulla. It appears in the angle between the pons, the medulla and the cerebellum.

Question 5219. A constructed illustration: one side of a face is weak from the forehead down, the eye on that side cannot be closed, and tears no longer form on it. Where does the lesion most likely lie? The answer is the facial canal, proximal to the geniculate ganglion. Weakness of the whole side shows a lower motor neuron lesion, and the loss of tears places the damage before the greater petrosal nerve.

Question 5220. A lesion at the stylomastoid foramen would interrupt which of these: the movement of the lips, the sense of taste in the anterior tongue, the formation of tears, or the dampening action of the stapedius? The answer is the movement of the lips. The chorda tympani, the greater petrosal nerve and the branch to the stapedius all leave the trunk inside the facial canal.

Question 5221. Which arrangement explains why the forehead escapes in a stroke but not in Bell palsy: the upper face receives fibers from both sides of the cortex, it has no cortical supply, the facial nucleus lies below the lesion, or the trigeminal nerve supplies it? The answer is that the upper face receives fibers from both sides of the cortex. Bell palsy damages the nerve itself, so the whole side is weak.

Question 5222. Which cranial nerve carries the sense of hearing from the inner ear to the brainstem? The answer is the eighth cranial nerve, the vestibulocochlear nerve. It is a purely sensory nerve with two divisions. The cochlear division serves hearing and the vestibular division serves balance.

Question 5223. What are the two divisions of the eighth cranial nerve, and what does each serve? The answer is the cochlear division, which serves hearing, and the vestibular division, which serves balance and the sense of the position of the head. The two run together from the inner ear to the brainstem.

Question 5224. In which ganglion do the sensory cell bodies of the cochlear nerve lie? The answer is the spiral ganglion. It lies within the modiolus, the bony core around which the cochlea coils. Its fibers run from the hair cells of the organ of Corti to the cochlear nuclei.

Question 5225. Which structure sits against the oval window at the entrance to the inner ear: the malleus, the incus, the stapes, or the tympanic membrane? The answer is the stapes. Its footplate fits the oval window and passes the vibrations into the fluid of the inner ear. The tympanic membrane lies at the outer end of the chain.

Question 5226. On which membrane do the hair cells for hearing rest, and in which channel of the cochlea do they lie? The answer is the basilar membrane, in the cochlear duct. The cochlear duct is the middle of the three channels and contains endolymph. Above it lies the scala vestibuli and below it the scala tympani, both filled with perilymph.

Question 5227. A described section through the inner ear shows a spiral tube divided into three channels that meet at the apex. Which channels contain perilymph? The answer is the scala vestibuli and the scala tympani. They communicate with each other at the apex through the helicotrema.

Question 5228. The vestibular division carries fibers from which receptors? The answer is the cristae of the three semicircular canals and the maculae of the utricle and the saccule. The cristae respond to rotation, the maculae to tilting and straight-line acceleration. The fibers travel to the vestibular nuclei in the medulla and the pons.

Question 5229. True or false: the eighth cranial nerve carries parasympathetic fibers to the inner ear. The answer is false. The eighth nerve is purely sensory and has no motor or secretomotor fibers at all. The nerve beside it in the internal acoustic meatus, the facial nerve, carries the parasympathetic fibers of that region.

Question 5230. A described radiograph of the skull base shows a tumor in the angle between the cerebellum and the pons, and the person has ringing in one ear and poor hearing on that side. Which division of which nerve is involved? The answer is the vestibular division of the eighth cranial nerve. A tumor there often begins on the vestibular fibers and presses on the cochlear fibers.

Question 5231. A constructed illustration: a person has attacks of a spinning sensation and unsteady walking, while hearing is normal on both sides. Which division of the eighth nerve is most likely affected? The answer is the vestibular division. The cochlear division is spared, which is why hearing remains normal. The receptors involved lie in the semicircular canals, the utricle and the saccule.

Question 5232. Which of these pairs belongs together: the semicircular canals with rotation of the head, the semicircular canals with straight-line acceleration, the cochlea with balance, or the utricle with hearing? The answer is the semicircular canals with rotation of the head. Their cristae respond to turning. The utricle and the saccule respond instead to straight-line acceleration and to gravity.

Question 5233. A described cross section of the internal acoustic meatus shows four bundles of nerve fibers in four quadrants. Which bundle lies in the upper front quadrant? The answer is the facial nerve. The cochlear nerve lies below it in the lower front quadrant. The two divisions of the vestibular nerve occupy the two rear quadrants.

Question 5234. In the internal acoustic meatus, which nerve lies in front of the vestibulocochlear nerve, and what does that position explain? The answer is the facial nerve. Because the facial nerve lies in front and above, a tumor of the eighth nerve often presses on it.

Question 5235. Which two openings of the inner ear are closed by membranes, and which structure closes each? The answer is the oval window, closed by the footplate of the stapes, and the round window, closed by the secondary tympanic membrane. The round window gives the fluid somewhere to move when the stapes pushes inward.

Question 5236. A constructed illustration: a person is struck on the side of the head and afterward hears a continuous ringing sound with reduced hearing, while balance is normal and the eardrum is intact. Which part of the ear most likely holds the injury? The answer is the cochlear division of the eighth nerve or the organ of Corti. The vestibular fibers and the eardrum are spared, since balance and the drum are normal.

Question 5237. Which statement about the fibers of the eighth cranial nerve is correct: the cochlear fibers end in the cochlear nuclei of the brainstem, the cochlear fibers end in the thalamus directly, the vestibular fibers end in the spinal cord, or all of its fibers cross to the opposite side? The answer is that the cochlear fibers end in the cochlear nuclei of the brainstem. The vestibular fibers end mainly in the vestibular nuclei.

Question 5238. A described transverse section of the brainstem at the junction of the pons and the medulla shows a large nerve entering from the side. Which nerve is it, and where do most of its fibers end? The answer is the eighth cranial nerve, and most of its fibers end in the cochlear and vestibular nuclei of that region. The vestibular nuclei lie deeper, beneath the floor of the fourth ventricle.

Question 5239. Which cranial nerve carries the secretomotor fibers that make the parotid gland produce saliva? The answer is the ninth cranial nerve, the glossopharyngeal nerve. Its fibers reach the gland by a long route through the middle ear and the infratemporal fossa. The facial nerve passes through the parotid gland but does not supply it.

Question 5240. Through which opening does the glossopharyngeal nerve leave the skull: the stylomastoid foramen, the jugular foramen, the foramen rotundum, or the hypoglossal canal? The answer is the jugular foramen. The ninth, tenth and eleventh cranial nerves all pass through it, together with the internal jugular vein. The stylomastoid foramen carries the facial nerve.

Question 5241. Which is the only muscle supplied by the ninth cranial nerve? The answer is the stylopharyngeus. It runs from the styloid process down into the wall of the pharynx and helps to lift the pharynx during swallowing. Every other muscle of the pharynx is supplied by the tenth cranial nerve.

Question 5242. From which part of the tongue does the ninth cranial nerve carry taste: the anterior two thirds, the posterior one third, the whole tongue, or the tip only? The answer is the posterior one third. The posterior third is also the part from which the ninth nerve carries general sensation.

Question 5243. Which nerve arises from the inferior ganglion of the ninth cranial nerve, forms a plexus on the promontory of the middle ear, and gives off the lesser petrosal nerve? The answer is the tympanic nerve. It carries the parasympathetic fibers destined for the parotid gland. The lesser petrosal nerve leaves that plexus and ends in the otic ganglion.

Question 5244. Which of these structures does the ninth cranial nerve supply with general sensation: the posterior one third of the tongue, the anterior two thirds of the tongue, the lower lip, or the skin of the forehead? The answer is the posterior one third of the tongue. The anterior two thirds take their general sensation from the lingual nerve.

Question 5245. The carotid sinus nerve, a branch of the ninth cranial nerve, ends beside which two structures in the neck, and what does each do? The answer is the carotid sinus and the carotid body. The sinus is watched for changes in blood pressure and the body for changes in the oxygen content of the blood.

Question 5246. In the gag reflex, which nerve carries the signal in and which nerve carries the movement out? The answer is the ninth cranial nerve inward and the tenth cranial nerve outward. Touching the back of the throat sends sensation along the ninth nerve to the brainstem. The muscles of the pharynx then contract through the tenth nerve.

Question 5247. True or false: the ninth cranial nerve carries both taste and general sensation from the back of the tongue. The answer is true. Its fibers for taste end in the nucleus of the tractus solitarius, and its fibers for touch and pain end in the spinal nucleus of the trigeminal nerve.

Question 5248. A described dissection of the neck shows a nerve lying between the internal and external carotid arteries and giving off a small branch that climbs to the fork of the carotid. Which nerve is shown, and which structure does that branch watch? The answer is the ninth cranial nerve, and the branch is the carotid sinus nerve. It watches the carotid sinus, a small swelling at the fork.

Question 5249. Which of these pairs belongs together: the glossopharyngeal nerve with the parotid gland, the glossopharyngeal nerve with the lacrimal gland, the facial nerve with the parotid gland, or the hypoglossal nerve with the submandibular gland? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve with the parotid gland. It passes through the parotid gland without supplying it.

Question 5250. A described section of the brainstem shows a nucleus lying deep in the medulla, close to the fourth ventricle, whose fibers travel to the parotid gland. Which nucleus is it? The answer is the inferior salivatory nucleus. Its fibers join the ninth cranial nerve and leave by the tympanic nerve.

Question 5251. Name the three structures from which the ninth cranial nerve carries sensation, spoken as a list. The answer is the posterior one third of the tongue, the tonsil, and the upper part of the pharynx. The middle ear is a fourth. All of these are places a listener can point to in the mouth and throat.

Question 5252. A described specimen shows a nerve that leaves the skull through the jugular foramen and curves forward to lie on a long slender muscle running from the styloid process to the pharynx. Which nerve is shown, and which movement does that muscle help to perform? The answer is the ninth cranial nerve, and the muscle is the stylopharyngeus, which lifts the pharynx during swallowing.

Question 5253. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the throat the gag reflex is absent on one side and taste is lost at the back of the tongue on that side, while taste at the front is normal. Which nerve was injured? The answer is the ninth cranial nerve. The gag reflex needs it for the incoming signal, and it carries taste from the posterior third.

Question 5254. Which statement about the ninth cranial nerve is correct: it carries parasympathetic fibers to the parotid gland, it carries motor fibers to the muscles of the face, it carries taste from the anterior two thirds of the tongue, or it leaves the skull through the stylomastoid foramen? The answer is that it carries parasympathetic fibers to the parotid gland.

Question 5255. Which cranial nerve supplies the muscles of the larynx and the greater part of the pharynx? The answer is the tenth cranial nerve, the vagus nerve. It is the longest cranial nerve and reaches from the neck into the abdomen. It also carries parasympathetic fibers to the heart, the lungs and the gut.

Question 5256. Through which opening does the vagus nerve leave the skull, and which other nerves pass through the same opening? The answer is the jugular foramen, through which the ninth and eleventh cranial nerves pass as well. The internal jugular vein leaves the skull through the same opening. The vagus then runs down the neck inside the carotid sheath.

Question 5257. Which muscle of the soft palate is one of the few that the vagus nerve does not supply? The answer is the tensor veli palatini, which is supplied by the trigeminal nerve. All the other muscles of the palate are reached through the pharyngeal plexus of the vagus. In the pharynx the stylopharyngeus is a separate exception.

Question 5258. Which branch of the vagus nerve supplies the cricothyroid muscle: the internal laryngeal nerve, the external laryngeal nerve, the recurrent laryngeal nerve, or the pharyngeal branch? The answer is the external laryngeal nerve. It is the smaller of the two branches of the superior laryngeal nerve. The internal laryngeal branch carries sensation from above the vocal folds.

Question 5259. Which nerve hooks around the aortic arch on the left side and around the subclavian artery on the right? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve, a branch of the vagus. On the left it turns beneath the arch close to the ligamentum arteriosum. On the right it turns beneath the subclavian artery before climbing back to the larynx.

Question 5260. Which muscles of the larynx does the recurrent laryngeal nerve supply: all of them, all of them except the cricothyroid, only the cricothyroid, or only the muscles of the epiglottis? The answer is all of them except the cricothyroid. That muscle takes its supply from the external laryngeal nerve. The recurrent nerve also carries sensation from below the vocal folds.

Question 5261. Which two vessels lie immediately in front of the vagus nerve in the carotid sheath? The answer is the common carotid artery medially and the internal jugular vein laterally. The vagus lies behind and between them. All three structures share the sheath, which runs from the base of the skull to the root of the neck.

Question 5262. True or false: the vagus nerve carries taste from the epiglottis. The answer is true. The fibers end in the nucleus of the tractus solitarius, the same nucleus that receives taste from the back of the tongue. The vagus also carries sensation from the lining of the larynx.

Question 5263. A described dissection of the neck shows a long nerve running down inside a fibrous sheath together with an artery and a vein. Which nerve is shown, what is the sheath, and how are the three arranged? The answer is the vagus nerve, inside the carotid sheath. The artery lies medial and the vein lateral, with the nerve behind and between them.

Question 5264. Which of these pairs belongs together: the vagus nerve with the jugular foramen, the vagus nerve with the stylomastoid foramen, the facial nerve with the jugular foramen, or the hypoglossal nerve with the foramen ovale? The answer is the vagus nerve with the jugular foramen. The ninth and eleventh cranial nerves leave by the same opening.

Question 5265. Which branch of the vagus nerve lies in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus and is at risk during operations on the thyroid gland? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It climbs upward in that groove to reach the larynx. Damage to it weakens the muscles of the vocal fold on the same side.

Question 5266. Which statement about the two vagus nerves below the lungs is correct: the left forms the anterior vagal trunk and the right the posterior trunk, the right forms the anterior trunk, the two join into a single trunk, or neither of them enters the abdomen? The answer is that the left forms the anterior vagal trunk and the right the posterior trunk.

Question 5267. A described specimen of the thorax shows a nerve on the left side descending in front of the arch of the aorta and then turning upward beneath it. Which nerve is shown, and what does it supply? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve. It supplies almost all the muscles of the larynx and carries sensation from below the vocal folds.

Question 5268. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the lower neck a person is hoarse and one vocal fold does not move, while swallowing and taste are normal. Which nerve was most likely injured? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve on that side. The fibers that carry sensation above the vocal folds lie elsewhere, so swallowing is spared. The care of such a person belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 5269. Which statement explains why a tumor at the apex of the right lung may first show as hoarseness: the right recurrent laryngeal nerve loops beneath the right subclavian artery close to the apex, the right phrenic nerve runs over the apex, the right vagus lies in the neck, or the trachea is pressed? The answer is that the right recurrent laryngeal nerve loops beneath the right subclavian artery close to the apex.

Question 5270. A constructed illustration: a person has a weak cough and a hoarse voice, and on the same side the soft palate droops and the pharynx does not move when the back of the throat is touched. Which nerve has been injured, and where does the injury most likely lie? The answer is the tenth cranial nerve, most likely where it leaves the skull at the jugular foramen.

Question 5271. A described transverse section of the neck at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra shows a fibrous sheath containing three structures. Which are they, and which of the three lies between the other two? The answer is the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve. The nerve lies behind and between them, inside the sheath.

Question 5272. Which cranial nerve supplies the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius muscles? The answer is the eleventh cranial nerve, the accessory nerve. It is a motor nerve with a cranial root and a spinal root. The spinal root arises from the upper part of the spinal cord and climbs into the skull.

Question 5273. The eleventh cranial nerve leaves the skull through which opening: the jugular foramen, the foramen magnum, the hypoglossal canal, or the stylomastoid foramen? The answer is the jugular foramen. Its spinal root, however, enters the skull through the foramen magnum before joining the cranial root. Both openings are part of its course.

Question 5274. From which part of the nervous system does the spinal root of the eleventh cranial nerve arise? The answer is the anterior horn of the upper five or six segments of the spinal cord. Its fibers leave the cord as a series of small rootlets between the front and back roots of the spinal nerves.

Question 5275. Which movement is most weakened when the eleventh cranial nerve is injured on the right side: shrugging the right shoulder, closing the right eye, protruding the tongue, or raising the right eyebrow? The answer is shrugging the right shoulder, because the trapezius on that side is weak. The trapezius also helps to rotate the shoulder blade when the arm is lifted.

Question 5276. Which two muscles form the front and back borders of the posterior triangle of the neck, and which cranial nerve crosses that triangle? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid in front and the trapezius behind, with the eleventh cranial nerve crossing the triangle. The nerve runs down toward the shoulder just beneath the skin, where it is easily injured.

Question 5277. A described dissection of the neck shows a thin nerve crossing a triangle between two large muscles and running downward toward the shoulder. Which nerve is shown, and what does it supply? The answer is the eleventh cranial nerve, and it supplies the trapezius. Because it lies just beneath the skin, it is easily injured.

Question 5278. Which of these pairs belongs together: the eleventh cranial nerve with the sternocleidomastoid, the eleventh cranial nerve with the tongue, the twelfth cranial nerve with the trapezius, or the tenth cranial nerve with the shoulder? The answer is the eleventh cranial nerve with the sternocleidomastoid. The same nerve supplies the trapezius as well. The twelfth nerve supplies the tongue.

Question 5279. A constructed illustration: after a biopsy of a lymph node in the back of the neck, a person cannot lift the arm above the horizontal on that side and the shoulder droops. Which nerve was injured? The answer is the eleventh cranial nerve in the posterior triangle. The trapezius no longer rotates the shoulder blade, so the arm cannot be raised above the horizontal.

Question 5280. Which part of the eleventh cranial nerve joins the vagus nerve and is carried with it to the muscles of the pharynx and the larynx? The answer is the cranial root. It arises from the nucleus ambiguus in the medulla. The spinal root, by contrast, supplies the sternocleidomastoid and the trapezius.

Question 5281. Which statement about the eleventh cranial nerve is correct: it carries sensation from the skin of the shoulder, it enters the skull through the foramen magnum and leaves through the jugular foramen, it supplies the muscles of the tongue, or it carries parasympathetic fibers to the larynx? The answer is that it enters through the foramen magnum and leaves through the jugular foramen. It carries no parasympathetic fibers.

Question 5282. A described coronal section through the base of the skull shows a small trunk passing upward through a wide opening in the occipital bone and then running sideways to a smaller opening. Which nerve is shown, and which are the two openings? The answer is the eleventh cranial nerve. It enters through the foramen magnum and leaves through the jugular foramen.

Question 5283. In a described section, the spinal root of the eleventh cranial nerve and the vertebral artery both pass through one opening at the base of the skull. Which opening is it, and how do the two lie in relation to each other? The answer is the foramen magnum, with the nerve behind the artery.

Question 5284. After the eleventh cranial nerve is divided on the right side, turning the head to the left becomes weak. Which muscle and which side of the body explain that weakness: the right sternocleidomastoid, the left sternocleidomastoid, the right trapezius, or the left trapezius? The answer is the right sternocleidomastoid. Each sternocleidomastoid turns the head to the opposite side.

Question 5285. Name the two muscles supplied by the spinal root of the eleventh cranial nerve, spoken as a list, and say which of them lifts the shoulder. The answer is the sternocleidomastoid and the trapezius, and the trapezius lifts the shoulder. The sternocleidomastoid turns the head to the opposite side. Both muscles are supplied on the same side as the nerve.

Question 5286. Which cranial nerve supplies the muscles of the tongue? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve. It supplies all the intrinsic and extrinsic muscles of the tongue except the palatoglossus. That one muscle is supplied by the vagus nerve instead.

Question 5287. Through which opening does the hypoglossal nerve leave the skull: the jugular foramen, the hypoglossal canal, the foramen ovale, or the internal acoustic meatus? The answer is the hypoglossal canal. It lies in the occipital bone just above the occipital condyle. The other three openings carry other nerves and vessels.

Question 5288. Which muscle of the tongue pushes it forward and out of the mouth when it contracts: the styloglossus, the hyoglossus, the genioglossus, or the palatoglossus? The answer is the genioglossus. It runs from the front of the mandible back into the tongue and pulls the tongue forward and down. The styloglossus pulls the tongue up and back instead.

Question 5289. Which of these muscles of the tongue is not supplied by the twelfth cranial nerve: the genioglossus, the hyoglossus, the styloglossus, or the palatoglossus? The answer is the palatoglossus, which is supplied by the vagus nerve. The other three, and all the intrinsic muscles, belong to the twelfth nerve.

Question 5290. Between which two structures does the hypoglossal nerve lie as it descends in the upper part of the neck? The answer is the internal carotid artery and the internal jugular vein. The nerve passes between them, lying lateral to the artery and medial to the vein. It then turns forward toward the tongue.

Question 5291. When the tongue is protruded, a person with a lesion of the twelfth cranial nerve on the left side shows the tip deviating in which direction: to the left, to the right, upward, or not at all? The answer is to the left, the side of the lesion. The intact right muscle pushes the tongue toward the weak side.

Question 5292. The hypoglossal nerve runs forward deep to which two muscles after it crosses the carotid arteries, and on which muscle does it come to lie? The answer is the posterior belly of the digastric and the stylohyoid, and it then lies on the hyoglossus. It continues forward deep to the mylohyoid. The lingual artery runs deep to the hyoglossus, below the nerve.

Question 5293. True or false: a lesion of the fibers that reach the hypoglossal nucleus from the motor cortex makes the tongue deviate toward the side of the brain lesion. The answer is false. It deviates away from the side of the brain lesion. A lesion of the nerve itself makes the tongue deviate toward the side of the lesion.

Question 5294. A described dissection of the floor of the mouth shows a nerve lying on the surface of a flat muscle that runs from the hyoid bone up into the tongue. Which nerve is shown, and on which muscle is it lying? The answer is the hypoglossal nerve, lying on the hyoglossus.

Question 5295. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the upper part of the neck a person's tongue deviates to the right when it is put out, and the right half of the tongue looks shrunken. Which nerve was injured? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve on the right side. Weakness of the right genioglossus lets the left side push the tongue to the right.

Question 5296. A described series of sections through the base of the skull follows a small canal that runs forward and slightly downward beside the occipital condyle. Which nerve passes through it, and which other structures does it carry? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, together with a small meningeal branch and an emissary vein.

Question 5297. In a described transverse section of the neck just below the base of the skull, one nerve lies between two large vessels. Name the nerve and the two vessels, and state which vessel lies medial to the nerve. The answer is the hypoglossal nerve, between the internal carotid artery medially and the internal jugular vein laterally.

Question 5298. Which statement explains why the tongue may be weak on both sides after a single small lesion in the medulla: the two hypoglossal nuclei lie close together in the midline, the genioglossus crosses the midline, the cortical fibers for the tongue cross twice, or the twelfth nerve supplies both sides of the tongue? The answer is that the two hypoglossal nuclei lie close together in the midline.

Question 5299. A constructed illustration: a person has a shrunken tongue that cannot be moved to either side, with weakness of the arms and the legs. Which nerve is failing, and what does that suggest about the site of the trouble? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, with loss of the motor neurons that supply it. A tongue weak on both sides points to the nerve cells rather than to one cortical pathway.

Question 5300. A described magnetic resonance image of the head and the upper part of the neck shows a nerve leaving the base of the skull beside the occipital condyle and running forward between two vessels. Which nerve is shown, and which group of muscles will it reach? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve, which will reach the muscles of the tongue.

To summarize, the seventh cranial nerve supplies the muscles of facial expression and carries taste from the anterior two thirds of the tongue and secretomotor fibers to the lacrimal, submandibular and sublingual glands, and it leaves the skull by the stylomastoid foramen. The eighth nerve is purely sensory, with a cochlear division for hearing and a vestibular division for balance, and it runs beside the facial nerve in the internal acoustic meatus. The ninth nerve supplies the stylopharyngeus, carries taste and sensation from the posterior third of the tongue and the pharynx, and sends parasympathetic fibers through the tympanic and lesser petrosal nerves to the parotid gland. The tenth nerve supplies the pharynx and the larynx and carries the recurrent laryngeal nerve around the subclavian artery on the right and the aortic arch on the left. The eleventh nerve has a cranial root that joins the vagus and a spinal root that supplies the sternocleidomastoid and the trapezius, while the twelfth nerve supplies the muscles of the tongue, leaves by the hypoglossal canal, and turns the tongue toward the weak side when it fails.
