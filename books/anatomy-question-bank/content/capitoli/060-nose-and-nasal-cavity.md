# Chapter 60 — Nose and Nasal Cavity

This chapter works through the nose, the nasal cavity and the paranasal sinuses, and then the pharynx, the muscular tube that carries air and food onward behind them. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind. The nose warms, moistens and filters the air, and the sinuses around it drain into the passages beneath the conchae. The pharynx continues that airway behind the mouth and the larynx, and its muscles make swallowing possible. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 5901. Which two bones meet in the midline to form the bridge of the nose? The answer is the two nasal bones. Each joins its fellow in the middle and the frontal bone above. Below and behind, each meets the frontal process of the maxilla, and together they give the nose its fixed roof.

Question 5902. Which part of the external nose forms the rounded lateral wall of each nostril: the root, the dorsum, the ala, or the columella? The answer is the ala. It is the curved wing on each side of the nostril, supported within by the major alar cartilage. The columella is the strip of skin between the two nostrils.

Question 5903. A described photograph shows the base of the nose, with a median strip of skin between two openings and a groove running from the upper lip to that strip. Which structures are described? The answer is the columella and the philtrum. The columella is the skin-covered lower end of the nasal septum, and the philtrum is the groove of the upper lip.

Question 5904. Which cartilage of the external nose lies immediately deep to the skin at the tip and the ala, and is attached to no bone at all? The answer is the major alar cartilage. Its medial crus forms the lower part of the columella and its lateral crus curls through the ala. It joins the septal cartilage in the midline but reaches no bone.

Question 5905. A constructed illustration: a blow to the front of the nose leaves the organ bent to one side and the airway narrowed. Which structure has most likely been displaced? The answer is the nasal septum, whose front part is the septal cartilage. That cartilage is thin and mobile and is easily pushed off the midline. Blood may also gather under its lining.

Question 5906. True or false: the inferior nasal concha is a part of the ethmoid bone. The answer is false. The superior and middle conchae are projections of the ethmoid, but the inferior concha is a separate bone. It has its own ossification and is the largest of the three.

Question 5907. Which of these forms the roof of the nasal cavity: the cribriform plate of the ethmoid, the horizontal plate of the palatine bone, the body of the maxilla, or the vomer? The answer is the cribriform plate of the ethmoid. Its many small holes let the fibers of the olfactory nerve pass upward to the olfactory bulb.

Question 5908. Which two bones form the floor of the nasal cavity, the hard palate that separates the nose from the mouth? The answer is the palatine process of the maxilla and the horizontal plate of the palatine bone. The maxilla forms the front three quarters and the palatine bone the back quarter of that floor.

Question 5909. A described coronal section through the head shows a thin vertical partition in the middle, with a curled shelf of bone on each side above a narrow passage. Which structures are shown? The answer is the nasal septum with the middle concha above the middle meatus on each side. A meatus is the passage beneath a concha.

Question 5910. Which of these opens into the sphenoethmoidal recess, the narrow space above the superior concha: the sphenoid sinus, the frontal sinus, the maxillary sinus, or the nasolacrimal duct? The answer is the sphenoid sinus. It lies in the body of the sphenoid bone and drains forward into that recess. The others open lower down.

Question 5911. Into which passage of the lateral wall of the nose does the nasolacrimal duct open? The answer is the inferior meatus, the space beneath the inferior nasal concha. The duct carries tears from the lacrimal sac down into the nose. That is why weeping makes the nose run.

Question 5912. Which opening lies immediately behind the posterior end of the middle concha, in the lateral wall of the nose? The answer is the sphenopalatine foramen. Through it pass the sphenopalatine artery and the nasopalatine nerve. It opens into the superior meatus and is the gap a surgeon uses when bleeding comes from the back of the nose.

Question 5913. Which of these arteries supplies most of the posterior part of the nasal cavity: the sphenopalatine artery, the facial artery, the ophthalmic artery, or the superior thyroid artery? The answer is the sphenopalatine artery, a branch of the maxillary artery. It enters through the sphenopalatine foramen. The facial and ophthalmic arteries reach only the front.

Question 5914. Name two of the arteries that meet in the anteroinferior part of the nasal septum, where most nosebleeds begin. The answer is the anterior ethmoidal artery and the sphenopalatine artery, joined there by the greater palatine artery and the septal branch of the superior labial artery. That network is why bleeding there is often brisk.

Question 5915. A constructed illustration: a person blows the nose hard and bright red blood comes from the front of one nostril. From which part of the nasal cavity is the blood most likely coming? The answer is the anteroinferior part of the nasal septum, where four arteries meet. The mucosa there is thin and tears easily when the air is dry.

Question 5916. Which of these lies immediately lateral to the posterior ethmoidal air cells, so that a cell extending too far puts it at risk: the optic nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the maxillary artery, or the mandibular nerve? The answer is the optic nerve. Those cells lie beside the optic canal, and a cell may surround the canal. That variation matters before sinus surgery.

Question 5917. Which nerve carries general sensation from the posterior part of the nasal septum, and through which opening does it reach the nose? The answer is the nasopalatine nerve, which enters through the sphenopalatine foramen. It runs down the septum to the incisive canal. It is a branch of the maxillary nerve.

Question 5918. A described computed tomography scan of the face shows a large air-filled cavity in the body of the maxilla on each side, with a narrow opening high on its medial wall. Which sinus is shown, and why does it drain poorly? The answer is the maxillary sinus, whose opening lies near its roof. Mucus must be pushed upward to leave.

Question 5919. Which of these does not open into the middle meatus: the frontal sinus, the maxillary sinus, the anterior ethmoidal cells, or the posterior ethmoidal cells? The answer is the posterior ethmoidal cells, which open into the superior meatus. The frontal and maxillary sinuses and the anterior ethmoidal cells all drain below the middle concha.

Question 5920. Which of these pairs belongs together: the frontal sinus with the inferior meatus, the sphenoid sinus with the middle meatus, the posterior ethmoidal cells with the superior meatus, or the nasolacrimal duct with the middle meatus? The answer is the posterior ethmoidal cells with the superior meatus. The sphenoid sinus drains into the sphenoethmoidal recess.

Question 5921. What is the name of the nerve that carries the sense of smell, and through which bone do its fibers pass? The answer is the olfactory nerve, whose fibers pass through the cribriform plate of the ethmoid. It is the first cranial nerve. It is also the only cranial nerve that reaches the brain without relaying in the thalamus.

Question 5922. Which of these describes the epithelium of the olfactory region: ciliated columnar cells with goblet cells, stratified squamous cells with hairs, bipolar nerve cells with supporting cells, or transitional cells? The answer is bipolar nerve cells with supporting cells. The receptor cells of smell are nerve cells themselves. The ciliated columnar epithelium belongs to the respiratory region below.

Question 5923. Which structures lie immediately lateral to the sphenoid sinus, so that a tumor of the sinus may press on them? The answer is the cavernous sinus with the internal carotid artery inside it. The sinus lies in the body of the sphenoid, and only a thin plate of bone separates it from each lateral wall. Above it lies the pituitary fossa.

Question 5924. True or false: the maxillary sinus opens into the middle meatus. The answer is true. It opens through the hiatus semilunaris, the curved groove below the ethmoidal bulla. That opening lies high on the medial wall, near the roof of the sinus, which is why the sinus drains so poorly when its lining swells.

Question 5925. A constructed illustration: after a difficult extraction of an upper molar, air and fluid pass between the mouth and the nose on that side. Which structure has been breached? The answer is the floor of the maxillary sinus. The roots of the upper molar teeth lie just below that floor, and in some people the bone between them is paper thin.

Question 5926. Which of these is not a function usually credited to the paranasal sinuses: lightening the skull, adding resonance to the voice, helping to warm and moisten inspired air, or producing cerebrospinal fluid? The answer is producing cerebrospinal fluid, which is made within the ventricles of the brain. The other three are the functions commonly given for these air-filled spaces.

Question 5927. Which paranasal sinus is already present at birth, and which two appear only after birth? The answer is the maxillary sinus, with the frontal and sphenoid sinuses appearing later. The maxillary sinus begins to grow before birth, and the frontal sinus is not visible for some years afterward. The sphenoid sinus keeps enlarging through adolescence.

Question 5928. A described series of computed tomography scans of the same child, taken years apart, shows one air-filled space in the frontal bone that is absent in the first scan and large in the last. Which sinus is shown, and why was it absent? The answer is the frontal sinus, which begins to pneumatize only after birth and enlarges through childhood.

Question 5929. Which of these bones takes part in the lateral wall of the nasal cavity but not in the nasal septum: the vomer, the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, the inferior nasal concha, or the septal cartilage? The answer is the inferior nasal concha. The other three all belong to the septum, and the concha is a bone of the lateral wall.

Question 5930. Which part of the nasal cavity is lined by skin rather than mucous membrane, and what does it carry? The answer is the vestibule, the small space just inside each nostril. It carries coarse hairs and glands that trap large particles from the air. A boil in that skin is painful because the skin is bound tightly to the cartilage beneath.

Question 5931. Which of these explains why one nasal passage is often more open than the other at a given moment: the nasal cycle, the position of the septal cartilage, the drainage of the sinuses, or the size of the choanae? The answer is the nasal cycle. The erectile tissue of the conchae engorges and shrinks in turn over some hours.

Question 5932. Which groove lies immediately below the ethmoidal bulla in the middle meatus, and what opens into it? The answer is the hiatus semilunaris, which receives the frontal sinus and the maxillary sinus. The bulla is the rounded elevation above the groove. The uncinate process forms the lower lip of the groove.

Question 5933. A constructed illustration: after a head injury, clear fluid drips from one nostril and the person cannot smell. Which part of the nasal cavity has most likely been fractured? The answer is the cribriform plate of the ethmoid. A break there tears the fibers of the olfactory nerve and lets cerebrospinal fluid escape into the nose.

Question 5934. Which of these arteries is most likely torn by a fracture through the roof of the nasal cavity: the anterior ethmoidal artery, the greater palatine artery, the facial artery, or the superior labial artery? The answer is the anterior ethmoidal artery, which runs in the roof beside the cribriform plate. Bleeding from it can be profuse.

Question 5935. A described view through a nasal endoscope shows a rounded swelling above a curved groove, with the openings of two sinuses in the groove beneath. Which region is being viewed? The answer is the middle meatus, with the ethmoidal bulla above the hiatus semilunaris and the uncinate process below it.

Question 5936. Which thin plate of bone forms the medial wall of the orbit and also part of the lateral wall of the nasal cavity, and why is it easily broken? The answer is the orbital plate of the ethmoid, often called the paper plate. A blow to the eye may drive it inward, so air and infection can pass from the nose into the orbit.

Question 5937. Which of these opens into the inferior meatus of the nose: the nasolacrimal duct, the frontal sinus, the sphenoid sinus, or the posterior ethmoidal cells? The answer is the nasolacrimal duct. It opens beneath the inferior nasal concha, well below the other openings, and it drains tears into the nose.

Question 5938. Which of these pairs belongs together: the frontal sinus with the inferior meatus, the nasolacrimal duct with the superior meatus, the maxillary sinus with the middle meatus, or the sphenoid sinus with the middle meatus? The answer is the maxillary sinus with the middle meatus. The nasolacrimal duct opens below the inferior concha.

Question 5939. Name the three structures that form the nasal septum, spoken as a list. The answer is the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, the vomer and the septal cartilage. The perpendicular plate lies above and behind, the vomer below and behind, and the septal cartilage in front. Narrow crests of the maxilla and the palatine bone run along the floor.

Question 5940. Which of these best explains why the maxillary sinus is the paranasal sinus most often infected: its opening lies high on its medial wall, so that drainage depends on ciliary action; its cavity is the largest of the sinuses; it lies nearest the surface of the face; or it receives no arterial blood? The answer is the high opening. Mucus must be carried upward to leave it.

Question 5941. Which nerve supplies the skin over the upper part of the external nose and the front of the nasal septum? The answer is the anterior ethmoidal nerve, a branch of the ophthalmic division of the fifth cranial nerve. Its external nasal branch reaches the skin of the dorsum and the tip. The lower part of the nose is supplied instead by the infraorbital nerve.

Question 5942. Through which opening does the pterygopalatine fossa communicate with the nasal cavity, and what passes through it? The answer is the sphenopalatine foramen, which carries the sphenopalatine artery and the nasopalatine nerve. The fossa lies behind the maxilla and holds the pterygopalatine ganglion. Its opening lies behind the middle concha.

Question 5943. A constructed illustration: a surgeon reaches the pituitary gland through the nose, passing between the conchae and then through the sphenoid sinus. Why does that route give access to the pituitary fossa? The answer is that the sphenoid sinus lies directly below the fossa, and its roof is a thin plate of bone.

Question 5944. Which of these veins offers a route by which infection from the nose may reach the cavernous sinus: the ophthalmic veins, the facial vein, the retromandibular vein, or the external jugular vein? The answer is the ophthalmic veins. They run back through the orbit and the superior orbital fissure, and the channels between them have no valves.

Question 5945. A described radiograph taken with the head tilted back shows two large air-filled triangles below the orbits, their upper borders curved and their lower borders lost against the teeth. Which structures are shown, and where do they drain? The answer is the maxillary sinuses, which drain into the middle meatus and lie just above the molar roots.

Question 5946. True or false: the frontal sinus is present and air filled at birth. The answer is false. The frontal sinus begins to develop only after birth and is not visible on a radiograph for some years. The maxillary sinus is the one already present before birth, and the sphenoid sinus enlarges through adolescence.

Question 5947. Which of these describes the autonomic supply of the glands of the nasal mucosa: secretomotor fibers from the facial nerve with vasoconstrictor fibers from the superior cervical ganglion, fibers from the vagus alone, fibers from the glossopharyngeal nerve alone, or no autonomic supply? The answer is the first of these, joined in the nerve of the pterygoid canal.

Question 5948. Which cells of the olfactory epithelium are themselves the receptor cells, and from which cells are they replaced? The answer is the bipolar olfactory neurons, which are replaced throughout adult life from the basal cells. Each neuron sends one process to the surface and a fine fiber upward through the cribriform plate. They are exposed directly to the air.

Question 5949. Which paranasal sinus is the largest, and in which bone does it lie? The answer is the maxillary sinus, which occupies the body of the maxilla. It is shaped like a pyramid with its base against the lateral wall of the nose. Its volume in the adult is about 15 milliliters.

Question 5950. Which of these explains lasting loss of smell after a blow to the back of the head: the brain moves and tears the olfactory fibers at the cribriform plate, the nasal bones break and block the airway, the olfactory bulb is supplied by the facial nerve, or the mucosa swells over the receptors? The answer is that the brain moves within the skull. Those fibers are torn as they pass through that plate.

Question 5951. What name is given to the two openings at the back of the nasal cavity, through which it communicates with the nasopharynx? The answer is the choanae. Each is bounded above by the body of the sphenoid, below by the horizontal plate of the palatine bone, and laterally by the medial pterygoid plate.

Question 5952. A described cross section through the head at the level of the cheek shows the nasal cavity in the middle, each half closed by a thin vertical partition, with curled shelves of bone on the outer walls. What are those shelves called? The answer is the conchae, with a meatus lying beneath each one. The partition is the nasal septum.

Question 5953. Which artery reaches the anterior part of the nasal septum by running up through the incisive canal: the greater palatine artery, the facial artery, the anterior ethmoidal artery, or the posterior ethmoidal artery? The answer is the greater palatine artery, a branch of the maxillary artery. It comes up from the palate and joins the network on the septum.

Question 5954. A constructed illustration: a small child has pushed a bead into one nostril and it cannot be seen at the front. Which part of the nasal cavity should be examined, and why does this matter? The answer is the region beneath the inferior nasal concha, where such an object usually lodges. Removal belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 5955. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterosuperior part of the nasal cavity with the ophthalmic nerve, the posteroinferior part with the ophthalmic nerve, the vestibule with the olfactory nerve, or the olfactory region with the trigeminal nerve? The answer is the anterosuperior part with the ophthalmic nerve, through its anterior ethmoidal branch.

Question 5956. The nasal septum stands vertically in the middle of the face. In which plane of the body does it lie, and what does that plane divide? The answer is the median plane, which divides the body into right and left halves. The septum lies in that plane above the hard palate. A slight deviation to one side is common.

Question 5957. Which of these lies immediately above and behind the superior nasal concha: the sphenoethmoidal recess, the middle meatus, the inferior meatus, or the vestibule? The answer is the sphenoethmoidal recess, into which the sphenoid sinus opens. It is the highest part of the nasal cavity, and the olfactory region lies close to it.

Question 5958. Which structure forms the medial wall of the maxillary sinus, and what opens near the top of that wall? The answer is the lateral wall of the nasal cavity, with the ostium of the sinus near its upper end. The ostium opens into the middle meatus beneath the middle concha. The wall is thin enough for a surgeon to open it.

Question 5959. A described dried skull, viewed from behind with the nasal septum removed, shows three curled shelves of bone on the lateral wall and a passage beneath each one. Which shelf is a separate bone, and what does the lowest passage receive? The answer is the inferior nasal concha, and the inferior meatus receives the nasolacrimal duct.

Question 5960. Which of these best explains why a boil on the upper lip or the side of the nose is treated with concern: venous blood from there can pass by valveless channels to the cavernous sinus, the skin there has no lymphatic drainage, the facial artery there comes from the internal carotid, or the region is supplied by the vagus? The answer is the valveless venous channels.

Question 5961. True or false: the whole of the nasal cavity is lined by mucous membrane. The answer is false. The vestibule just inside each nostril is lined by skin, complete with hairs and glands. Only behind the vestibule does the mucous membrane begin, and only its upper part carries the sense of smell.

Question 5962. A constructed illustration: an older person has repeated heavy nosebleeds that begin at the back of the nose and run down the throat. Which vessel is the likely source, and why is the bleeding not controlled from the front? The answer is the sphenopalatine artery and its branches. They enter behind the middle concha and cannot be seen from the nostril.

Question 5963. Which statement about the middle nasal concha is correct: it is a projection of the ethmoid bone, it is a separate bone of the skull, it lies below the inferior concha, or it has no passage beneath it? The answer is that it is a projection of the ethmoid bone. Beneath it lies the middle meatus.

Question 5964. Which part of the nasal cavity is the narrowest, and why does that matter for the sense of airflow? The answer is the internal nasal valve, the narrow angle where the upper and lower lateral cartilages meet. It offers the greatest resistance in the airway during quiet breathing. A slight narrowing there is felt as a blocked nose.

Question 5965. Which structure forms the roof of the maxillary sinus, and which nerve runs in a canal along that roof? The answer is the floor of the orbit, with the infraorbital nerve in its canal. The nerve is a branch of the maxillary nerve and emerges on the face below the eye, supplying the cheek and the upper lip.

Question 5966. A described midline section of the head shows the nasal cavity above the hard palate, opening behind into the nasopharynx, with the soft palate hanging below that opening. Which opening joins the nose to the pharynx, and what closes it during swallowing? The answer is the choana, which the soft palate seals.

Question 5967. A described computed tomography scan of the face shows a horizontal line across one maxillary sinus, with fluid below it and air above. What does that appearance mean, and which wall of the sinus lies closest to the teeth? The answer is a fluid level, which suggests that the sinus holds secretion. Its floor lies above the molar roots.

Question 5968. A described dissection after removal of the middle nasal concha shows a rounded swelling with a curved groove beneath it, and several small openings along the groove. Which structures have been exposed? The answer is the ethmoidal bulla with the hiatus semilunaris below it, which receive the frontal, maxillary and anterior ethmoidal drainage.

Question 5969. A constructed illustration: a tube is passed along the floor of the nose into the nasopharynx without touching the conchae. Which structures form that floor, and why is the route chosen? The answer is the palatine process of the maxilla and the horizontal plate of the palatine bone. The floor is straight and wide.

Question 5970. Which of these pairs belongs together: the maxillary sinus with the supraorbital nerve, the frontal sinus with the infraorbital nerve, the maxillary sinus with the infraorbital nerve, or the sphenoid sinus with the olfactory nerve? The answer is the maxillary sinus with the infraorbital nerve and its alveolar branches. The frontal sinus is supplied by the supraorbital nerve.

Question 5971. Which single bone takes part in the lateral wall of the nasal cavity, the medial wall of the orbit and the floor of the anterior cranial fossa? The answer is the ethmoid bone. Its cribriform plate forms part of the roof of the nose, and its orbital plate forms the medial wall of the orbit. Its conchae project into the nose.

Question 5972. Which of these best explains why blood collecting between the septal cartilage and its lining can destroy the cartilage: the cartilage has no vessels of its own and is nourished by diffusion from its lining, the blood is under high pressure, the cartilage is really bone, or the collection presses on the ethmoid? The answer is that the cartilage is avascular.

Question 5973. A described computed tomography scan of the skull base shows the sphenoid sinus with one lateral wall so thin that the internal carotid artery seems to lie against the lining. Why does this matter when the sinus is entered? The answer is that the artery may lie within the wall of the sinus in some people.

Question 5974. Which part of the nasal cavity lies directly below the cribriform plate, and what passes through that plate? The answer is the olfactory region in the roof of the nose, where the receptor cells lie, and the fine fibers of the olfactory nerve pass through the holes of the plate to the olfactory bulb above.

Question 5975. Which of these statements is correct: the maxillary sinus drains upward through an opening near its roof, all sinuses drain downward through openings in their floors, the frontal sinus has no duct, or the sphenoid sinus opens into the inferior meatus? The answer is that the maxillary sinus drains upward. Ciliary action, not gravity, moves the mucus out.

Question 5976. From which level does the pharynx begin, and at which level does it become the esophagus? The answer is that it begins at the base of the skull and ends at the lower border of the cricoid cartilage, in front of the sixth cervical vertebra. Below that level it continues downward as the esophagus.

Question 5977. Which of these names the three parts of the pharynx in order from above downward: nasopharynx, oropharynx and laryngopharynx; oropharynx, nasopharynx and laryngopharynx; nasopharynx, laryngopharynx and oropharynx; or hypopharynx, oropharynx and nasopharynx? The answer is the nasopharynx, the oropharynx and the laryngopharynx. The soft palate and the epiglottis divide them.

Question 5978. A described midline section of the head shows a muscular tube behind the nose, the mouth and the larynx, open in front to all three and closed behind by a median seam. What is that seam, and what is the tube? The answer is the median pharyngeal raphe, and the tube is the pharynx, where the constrictors meet.

Question 5979. Which structures lie immediately in front of the pharynx, from above downward? The answer is the nasal cavity, the mouth and the larynx, each separated from the pharynx by soft tissue only. Behind the pharynx lie the prevertebral fascia and the cervical spine. That is why a collection there pushes the back wall forward.

Question 5980. All the muscles of the pharynx are supplied by the vagus nerve through the pharyngeal plexus, with one exception. Which of these is that exception: the stylopharyngeus, the superior constrictor, the palatopharyngeus, or the cricopharyngeus? The answer is the stylopharyngeus, which the glossopharyngeal nerve supplies. It is the only muscle of the pharynx that nerve moves.

Question 5981. A constructed illustration: some days after an operation on the tonsil, a person complains of pain in one ear although the ear looks normal. Which nerve explains the pain? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve, which supplies the region of the tonsil and also sends a branch to the middle ear.

Question 5982. Which muscle forms the floor of the fossa in which the palatine tonsil lies, and which fascia covers that muscle? The answer is the superior constrictor of the pharynx, covered by the pharyngobasilar fascia. Lateral to it runs the carotid sheath. The tonsil is therefore dissected close to the great vessels of the neck.

Question 5983. Which of these is the site at which a pharyngeal pouch pushes backward through the wall of the pharynx: the gap between the thyropharyngeus and the cricopharyngeus, the gap between the superior and middle constrictors, the opening of the auditory tube, or the fossa behind the palatine tonsil? The answer is the first of these, in the midline behind.

Question 5984. A described radiograph taken during a swallow shows a rounded out-pouching from the back of the lower pharynx, in the midline, above the level of the cricoid cartilage. Which structure has herniated, and through which gap? The answer is the mucous membrane of the pharynx, through the gap between the thyropharyngeus and the cricopharyngeus.

Question 5985. Which of these describes the position of the pharyngeal tonsil: in the roof and posterior wall of the nasopharynx, on the lateral wall of the nasopharynx, in the tonsillar fossa between the two arches, or at the base of the tongue? The answer is the roof and the posterior wall of the nasopharynx. When it enlarges it blocks the airway.

Question 5986. Which structure lies in the lateral wall of the nasopharynx behind the opening of the auditory tube, and which large vessel lies close to it? The answer is the pharyngeal recess, with the internal carotid artery beyond its thin wall. That recess is a common site for a tumor to begin, and the artery explains why a biopsy there is taken with care.

Question 5987. Into which part of the pharynx does the auditory tube open, and what is the ridge above that opening called? The answer is the nasopharynx, and the ridge is the torus tubarius. The tube equalizes pressure on the two sides of the eardrum. The salpingopharyngeal fold runs downward from the ridge.

Question 5988. Which of these groups forms the ring of lymphoid tissue around the entrance of the air and food passages: the pharyngeal, tubal, palatine and lingual tonsils; the palatine and lingual tonsils alone; the thymus and the spleen; or the lymph nodes of the neck? The answer is the four sets of tonsils, which surround the upper end of the pharynx.

Question 5989. Which of these pairs belongs together: the nasopharynx with the palatine tonsil, the oropharynx with the pharyngeal tonsil, the laryngopharynx with the piriform fossa, or the nasopharynx with the vallecula? The answer is the laryngopharynx with the piriform fossa. The palatine tonsil lies in the oropharynx, and the pharyngeal tonsil in the nasopharynx.

Question 5990. Name the three constrictor muscles of the pharynx in order from above downward, spoken as a list. The answer is the superior, the middle and the inferior constrictor. The superior arises from the pterygoid hamulus, the pterygomandibular raphe and the mandible, the middle from the stylohyoid ligament and the hyoid bone, and the inferior from the thyroid and cricoid cartilages.

Question 5991. Which of these passes through the gap between the upper border of the superior constrictor and the base of the skull: the auditory tube with the muscle that lifts the soft palate, the internal carotid artery, the hypoglossal nerve, or the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the auditory tube with the levator veli palatini. The gap is closed in front by fascia.

Question 5992. A constructed illustration: a person with a severe throat infection has a smooth swelling of the back wall of the pharynx and holds the neck stiff. Which space has the collection entered, and why is it dangerous? The answer is the retropharyngeal space between the pharynx and the prevertebral fascia, which runs down behind the esophagus into the chest.

Question 5993. A described view into an open mouth shows two curved folds on each side with a rounded mass between them, and a midline fold running back to the tongue. Which structures are seen? The answer is the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal arches with the palatine tonsil between them. The midline fold is the glossoepiglottic fold.

Question 5994. Which fold forms the medial wall of the piriform fossa, and which nerve lies beneath its lining? The answer is the aryepiglottic fold, with the internal laryngeal nerve lying just under the mucosa of the fossa. The fossa lies on each side of the laryngeal inlet. A sharp object such as a fish bone may lodge there.

Question 5995. Which muscle acts as the sphincter at the junction of the pharynx and the esophagus, and what is its state at rest? The answer is the cricopharyngeus, the lower part of the inferior constrictor. It stays contracted at rest and relaxes at the moment of swallowing. Failure of that relaxation is one cause of difficulty in swallowing.

Question 5996. Which of these happens during the pharyngeal stage of swallowing: the soft palate rises to seal the nasopharynx and the constrictors contract in sequence, the soft palate drops to close the mouth, the cricopharyngeus contracts tightly, or the larynx descends? The answer is that the soft palate rises and the constrictors contract in order.

Question 5997. True or false: the muscles of the pharynx are supplied by the vagus nerve alone, with no other nerve taking part. The answer is false. The vagus supplies them through the pharyngeal plexus except for the stylopharyngeus, which the glossopharyngeal nerve supplies. The plexus also receives sympathetic fibers.

Question 5998. Which of these best explains why a tumor in the piriform fossa may first be noticed as pain in the ear: the fossa and the ear are both supplied by branches of the vagus nerve, the fossa lies next to the eardrum, the facial nerve passes through it, or it drains to the ear? The answer is the shared nerve supply through the vagus.

Question 5999. The pharynx is described as ending in front of the sixth cervical vertebra, where the esophagus begins. Which structure lies immediately in front of that junction, and in which direction does the tube continue? The answer is the cricoid cartilage, and the tube continues downward and somewhat to the left toward the chest.

Question 6000. Which part of the pharynx lies above the soft palate and holds the pharyngeal tonsil in its roof, and is a common site for a tumor in the adult? The answer is the nasopharynx. From the front it is entered through the choanae, and a tumor often begins in its lateral recess.

To summarize, the nasal cavity is divided by the septum and opens behind through the choanae into the nasopharynx, and its lateral wall carries three conchae with a passage beneath each one. The sphenoid sinus opens into the sphenoethmoidal recess, the posterior ethmoidal cells into the superior meatus, the frontal, maxillary and anterior ethmoidal sinuses into the middle meatus, and the nasolacrimal duct into the inferior meatus. The anterior ethmoidal, sphenopalatine, greater palatine and superior labial arteries meet on the anteroinferior part of the septum, where most nosebleeds begin, and the cribriform plate above is both the roof of the nose and the route of the olfactory fibers. The pharynx has three parts, its muscles are supplied by the vagus through the pharyngeal plexus except for the stylopharyngeus, and the cricopharyngeus holds the lower end closed except during swallowing.
