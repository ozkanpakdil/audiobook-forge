# Chapter 55 — Spinal Cord, Part Two

This chapter follows the nerve cells of the spinal cord out of the vertebral canal and into the limbs. It begins with the ventral horn, the column of motor neurons that commands the skeletal muscles, and then follows the spinal roots that carry those axons out and bring sensation in. The second half turns to the spinal nerves of the neck, first the cervical plexus and then the brachial plexus, the network that supplies the whole upper limb. It holds one hundred questions, and each one states its answer in the same paragraph as the question, so nothing has to be carried in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 5401. Which part of the gray matter of the spinal cord holds the cell bodies of the neurons that command the skeletal muscles? The answer is the ventral horn. It is the anterior column of gray matter, and it is largest where the limb muscles are supplied. The dorsal horn receives incoming sensory fibers and holds no motor neurons.

Question 5402. Which of these cells lies in the ventral horn and ends on an ordinary skeletal muscle fiber: the alpha motor neuron, the gamma motor neuron, the dorsal root ganglion cell, or the postganglionic sympathetic neuron? The answer is the alpha motor neuron. It commands the extrafusal fibers that produce the force of a contraction. The gamma motor neuron ends instead on the intrafusal fibers within a muscle spindle.

Question 5403. Through which structure do the axons of the ventral horn motor neurons leave the spinal cord? The answer is the ventral root. Its rootlets leave the surface along the anterolateral sulcus and gather into one root. The dorsal root beside it carries fibers in the opposite direction.

Question 5404. A described transverse section of the cord shows a broad rounded column of gray matter on each side of the midline, far larger than the narrow column behind it. Which part of the cord is this, and where is it largest? The answer is the ventral horn, which is largest at the cervical and lumbosacral enlargements. Those segments supply the muscles of the upper and lower limbs.

Question 5405. Where does the gray matter of the ventral horn lie in relation to the central canal: in front of it, behind it, above it, or beside it? The answer is in front of it. The canal sits at the center of the H-shaped gray matter. The dorsal horn lies behind the canal and the ventral horn in front.

Question 5406. True or false: the ventral horn contains the cell bodies of sensory neurons. The answer is false. Sensory cell bodies sit in the dorsal root ganglion outside the cord, and their fibers end in the dorsal horn. Every cell body inside the ventral horn belongs to a motor neuron or an interneuron.

Question 5407. In the numbering of the gray matter from the dorsal horn forward, which lamina holds the columns of motor neurons: the fourth, the sixth, the eighth, or the ninth? The answer is the ninth. The eighth lamina holds interneurons that project to those motor neurons. The tenth lamina surrounds the central canal.

Question 5408. Which segments of the cord carry the largest ventral horns: the upper thoracic, the cervical and lumbosacral enlargements, the lower thoracic, or the coccygeal? The answer is the cervical and lumbosacral enlargements. The ventral horn grows where many motor neurons are needed. The limb muscles are supplied from those two regions.

Question 5409. Which small inhibitory interneuron of the ventral horn is excited by a branch of a motor axon that turns back into the gray matter? The answer is the Renshaw cell. That recurrent collateral excites it, and it then inhibits the motor neuron that has just fired. The loop keeps the discharge of a motor neuron within bounds.

Question 5410. Which of these pairs belongs together: the medial group of motor neurons with the limb muscles, the lateral group with the muscles of the trunk, the intermediolateral column with the preganglionic sympathetic neurons, or the tenth lamina with the diaphragm motor neurons? The answer is the intermediolateral column with the preganglionic sympathetic neurons. The medial group supplies the trunk muscles and the lateral group the limb muscles.

Question 5411. Which white matter lies immediately in front of the gray matter of the ventral horn? The answer is the anterior funiculus. The ventral horn stops short of the surface, and a thin layer of white matter covers it. The anterior corticospinal tract descends within that layer.

Question 5412. Which of these neurons ends on the intrafusal fibers of a muscle spindle and adjusts its sensitivity: the alpha motor neuron, the gamma motor neuron, the Renshaw cell, or the dorsal root ganglion cell? The answer is the gamma motor neuron. It is smaller than the alpha cell and lies among the motor columns. Its axon reaches the spindle through the same ventral root.

Question 5413. A described transverse section of the upper thoracic cord shows a slender anterior column of gray matter on each side, much smaller than the one seen at the neck. Why is the ventral horn so thin at this level? The answer is that these segments supply few limb muscles. Their motor neurons serve the trunk and the intercostal muscles instead.

Question 5414. A constructed illustration: a person has sudden flaccid weakness of one arm with wasting of the small hand muscles, and sensation in that limb is normal. Which part of the cord is the lesion likely to involve? The answer is the ventral horn. Losing those motor neurons causes weakness, wasting and loss of the reflex, while sensation stays intact.

Question 5415. Which nucleus of the ventral horn supplies the diaphragm, and at which segments does it lie? The answer is the phrenic nucleus, in the ventral horn of the third, fourth and fifth cervical segments. The fourth segment contributes the largest share. Its axons gather into the phrenic nerve and descend through the neck.

Question 5416. From which part of the cord do the fibers of the eleventh cranial nerve arise? The answer is the ventral horn of the upper cervical segments. Those fibers leave with the ventral roots of the first five or six cervical nerves and then climb through the foramen magnum. They supply the sternocleidomastoid and the trapezius.

Question 5417. Which part of the ventral horn receives the fibers of the corticospinal tract as they end in the cord? The answer is the motor neurons and the interneurons that lie among them. Fibers that crossed in the medulla end on the lateral side of the horn. A single motor neuron may receive thousands of such endings.

Question 5418. A constructed illustration: a cavity forms in the center of the cervical cord, first interrupting the fibers that cross the midline and later spreading into one ventral horn. What pattern of loss would be expected? The answer is loss of pain and temperature over the shoulders and arms, followed by weakness and wasting of the hand muscles. The dorsal columns are spared.

Question 5419. Which group of ventral horn neurons supplies the external sphincters of the bladder and the anus: the phrenic nucleus, Onuf's nucleus, the intermediolateral column, or the nucleus dorsalis? The answer is Onuf's nucleus, in the ventral horn of the second to fourth sacral segments. It is a somatic motor group, yet it works closely with the sacral parasympathetic neurons beside it.

Question 5420. A described magnetic resonance image of the neck shows damage confined to the anterior gray matter on both sides at the third and fourth cervical segments. Which muscle would be paralyzed? The answer is the diaphragm. The motor neurons for that muscle form the phrenic nucleus at those segments. Because the lesion lies in the gray matter, sensation is preserved.

Question 5421. Name two things that happen to a skeletal muscle when the ventral horn cells that supply it are destroyed, spoken as a list. The answer is flaccid paralysis and wasting. The muscle loses its only source of command, so it cannot contract, and it shrinks. The tendon reflex that depends on it disappears as well.

Question 5422. Which of these structures is found in the ventral horn but never in the dorsal horn: the cell bodies of large motor neurons, the substantia gelatinosa, the nucleus proprius, or the nucleus dorsalis? The answer is the cell bodies of large motor neurons. The substantia gelatinosa and the nucleus proprius belong to the dorsal horn. The nucleus dorsalis lies at its base in the thoracic cord.

Question 5423. A described section of the cord shows two changes at once: the anterior gray columns are thin and depleted of large cells, while the lateral white columns are pale where the corticospinal fibers descend. Which condition fits this picture? The answer is amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, which destroys ventral horn cells and corticospinal fibers together. It combines wasting with spasticity.

Question 5424. Which layer of white matter separates the gray matter of the ventral horn from the surface of the cord, and what does that layer contain? The answer is the anterior funiculus, which contains the anterior corticospinal tract and other descending fibers. The ventral horn approaches the surface but never reaches it. The ventral rootlets emerge just lateral to that covering.

Question 5425. A person has weakness and wasting of the hand muscles, yet the elbow reflex is exaggerated rather than reduced. Which is the likely lesion: the ventral horn only, the corticospinal tract only, both together, or the dorsal column only? The answer is both together. Wasting points to the lower motor neuron, and the brisk reflex to the upper motor neuron above it.

Question 5426. Which structure holds the cell bodies of the sensory neurons that enter the spinal cord? The answer is the dorsal root ganglion. It sits on the dorsal root as a small swelling just outside the cord. Each cell there sends one process out to the tissues and another into the dorsal horn.

Question 5427. Which root of a spinal nerve carries fibers away from the spinal cord? The answer is the ventral root. Its fibers are the axons of motor neurons in the ventral horn and of autonomic neurons beside them. The dorsal root beside it carries fibers in the opposite direction.

Question 5428. Which structure is formed where the dorsal root and the ventral root join: the dorsal ramus, the spinal nerve, the sympathetic trunk, or the filum terminale? The answer is the spinal nerve. The union takes place in the intervertebral foramen, just beyond the ganglion. The nerve is short and soon divides.

Question 5429. True or false: a spinal nerve carries both sensory and motor fibers. The answer is true. It is formed by the joining of a sensory dorsal root and a motor ventral root. Once past the foramen it divides into a dorsal ramus and a ventral ramus, and both are mixed.

Question 5430. Into which two branches does a spinal nerve divide after it leaves the intervertebral foramen: a dorsal ramus and a ventral ramus, a medial and a lateral ramus, an ascending and a descending ramus, or a superficial and a deep ramus? The answer is a dorsal ramus and a ventral ramus. The dorsal ramus turns backward to the muscles and skin of the back.

Question 5431. Which of the two rami of a spinal nerve is the larger, and what does it supply? The answer is the ventral ramus, which supplies the limbs and the front and sides of the trunk. The dorsal ramus is smaller and stays in the back. In the thorax the ventral rami run on as the intercostal nerves.

Question 5432. Which ligament of the cord lies between the ventral root and the dorsal root as they leave the cord? The answer is the denticulate ligament. It is a sheet of pia mater whose toothed edge is pinned to the dura at intervals on each side of the cord. It holds the cord in the middle of the dural sac.

Question 5433. Along which groove of the cord do the dorsal rootlets attach? The answer is the dorsolateral sulcus, which runs down the back of the cord on each side. It lies between the dorsal median sulcus in the midline and the line of the ventral rootlets in front. Both sulci mark the surface.

Question 5434. How many pairs of spinal nerves leave the vertebral column: twenty-eight, thirty-one, thirty-three, or thirty-six? The answer is thirty-one. They are 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral and 1 coccygeal. The first cervical nerve leaves between the skull and the first cervical vertebra.

Question 5435. A described magnetic resonance image of the lower back shows the cord ending in a point at the first lumbar vertebra, with a bundle of fine roots below it. What are the two structures, and why does the point lie so high? The answer is the conus medullaris and the cauda equina. The cord and the vertebral column grow at different rates, so the cord ends above the sacrum.

Question 5436. The eighth cervical nerve leaves the vertebral column at which level: above the first cervical vertebra, above the seventh cervical vertebra, below the seventh cervical vertebra, or below the first thoracic vertebra? The answer is below the seventh cervical vertebra. There are 8 cervical nerves but only 7 cervical vertebrae. The first 7 nerves leave above their own vertebra.

Question 5437. A described dissection of a spinal nerve at the intervertebral foramen shows the nerve wrapped in a sleeve of fibrous tissue that continues from the covering of the cord. Which layer forms that sleeve, and what lies inside it? The answer is the dura mater, with the arachnoid mater inside it. A thin film of cerebrospinal fluid extends along the root for a short distance.

Question 5438. Where does the dorsal root ganglion lie in relation to the intervertebral foramen? The answer is that it lies within the foramen, just proximal to the point where the two roots unite. Because the ganglion holds the cell bodies of the sensory neurons, pressure on it causes pain rather than weakness. A herniated disc can press on it there.

Question 5439. A constructed illustration: a disc protrudes between the fourth and fifth lumbar vertebrae and presses on the root that passes through that gap. Which root is compressed, and what would the person notice? The answer is the fifth lumbar root. Pain runs down the outer side of the leg to the top of the foot, and the ankle reflex is usually retained.

Question 5440. Which fibers join the ventral root in addition to the axons of motor neurons: preganglionic autonomic fibers, postganglionic autonomic fibers, sensory fibers from the skin, or fibers from the cerebellum? The answer is preganglionic autonomic fibers, and only between the first thoracic and the second lumbar segments. They leave the root through the white rami communicantes. The sacral outflow leaves with the sacral roots.

Question 5441. Which of these fiber types is found in the dorsal root: somatic motor fibers, preganglionic autonomic fibers, visceral sensory fibers, or postganglionic autonomic fibers? The answer is visceral sensory fibers. The dorsal root carries both somatic and visceral sensory fibers, and nothing else. Motor and preganglionic autonomic fibers leave the cord through the ventral root.

Question 5442. Which small nerve arises from the spinal nerve in the intervertebral foramen, turns back into the vertebral canal, and supplies the dura mater and the outer part of the disc? The answer is the recurrent meningeal nerve. It receives a twig from the gray ramus communicans of the sympathetic trunk. It helps explain pain from a disc that does not follow a dermatome.

Question 5443. A described transverse section of a spinal nerve shows a bundle of fibers wrapped in a fibrous sheath, with no cell bodies inside the nerve itself. Which sheaths surround the nerve, and why are no cell bodies present? The answer is the epineurium, the perineurium and the endoneurium. Sensory cell bodies lie in the dorsal root ganglion and motor cell bodies in the ventral horn.

Question 5444. A constructed illustration: an older person develops a band of painful blisters on one side of the chest, following the line of a single intercostal nerve. Where has the virus been lying dormant? The answer is in the dorsal root ganglion of that segment. The virus travels down the sensory axon to the skin and produces a rash in one dermatome. Weakness is uncommon.

Question 5445. Within the intervertebral foramen the spinal nerve lies in the upper part of the opening. Which structure then lies directly in front of it? The answer is the intervertebral disc. Because the nerve lies above the disc, a disc that herniates backward and sideways usually presses on the root of the segment below.

Question 5446. Name two structures, besides the spinal nerve itself, that lie within an intervertebral foramen, spoken as a list. The answer is the dorsal root ganglion and the recurrent meningeal nerve. A small spinal artery and its branches also run there. The foramen is a short tunnel between the pedicles of two vertebrae.

Question 5447. Below which vertebral level does the subarachnoid space contain only the roots of the cauda equina: the tenth thoracic, the second lumbar, the fourth lumbar, or the first sacral? The answer is the second lumbar. The cord ends at about the first lumbar vertebra in the adult, though it lies lower in a newborn. Below that level only roots and fluid remain.

Question 5448. A described dissection of the lower dural sac shows the cord tapering to a point, a spray of roots descending around a delicate thread, and the thread running on to the coccyx. Which structures are shown, and why do the roots descend? The answer is the conus medullaris, the cauda equina and the filum terminale. The cord stops growing early, so each lower root travels down to its own foramen.

Question 5449. A constructed illustration: one person loses the knee reflex and the feeling of pain in a band on the thigh, while a second has weakness and wasting of the thigh muscles with normal feeling. Which roots are at fault? The answer is the dorsal root in the first and the ventral root in the second. Sensory loss follows the dorsal root and weakness the ventral root.

Question 5450. Which of these pairs belongs together: the dorsal root with the axons of the ventral horn cells, the ventral root with axons from the dorsal root ganglion, the dorsal root ganglion with the fibers that enter the dorsolateral sulcus, or the ventral root with sensory cell bodies? The answer is the dorsal root ganglion with the fibers that enter the dorsolateral sulcus. The dorsal root is sensory, the other motor.

Question 5451. Which ventral rami unite to form the cervical plexus? The answer is the ventral rami of the first four cervical nerves. They lie deep in the neck and exchange fibers with one another. The fifth cervical ramus belongs to the brachial plexus, though some of its fibers reach the diaphragm through the phrenic nerve.

Question 5452. Which spinal nerves give the phrenic nerve its fibers? The answer is the third, fourth and fifth cervical nerves. The fourth contributes the largest share. The three roots join in the neck into one trunk, and the nerve then descends through the chest to the muscle. Each side has its own phrenic nerve.

Question 5453. Which nerve supplies the diaphragm: the vagus nerve, the phrenic nerve, the sympathetic trunk, or the intercostal nerves? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It carries the motor fibers to the whole muscle on each side. The lower intercostal nerves carry sensation from the outer rim of the diaphragm but do not move it.

Question 5454. From behind which muscle do the cutaneous branches of the cervical plexus come into view: the trapezius, the sternocleidomastoid, the platysma, or the anterior scalene? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid. They appear at about the middle of its posterior border and fan out across the neck. The plexus itself lies deep to that muscle.

Question 5455. Which two cervical nerves join to form the great auricular nerve? The answer is the second and third cervical nerves. The nerve winds around the middle of the posterior border of the sternocleidomastoid. It then runs up over that muscle to supply the skin of the ear and the parotid region.

Question 5456. True or false: the cervical plexus lies deep to the sternocleidomastoid muscle. The answer is true. The plexus rests on the muscles behind it, which include the scalenus medius and the levator scapulae. Its branches either stay deep as muscular nerves or turn to the surface as cutaneous nerves.

Question 5457. Which area of skin is supplied by the lesser occipital nerve: the front of the neck, the scalp behind the ear, the point of the shoulder, or the skin over the parotid gland? The answer is the scalp behind the ear. It is a branch of the second cervical nerve. The great auricular nerve covers the ear and the parotid region.

Question 5458. Which muscles are supplied by the ansa cervicalis, the loop of nerve that lies on the carotid sheath? The answer is the infrahyoid or strap muscles of the neck. They are the sternohyoid, the sternothyroid and the omohyoid. The loop is formed by a superior root and an inferior root that meet on the sheath.

Question 5459. On the surface of which muscle does the phrenic nerve descend through the neck? The answer is the anterior scalene muscle. The nerve crosses it from lateral to medial, lying in front of the muscle and behind the prevertebral fascia. Below, it slips between the subclavian vein and the subclavian artery.

Question 5460. A described dissection of the neck shows a nerve running down the front of the anterior scalene muscle and passing behind the clavicle between two large vessels. Which nerve is it? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It passes between the subclavian vein in front and the subclavian artery behind. It then descends through the chest to the diaphragm.

Question 5461. Which of the infrahyoid muscles escapes the ansa cervicalis and is supplied instead by fibers that travel with the hypoglossal nerve: the sternohyoid, the sternothyroid, the omohyoid, or the thyrohyoid? The answer is the thyrohyoid. Its fibers come from the first cervical nerve and join the hypoglossal nerve for part of its course.

Question 5462. A described transverse section across the neck shows a bundle of nerves lying behind the internal jugular vein and in front of the muscles that cover the vertebral column. In which plane was the cut made, and where does the bundle lie? The answer is a transverse plane, with the bundle in front of the vertebral column. That bundle is the cervical plexus.

Question 5463. A described dissection of the posterior triangle of the neck shows a nerve crossing the triangle into the deep surface of the trapezius, joined by a small twig from the cervical plexus. Why does that twig join the nerve? The answer is to carry proprioceptive fibers from the muscle. The eleventh cranial nerve supplies the trapezius but carries no such fibers itself.

Question 5464. Which large vein lies in front of the cervical plexus in the neck, within the carotid sheath? The answer is the internal jugular vein. The plexus lies behind the sheath, resting on the prevertebral muscles. The cutaneous branches therefore have to travel backward to reach the skin of the neck.

Question 5465. A constructed illustration: after an operation on the side of the neck, one shoulder droops and the arm cannot be raised above the horizontal. Which muscle and nerve are involved, and which plexus contributed a branch to that nerve? The answer is the trapezius and the eleventh cranial nerve, with a branch from the cervical plexus. The nerve is at risk in the posterior triangle.

Question 5466. A described dissection shows a nerve crossing the surface of the sternocleidomastoid alongside a vein that runs down the neck toward the root of the neck. Which nerve and which vein are shown? The answer is the great auricular nerve and the external jugular vein. The nerve belongs to the cervical plexus and is formed by the second and third cervical nerves.

Question 5467. Why does the cervical plexus receive only gray rami communicantes and no white rami: because the sympathetic outflow begins below the neck, because the plexus carries only sensory fibers, because gray rami carry motor fibers, or because white rami exist only below the sacrum? The answer is that the sympathetic outflow begins below the neck. White rami carry preganglionic fibers, and there are none at this level.

Question 5468. Which structure carries the fibers of the first cervical nerve down to the superior root of the ansa cervicalis: the vagus nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the sympathetic trunk, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the hypoglossal nerve. The first cervical fibers join that nerve for part of its course and then leave it as the superior root. The twelfth cranial nerve supplies the tongue.

Question 5469. A constructed illustration: irritation of the diaphragm on one side is felt as pain at the tip of the shoulder. Through which nerve fibers is that pain carried, and why is it felt so far from the muscle? The answer is the phrenic nerve, whose fibers come from the third, fourth and fifth cervical segments. Those segments also supply the skin over the shoulder.

Question 5470. A described dissection of the root of the neck shows the phrenic nerve lying on the anterior scalene muscle, in front of the artery that passes between the scalene muscles. Which artery is behind it, and what lies in front of the nerve? The answer is the subclavian artery, with the subclavian vein in front of the nerve. The brachial plexus leaves the neck beside them.

Question 5471. Which of these nerves is a muscular branch of the cervical plexus rather than a cutaneous one: the great auricular nerve, the transverse cervical nerve, the nerve to the levator scapulae, or the supraclavicular nerves? The answer is the nerve to the levator scapulae. The other three are cutaneous and appear from behind the sternocleidomastoid. The muscular branches stay deep.

Question 5472. True or false: the phrenic nerve carries sensory fibers from the whole of the diaphragm. The answer is false. It carries sensation from the central part of the diaphragm and from the pleura and pericardium that touch it. The outer rim of the diaphragm is supplied by the lower intercostal nerves.

Question 5473. A constructed illustration: a wound high in the neck divides a nerve that runs forward to the tongue, and a week later the strap muscles on that side are weak. Which two sets of fibers explain both findings? The answer is the twelfth cranial nerve and the first cervical fibers that travel with it. Those fibers leave the nerve as the superior root of the ansa cervicalis.

Question 5474. Which of these pairs belongs together: the great auricular nerve with the front of the neck, the transverse cervical nerve with the skin over the parotid gland, the supraclavicular nerves with the skin over the shoulder, or the lesser occipital nerve with the point of the shoulder? The answer is the supraclavicular nerves with the skin over the shoulder. The great auricular nerve supplies the ear and the parotid region.

Question 5475. A described image of the upper cervical cord shows that the large cells of the ventral horn have disappeared on both sides at the third and fourth cervical segments, while the white matter looks normal. Which muscle will fail, and why? The answer is the diaphragm, because the phrenic nucleus lies at those segments. With the white matter intact, the failure is a lower motor neuron one.

Question 5476. Which ventral rami form the brachial plexus? The answer is the ventral rami of the fifth cervical to the first thoracic nerves. They emerge between the anterior and middle scalene muscles. The first thoracic ramus contributes only a small part, and the rest of it runs on as the first intercostal nerve.

Question 5477. Which of these sets of roots forms the upper trunk of the brachial plexus: the fifth and sixth cervical, the seventh cervical alone, the eighth cervical and first thoracic, or the fifth cervical to the first thoracic? The answer is the fifth and sixth cervical roots. The seventh cervical root forms the middle trunk on its own. The eighth cervical and first thoracic roots form the lower trunk.

Question 5478. The cords of the brachial plexus take their names from their relation to which structure? The answer is the axillary artery. The lateral, medial and posterior cords surround the second part of the artery behind the pectoralis minor. Each cord then gives off branches in the axilla.

Question 5479. True or false: the brachial plexus is formed by ventral rami rather than by dorsal rami. The answer is true. Only ventral rami form the limb plexuses, because they supply the limbs. The dorsal rami stay in the back and supply its muscles and skin.

Question 5480. Which of these is a terminal branch of the brachial plexus: the dorsal scapular nerve, the long thoracic nerve, the musculocutaneous nerve, or the suprascapular nerve? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve. The other three arise higher, from the roots or from the upper trunk. The five terminal branches all arise in the axilla.

Question 5481. Which nerve supplies the rhomboid muscles, and from which part of the plexus does it arise? The answer is the dorsal scapular nerve, from the fifth cervical root. It pierces the middle scalene muscle on its way back to the scapula. The rhomboids pull the scapula toward the spine.

Question 5482. From which part of the brachial plexus does the suprascapular nerve arise: the upper trunk, the middle trunk, the lower trunk, or the posterior cord? The answer is the upper trunk. It arises close to the formation of that trunk and runs backward toward the scapula. It passes below the transverse scapular ligament to reach the shoulder.

Question 5483. Which nerve runs down the side of the chest wall on the surface of the serratus anterior, and from which roots is it formed? The answer is the long thoracic nerve, from the fifth, sixth and seventh cervical roots. It is formed within the scalene muscles before the plexus proper takes shape. It is easily injured in the axilla.

Question 5484. Which nerve of the brachial plexus pierces the coracobrachialis muscle on its way to the front of the arm? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve. It leaves the lateral cord and passes through the muscle. It then supplies the biceps brachii, the brachialis and the coracobrachialis.

Question 5485. The lateral cord of the brachial plexus is formed by which divisions: the anterior divisions of the upper and middle trunks, the posterior divisions of all three trunks, the anterior division of the lower trunk alone, or the posterior divisions of the upper and middle trunks? The answer is the anterior divisions of the upper and middle trunks. The anterior division of the lower trunk forms the medial cord.

Question 5486. Which structures unite to form the posterior cord of the brachial plexus: the anterior divisions of the upper and middle trunks, the posterior divisions of all three trunks, the anterior division of the lower trunk, or the roots of the fifth and sixth cervical nerves? The answer is the posterior divisions of all three trunks. That cord gives the subscapular nerves, the thoracodorsal, axillary and radial nerves.

Question 5487. Which artery is embraced by the two roots of the median nerve as they join in the axilla? The answer is the axillary artery. The lateral root comes from the lateral cord and the medial root from the medial cord. They unite in front of the artery in the shape of a V.

Question 5488. A described dissection shows a nerve and an artery passing together through a gap bounded by the teres minor above, the teres major below, the long head of the triceps medially and the surgical neck of the humerus laterally. Which two structures are they? The answer is the axillary nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery. The nerve supplies the deltoid and the skin over it.

Question 5489. Between which two muscles do the roots of the brachial plexus emerge in the neck? The answer is the anterior and the middle scalene muscles. The subclavian artery passes through the same gap with them. Once clear of the muscles, the roots unite into three trunks in the lower part of the neck.

Question 5490. A described photograph of a person with a birth injury shows one arm hanging by the side, rotated inward, with the elbow straight and the palm turned backward. Which part of the plexus is injured, and which roots are involved? The answer is the upper trunk, from the fifth and sixth cervical roots. Sensation is lost over the outer side of the arm.

Question 5491. Which nerve arises from the posterior cord and supplies the latissimus dorsi: the upper subscapular nerve, the thoracodorsal nerve, the medial pectoral nerve, or the suprascapular nerve? The answer is the thoracodorsal nerve. It runs down the back of the axilla to reach the muscle. The suprascapular nerve arises from the upper trunk instead.

Question 5492. A constructed illustration: a fall on the shoulder fractures the surgical neck of the humerus, and the person cannot lift the arm sideways, with numbness over the lower part of the shoulder. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the axillary nerve. It wraps around the surgical neck close to the bone. It supplies the deltoid and the skin over that muscle.

Question 5493. Name two muscles of the arm that are supplied by the musculocutaneous nerve, spoken as a list. The answer is the biceps brachii and the brachialis. Both bend the elbow. The third muscle it supplies, the coracobrachialis, is pierced by the nerve on its way down the arm.

Question 5494. Which of these nerves arises directly from the roots of the brachial plexus rather than from a trunk or a cord: the suprascapular nerve, the long thoracic nerve, the thoracodorsal nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the long thoracic nerve. The dorsal scapular nerve also comes from the roots. The suprascapular nerve comes from the upper trunk.

Question 5495. A described dissection shows that the upper trunk of the brachial plexus is larger than usual and receives a contribution from the fourth cervical root. What is this arrangement called, and what does it mean? The answer is a prefixed plexus, and it is a normal variation. The opposite arrangement, with a contribution from the second thoracic root, is called postfixed.

Question 5496. A constructed illustration: a person cannot lift the arm above the head, and the medial border of the scapula stands away from the chest wall when the arm is pushed forward against resistance. Which nerve is injured, and which muscle has failed? The answer is the long thoracic nerve and the serratus anterior, which holds the scapula flat.

Question 5497. A constructed illustration: a person has numbness of the thumb and the first two fingers and cannot oppose the thumb, while the little finger works normally. Which nerve is compressed, and where does it lie at that point? The answer is the median nerve, in the carpal tunnel at the wrist. It lies in the middle of the wrist, beneath the transverse carpal ligament.

Question 5498. Which of these pairs belongs together: the axillary nerve with the triceps, the radial nerve with the deltoid, the musculocutaneous nerve with the coracobrachialis, or the ulnar nerve with the flexor carpi radialis? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve with the coracobrachialis. The axillary nerve supplies the deltoid, and the radial nerve supplies the triceps.

Question 5499. Which of these pairs belongs together: the upper trunk with clawing of the hand, the lower trunk with loss of feeling on the outer arm, the long thoracic nerve with winging of the scapula, or the musculocutaneous nerve with loss of elbow extension? The answer is the long thoracic nerve with winging of the scapula. The upper trunk gives the waiter's tip and the lower trunk gives clawing.

Question 5500. A described transverse section through the axilla shows the axillary artery in the middle, one cord of the plexus on each side of it, and a third cord directly behind it. In which plane is the section cut, and which cord lies behind the artery? The answer is a transverse plane, and the posterior cord lies behind the artery. The cords are named for their position around it.

To summarize, the ventral horn holds the cell bodies of the motor neurons that drive skeletal muscle, and it swells at the cervical and lumbosacral enlargements where the limb muscles are supplied. Its medial group serves the trunk and its lateral group serves the limbs, while special groups within it supply the diaphragm at the third to fifth cervical segments and the sphincters at the second to fourth sacral segments. The ventral root carries those axons out of the cord while the dorsal root carries sensation in, the two unite in the intervertebral foramen to form a mixed spinal nerve, and the roots of the cauda equina descend below the end of the cord at the first lumbar vertebra. The cervical plexus, formed by the first four cervical nerves, gives the four cutaneous branches of the neck, the ansa cervicalis to the strap muscles and the phrenic nerve to the diaphragm, while the brachial plexus, from the fifth cervical to the first thoracic roots, forms three trunks, six divisions and three cords around the axillary artery before dividing into the five nerves of the upper limb. Knowing which root and which cord each branch comes from is what turns the plexus from a tangle into a map.
