# Chapter 58 — Meninges and Cerebrospinal Fluid

Every part of the central nervous system is wrapped in three membranes and floats in a clear fluid, and this chapter examines both the wrappings and the fluid. It begins with the dura mater, the arachnoid mater and the pia mater, with the dural folds and the venous sinuses they enclose, and with the cerebrospinal fluid of the ventricles and the subarachnoid space. The second half follows the great sensory and motor pathways as they run between the body and the cortex. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 5701. Which of the three membranes that cover the brain and the spinal cord is the outermost, the thickest and the toughest? The answer is the dura mater. Its Latin name means tough mother, and it is a dense fibrous coat. The arachnoid mater and the pia mater lie deep to it. It also forms the folds that divide the cranial cavity and the sinuses that drain its blood.

Question 5702. Which dural fold forms a tent above the cerebellum and separates it from the cerebrum: the falx cerebri, the falx cerebelli, the tentorium cerebelli, or the diaphragma sellae? The answer is the tentorium cerebelli. Its free edge leaves a gap, the tentorial notch, through which the midbrain passes. The falx cerebri stands upright between the two cerebral hemispheres.

Question 5703. Which statement about the space between the dura mater and the arachnoid mater is correct: it is a wide cavity, it is a potential space holding only a thin film of fluid, it lies outside the dura mater, or it contains the choroid plexus? The answer is that it is a potential space. Blood collects there when the bridging veins that cross it are torn.

Question 5704. A described computed tomography scan of the head shows a biconvex collection of fresh blood in the temple region, bulging against the brain. Which vessel has most likely been torn? The answer is the middle meningeal artery. It runs in a groove on the inner surface of the skull close to the pterion. A blow there can crack the thin bone and tear the vessel.

Question 5705. Which dural venous sinus runs in the midline along the upper margin of the falx cerebri and receives the superior cerebral veins? The answer is the superior sagittal sinus. It begins in front at the crista galli and ends behind at the confluence of the sinuses. The inferior sagittal sinus runs lower, along the free edge of the falx.

Question 5706. A constructed illustration: a person struck on the side of the head is briefly dazed, recovers, and then becomes drowsy an hour later. Which vessel is the likely source of the bleeding? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, torn where it crosses the pterion. A small first bleed explains the quiet interval before the artery opens further. Any management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 5707. Which small tufted projections of the arachnoid mater push into the dural venous sinuses and return cerebrospinal fluid to the blood? The answer is the arachnoid granulations. They cluster along the superior sagittal sinus and leave small pits on the inner surface of the skull vault. They behave as one-way valves.

Question 5708. Cerebrospinal fluid made in the lateral ventricles reaches the third ventricle through which opening: the cerebral aqueduct, the interventricular foramen, the median aperture, or the central canal? The answer is the interventricular foramen. The third ventricle lies in the midline below the lateral ventricles. The cerebral aqueduct carries the fluid onward from the third ventricle to the fourth.

Question 5709. True or false: the subarachnoid space is filled with cerebrospinal fluid and contains the larger blood vessels that lie on the surface of the brain. The answer is true. The space lies between the arachnoid mater and the pia mater, which follows every surface of the brain. Its widening around the brainstem and the cord forms the subarachnoid cisterns.

Question 5710. Which ventricle lies in the midline between the two thalami, is crossed by the interthalamic adhesion, and drains through the cerebral aqueduct? The answer is the third ventricle. It is a narrow slit in the midline of the diencephalon. The cerebral aqueduct runs through the midbrain to reach the fourth ventricle.

Question 5711. A described magnetic resonance imaging study of a child's brain shows enlarged lateral and third ventricles, a normal fourth ventricle, and a thin stream of fluid in the aqueduct. Where is the blockage? The answer is in the cerebral aqueduct. Fluid is trapped above the block and the fourth ventricle fills from below. This pattern is described as non-communicating hydrocephalus.

Question 5712. Where is most cerebrospinal fluid produced: in the choroid plexus of the ventricles, in the arachnoid granulations, in the pia mater, or in the dural venous sinuses? The answer is in the choroid plexus. It is a fringe of pia mater and blood vessels covered by ependymal cells. A smaller amount comes from the interstitial fluid of the brain itself.

Question 5713. In an adult, where does the spinal cord end, and where is a lumbar puncture made: at the twelfth thoracic vertebra with the needle below it, at the first or second lumbar vertebra with the needle lower, at the fifth lumbar vertebra, or in the sacrum? The answer is at the first or second lumbar vertebra, with the needle placed lower, where only the cauda equina lies.

Question 5714. Name two structures found in the lumbar cistern below the end of the spinal cord, spoken as a list. The answer is the cauda equina and cerebrospinal fluid. The roots of the lower spinal nerves float in the fluid like a horse's tail, which gives the bundle its name. A needle can pass among them with little risk of injury.

Question 5715. Which statement about the chemistry of cerebrospinal fluid is correct: it is as rich in protein as plasma, its glucose is about two thirds of the blood glucose, it contains many red cells in health, or it is identical to plasma? The answer is that its glucose is about two thirds of the blood glucose. Protein is far lower than in plasma and the fluid is clear.

Question 5716. Which of the three meninges is the thin delicate membrane that clings to the surface of the brain and dips into every sulcus? The answer is the pia mater. It carries the small vessels that supply the cortex. The arachnoid mater lies outside it and bridges across the sulci instead of following them.

Question 5717. A constructed illustration: a person has a stiff neck, an ache in the head and a dislike of bright light, and fluid is taken from the lumbar cistern. Which two meninges are inflamed together in this picture? The answer is the pia mater and the arachnoid mater. They are inflamed as one sheet, which is why the condition is named after the meninges. All management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 5718. Which pair belongs together: the superior sagittal sinus with the upper margin of the falx cerebri, the straight sinus with the diaphragm of the sella, the transverse sinus with the falx cerebelli, or the cavernous sinus with the vault of the skull? The answer is the superior sagittal sinus with the upper margin of the falx cerebri. The straight sinus lies where the falx cerebri meets the tentorium.

Question 5719. A described CT scan of an older adult shows a crescent-shaped collection of blood over one hemisphere, crossing the suture lines and pressing the cortex inward. Which vessels are the usual source? The answer is the bridging cerebral veins. They cross the subdural space to reach the dural sinuses and stretch as the brain shrinks with age. The collection is a subdural hematoma.

Question 5720. Which two structures lie within the cavernous sinus: the internal carotid artery and the sixth cranial nerve, the middle meningeal artery and the facial nerve, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve, or the vertebral artery and the twelfth cranial nerve? The answer is the internal carotid artery and the sixth cranial nerve. The sixth nerve lies free beside the artery, and the third and fourth cranial nerves run in the wall.

Question 5721. Which cranial nerve runs in the lateral wall of the cavernous sinus rather than through its cavity, and why does that matter when the sinus is diseased? The answer is the third cranial nerve, together with the fourth and the first two divisions of the fifth. The sixth cranial nerve lies free in the cavity beside the internal carotid artery, so it is the first to be pressed upon.

Question 5722. True or false: the epidural space of the spinal canal contains fat and a plexus of veins. The answer is true. It lies between the dura mater and the periosteum of the vertebrae. The venous plexus there is valveless, and it can be a route by which a tumor spreads to the spine.

Question 5723. Which saw-toothed fold of pia mater runs along each side of the spinal cord and anchors it to the dura mater? The answer is the denticulate ligament. It lies between the dorsal and the ventral roots of the spinal nerves. It is one of the few places where the pia mater does not merely follow the surface.

Question 5724. A constructed illustration: a person with a rapidly expanding lesion in one cerebral hemisphere becomes drowsy and one pupil is widely dilated and fixed. Which structure has been compressed? The answer is the third cranial nerve, caught in the tentorial notch as the temporal lobe herniates. The fibers that constrict the pupil fail first. The detail is an illustration and not a record of a real case.

Question 5725. About how much cerebrospinal fluid does an adult hold at any one time: 15 milliliters, 50 milliliters, 150 milliliters, or 500 milliliters? The answer is 150 milliliters. About a quarter of that lies in the ventricles and the rest surrounds the brain and the cord. The choroid plexus renews the whole volume several times a day.

Question 5726. A described radiograph of the lower back shows a needle tip between the third and fourth lumbar spines, with bone above and below and no cord in its track. Why is that level chosen? The answer is that the spinal cord has already ended above it. Only the cauda equina occupies the lumbar cistern. The needle enters the subarachnoid space without touching the cord.

Question 5727. What is the name of the large subarachnoid cistern that lies behind the cerebellum and above the medulla, and is entered when fluid is taken near the base of the skull? The answer is the cisterna magna, or cerebellomedullary cistern. It lies between the cerebellum and the medulla. The fourth ventricle opens into it through the median aperture.

Question 5728. Which dural venous sinus lies immediately lateral to the body of the sphenoid bone, receives blood from the ophthalmic veins, and drains backward through the petrosal sinuses? The answer is the cavernous sinus. It lies on each side of the sella turcica. The internal carotid artery runs through it.

Question 5729. Which opening connects the fourth ventricle with the subarachnoid space: the interventricular foramen, the cerebral aqueduct, the median aperture, or the central canal? The answer is the median aperture. Two lateral apertures also open into the subarachnoid space, one on each side. The central canal continues downward into the spinal cord.

Question 5730. A constructed illustration: after fluid is taken from the lumbar cistern, a person develops a severe headache that is worse on standing. Which change explains it? The answer is a leak of cerebrospinal fluid from the dural sac, which lowers the pressure around the brain. The brain loses some of its buoyancy and pulls on its pain-sensitive supports. The detail is an illustration.

Question 5731. A described CT scan shows blood filling the subarachnoid space and collecting in the midline between the two frontal lobes. Which vessel has most likely bled? The answer is the anterior communicating artery. Aneurysms of the vessels at the base of the brain bleed into the subarachnoid space. Blood settles in the cisterns close to the point of rupture.

Question 5732. What name is given to the narrow channel that connects the third ventricle with the fourth and passes through the midbrain? The answer is the cerebral aqueduct. It is a slender tube through the midbrain. A block here enlarges the ventricles above it.

Question 5733. Which pair belongs together: the lateral apertures with the fourth ventricle, the median aperture with the third ventricle, the interventricular foramen with the fourth ventricle, or the cerebral aqueduct with the lateral ventricles? The answer is the lateral apertures with the fourth ventricle. The two lateral apertures open from the fourth ventricle into the subarachnoid space. The median aperture does the same in the midline.

Question 5734. Which statement about the spaces around the spinal cord is correct: the epidural space lies deep to the dura mater, the subarachnoid space contains cerebrospinal fluid, the subdural space is a wide cavity, or the epidural space contains the cauda equina? The answer is that the subarachnoid space contains cerebrospinal fluid. The epidural space lies outside the dura mater and holds fat and veins.

Question 5735. Which layer of the dura mater forms the periosteum of the inner surface of the skull? The answer is the outer, periosteal layer. It is tightly fused to the bone, especially along the sutures. The inner, meningeal layer is the one that folds inward to form the dural septa.

Question 5736. Which statement about the blood supply of the dura mater is correct: it has no blood supply, the middle meningeal artery supplies most of the vault, the cerebral arteries supply it, or only the scalp vessels reach it? The answer is that the middle meningeal artery supplies most of the vault. It is a branch of the maxillary artery and enters through the foramen spinosum. The dura mater is sensitive to pain.

Question 5737. A constructed illustration: a scalp wound over the temple is explored and the bone beneath is found to be fractured. Which artery is at risk? The answer is the middle meningeal artery, which lies in a groove on the inner surface of the bone. It is most exposed at the pterion, where four bones meet. The illustration shows how a surface injury can threaten a deep vessel.

Question 5738. A described magnetic resonance imaging study of the spine shows a cord whose lower end reaches the sacrum. Which normal structure has failed to regress? The answer is the filum terminale, the thread of pia mater that normally anchors the cord to the coccyx. When it is short and thick it holds the cord low.

Question 5739. Which dural fold lies immediately above the pituitary gland and forms the roof of the sella turcica? The answer is the diaphragma sellae. It is a small circular fold pierced by the stalk of the gland. It separates the pituitary below from the optic chiasma above.

Question 5740. True or false: the brain itself is insensitive to pain, so a headache arises from the meninges, the vessels and the dural sinuses. The answer is true. The brain substance carries no pain fibers. The dura mater at the base of the skull, the cerebral arteries and the great venous sinuses are all sensitive.

Question 5741. Which fluid-filled space follows the optic nerve forward from the cranial cavity and surrounds it as far as the back of the eye? The answer is the subarachnoid space. It extends along the nerve within its sheath. Raised pressure inside the skull is therefore transmitted to the optic disc.

Question 5742. Which statement about the superior sagittal sinus is correct: it begins at the confluence of the sinuses, it ends at the confluence of the sinuses near the internal occipital protuberance, it lies along the floor of the middle cranial fossa, or it opens into the internal jugular vein? The answer is that it ends at the confluence of the sinuses. Blood then passes into the transverse sinuses.

Question 5743. Within the ventricular system, does the third ventricle lie above or below the lateral ventricles, and in front of or behind the fourth? The answer is that the third ventricle lies below the lateral ventricles and above and in front of the fourth. The interventricular foramina connect it with the lateral ventricles above. The cerebral aqueduct carries fluid back and downward to the fourth.

Question 5744. A described specimen shows the base of the skull with the brain removed and a thick white sheet standing in the midline between the two halves of the cerebellum. Which fold is shown? The answer is the falx cerebelli. It runs downward behind the fourth ventricle. The tentorium cerebelli lies above it and covers the upper surface of the cerebellum.

Question 5745. A blow to the vertex tears vessels along the midline without any fracture. Which statement is correct: the bleeding comes from the middle meningeal artery, from the bridging veins entering the superior sagittal sinus, from the ophthalmic artery, or from the vertebral artery? The answer is the bridging veins entering the superior sagittal sinus. They cross the subdural space near the midline of the vertex.

Question 5746. Which cistern lies immediately behind the optic chiasma and contains the ring of vessels at the base of the brain? The answer is the interpeduncular cistern. It lies between the two cerebral peduncles. The chiasmatic cistern lies in front of it, around the optic chiasma.

Question 5747. Which statement about the fourth ventricle is correct: it lies within the midbrain, it lies between the pons and the cerebellum, it lies between the two thalami, or it lies within the frontal lobe? The answer is that it lies between the pons and the cerebellum. Its floor is formed by the pons and the medulla and its roof by the cerebellar vermis.

Question 5748. A described CT scan shows a small rounded mass at the roof of the third ventricle, blocking both interventricular foramina and enlarging both lateral ventricles. Which lesion is described? The answer is a colloid cyst. It arises at the roof of the third ventricle and obstructs the flow from both lateral ventricles at once. The ventricles above the block enlarge.

Question 5749. Which meningeal layer is a delicate transparent membrane that bridges across the sulci of the brain rather than following them into their depths? The answer is the arachnoid mater. It lies between the dura mater outside and the pia mater within. The subarachnoid space beneath it is filled with cerebrospinal fluid.

Question 5750. Which statement about the fate of cerebrospinal fluid is correct: it leaves by the arachnoid granulations into the dural sinuses, it leaves by the choroid plexus into the veins, it leaves by the denticulate ligament, or it is absorbed by the pia mater? The answer is that it leaves by the arachnoid granulations into the dural sinuses. The granulations project into the superior sagittal sinus.

Question 5751. Which pathway carries fine touch, vibration and the sense of position from the trunk and the limbs to the brain? The answer is the dorsal column and medial lemniscus pathway. Its fibers ascend in the posterior funiculus of the spinal cord. They cross in the medulla oblongata and end in the thalamus.

Question 5752. Which of these sensations is carried by the spinothalamic tract: fine touch, vibration, pain and temperature, or the sense of position? The answer is pain and temperature. The tract also carries crude touch. Fine touch, vibration and position sense travel in the dorsal columns instead.

Question 5753. Which part of the posterior funiculus carries fibers from the lower limb and lies nearest the midline? The answer is the fasciculus gracilis. Fibers from the upper limb lie lateral to it in the fasciculus cuneatus. The gracile nucleus lies in the lower medulla and the cuneate nucleus above it.

Question 5754. A described microscopic section of the spinal cord shows a large bundle of myelinated fibers in the posterior funiculus close to the midline. From which part of the body do those fibers come? The answer is the lower limb and the lower trunk. The medial bundle is the fasciculus gracilis. Fibers from the upper limb lie lateral to it.

Question 5755. Which crossing lies on the anterior surface of the lower medulla, and which tract does it carry? The answer is the decussation of the pyramids, which carries the corticospinal tract. The fibers pass from the front of one side to the lateral funiculus of the other. The medial lemniscus crosses higher up.

Question 5756. Where does the second order neuron of the spinothalamic tract lie: in the dorsal root ganglion, in the posterior horn of the spinal cord, in the nucleus gracilis, or in the thalamus? The answer is in the posterior horn. Its fiber crosses to the opposite side within one or two segments and then ascends. The first order neuron lies in the dorsal root ganglion.

Question 5757. Which nucleus of the thalamus receives the fibers of the spinothalamic tract and the medial lemniscus on their way to the cortex? The answer is the ventral posterolateral nucleus. It lies in the ventral tier of the thalamus. Fibers for the face end in the ventral posteromedial nucleus beside it.

Question 5758. A described section of the medulla shows a wedge-shaped nucleus in its lower part, receiving fibers from the medial part of the posterior column. Which structure is described, and where do its fibers go? The answer is the nucleus gracilis. Its fibers cross as the internal arcuate fibers and form the medial lemniscus on the opposite side. From there they ascend to the thalamus.

Question 5759. A constructed illustration: a person loses pain and temperature sense on the right side of the body, and position sense on the left. Where is the single lesion? The answer is a hemisection of the left side of the spinal cord. The crossed spinothalamic fibers are cut, so pain sense is lost on the opposite side, and the uncrossed dorsal column fibers are cut, so position sense is lost on the same side.

Question 5760. Which structure holds the cell body of the first order neuron for fine touch from the hand: the dorsal root ganglion, the posterior horn, the nucleus cuneatus, or the thalamus? The answer is the dorsal root ganglion. Its fiber enters the cord and ascends in the cuneate fasciculus. It ends in the nucleus cuneatus of the medulla.

Question 5761. True or false: the corticospinal tract takes most of its fibers from the precentral gyrus of the frontal lobe. The answer is true. The large motor cells of that gyrus give rise to the tract. Its fibers pass through the internal capsule and the brainstem to reach the spinal cord.

Question 5762. Which part of the internal capsule carries the corticospinal fibers on their way from the cortex to the brainstem: the anterior limb, the genu, the posterior limb, or the part behind the lentiform nucleus? The answer is the posterior limb. It is a narrow band of white matter between the thalamus and the lentiform nucleus. A small lesion there causes a dense weakness of the opposite side.

Question 5763. Roughly what share of the corticospinal fibers cross in the medulla oblongata, and what is that crossing called? The answer is about nine in ten, at the decussation of the pyramids. The remainder descends uncrossed as the anterior corticospinal tract. Those fibers cross later, at the segment where they end.

Question 5764. A described section of the spinal cord shows a small bundle of fibers beside the anterior median fissure on each side. Which tract is shown: the anterior corticospinal tract, the lateral corticospinal tract, the dorsal column, or the spinothalamic tract? The answer is the anterior corticospinal tract. It holds the fibers that did not cross in the medulla, and they cross in the cord where they end.

Question 5765. Which pair belongs together: the lateral corticospinal tract with the distal muscles of the limbs, the anterior corticospinal tract with the muscles of the face, the dorsal column with pain, or the spinothalamic tract with vibration? The answer is the lateral corticospinal tract with the distal muscles of the limbs. The anterior tract supplies the axial muscles of the trunk. Fine touch and vibration belong to the dorsal columns.

Question 5766. Which descending tract carries fibers that end on the motor nuclei of the cranial nerves and control the muscles of the face, the tongue and the throat? The answer is the corticobulbar tract. It runs with the corticospinal fibers through the internal capsule. The word bulbar refers to the brainstem.

Question 5767. Which statement about the endings of the corticospinal tract is correct: every fiber ends on a lower motor neuron, most fibers end on interneurons of the spinal gray matter, the tract carries sensation upward, or all of it crosses in the spinal cord? The answer is that most of its fibers end on interneurons. Only a few end directly on the large motor cells of the fingers and the toes.

Question 5768. A constructed illustration: after a small stroke, one arm, one leg and the lower part of the face are weak on the same side of the body. Where is the lesion? The answer is in the internal capsule on the opposite side. Motor fibers run close together there, so one small lesion can weaken a whole side. The illustration is not a record of a real case.

Question 5769. Which large cells of the motor cortex give rise to the thickest fibers of the corticospinal tract? The answer is the large pyramidal cells of Betz. They lie in the precentral gyrus. Their thick axons conduct impulses quickly to the lower motor neurons of the limbs.

Question 5770. A described drawing of the precentral gyrus shows a map in which the foot lies at the top and the face at the bottom, with a huge area for the hand. Which map is shown? The answer is the motor homunculus. The body is represented upside down, and the hand and the face take a share far larger than their size.

Question 5771. Which gyrus lies immediately behind the central sulcus and receives the fibers of the sensory pathways? The answer is the postcentral gyrus of the parietal lobe. It is the primary somatosensory cortex. The precentral gyrus in front of the sulcus is the motor cortex.

Question 5772. Which artery supplies most of the lateral surface of the hemisphere, including the motor and sensory areas for the face and the hand: the anterior cerebral artery, the middle cerebral artery, the posterior cerebral artery, or the basilar artery? The answer is the middle cerebral artery. Its branches run in the lateral sulcus. The anterior cerebral artery supplies the medial surface as far back as the foot area.

Question 5773. Which tract carries the sense of position from the leg to the cerebellum without crossing, and through which peduncle does it enter? The answer is the dorsal spinocerebellar tract, which enters through the inferior cerebellar peduncle. It carries information from the muscle spindles and tendons of the lower limb. The cerebellum uses it to adjust posture and movement.

Question 5774. Which pair belongs together: the cuneocerebellar tract with the upper limb, the dorsal spinocerebellar tract with the face, the ventral spinocerebellar tract with the eye, or the olivocerebellar fibers with the cerebral cortex? The answer is the cuneocerebellar tract with the upper limb. It carries position sense from the arm and the neck. The dorsal spinocerebellar tract serves the lower limb and the trunk.

Question 5775. Which structure lies between the thalamus and the lentiform nucleus and carries both motor and sensory fibers: the corpus callosum, the internal capsule, the fornix, or the cingulum? The answer is the internal capsule. Its fibers run between the two masses of gray matter. A lesion there interrupts a wide range of ascending and descending traffic.

Question 5776. True or false: in an upper motor neuron lesion the muscles become flaccid and wasted and the reflexes are lost. The answer is false. Those are the signs of a lower motor neuron lesion. An upper motor neuron lesion produces spasticity, increased reflexes and an extensor plantar response.

Question 5777. A constructed illustration: one corner of the mouth droops and the arm and the leg on that same side are weak and spastic. Is the lesion above or below the crossing of the corticospinal tract? The answer is above the crossing, in the brain or the brainstem. A single lesion above the medulla weakens the face and the limbs on one side, while a lesion below it would spare the face.

Question 5778. Name two signs of an upper motor neuron lesion and two signs of a lower motor neuron lesion, spoken as a list. The answer is spasticity and increased reflexes for the upper lesion, and flaccidity and wasting for the lower one. The plantar response is extensor in the upper lesion. The tone falls and the reflexes fade in the lower one.

Question 5779. Within the posterior funiculus, which bundle lies medial and which lateral, and what does that arrangement reflect about the body? The answer is that the gracile bundle lies medial and the cuneate bundle lateral. Fibers from the lower limb enter first and are pushed medially as the cord is built upward. Fibers from the upper limb enter higher and stay lateral.

Question 5780. A described section of the midbrain shows a pair of large bundles at the front, each filled with fibers descending from the cortex. Which tracts do those bundles carry? The answer is the corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts, within the cerebral peduncles. They lie at the front of the midbrain, in the crus cerebri. The substantia nigra lies just behind them.

Question 5781. Which nucleus lies immediately lateral to the cuneate nucleus in the lower medulla, and what sensation does it serve? The answer is the spinal nucleus of the trigeminal nerve, which serves pain and temperature from the face. It lies dorsolateral to the cuneate nucleus. The fibers that end there descend in the spinal tract of the same nerve before they cross.

Question 5782. Where do the fibers of the dorsal column pathway cross to the opposite side: in the spinal cord, in the medulla oblongata, in the pons, or in the thalamus? The answer is in the medulla oblongata. They cross as the internal arcuate fibers. The spinothalamic fibers cross in the spinal cord instead.

Question 5783. Which structure is the last relay before the sensory cortex for touch and position sense from the body? The answer is the ventral posterolateral nucleus of the thalamus. It lies in the ventral tier of the thalamus. From there the fibers pass through the internal capsule to the postcentral gyrus.

Question 5784. A constructed illustration: a person loses pain and temperature sense over the right side of the face and over the left side of the body, with hoarseness and difficulty in swallowing. Where is the lesion? The answer is the lateral medulla on the right side. The spinal tract and nucleus of the trigeminal nerve are involved before they cross, so the face loses the same sensations on the same side.

Question 5785. A described microscopic section of the spinal cord is stained to show myelin, and a wedge of white matter lies between the posterior gray horn and the posterior median septum. Which funiculus is shown? The answer is the posterior funiculus. It carries the dorsal column fibers upward. Its two bundles are the gracile and the cuneate fasciculi.

Question 5786. Which statement about the spinothalamic tract is correct: it crosses where it enters, it crosses one or two segments above entry, it crosses in the medulla, or it does not cross at all? The answer is that it crosses one or two segments above entry. That crossing lies in the anterior white commissure, so a small cord lesion can spare pain sense at the entering segment.

Question 5787. Which statement is correct: all these sensations travel in one tract, fine touch and vibration travel in the dorsal columns while pain travels in the spinothalamic tract, pain travels in the dorsal columns with vibration, or both pathways cross at the same level? The answer is that fine touch and vibration travel in the dorsal columns while pain travels in the spinothalamic tract. They cross at different levels.

Question 5788. Which pair belongs together: the precentral gyrus with motor control, the postcentral gyrus with the primary visual area, the occipital lobe with hearing, or the temporal lobe with touch? The answer is the precentral gyrus with motor control. The postcentral gyrus is the primary sensory area. Hearing belongs to the temporal lobe and vision to the occipital lobe.

Question 5789. Which long descending tract lies in the anterior funiculus, comes from the vestibular nuclei, and helps to keep posture and muscle tone? The answer is the vestibulospinal tract. It descends uncrossed for the most part. The reticulospinal tract lies beside it and serves similar postural work.

Question 5790. A described brain slice shows a band of white matter running between the thalamus and the lentiform nucleus, with a smaller band in front of the thalamus. Which parts of the internal capsule are shown? The answer is the posterior limb and the anterior limb. The genu lies between them and carries the corticobulbar fibers. The posterior limb carries the corticospinal and the sensory fibers.

Question 5791. Which tract lies immediately behind the anterior median fissure of the spinal cord, and which tract lies just lateral to it? The answer is the anterior corticospinal tract, with the anterior spinothalamic tract beside it. Both run in the anterior funiculus. The lateral funiculus behind them holds the lateral corticospinal and the lateral spinothalamic tracts.

Question 5792. Which function belongs to the cerebellum: the conscious perception of touch, the coordination of movement, the secretion of cerebrospinal fluid, or the relay of pain to the cortex? The answer is the coordination of movement. The cerebellum compares the intention of the motor cortex with what the body is actually doing. It acts through the thalamus and the brainstem rather than directly on the lower motor neurons.

Question 5793. A constructed illustration: a person has a tremor that is worst at rest, with rigidity and slow movement, and degeneration is found in the midbrain. Which cells have been lost? The answer is the pigmented cells of the substantia nigra. They send fibers to the striatum and help to smooth the output of the motor cortex, so their loss makes movement slow and stiff.

Question 5794. How does the medial lemniscus run through the brainstem: downward to the spinal cord, upward and laterally through the medulla, the pons and the midbrain to the thalamus, sideways between the hemispheres, or upward through the cerebellum? The answer is upward and laterally through the medulla, the pons and the midbrain to the thalamus. It lies near the midline at first and swings outward as it ascends.

Question 5795. In which part of the nervous system do the cell bodies of the lower motor neurons that supply the muscles of the arm lie? The answer is the anterior horn of the cervical spinal cord. Their axons leave through the ventral roots. They end on the muscle fibers at the motor end plates.

Question 5796. A described cross section of the spinal cord shows a great deal of white matter around a small central mass of gray matter, with a slender posterior horn. Which region of the cord is shown? The answer is the cervical region. White matter is greatest there because many fibers have not yet left the cord. The anterior horn is broad in the regions that supply the limbs.

Question 5797. A constructed illustration: after a penetrating wound of the back, a person has lost position sense in the right leg but retains normal pain and temperature sense. Which tract has been cut: the right dorsal column, the left dorsal column, the right spinothalamic tract, or the left spinothalamic tract? The answer is the right dorsal column, whose fibers have not crossed, so the loss lies on the same side.

Question 5798. Which nucleus of the trigeminal nerve carries the sense of position from the face and the jaw, and where does it lie? The answer is the mesencephalic nucleus, which lies in the midbrain. It is the only sensory nucleus of the brainstem that holds the cell bodies of first order neurons. Its fibers join the pathway that runs to the thalamus.

Question 5799. Which two tracts lie side by side in the anterior funiculus of the spinal cord, one carrying sensation and the other carrying movement? The answer is the anterior spinothalamic tract and the anterior corticospinal tract. The sensory tract lies nearer the surface and the motor tract near the anterior median fissure. Both are small beside the lateral tracts.

Question 5800. Which statement about the crossing of the sensory pathways is correct: all sensory fibers cross where they enter, all cross in the brainstem, some cross in the cord and some in the brainstem, or none of them cross? The answer is that some cross in the cord and some in the brainstem. Pain and temperature cross in the spinal cord, while fine touch and position sense cross in the medulla.

To summarize, the brain and the spinal cord are wrapped in the dura mater, the arachnoid mater and the pia mater, and the cerebrospinal fluid that fills the ventricles and the subarachnoid space is made by the choroid plexus and returned to the blood by the arachnoid granulations. The dural folds divide the cranial cavity, and the venous sinuses they enclose drain the blood of the brain; the middle meningeal artery, the bridging veins and the cavernous sinus are the structures most often named in clinical accounts of the meninges. Pain and temperature cross in the spinal cord while fine touch and position sense cross in the medulla, and the motor pathways descend through the internal capsule and the brainstem. Knowing where each pathway crosses is what turns a lost sensation or a lost strength into a named lesion.
