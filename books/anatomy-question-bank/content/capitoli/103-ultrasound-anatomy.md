# Chapter 103 — Ultrasound Anatomy

This chapter reads two imaging methods as maps of the body: twenty-five questions on magnetic resonance imaging, followed by seventy-five questions on ultrasound. Magnetic resonance imaging comes first, because its contrast between fat, fluid, muscle and bone shows the deep structures clearly, and ultrasound follows, because it places a probe on the skin and pictures the organs beneath it. Both are spoken in words, since nothing can be shown, so the listener hears what a section or a scan would display. The chapter holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10201. A described magnetic resonance image of the head taken in the midline from the side shows a broad curved band of white matter arching above the thalamus and joining the two hemispheres. Which structure is it? The answer is the corpus callosum. Its fibers cross the midline and connect matching areas of the two hemispheres, and the band is seen in full only in that midline section.

Question 10202. On a magnetic resonance image weighted to show fluid brightly, which structure is the brightest: the cerebrospinal fluid, the spinal cord, the cortical bone, or the outer ring of an intervertebral disc? The answer is the cerebrospinal fluid. It fills the spinal canal as a bright column around the darker cord.

Question 10203. A described magnetic resonance image of the knee from the side shows two dark triangular wedges between the rounded end of the femur and the flat top of the tibia. Which structures are the wedges? The answer is the menisci. Fibrocartilage gives little signal, so each appears as a dark triangle in section, deepening the socket of the joint.

Question 10204. On a described cross-sectional magnetic resonance image of the abdomen at the third lumbar vertebra, a round vessel lies immediately in front of the vertebral body and a little to the left of the midline. Which vessel is it? The answer is the abdominal aorta. It descends in front of the spine and slightly to the left, with the inferior vena cava to its right.

Question 10205. In a described midline magnetic resonance image of the head, a small rounded structure sits in the hollow of the sphenoid bone, below the crossing of the optic nerves. Which structure is it? The answer is the pituitary gland. It rests in the sella turcica and is joined to the brain above by a thin stalk.

Question 10206. On a magnetic resonance image weighted to show fat brightly, which tissue returns the strongest signal: fat, cortical bone, skeletal muscle, or cerebrospinal fluid? The answer is fat. It outlines the marrow, the subcutaneous layer and the spaces between muscles, while cortical bone and flowing blood return little signal.

Question 10207. A constructed illustration: a person with pain shooting down the back of the leg has a magnetic resonance image showing a disc bulging backward and to one side between the fourth and fifth lumbar vertebrae. Which nerve root is most likely compressed? The answer is the fifth lumbar nerve root. A disc at that level presses the root that runs on past it toward the foot.

Question 10208. True or false: on most magnetic resonance images, cortical bone and ligaments return little signal and appear dark. The answer is true. Dense collagen and mineralized bone hold few mobile protons, so they give almost no signal, while fat and fluid stand out as bright.

Question 10209. Within an intervertebral disc on a described magnetic resonance image, which part is bright on a sequence that emphasizes fluid: the central nucleus pulposus, the outer annulus fibrosus, the vertebral end plate, or the posterior longitudinal ligament? The answer is the central nucleus pulposus. It holds water and gives a bright center, while the fibrous ring around it stays dark.

Question 10210. A described cross-sectional magnetic resonance image of the brain shows a band of white matter shaped like a bent V, between a deep gray mass on its inner side and a lens-shaped gray mass on its outer side. Which structure is the band? The answer is the internal capsule. Its fibers carry messages between the cerebral cortex and the structures below it.

Question 10211. In a described cross-sectional magnetic resonance image of the pelvis, a large nerve leaves the pelvis below a muscle that runs out through the greater sciatic foramen. Which nerve lies immediately inferior to that muscle? The answer is the sciatic nerve. The muscle is the piriformis, and in a small minority of people the nerve passes through it instead.

Question 10212. A described magnetic resonance image of the ankle shows a thick dark tendon curving behind the medial malleolus and running on to the bones of the midfoot. Which tendon is it? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior muscle. A fibrous band holds it down behind the malleolus, and its main insertion is on the navicular bone.

Question 10213. In a described four-chamber magnetic resonance image of the heart, which chamber forms the front wall and lies closest to the chest wall: the right ventricle, the left ventricle, the left atrium, or the right atrium? The answer is the right ventricle. It wraps around the front of the heart, while the left ventricle forms the apex and the back.

Question 10214. A described magnetic resonance image of the leg shows a nerve winding around the neck of the fibula, where a small mass presses on it. Loss of which movement would follow: turning the sole outward and lifting the toes, turning the sole inward, bending the knee, or straightening the hip? The answer is turning the sole outward and lifting the toes. That is the work of the common fibular nerve.

Question 10215. Which of these pairs belongs together: a T2-weighted image with bright fat, a T1-weighted image with bright cerebrospinal fluid, a T2-weighted image with bright cerebrospinal fluid, or a T1-weighted image with dark fat? The answer is a T2-weighted image with bright cerebrospinal fluid. Fluid is bright on that sequence, and fat is bright on the T1-weighted one.

Question 10216. Name two tissues that return little signal on both T1-weighted and T2-weighted magnetic resonance images, spoken as a list. The answer is cortical bone and tendons. Neither holds much free water, so each stays dark on both sequences, while fat and fluid change brightness most between them.

Question 10217. A described magnetic resonance image of the abdomen taken across the body shows a kidney split into an outer rim and a set of darker triangular wedges that point toward the center. Which parts are the rim and the wedges? The answer is the cortex outside and the medullary pyramids inside, and the cortex also dips between the pyramids.

Question 10218. In a described magnetic resonance image of the liver, which vessel separates the right lobe from the left lobe: the middle hepatic vein, the right hepatic vein, the portal vein, or the inferior vena cava? The answer is the middle hepatic vein. It runs in the plane between the two halves, and the right hepatic vein divides the right half further.

Question 10219. In a described cross-sectional magnetic resonance image of the upper abdomen, a long vessel crosses in front of the aorta on its way to the left kidney. Which vessel is it? The answer is the left renal vein. It is longer than the right renal vein because the inferior vena cava lies on the right.

Question 10220. In a described cross-sectional magnetic resonance image of the neck, two oval lobes lie on either side of the trachea and in front of the great vessels. Which gland forms them? The answer is the thyroid gland. The lobes are joined across the front of the airway by the isthmus, and the gland moves when the person swallows.

Question 10221. A described magnetic resonance image of the female pelvis from the side shows a pear-shaped organ behind the bladder with a bright central band and a darker ring around it. Which layer is the bright band? The answer is the endometrium, the lining of the uterus. The darker ring is the myometrium, the muscle of the uterine wall.

Question 10222. In an adult, the lower end of the spinal cord lies at the first or second lumbar vertebra. Which explanation fits: the vertebral column grows faster than the cord, a fibrous thread pulls the cord down, the sacrum forms only after birth, or the cord stops growing at birth? The answer is that the vertebral column grows faster. The two are level in the fetus, and the canal then outgrows the cord.

Question 10223. A described magnetic resonance image of the shoulder shows a tendon passing beneath the arch of the acromion and above the head of the humerus. Which tendon is it: the supraspinatus, the subscapularis, the teres major, or the long head of the biceps? The answer is the supraspinatus tendon. It runs through the narrow space under the acromion, where it is the tendon most often torn.

Question 10224. A constructed illustration: a person has ringing in one ear and unsteady balance, and a magnetic resonance image shows a small mass in the angle between the cerebellum and the pons. Which cranial nerve is involved? The answer is the eighth cranial nerve, the vestibulocochlear nerve. It runs through that angle carrying fibers for hearing and for balance.

Question 10225. A described image of the head shows a section running across the body at right angles to its long axis, displaying the two lateral ventricles side by side, the third ventricle in the midline, and a deep groove on each side. Which plane produced it, and what does it divide? The answer is the transverse plane, which divides the body into an upper and a lower part.

Question 10226. On a described ultrasound image of the right upper abdomen, a pear-shaped sac lies against the under surface of the liver with a black interior and a bright thin wall. Which structure is it? The answer is the gallbladder. Bile is fluid, so it returns no echoes and appears black.

Question 10227. On an ultrasound image, which of these structures appears black because it contains fluid: the lumen of the gallbladder, the pancreas, the spleen, or the wall of the stomach? The answer is the lumen of the gallbladder. Fluid lets sound pass through without reflecting it, so it looks black, while solid organs have an even gray texture.

Question 10228. A described ultrasound image shows a bright curved line inside the gallbladder, and behind it the picture is dark, with the deep tissues hidden. What does that bright line represent? The answer is a gallstone. The stone reflects sound strongly, and the dark band behind it is the acoustic shadow it casts.

Question 10229. On a described ultrasound image of the liver, the gallbladder lies in a shallow bed between the right part of the liver and a small square lobe. Which lobe lies immediately to the left of that bed? The answer is the quadrate lobe. It lies on the under surface of the liver, beside the groove for the round ligament.

Question 10230. A described ultrasound image of a kidney shows an outer layer that is slightly darker than the bright middle of the organ and sends small columns inward between darker triangles. Which part is that outer layer? The answer is the renal cortex. The triangles are the medullary pyramids, and the cortex arches over their bases.

Question 10231. In the normal kidney on ultrasound, which part is the brightest: the renal sinus, the renal cortex, the medullary pyramid, or the blood in the renal vein? The answer is the renal sinus. It holds fat around the collecting system, and fat reflects sound strongly. The pyramids are the darkest part.

Question 10232. A described ultrasound image of a kidney shows a black branching structure in the center of the organ, with rounded black spaces spreading into it and the cortex stretched thin around them. What does the image show? The answer is a kidney swollen by trapped urine, with the collecting system widened by fluid that cannot drain.

Question 10233. A constructed illustration: a person has severe cramping pain in the flank that comes in waves, and ultrasound of the kidney shows a bright stone with a dark shadow behind it, lodged where the collecting system leaves the organ. Which structure holds the stone? The answer is the ureter, where it begins at the renal pelvis. A stone there blocks the flow of urine.

Question 10234. On a described cross-sectional ultrasound image of the neck, a thin-walled vessel that can be pressed flat lies immediately lateral to the common carotid artery. Which vessel is it: the internal jugular vein, the external carotid artery, the vertebral artery, or the inferior thyroid artery? The answer is the internal jugular vein, which lies beside the artery in the carotid sheath.

Question 10235. Which of these vessels is easily pressed flat by the ultrasound probe because its wall is thin and its pressure is low: the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, the femoral artery, or the abdominal aorta? The answer is the internal jugular vein. Veins collapse when the probe presses on them, while arteries keep their round shape.

Question 10236. A described ultrasound image of the front of the neck shows a gland shaped like a butterfly, with two lobes lying on either side of the windpipe and joined across the front. Which gland is it? The answer is the thyroid gland. It moves with the larynx when the person swallows.

Question 10237. A described ultrasound image of the neck taken across the body shows a butterfly-shaped structure in front of the trachea, with a very even gray texture, and two round dark vessels beside it. Which structure is in the middle? The answer is the thyroid gland, whose lobes lie on either side of the airway. The dark vessels are the common carotid artery and the internal jugular vein.

Question 10238. True or false: on ultrasound, a stone casts a dark shadow behind it, while a simple cyst makes the tissues behind it look brighter than their neighbors. The answer is true. A stone reflects sound back and leaves a shadow, and fluid transmits it well, so the tissue behind a cyst is brightened.

Question 10239. On a described ultrasound image across the abdomen, two large vessels lie in front of the spine. One pulsates, and the vessel beside it is thinner walled and changes width with breathing. Which vessel is the second one: the inferior vena cava, the aorta, the superior mesenteric artery, or the left renal vein? The answer is the inferior vena cava, which lies to the right of the aorta.

Question 10240. Which of these pairs belongs together: the head of the pancreas lying against the inferior vena cava, the tail of the pancreas against the gallbladder, the body of the pancreas against the spleen, or the neck of the pancreas against the left kidney? The answer is the head of the pancreas with the inferior vena cava, which lies behind it.

Question 10241. A constructed illustration: an older person has a pulsing feeling in the middle of the abdomen, and ultrasound shows a segment of a vessel wider than the part above it that expands with every beat. Which vessel is involved? The answer is the abdominal aorta. The widening is described as an illustration, and its cause belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 10242. Which of these pairs belongs together: the spleen with the left upper abdomen behind the stomach, the gallbladder with the left upper abdomen, the right kidney with the left upper abdomen, or the tail of the pancreas with the right iliac fossa? The answer is the spleen with the left upper abdomen behind the stomach. The gallbladder and the right kidney lie on the right.

Question 10243. Name two organs of the right upper abdomen that lie against the diaphragm, spoken as a list. The answer is the liver and the gallbladder. The dome of the liver lies against the diaphragm, and the gallbladder rests on the under surface of the liver. Both move with the diaphragm during breathing.

Question 10244. A described ultrasound image of the liver shows a branching tube whose walls are bright and whose lumen is black, unlike the hepatic veins nearby, whose walls are barely seen. Which vessel is it? The answer is the portal vein. Its fibrous sheath reflects sound and shows as a bright wall.

Question 10245. A described ultrasound image taken along the free edge of the lesser omentum shows three tubes running together toward the liver: one with a bright wall and a black lumen, one with a thick wall that pulsates, and one with a thin wall. Which structures form that bundle? The answer is the portal vein, the proper hepatic artery and the common bile duct, which run together as the portal triad.

Question 10246. In a described ultrasound image taken along the free edge of the lesser omentum, which of these structures lies in that fold together with the proper hepatic artery and the common bile duct: the portal vein, the inferior vena cava, the splenic vein, or the left gastric artery? The answer is the portal vein. The three structures form the portal triad.

Question 10247. Within the portal triad, one tube lies behind the other two. The proper hepatic artery lies to the left and in front, and the common bile duct lies in front and to the right. Which structure lies most posteriorly? The answer is the portal vein, which forms the back of the triad and is the largest of the three.

Question 10248. A constructed illustration: a person has yellow skin and eyes, and ultrasound shows black branching tubes throughout the liver, with the main duct below the liver wider than the vessel beside it. Which structure is most likely blocked? The answer is the common bile duct. Bile is dammed up behind the block, so the ducts widen.

Question 10249. Which organ on ultrasound has a smooth bright capsule around an even texture, and is crossed by bright lines that follow the branches of the portal vein: the liver, the spleen, the pancreas, or the kidney? The answer is the liver. The kidney, by contrast, is clearly divided into cortex and medulla.

Question 10250. A described ultrasound image of the left upper abdomen shows a crescent-shaped organ with an even texture, lying behind the stomach and above the left kidney. Which organ is it? The answer is the spleen. It lies against the diaphragm and is tucked behind the stomach and the tail of the pancreas.

Question 10251. A described ultrasound image across the left upper abdomen shows a crescent of even gray tissue behind a hollow organ with a bright folded lining, and above a bean-shaped organ with a bright center. Which structure is the crescent? The answer is the spleen. The hollow organ in front of it is the stomach, and the bean-shaped one below is the left kidney.

Question 10252. Which part of the pancreas lies closest to the spleen and reaches farthest to the left: the head, the neck, the body, or the tail? The answer is the tail. It runs toward the spleen along the splenic vessels, while the head lies on the right against the duodenum.

Question 10253. Which part of the pancreas lies directly in front of the place where the splenic vein joins the superior mesenteric vein: the head, the neck, the body, or the tail? The answer is the neck. The portal vein is formed behind the neck, so the gland lies in front of the vein at that point.

Question 10254. A described ultrasound image of the stomach shows its wall as five layers lying one over another, from a bright inner line to a bright outer line, with dark bands between them. What does that pattern represent? The answer is the coats of the stomach wall together with the surfaces between them. Mucosa, submucosa, muscle and serosa each reflect sound differently.

Question 10255. True or false: the pancreas lies behind the stomach and in front of the splenic vein. The answer is true. It is a retroperitoneal organ, so the peritoneum covers only its front, and the stomach lies in front of it. The splenic vein runs along its back.

Question 10256. A described ultrasound image of the female pelvis taken in the midline shows a pear-shaped organ lying behind the bladder with a bright line down its center. Which organ is it? The answer is the uterus. It is tipped forward over the bladder, and the bright line is its lining. Behind it lies the rectum.

Question 10257. A described ultrasound image of the female pelvis shows a pear-shaped organ behind the bladder, and within it a bright band that thickens and thins with the phase of the monthly cycle. Which structure is the band? The answer is the endometrium, the lining of the uterus. The muscle around it, the myometrium, is darker.

Question 10258. A described ultrasound image of the female pelvis shows an oval organ beside the uterus, and inside it several small black circles arranged around the edge. Which organ is it, and what are the circles: the ovary with follicles, the uterus with glands, the bladder with pouches, or the rectum with gas? The answer is the ovary with follicles, which are filled with fluid and so appear black.

Question 10259. A described ultrasound image of the female pelvis shows the ovary hanging beside the uterus. Which vessel of the pelvic wall lies immediately lateral to and behind the ovary? The answer is the internal iliac artery. The ovary lies in a shallow hollow on the lateral wall of the pelvis.

Question 10260. Name two structures that lie in front of the uterus in the female pelvis and are seen together in a midline scan, spoken as a list. The answer is the urinary bladder and the pubic symphysis. Behind the uterus lie the rectum and the sacrum.

Question 10261. A constructed illustration: a person in the first weeks of pregnancy has an ultrasound scan of the pelvis, which shows a small black sac inside the uterus and, within the sac, a small round ring. Which structure is the ring? The answer is the yolk sac. It shows that the sac is a true pregnancy sac within the uterus.

Question 10262. In a described ultrasound image of a pregnant uterus, a dark fluid surrounds a small curled shape and lies between that shape and the wall of the uterus. What is the dark fluid? The answer is amniotic fluid, which fills the amniotic cavity and lets the fetus move and grow. Being fluid, it returns no echoes.

Question 10263. A described ultrasound image of the pelvis shows a thin-walled black rounded structure lying just behind the pubic bone and rising into the lower abdomen. Which structure is it? The answer is the urinary bladder. When it is full its wall is thin and its interior is black.

Question 10264. Which structure lies immediately behind the pubic symphysis when it is full, and is used on ultrasound as a window to the organs behind it: the urinary bladder, the rectum, the uterus, or the prostate gland? The answer is the urinary bladder. Filled with urine, it lets the deeper organs be seen.

Question 10265. A described ultrasound image of the shoulder shows a structure running to the bone with bright parallel lines inside it, and its surface is wrapped in a thin dark rim. Which structure and which covering are shown? The answer is a tendon with its sheath. The bright parallel lines are the fibers of the tendon.

Question 10266. On ultrasound, which tissue produces a bright line at its surface with a dark band beneath it, hiding everything under the surface: fat, muscle, the cortex of a bone, or a tendon? The answer is the cortex of a bone. Sound cannot pass through bone, so it is reflected at the surface and casts a shadow.

Question 10267. In a described ultrasound image of the shoulder, one tendon passes beneath the acromion and above the head of the humerus. Which tendon is it, and where does it lie? The answer is the supraspinatus tendon, which lies above the head of the humerus and below the acromion. It is one of the four tendons of the rotator cuff.

Question 10268. A constructed illustration: a person has pain when lifting the arm sideways, and ultrasound shows a small black collection between the acromion and the tendon beneath it. Which space holds that fluid? The answer is the subacromial bursa, which lies between the acromion and the supraspinatus tendon and lets it glide.

Question 10269. A described ultrasound image of the back of the knee shows a black collection lying between a tendon on the inner side and a muscle beside it, just below the joint line. Between which two structures does it lie? The answer is the semimembranosus tendon and the medial head of the gastrocnemius, where a popliteal cyst forms.

Question 10270. Which recess of the knee joint lies above the kneecap, between the femur and the quadriceps tendon, and is where fluid is most easily seen on ultrasound: the suprapatellar recess, the subpopliteal recess, the posterior recess, or the cruciate recess? The answer is the suprapatellar recess, the upper part of the joint cavity.

Question 10271. A described ultrasound image of the abdomen shows the abdominal aorta as a long tube running from the top of the picture to the bottom, with the bodies of the vertebrae as a bright line behind it and the inferior vena cava beside it. Along which axis was this section taken? The answer is the long axis of the body, in a sagittal plane.

Question 10272. A described ultrasound image taken across the upper abdomen shows two black circles side by side in front of the spine. Which one lies on the right side of the person being examined, and what is its relation to the other? The answer is the inferior vena cava, and it lies immediately to the right of the aorta. The side of the picture is not the side of the person.

Question 10273. In a described ultrasound image of the groin, three structures lie in a row within the femoral triangle; from lateral to medial they are a nerve, an artery and a vein. Which structure is in the middle? The answer is the femoral artery. It lies between the femoral nerve, which is lateral to it, and the femoral vein, which is medial.

Question 10274. True or false: in the femoral triangle, the femoral nerve lies lateral to the femoral artery. The answer is true. The order from lateral to medial is nerve, artery, vein, and the narrow space beside the vein lies most medially. The nerve leaves the triangle above the vessels.

Question 10275. Which vein lies just under the skin of the inner thigh and joins the femoral vein below the inguinal ligament: the great saphenous vein, the small saphenous vein, the popliteal vein, or the external iliac vein? The answer is the great saphenous vein, the longest vein in the body. The small saphenous vein ends behind the knee.

Question 10276. In the femoral triangle, the femoral vein lies immediately medial to the femoral artery. Which structure lies immediately medial to the femoral vein, forming the most medial compartment of the triangle? The answer is the femoral canal, a short passage that carries lymph vessels and a lymph node.

Question 10277. A described ultrasound image of a leg vein shows that the vein does not close when the probe presses on it, and that its lumen holds bright material instead of being black. Which interpretation fits: the vein contains a clot, the vein is normal, the structure is an artery, or the structure is a nerve? The answer is that the vein contains a clot. A normal vein closes under gentle pressure.

Question 10278. True or false: a normal vein is easily pressed flat by the ultrasound probe, while a normal artery keeps its round shape under the same pressure. The answer is true. The wall of a vein is thin and its pressure is low, so the probe closes it, while an artery stays open.

Question 10279. A described ultrasound image of the upper abdomen shows a bright curved line above the liver, and above that line a second picture of the liver itself, with its vessels running the wrong way. What do the line and the second picture represent? The answer is the diaphragm and a mirror artifact. The dome reflects sound back, so the machine places the echoes beyond it.

Question 10280. In a described scan of the chest wall between two ribs, which structure is seen sliding to and fro against the inner surface of the chest wall with each breath: the visceral pleura, the parietal pleura, the pericardium, or the diaphragm? The answer is the visceral pleura. It covers the lung and moves with it, while the parietal pleura stays still.

Question 10281. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the chest, ultrasound of the lower chest shows a black collection lying above the bright line of the diaphragm and below the lung. Which space holds the fluid? The answer is the pleural cavity, and the collection is fluid or blood in it. What the collection is belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 10282. A described ultrasound image taken between two ribs shows a bright line at the surface of the lung, and below and behind it a black band that widens as the person breathes in. What does the picture show? The answer is fluid in the pleural cavity. The black band separates the lung from the chest wall and gathers at the lowest point.

Question 10283. In a described ultrasound image of the upper abdomen, a small black collection of fluid lies between the liver and the right kidney in a person lying flat. Which recess of the peritoneal cavity is that? The answer is the hepatorenal pouch, the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity when the person lies on the back.

Question 10284. A described ultrasound image of an infant's head, taken through the soft spot on the top of the skull, shows two black wing-shaped spaces lying one on each side of the midline. Which spaces are they? The answer is the lateral ventricles, filled with cerebrospinal fluid, which appears black on ultrasound.

Question 10285. A constructed illustration: an infant's head is growing faster than expected, and an ultrasound scan through the soft spot on the skull shows that the lateral ventricles are wide and full of fluid. What does the scan show? The answer is a build-up of cerebrospinal fluid within the ventricles. The cause and the care belong to the treating clinician.

Question 10286. Why can an ultrasound beam pass through the soft spot at the top of an infant's skull and show the brain: because that spot is a membrane-filled gap between bones that have not yet joined, because the bone there is thinner, because the fluid of the brain is nearer the surface, or because the beam is bent by the bone? The answer is a membrane-filled gap between bones that have not yet joined.

Question 10287. A described ultrasound image of the eye shows a black rounded chamber behind the lens and in front of the back wall of the eye. Which structure fills that chamber? The answer is the vitreous body, a clear gel that fills the eye behind the lens. Being mostly water, it returns no echoes.

Question 10288. A described ultrasound image of the eye shows a small oval structure with a bright curved front and a dark middle, lying just behind the colored part of the eye. Which structure is it, and what lies behind it? The answer is the lens, and behind it lies the vitreous body. The front of the lens reflects sound.

Question 10289. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the eye, ultrasound shows a bright folded membrane lifted away from the back wall of the eye and floating in the black gel. Which structure is lifted? The answer is the retina. It has separated from the wall of the eye. The decisions belong to the treating clinician.

Question 10290. Which of these pairs belongs together: the renal sinus with the bright center of the kidney, the renal cortex with the bright center of the kidney, the renal capsule with the medullary pyramids, or the renal pelvis with the outer rim of the kidney? The answer is the renal sinus with the bright center, because fat around the collecting system reflects sound strongly.

Question 10291. A described ultrasound image shows a round black structure in the liver, and the tissue immediately behind it is brighter than the same tissue beside it. What does that brightening mean: the structure is a simple cyst filled with fluid, the structure is a solid tumor, the structure is a stone, or the structure is air in the bowel? The answer is a simple cyst filled with fluid, which transmits sound well.

Question 10292. Which of these pairs belongs together: a stone with a dark band behind it, a cyst with a dark band behind it, a stone with brightening behind it, or air with brightening behind it? The answer is a stone with a dark band behind it. A stone reflects sound and casts a shadow, while a cyst brightens the tissue behind it.

Question 10293. A described ultrasound image of the scrotum shows an oval organ with an even gray texture and a bright band at its back where the vessels enter. What is the bright band? The answer is the mediastinum of the testis, a fibrous thickening at the back of the organ. The sperm-forming tubules drain toward it.

Question 10294. A described ultrasound image of the scrotum shows a small elongated structure draped over the back and top of the testis, with a slightly different texture from the testis itself. Which structure is it? The answer is the epididymis. Its head sits on the top of the testis and its body and tail run down behind it.

Question 10295. A described ultrasound image of the scrotum shows a black collection surrounding the front of the testis and separating it from the wall of the scrotum, while the testis itself looks normal. What is the collection: fluid in the sac around the testis, a tumor of the testis, a twisted testis, or a hernia of the bowel? The answer is fluid in the sac around the testis. The testis is uninvolved.

Question 10296. A described ultrasound image taken through the wall of the rectum shows a chestnut-shaped gland lying immediately in front of the probe and below the bladder. Which gland is it? The answer is the prostate gland. It surrounds the first part of the urethra as it leaves the bladder, and its back lies against the rectum.

Question 10297. A described ultrasound image across the front of the abdomen above the navel shows a defect in the midline between the two halves of the rectus muscle, and through it a loop of bowel bulges forward under the skin. Which structure has failed? The answer is the linea alba, the fibrous seam between the flat muscles of the abdominal wall.

Question 10298. In a described ultrasound image of the groin, a small black loop of bowel lies above and medial to the pubic tubercle, and the swelling passes along a canal that follows the inguinal ligament. Which opening has it passed through? The answer is the superficial inguinal ring, at the end of the inguinal canal.

Question 10299. On a described ultrasound image of the breast, the skin is a thin bright line, and immediately deep to it lies a dark layer that surrounds the glandular tissue. Which layer is the dark one: the subcutaneous fat, the glandular tissue itself, the fascia over the chest wall, or a rib? The answer is the subcutaneous fat, which is darker than glandular tissue.

Question 10300. A described ultrasound image taken below the ribs shows the inferior vena cava entering a chamber at the top of the picture. Which chamber is it, and what lies between the vein and the chamber: the right atrium with the diaphragm, the left atrium with the aorta, the right ventricle with the stomach, or the left ventricle with the spleen? The answer is the right atrium, with the diaphragm between them.

To summarize, magnetic resonance imaging separates tissues by the signal they return: fat is bright on a T1-weighted image, fluid is bright on a T2-weighted one, and cortical bone and tendons stay dark on both. Its sections show the corpus callosum in the midline, the internal capsule beside the thalamus, the menisci as dark triangles in the knee, the medullary pyramids inside the renal cortex, the middle hepatic vein between the two halves of the liver, and the lower end of the spinal cord at the first or second lumbar vertebra. Ultrasound works differently, because fluid is black, fat is dark, and bone is bright at its surface with a shadow behind it, so the gallbladder, the bladder and the great veins show as black tubes and sacs while the diaphragm and the renal sinus shine. On its cross-sections the aorta lies in front of the spine with the inferior vena cava to its right, the thyroid wraps the trachea, the portal triad runs in the free edge of the lesser omentum, and the femoral nerve, artery and vein lie in a row from lateral to medial.
