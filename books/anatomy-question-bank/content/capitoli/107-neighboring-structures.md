# Chapter 107 — Neighboring Structures

This chapter explores the structures that lie next to one another throughout the body, from the front of the neck to the ankle, and asks which neighbor is which. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately and then explains why it is right. Neighboring relations are what a listener needs at the bedside, where a structure is found by the landmarks around it. This is educational material and supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10601. Which structure lies immediately posterior to the isthmus of the thyroid gland in the front of the neck? The answer is the trachea. The isthmus crosses the front of the airway, usually over the second, third, and fourth tracheal rings. The two lobes wrap around the sides of the larynx and the trachea.

Question 10602. Which of these structures lies within the carotid sheath in the neck: the phrenic nerve, the internal jugular vein, the sympathetic trunk, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the internal jugular vein. The sheath also holds the common carotid artery and the vagus nerve. The phrenic nerve lies on the front of the scalenus anterior, and the sympathetic trunk lies behind the sheath.

Question 10603. Within the carotid sheath, where does the vagus nerve lie in relation to the common carotid artery and the internal jugular vein? The answer is behind both and between them. The vein lies lateral to the artery, and the nerve runs in the groove behind them. That is why the nerve is reached last when the sheath is opened.

Question 10604. A described transverse section of the neck passes through the middle of the thyroid gland, with a ring of gland around the airway. Which structure lies immediately posterolateral to each lobe? The answer is the carotid sheath. It holds the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, and the vagus nerve. The recurrent laryngeal nerve lies nearer the midline.

Question 10605. A constructed illustration: a surgeon frees the lower pole of the thyroid gland, near the inferior thyroid artery, and the voice becomes hoarse afterwards. Which nerve lies at risk there? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It ascends in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus, behind the gland. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10606. True or false: the parathyroid glands usually lie against the posterior surface of the thyroid gland. The answer is true. There are usually four of them, two on each side, tucked against the back of the lobes. The upper pair sits near the middle of the lobe and the lower pair near its lower pole.

Question 10607. Which salivary gland curves around the posterior border of the mylohyoid muscle, so that part of it lies above that muscle in the floor of the mouth? The answer is the submandibular gland. Its superficial part lies below the muscle and its deep part above it. The sublingual gland lies entirely above the muscle.

Question 10608. Which of these structures passes through the parotid gland: the facial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the lingual nerve, or the maxillary artery? The answer is the facial nerve. Its trunk divides inside the gland into branches that fan out across the face. The maxillary artery lies deep to the gland, and the hypoglossal and lingual nerves lie lower.

Question 10609. After leaving the front of the parotid gland, which muscle does the parotid duct cross before it pierces the cheek? The answer is the masseter. The duct runs forward across the surface of that muscle, turns inward at its front border, and pierces the buccinator opposite the upper second molar tooth. The facial nerve crosses the same muscle, but higher up.

Question 10610. Name the two muscles that form the floor of the submandibular triangle. The answer is the mylohyoid and the hyoglossus. The mylohyoid forms the muscular floor of the mouth, and the hyoglossus lies deep to it. The submandibular gland rests on both, and the hypoglossal and lingual nerves pass between them.

Question 10611. A described specimen of the side of the neck shows a vein running obliquely across a broad strap muscle spanning from the mastoid region to the sternum and the clavicle. Which vein is shown? The answer is the external jugular vein. It lies superficial to the sternocleidomastoid muscle and drains into the subclavian vein.

Question 10612. Which of these cranial nerves runs in the lateral wall of the cavernous sinus: the sixth, the fourth, the optic, or the twelfth? The answer is the fourth cranial nerve. The third and the first and second divisions of the fifth lie in the same wall. The sixth nerve passes through the cavity of the sinus itself.

Question 10613. Within the cavernous sinus, which cranial nerve lies immediately lateral to the internal carotid artery as the artery runs forward? The answer is the sixth cranial nerve. It crosses the cavity of the sinus rather than lying in its wall, and it is the only cranial nerve that does so. That relation explains why it is often affected early by disease there.

Question 10614. A constructed illustration: a patient has a drooping upper eyelid, a fixed and dilated pupil, and double vision, with the eye turned outward. Which nerve is affected? The answer is the third cranial nerve. It supplies the muscle that lifts the upper lid and most of the muscles that move the eye. The sixth nerve could weaken one muscle but could not drop the lid.

Question 10615. Which of these passes through the superior orbital fissure: the optic nerve, the ophthalmic division of the fifth cranial nerve, the ophthalmic artery, or the maxillary nerve? The answer is the ophthalmic division of the fifth cranial nerve. The third, fourth, and sixth cranial nerves pass through the same fissure. The optic nerve and the ophthalmic artery use the optic canal instead.

Question 10616. Which cavity of the skull lies immediately above the roof of the orbit, separated from it by a thin plate of bone? The answer is the anterior cranial fossa. The roof of the orbit is formed by the orbital plate of the frontal bone, and the frontal lobe rests on its upper surface. The floor of the orbit lies above the maxillary sinus.

Question 10617. True or false: the medial wall of the orbit is thinner than its roof and its floor. The answer is true. The medial wall is formed largely by the paper-thin orbital plate of the ethmoid bone. The roof is the orbital plate of the frontal bone, and the floor is the orbital surface of the maxilla, and both are stouter.

Question 10618. A described coronal section through the orbits shows the eyeballs on each side of the nasal cavity, with a thin plate of bone between the orbit and the ethmoid air cells. Which wall of the orbit is being cut? The answer is the medial wall. It separates the orbit from the ethmoid air cells and the nasal cavity.

Question 10619. Into which of the nasal meatuses does the nasolacrimal duct open? The answer is the inferior meatus. The duct drains tears from the lacrimal sac down the side of the nose and opens beneath the inferior nasal concha. The maxillary sinus opens into the middle meatus instead.

Question 10620. Which pair belongs together: the maxillary sinus with the inferior meatus, the frontal sinus with the middle meatus, the posterior ethmoid cells with the inferior meatus, or the sphenoid sinus with the middle meatus? The answer is the frontal sinus with the middle meatus. The maxillary sinus opens into the middle meatus too. The posterior ethmoid cells open into the superior meatus and the sphenoid sinus above it.

Question 10621. The floor of the maxillary sinus lies immediately above which structures: the roots of the upper molar teeth, the hard palate, the zygomatic arch, or the temporalis muscle? The answer is the roots of the upper molar teeth. The floor is formed by the alveolar process, and its thin bone may be pierced by the roots. A dental infection can spread into the sinus.

Question 10622. A constructed illustration: a person has bleeding from one nostril after a blow to the front of the nose. Which region of the nasal cavity, rich in anastomosing vessels, is the usual source? The answer is the anterior part of the nasal septum. Several arteries meet there in a plexus on the mucosa. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10623. A transverse section is taken through the middle of the orbit, cutting the eyeball and the optic nerve. Does the lacrimal gland lie medial or lateral to the eyeball at that level? The answer is lateral. The gland sits in a fossa in the frontal bone at the upper outer corner of the orbit. The medial side of the section holds the ethmoid air cells.

Question 10624. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the zygomatic arch: the temporalis, the masseter, the medial pterygoid, or the lateral pterygoid? The answer is the temporalis. It fills the temporal fossa, and its tendon passes deep to the arch to reach the coronoid process of the mandible. The masseter lies superficial to the arch and to the ramus of the mandible.

Question 10625. A described specimen of the infratemporal fossa shows a fan-shaped muscle filling the temporal fossa, a muscle running downward and backward to the angle of the mandible, and a nerve lying between them. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the mandibular division of the fifth cranial nerve. It enters through the foramen ovale and supplies the muscles of mastication.

Question 10626. Through which opening does the mental nerve leave the mandible? The answer is the mental foramen. It lies on the outer surface of the body of the mandible, below the second premolar tooth. The nerve is a branch of the inferior alveolar nerve and supplies the skin of the chin and the lower lip.

Question 10627. Which of these structures lies in the submental triangle: the submandibular gland, the submental lymph nodes, the hypoglossal nerve, or the external carotid artery? The answer is the submental lymph nodes. The triangle is bounded by the two anterior bellies of the digastric muscle and the hyoid bone, with the mylohyoid as its floor. The submandibular gland lies in the triangle beside it.

Question 10628. In the posterior triangle of the neck, on which muscle does the accessory nerve run before it disappears beneath the trapezius? The answer is the levator scapulae. The nerve crosses the floor of the triangle just above the middle of that muscle. It is superficial there, which is why it is easily injured.

Question 10629. Between which two muscles does the brachial plexus emerge in the neck: the anterior and middle scalene, the middle and posterior scalene, the sternocleidomastoid and the anterior scalene, or the trapezius and the levator scapulae? The answer is the anterior and middle scalene. The subclavian artery also passes between them. The posterior scalene lies behind the middle scalene.

Question 10630. Which nerve lies on the anterior surface of the scalenus anterior muscle as it descends through the neck? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It runs almost vertically down the front of that muscle and then passes in front of the subclavian artery into the thorax. It is the motor nerve of the diaphragm.

Question 10631. A described radiograph of the cervical spine shows a vertical canal running through the side of each vertebra from the sixth upward. Which vessel passes through that canal? The answer is the vertebral artery. It enters the canal at the sixth cervical vertebra and ascends to the skull. Its first part lies between the anterior scalene muscle and the longus colli.

Question 10632. Which of these statements about the cervical sympathetic trunk is correct: it lies within the carotid sheath, it lies immediately behind the carotid sheath, it lies within the pretracheal fascia, or it lies in front of the trachea? The answer is that it lies behind the carotid sheath. It runs on the prevertebral muscles, separated from the sheath by the prevertebral fascia. The vagus nerve lies inside the sheath.

Question 10633. True or false: in the thorax the thoracic duct lies on the right of the midline for the whole of its course. The answer is false. It ascends first on the right and then crosses to the left at about the level of the fourth thoracic vertebra. It continues upward behind the esophagus and the arch of the aorta.

Question 10634. The recurrent laryngeal nerve on the right side loops around which structure: the arch of the aorta, the right subclavian artery, the right common carotid artery, or the first rib? The answer is the right subclavian artery. On the left, the nerve loops under the aortic arch beside the ligamentum arteriosum. Both then ascend between the trachea and the esophagus.

Question 10635. In the thorax, how do the phrenic nerve and the vagus nerve lie in relation to the root of the lung? The answer is that the phrenic nerve lies in front of the root and the vagus nerve behind it. Both descend through the mediastinum on opposite sides of the root. The phrenic nerve continues to the diaphragm.

Question 10636. At which bony landmark does the trachea divide into its two main bronchi? The answer is the sternal angle. Behind it the trachea bifurcates, at the lower border of the fourth thoracic vertebra. The arch of the aorta also begins and ends at that level, and the second costal cartilage joins the sternum there.

Question 10637. Which structure lies immediately behind the left atrium of the heart: the esophagus, the trachea, the thymus, or the sternum? The answer is the esophagus. The left atrium forms much of the posterior surface of the heart, and the esophagus runs behind it in the posterior mediastinum. The thymus lies in the anterior mediastinum.

Question 10638. A described lateral radiograph of the chest shows a narrow vertical channel passing down behind the heart shadow and in front of the vertebral bodies. Which structure is shown? The answer is the esophagus. It descends first behind the trachea and then behind the left atrium. The descending aorta lies behind it and slightly to the left.

Question 10639. A constructed illustration: a person has difficulty swallowing, and imaging shows a very large left atrium. Given its position, which structure is most likely compressed? The answer is the esophagus. The left atrium forms the posterior surface of the heart, and the esophagus lies directly behind it. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10640. Which statement about the two main bronchi is correct: the right is narrower and longer, the right is wider and more vertical, the two are equal in width, or the left is wider? The answer is that the right main bronchus is wider and more vertical. That is why an inhaled foreign body more often enters the right side. The left main bronchus runs more horizontally.

Question 10641. Which vein arches over the root of the right lung before it joins the superior vena cava? The answer is the azygos vein. It drains the posterior wall of the abdomen and the thorax and ascends on the right of the vertebral column. On the left the corresponding vein is the hemiazygos, which does not arch over the lung root.

Question 10642. In the costal groove, which of these lists the intercostal structures in the correct order from above downward: artery, vein, nerve; vein, artery, nerve; nerve, vein, artery; or vein, nerve, artery? The answer is vein, then artery, then nerve. They run between the internal and the innermost intercostal muscles, sheltered by the lower border of the rib. The nerve lies lowest and is the most exposed.

Question 10643. The aortic hiatus of the diaphragm lies at the level of which vertebra, and which two structures pass through it? The answer is the twelfth thoracic vertebra, with the aorta and the thoracic duct. The esophageal hiatus lies at the tenth thoracic vertebra and the opening for the inferior vena cava at the eighth. Each opening carries its own contents.

Question 10644. A described computed tomography section of the thorax at the level of the aortic arch shows a curved vessel passing backward on the left of the trachea, and a smaller vessel crossing in front of the vertebral column on the right. Which two structures are shown? The answer is the aortic arch and the azygos vein. The arch passes over the left main bronchus.

Question 10645. Which structure occupies the anterior mediastinum in a child, lying in front of the aortic arch and the brachiocephalic veins: the thymus, the trachea, the esophagus, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the thymus. It is largest in childhood and shrinks with age, but remains as fatty tissue in the adult. The thoracic duct lies in the posterior mediastinum.

Question 10646. Under which structure does the left recurrent laryngeal nerve loop before it ascends to the larynx? The answer is the arch of the aorta. The nerve hooks beneath the arch, just behind the ligamentum arteriosum, and then climbs between the trachea and the esophagus. On the right it loops under the right subclavian artery.

Question 10647. Which structure lies immediately behind the root of the left lung: the descending aorta, the superior vena cava, the right atrium, or the thymus? The answer is the descending aorta. It runs down the posterior mediastinum and forms a groove on the medial surface of the left lung. The superior vena cava lies on the right side.

Question 10648. A constructed illustration: a person has a fracture of a lower rib, and the fragment turns inward into the costal groove. Which structure in the groove is most exposed to injury? The answer is the intercostal nerve, which lies lowest. The intercostal artery lies above it and the intercostal vein above that. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10649. Which pair belongs together: the aortic hiatus with the twelfth thoracic vertebra, the esophageal hiatus with the twelfth, the caval opening with the tenth, or the esophageal hiatus with the eighth? The answer is the aortic hiatus with the twelfth thoracic vertebra. The esophageal hiatus lies at the tenth and the caval opening at the eighth.

Question 10650. A described cross-section of the thorax at the level where the trachea has just divided shows two bronchi, two large vessels, and a muscular tube lying behind the left bronchus. Which structure is that tube? The answer is the esophagus. It lies behind the left main bronchus and in front of the descending aorta.

Question 10651. Which structure lies immediately medial to the second part of the duodenum: the head of the pancreas, the right kidney, the gallbladder, or the transverse colon? The answer is the head of the pancreas. The second part descends in front of the right kidney, and the head of the pancreas lies in its curve. The gallbladder lies above the first part.

Question 10652. Against which ribs does the spleen lie in the left upper abdomen? The answer is the ninth, tenth, and eleventh ribs. The spleen sits in the left hypochondrium, behind the stomach and above the left kidney. Its long axis follows the line of the tenth rib, and the diaphragm separates it from the left pleural cavity.

Question 10653. Which two vessels lie immediately behind the neck of the pancreas: the superior mesenteric artery and vein, the aorta and the inferior vena cava, the splenic vessels, or the left renal vessels? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery and vein. The neck passes in front of them and the uncinate process hooks behind. The splenic vessels run along the upper border of the body.

Question 10654. Which large vein crosses in front of the abdominal aorta, between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery, on its way to the inferior vena cava? The answer is the left renal vein. It is longer than the right renal vein, which runs a short course to the inferior vena cava. The left gonadal and suprarenal veins drain into it.

Question 10655. A described computed tomography section of the upper abdomen shows a long gland across the midline in front of two large vessels. Which gland is shown, and which vessels lie behind its neck? The answer is the pancreas, with the superior mesenteric artery and vein behind its neck. The body lies in front of the aorta, and the splenic vein runs along its back.

Question 10656. Where is the portal vein formed: behind the neck of the pancreas, in front of the liver, at the hilum of the spleen, or within the lesser omentum? The answer is behind the neck of the pancreas. There the superior mesenteric vein joins the splenic vein. It then ascends in the free edge of the lesser omentum.

Question 10657. Why does the right kidney lie lower than the left kidney in the normal body? The answer is the size of the liver. The right lobe of the liver occupies the space above the right kidney and presses it downward. The left kidney lies higher, with the spleen and the stomach above it, and the right kidney lies against the duodenum.

Question 10658. Which structure crosses in front of the third part of the duodenum: the superior mesenteric artery, the inferior mesenteric artery, the common bile duct, or the portal vein? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery. The third part runs across the aorta and is crossed by the artery and its vein. The common bile duct lies behind the first part.

Question 10659. Name two large vessels that lie immediately posterior to the body of the pancreas. The answer is the aorta and the splenic vein. The body of the pancreas lies in front of the aorta and the superior mesenteric vessels, and the splenic vein runs in a groove on its posterior surface. The inferior vena cava lies further to the right.

Question 10660. A described radiograph of the abdomen shows the outlines of two bean-shaped organs on either side of the spine, the right one lower than the left. Which organs are shown, and what caps each above? The answer is the kidneys, each capped by an adrenal gland. The right kidney lies lower because of the liver above it.

Question 10661. Which statement about the appendix is correct: its base lies one third of the way from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus, it arises from the sigmoid colon, it lies within the lesser sac, or it always hangs over the pelvic brim? The answer is that its base lies there. The appendix arises from the cecum, and its position varies.

Question 10662. Where does the fundus of the gallbladder lie in relation to the anterior abdominal wall? The answer is at the tip of the ninth costal cartilage. The fundus projects below the inferior border of the liver and touches the anterior abdominal wall. The body rests against the duodenum and the transverse colon.

Question 10663. The ureter crosses the pelvic brim immediately in front of which structure: the bifurcation of the common iliac artery, the sacroiliac joint, the fifth lumbar vertebra, or the rectum? The answer is the bifurcation of the common iliac artery. The ureter then runs down the lateral wall of the pelvis in front of the internal iliac artery.

Question 10664. In the female pelvis, how does the ureter lie in relation to the uterine artery and the cervix? The answer is that it passes behind the uterine artery and lateral to the cervix. The artery crosses above it, which is why they are said to cross like a bridge over water. The ureter then enters the bladder.

Question 10665. A constructed illustration: during an operation in the female pelvis, a structure running behind the uterine artery and beside the cervix is divided, and urine later leaks from the vagina. Which structure was injured? The answer is the ureter. It passes close to the cervix before it enters the bladder. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10666. A described sagittal section of the male pelvis shows the bladder in front, a muscular tube behind it, and a gland below the bladder. Which structure lies directly between the bladder and the rectum? The answer is the seminal vesicle with the ampulla of the ductus deferens. The prostate lies below the bladder and is pierced by the urethra. The rectum lies behind them.

Question 10667. Which structure lies immediately in front of the rectum in the male: the prostate and the seminal vesicles, the sacrum, the coccyx, or the pubic symphysis? The answer is the prostate and the seminal vesicles. The prostate lies below the bladder and behind the pubic symphysis. The bladder lies above both.

Question 10668. What is the name of the peritoneal pouch that lies behind the cervix and in front of the rectum in the female? The answer is the rectouterine pouch. It is the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity when a person is standing. The corresponding pouch in the male lies between the bladder and the rectum.

Question 10669. Which statement about the inferior vena cava is correct: it lies to the left of the aorta, it lies to the right of the aorta behind the liver, it passes through the aortic hiatus, or it lies in front of the liver? The answer is that it lies to the right of the aorta and behind the liver. In the bare area it touches the liver directly.

Question 10670. Which pair belongs together: the left kidney with the spleen above it, the right kidney with the stomach above it, the left kidney with the liver above it, or the right kidney with the spleen above it? The answer is the left kidney with the spleen above it. The right kidney has the liver above it and the stomach in front.

Question 10671. A described magnetic resonance image of the female pelvis shows a pear-shaped organ in the midline, with a fold of peritoneum over it and a tube curving laterally. Which organ is shown, and what lies behind its lower part? The answer is the uterus, with the rectum behind the cervix. The bladder lies in front, and the ureters pass close to the cervix.

Question 10672. A lumbar puncture is performed at the third and fourth lumbar interspace. At which level does the spinal cord normally end: the lower border of the first lumbar vertebra, the fifth lumbar vertebra, the twelfth thoracic vertebra, or the second sacral vertebra? The answer is the lower border of the first lumbar vertebra. Below it lies the cauda equina. The dural sac ends lower still.

Question 10673. A constructed illustration: the gallbladder of a person becomes inflamed, and the inflammation spreads to the parts of the bowel that touch it. Which two parts would most likely be involved? The answer is the first part of the duodenum and the transverse colon. Its body rests against both. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10674. Which four organs form the bed on which the stomach rests when a person lies on the back? The answer is the spleen, the pancreas, the left kidney, and the left adrenal gland. The transverse colon and its mesocolon also lie against the back of the stomach. All of these lie behind the stomach in the left upper abdomen.

Question 10675. Which structures lie immediately posterior to the ascending colon: the right kidney and the duodenum, the spleen and the pancreas, the left kidney and the left ureter, or the stomach and the liver? The answer is the right kidney and the duodenum. The ascending colon lies on the posterior abdominal wall. On the left, the descending colon lies against the left kidney.

Question 10676. Where does the sciatic nerve lie in the gluteal region in relation to the ischial tuberosity and the greater trochanter? The answer is about midway between the two, deep to the gluteus maximus. It emerges from the pelvis below the piriformis muscle. It is the largest nerve in the body and passes down the back of the thigh.

Question 10677. A described transverse section through the gluteal region shows the sciatic nerve lying on the posterior surface of a muscle that fills the buttock, with a smaller muscle above it. Which two muscles are these? The answer is the gluteus maximus over the nerve and the piriformis above it. The gluteus medius lies higher and more lateral.

Question 10678. Which structures leave the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen above the piriformis muscle: the superior gluteal nerve and vessels, the sciatic nerve, the pudendal nerve, or the inferior gluteal nerve? The answer is the superior gluteal nerve and vessels. The sciatic, inferior gluteal, and pudendal nerves all leave below the piriformis.

Question 10679. Which nerve hooks around the ischial spine and the sacrospinous ligament before it re-enters the pelvis? The answer is the pudendal nerve. It leaves the pelvis below the piriformis, winds around the spine, and passes through the lesser sciatic foramen into the pudendal canal. From there it supplies the perineum.

Question 10680. True or false: in the femoral triangle the femoral nerve lies lateral to the femoral artery. The answer is true. The nerve lies outside the femoral sheath, while the artery and the vein lie inside it. From lateral to medial the order is nerve, artery, vein, and then the femoral canal.

Question 10681. Within the femoral sheath, which of these describes the arrangement from lateral to medial: vein, artery, canal; artery, vein, canal; canal, vein, artery; or artery, canal, vein? The answer is artery, then vein, then canal. The femoral canal occupies the most medial compartment and contains lymph nodes. The femoral nerve lies outside the sheath.

Question 10682. Which pair belongs together: the axillary nerve with the quadrangular space, the radial nerve with the carpal tunnel, the median nerve with the spiral groove, or the ulnar nerve with the cubital fossa? The answer is the axillary nerve with the quadrangular space. The radial nerve runs in the spiral groove and the median nerve through the carpal tunnel.

Question 10683. Which vein lies immediately in front of the medial malleolus at the ankle? The answer is the great saphenous vein. It ascends in front of the medial malleolus and then along the medial side of the leg and the thigh. It ends by joining the femoral vein below the inguinal ligament.

Question 10684. Behind the medial malleolus, which structure lies immediately behind the tendon of the tibialis posterior: the flexor digitorum longus, the Achilles tendon, the sural nerve, or the small saphenous vein? The answer is the flexor digitorum longus. From front to back the order is tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, the posterior tibial artery, the tibial nerve, and the flexor hallucis longus.

Question 10685. A described coronal magnetic resonance image of the ankle shows a rounded bony prominence on the inner side, with several tendons and a nerve passing behind it in a tunnel. Which nerve lies within that tunnel? The answer is the tibial nerve. It runs behind the medial malleolus with the posterior tibial artery. The tunnel is roofed by the flexor retinaculum.

Question 10686. A constructed illustration: a person fractures the neck of the fibula and afterwards cannot lift the foot, so the toes drag when walking. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the common fibular nerve. It winds around the neck of the fibula, where it is superficial and easily damaged. The tibial nerve lies behind the medial malleolus.

Question 10687. In the popliteal fossa, which structure lies most deeply, against the capsule of the knee joint: the popliteal artery, the popliteal vein, the tibial nerve, or the common fibular nerve? The answer is the popliteal artery. The popliteal vein lies superficial to it and the tibial nerve more superficial still. The common fibular nerve runs along the lateral wall.

Question 10688. In the cubital fossa, on which side of the brachial artery does the median nerve lie? The answer is the medial side. Within the fossa the structures lie in a line from lateral to medial: the radial nerve, the tendon of the biceps, the brachial artery, and the median nerve. The artery ends by dividing into the radial and ulnar arteries.

Question 10689. In the wrist, how does the median nerve lie in relation to the flexor retinaculum and to the tendons that share the carpal tunnel with it? The answer is that the nerve lies deep to the retinaculum, within the tunnel, and superficial to the tendons. It is the most superficial structure in the carpal tunnel. The ulnar nerve passes outside the tunnel.

Question 10690. Which nerve passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum at the wrist, alongside the ulnar artery: the ulnar nerve, the median nerve, the radial nerve, or the anterior interosseous nerve? The answer is the ulnar nerve. It passes through a small canal on the medial side of the wrist, outside the carpal tunnel. The median nerve passes deep to the retinaculum.

Question 10691. A described transverse section through the wrist shows a fibrous band over a row of tendons, with a single nerve just beneath the band and a small nerve and artery outside it. Which nerve lies beneath the band? The answer is the median nerve. The ulnar nerve and artery lie outside, on the little finger side.

Question 10692. True or false: the axillary vein lies in front of and medial to the axillary artery. The answer is true. The vein lies closest to the front of the axilla and partly overlaps the artery. The cords of the brachial plexus lie lateral, medial, and posterior to the artery.

Question 10693. Which cord of the brachial plexus lies immediately behind the axillary artery? The answer is the posterior cord. It gives rise to the axillary and radial nerves. The lateral and medial cords lie in front of the artery, one on each side of it.

Question 10694. Beneath which ligament does the suprascapular nerve pass as it enters the back of the shoulder region? The answer is the transverse scapular ligament. The nerve runs through the suprascapular foramen, while the suprascapular artery passes above the ligament. The nerve supplies the supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles.

Question 10695. A constructed illustration: a person fractures the middle of the humerus and afterwards cannot extend the wrist or the fingers. Which nerve has been injured, and where does it run? The answer is the radial nerve, in the spiral groove on the back of the humerus. It lies between the medial and lateral heads of the triceps. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10696. Which of these pairs belongs together: the thyrohyoid membrane with the superior laryngeal nerve, the cricothyroid membrane with the recurrent laryngeal nerve, the trapezius with the median nerve, or the diaphragm with the ulnar nerve? The answer is the thyrohyoid membrane with the superior laryngeal nerve. Its internal branch pierces the membrane to supply the larynx above the vocal folds.

Question 10697. A sagittal section is taken through the chest just to the left of the midline. Which chamber of the heart lies directly behind the sternum in that section? The answer is the right ventricle. It is the most anterior chamber and lies behind the sternum and the left costal cartilages. The left ventricle lies behind it and to the left.

Question 10698. Name two structures that lie in the popliteal fossa behind the knee. The answer is the popliteal artery and the tibial nerve. The popliteal vein and the common fibular nerve also pass through the fossa. The artery lies deepest, against the joint capsule, and the tibial nerve is the most superficial.

Question 10699. A constructed illustration: a person dislocates the shoulder, and afterwards the shoulder is flattened and a patch of skin over the deltoid is numb. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the axillary nerve. It winds through the quadrangular space close to the surgical neck of the humerus. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 10700. Which nerve lies in the groove behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus, beside the superior ulnar collateral artery? The answer is the ulnar nerve. It is the nerve that produces tingling in the little finger when the elbow is struck. It then passes between the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris into the forearm.

To summarize, a structure is remembered by the neighbors it keeps: the thyroid isthmus lies in front of the trachea, the carotid sheath holds the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, and the vagus nerve, and the recurrent laryngeal nerve climbs behind the gland between the trachea and the esophagus. The parotid duct crosses the masseter, the submandibular gland hooks around the mylohyoid, and the fourth cranial nerve runs in the lateral wall of the cavernous sinus while the sixth passes through its cavity. The phrenic nerve lies in front of the root of the lung and the vagus behind it, and the intercostal vein, artery, and nerve lie in that order from above downward. The pancreas lies in front of the aorta and the superior mesenteric vessels, the left renal vein crosses in front of the aorta, and the spleen, the pancreas, the left kidney, and the left adrenal gland form the bed of the stomach. The ureter passes behind the uterine artery, the sciatic nerve leaves the pelvis below the piriformis, the femoral nerve lies lateral to the femoral artery, and the median nerve lies deep to the flexor retinaculum while the ulnar nerve passes outside it.
