# Chapter 93 — Upper Limb, Part Three

The upper limb hangs from the trunk at the shoulder girdle and is built for reach and for grip, and this chapter walks through its regions in the order a dissection follows them. It begins at the clavicle and the scapula, crosses the axilla with its artery, its vein and the cords of the brachial plexus, then follows the arm, the cubital fossa, the forearm, the wrist and the hand, and it ends with the veins, the lymph nodes and the great nerve trunks of the limb. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The relations set out here are the ones a clinician uses when a limb is examined, imaged or injured. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9201. Which bone forms the only bony link between the trunk and the upper limb? The answer is the clavicle. Its medial end joins the manubrium of the sternum and its lateral end the acromion, and every other connection between the limb and the trunk is made by muscle.

Question 9202. On the inferior surface of the clavicle, which prominence gives attachment to the conoid part of the coracoclavicular ligament: the conoid tubercle, the deltoid tubercle, the subclavian groove, or the trapezoid line? The answer is the conoid tubercle, near the lateral end. The trapezoid line takes the trapezoid part of the same ligament, and the subclavian groove lies more medially.

Question 9203. Which shallow depression on the scapula faces the ribs and gives origin to the muscle that rotates the arm inward? The answer is the subscapular fossa. It covers most of the costal surface, and the subscapularis fills it and reaches the lesser tubercle of the humerus.

Question 9204. Which two structures lie immediately anterior to the subscapularis in the back wall of the armpit? The answer is the axillary artery and the cords of the brachial plexus. The subscapularis forms the greater part of that wall, and the vessels and the plexus lie in front of it in the axillary sheath.

Question 9205. A described radiograph of the shoulder shows a broad flat process above the head of the humerus and a smaller hook-shaped process below and in front of it. Which scapular processes are shown? The answer is the acromion above and the coracoid process below and in front. The acromion is the flattened end of the spine of the scapula.

Question 9206. A constructed illustration: a person falls on the shoulder and cannot lift the arm sideways, and the skin over the outer part of the shoulder is numb. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the axillary nerve, which winds around the surgical neck close to the bone and supplies the deltoid with the skin over it.

Question 9207. Which structures pass through the quadrangular space at the back of the shoulder: the circumflex scapular artery, the axillary nerve with the posterior circumflex humeral artery, the radial nerve with the deep artery of the arm, or the suprascapular nerve? The answer is the axillary nerve with the posterior circumflex humeral artery. The circumflex scapular artery passes through the triangular space instead.

Question 9208. Which ridge crosses the back of the scapula and ends in a broad flat process that forms the point of the shoulder? The answer is the spine of the scapula, which ends as the acromion. The spine separates the supraspinous fossa above from the infraspinous fossa below.

Question 9209. Which tendon runs in the groove between the greater and the lesser tubercles of the humerus, and which band holds it there? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps brachii, held by the transverse humeral ligament. That groove is also called the bicipital groove.

Question 9210. Which structure forms the lateral border of the quadrangular space at the back of the shoulder: the long head of the triceps, the surgical neck of the humerus, the teres major, or the medial border of the scapula? The answer is the surgical neck of the humerus. The teres minor bounds the space above, the teres major below and the triceps medially.

Question 9211. A described radiograph of the upper humerus shows a rounded head on a narrow neck, with a larger prominence on its outer side and a smaller one in front. Which two prominences are shown? The answer is the greater tubercle and the lesser tubercle. The greater receives the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus and the teres minor, and the lesser receives the subscapularis.

Question 9212. Which of these muscles does not form part of the rotator cuff: the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres major, or the subscapularis? The answer is the teres major. The cuff is the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor and the subscapularis, and the teres major is an adductor that lies below it.

Question 9213. Which three muscles attach to the coracoid process of the scapula? The answer is the short head of the biceps brachii, the coracobrachialis and the pectoralis minor. The pectoralis minor reaches it from the chest, and the other two pass from it into the arm.

Question 9214. The body of the scapula rests on the ribs at an angle rather than lying flat against the back of the chest. Why does that angle matter for the shoulder? The answer is that the glenoid cavity faces laterally, forward and slightly upward, so the joint works in an oblique plane. Abduction therefore needs the scapula to rotate upward as well.

Question 9215. True or false: the first few degrees of abduction at the shoulder come from the supraspinatus and the rest from the deltoid. The answer is true. The supraspinatus begins the movement and the deltoid continues it, and paralysis of either weakens the whole movement.

Question 9216. Which two muscles of the shoulder are supplied by the suprascapular nerve: the deltoid and the teres minor, the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus, the subscapularis and the teres major, or the rhomboid major and the levator scapulae? The answer is the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus. The nerve reaches them through the suprascapular notch and then the spinoglenoid notch.

Question 9217. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the supraspinatus with the lesser tubercle, the subscapularis with the greater tubercle, the teres major with the medial lip of the intertubercular groove, or the pectoralis major with the spine of the scapula? The answer is the teres major with the medial lip of the intertubercular groove. The supraspinatus and the subscapularis are the wrong way round.

Question 9218. Which nerve runs down the medial wall of the axilla on the surface of the serratus anterior, and what happens when it is divided? The answer is the long thoracic nerve, and the medial border of the scapula then stands away from the chest wall. The bone cannot be held against the ribs, so the arm cannot be raised above the shoulder.

Question 9219. Which muscle arises by fleshy digitations from the outer surfaces of the upper eight or nine ribs and passes backward to the medial border of the scapula? The answer is the serratus anterior. It pulls the scapula forward around the chest wall, and its lower digitations interlock with those of the external oblique muscle.

Question 9220. Which two muscles rotate the scapula upward when the arm is raised above the head: the rhomboid major and the levator scapulae, the trapezius and the serratus anterior, the latissimus dorsi and the pectoralis minor, or the subscapularis and the teres major? The answer is the trapezius and the serratus anterior. The rhomboid major and the levator scapulae rotate it downward.

Question 9221. The axillary artery is described in three parts by its relation to which muscle: the pectoralis major, the pectoralis minor, the subscapularis, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the pectoralis minor. The first part lies above the muscle, the second behind it and the third below it, and the cords of the brachial plexus are named from their position around the second part.

Question 9222. A described arteriogram of the axilla shows a large vessel passing behind a muscle that runs from the third to the fifth rib to the coracoid process. Which muscle and which part of the artery are shown? The answer is the pectoralis minor, with the second part of the axillary artery behind it. That part gives the thoracoacromial and the lateral thoracic arteries.

Question 9223. Which artery arises from the second part of the axillary artery and divides into pectoral, acromial, clavicular and deltoid branches? The answer is the thoracoacromial artery. The lateral thoracic artery also arises from the second part and runs down the side of the chest.

Question 9224. Which two branches arise from the subscapular artery: the thoracoacromial and the lateral thoracic, the circumflex scapular and the thoracodorsal, the anterior and posterior circumflex humeral arteries, or the suprascapular and the dorsal scapular? The answer is the circumflex scapular and the thoracodorsal. The first passes through the triangular space, and the second runs down to the latissimus dorsi.

Question 9225. A constructed illustration shows the head of the humerus driven forward and downward out of its socket after a fall, with the axillary nerve at risk. Why does the nerve lie in danger there? The answer is that it runs close to the front and lower part of the joint capsule before it winds backward. The axillary artery lies beside it but is injured far less often. Treatment belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9226. Which artery leaves the axilla through the triangular space and takes part in the anastomosis around the scapula? The answer is the circumflex scapular artery. It arises from the subscapular artery and curves around the lateral border of the scapula to join the suprascapular and dorsal scapular arteries.

Question 9227. Which structures bound the triangular space of the axilla? The answer is the teres minor above, the teres major below and the long head of the triceps laterally. The circumflex scapular artery passes through it, and the quadrangular space lies just lateral to it.

Question 9228. Which statement about the axillary vein is correct: it lies lateral to the axillary artery, it lies medial and in front of the artery, it lies behind the artery, or it lies between the cords of the plexus? The answer is that it lies medial and in front of the artery. It ends at the outer border of the first rib as the subclavian vein.

Question 9229. A described cross section through the middle of the arm shows the humerus, a large vessel lying on its medial side and a nerve crossing in front of that vessel. Which two structures form that neurovascular bundle? The answer is the brachial artery with the median nerve. The nerve crosses the artery from lateral to medial as it descends.

Question 9230. Which nerve lies against the back of the humerus in the spiral groove, between the lateral and the medial heads of the triceps? The answer is the radial nerve. It leaves the axilla behind the axillary artery and spirals down the bone, reaching the front of the arm above the elbow.

Question 9231. A described fracture of the middle of the shaft of the humerus injures a nerve, and the wrist cannot be straightened while the elbow still bends. Which nerve is injured: the median nerve, the ulnar nerve, the radial nerve, or the axillary nerve? The answer is the radial nerve, whose branches to the triceps leave it before the spiral groove, so elbow extension survives.

Question 9232. A constructed illustration: a deep cut across the front of the arm divides the nerve that pierces the coracobrachialis. The person can still bend the elbow weakly with the forearm held midway. Which nerve is divided, and why is some flexion left? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve, and the brachioradialis, supplied by the radial nerve, still bends the elbow.

Question 9233. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the musculocutaneous nerve with the triceps, the axillary nerve with the deltoid, the radial nerve with the coracobrachialis, or the median nerve with the flexor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the axillary nerve with the deltoid. The triceps belongs to the radial nerve and the coracobrachialis to the musculocutaneous nerve.

Question 9234. A described specimen of the humerus shows a rough elevation on the outer surface about halfway down the shaft, with a narrow groove winding behind it. Which two features are shown? The answer is the deltoid tuberosity and the spiral groove. The deltoid pulls on the tuberosity, and the radial nerve lay in the groove.

Question 9235. Name the three muscles of the front of the arm, spoken as a list. The answer is the coracobrachialis, the biceps brachii and the brachialis. All three bend the elbow and all three are supplied by the musculocutaneous nerve.

Question 9236. Along the arm, which structure lies immediately medial to the brachial artery, and which vein runs on that same side? The answer is the median nerve, with the basilic vein further medially. The median nerve is at first lateral to the artery and then crosses to its medial side.

Question 9237. Name the three cords of the brachial plexus and say where each lies in relation to the axillary artery. The answer is the lateral, the medial and the posterior cords, named by their position around the second part of the artery. The lateral cord lies on its outer side, the medial cord on its inner side and the posterior cord behind it.

Question 9238. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan through the middle of the arm shows a nerve lying between the biceps and the brachialis. Which nerve is shown, and what will its ending at the elbow become? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve, which becomes the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm. It has already supplied the three muscles of the front of the arm.

Question 9239. Which nerve pierces the coracobrachialis muscle on its way through the front of the arm: the axillary nerve, the musculocutaneous nerve, the median nerve, or the radial nerve? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve. It leaves the lateral cord of the brachial plexus and passes through the muscle to supply the biceps and the brachialis.

Question 9240. True or false: the median nerve supplies the muscles that bend the elbow. The answer is false. Those muscles are supplied by the musculocutaneous nerve, with the brachioradialis supplied by the radial nerve. The median nerve supplies most of the muscles that bend the wrist and the fingers.

Question 9241. Which triangular hollow on the front of the elbow is bounded above by a line between the two epicondyles, on the outer side by the brachioradialis and on the inner side by the pronator teres? The answer is the cubital fossa. Its floor is the brachialis and its roof the bicipital aponeurosis with the skin, and the pulse of the brachial artery is felt there.

Question 9242. Which structure lies between the tendon of the biceps and the median nerve in the cubital fossa: the brachial artery, the radial nerve, the ulnar artery, or the anterior interosseous nerve? The answer is the brachial artery. From the outer side to the inner side the order is the tendon of the biceps, the brachial artery and the median nerve.

Question 9243. Which two nerves lie one on each side of the cubital fossa, and what does each do beyond the elbow? The answer is the median nerve on the inner side and the radial nerve on the outer side. The median nerve passes between the two heads of the pronator teres, and the radial nerve divides over the supinator.

Question 9244. A constructed illustration: a child falls from a wall and the elbow is badly swollen, and afterwards the child cannot bend the last joint of the thumb or of the index finger, though the wrist bends normally. Which branch of which nerve has been injured? The answer is the anterior interosseous branch of the median nerve, which carries no skin fibers, so sensation is spared.

Question 9245. Which nerve lies in a groove behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus and can be felt there as a rounded cord? The answer is the ulnar nerve. Striking the bone at that point sends tingling into the little finger and the ring finger, and the nerve enters the forearm between the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris.

Question 9246. True or false: the radial nerve and its branches supply every muscle in the back of the forearm. The answer is true, although the muscular branches are carried by the deep branch. The radial nerve proper also supplies the triceps, the brachioradialis and the extensors of the wrist.

Question 9247. Which of these muscles is not supplied by the radial nerve: the brachioradialis, the extensor carpi radialis longus, the flexor digitorum profundus, or the extensor digitorum? The answer is the flexor digitorum profundus. Its lateral half is supplied by the median nerve and its medial half by the ulnar nerve.

Question 9248. A described cross section through the upper forearm shows two bones joined by a membrane, with one nerve lying on the front of that membrane between the deep flexor muscles. Which nerve is shown, and from which nerve does it arise? The answer is the anterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the median nerve, which runs beside the anterior interosseous artery.

Question 9249. Turning the palm from facing forward to facing backward happens without the elbow moving. About which axis does the movement take place, and what happens to the two bones of the forearm? The answer is a longitudinal axis running from the head of the radius to its lower end, and the radius crosses over the ulna.

Question 9250. Which artery runs down the front of the outer side of the forearm between the brachioradialis and the flexor carpi radialis? The answer is the radial artery. It lies in that groove for the lower two thirds of the forearm, covered only by skin and fascia, and its pulse is felt there above the wrist.

Question 9251. Which of these muscles lies in the front of the forearm and bends the wrist: the extensor carpi ulnaris, the flexor carpi radialis, the brachioradialis, or the abductor pollicis longus? The answer is the flexor carpi radialis, which lies just medial to the radial artery. The other three lie in the back of the forearm.

Question 9252. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the pronator teres with turning the palm upward, the supinator with turning the palm downward, the flexor carpi ulnaris with straightening the wrist, or the palmaris longus with tightening the palmar aponeurosis? The answer is the palmaris longus with tightening the palmar aponeurosis. The pronator teres turns the palm downward and the supinator turns it upward.

Question 9253. In the lower part of the forearm, which artery lies deep to the tendon of the flexor carpi ulnaris, with the ulnar nerve immediately on its inner side? The answer is the ulnar artery. It reaches that position after passing deep to the pronator teres, and it enters the hand beside the pisiform bone.

Question 9254. Which branch of the median nerve arises in the upper part of the forearm and runs down on the interosseous membrane? The answer is the anterior interosseous nerve. It supplies the flexor pollicis longus and the lateral half of the flexor digitorum profundus, and it carries no skin fibers.

Question 9255. Which muscles of the forearm are supplied by the ulnar nerve: all the flexors of the fingers, the flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial half of the flexor digitorum profundus, the brachioradialis and the supinator, or the pronator teres and the flexor carpi radialis? The answer is the flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial half of the flexor digitorum profundus.

Question 9256. A constructed illustration: a person strikes the inner side of the elbow against a table edge and feels a sharp tingling that runs down the inner side of the forearm into the little finger. Which nerve has been struck, and where does it lie? The answer is the ulnar nerve, in the groove behind the medial epicondyle, where almost no muscle covers it.

Question 9257. A described photograph shows a hand in which the ring finger and the little finger are pulled straight at the knuckles and bent at the joints beyond them, while the thumb and the index finger move normally. Which nerve is affected? The answer is the ulnar nerve, whose loss paralyzes the interossei and the medial two lumbricals.

Question 9258. Which tendon forms the border of the hollow on the back of the hand nearest the little finger: the extensor pollicis longus, the abductor pollicis longus, the extensor pollicis brevis, or the extensor indicis? The answer is the extensor pollicis longus, while the abductor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis form the border on the thumb side.

Question 9259. Which four bony points give attachment to the flexor retinaculum at the wrist? The answer is the tubercle of the scaphoid and the trapezium on the outer side, and the pisiform and the hook of the hamate on the inner side. The band turns the front of the wrist bones into a tunnel for the median nerve and the long flexors.

Question 9260. Which structure passes through the carpal tunnel together with the tendons of the flexors of the fingers: the ulnar nerve, the ulnar artery, the median nerve, or the tendon of the flexor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the median nerve, which lies just beneath the flexor retinaculum. The ulnar nerve and the ulnar artery pass over it on the little finger side.

Question 9261. Which slender tendon crosses the front of the wrist immediately superficial to the flexor retinaculum in the midline? The answer is the tendon of the palmaris longus. It is absent in a small proportion of people, which is a normal variation, and it fans out below the wrist into the palmar aponeurosis.

Question 9262. A described transverse section through the wrist shows a fibrous band arching over a row of small bones, with one nerve lying directly under the band and a second nerve with an artery lying outside it on one side. Which structures are described? The answer is the flexor retinaculum, with the median nerve inside it and the ulnar nerve and artery outside.

Question 9263. A constructed illustration: a person has tingling and numbness in the thumb, the index finger, the middle finger and the outer half of the ring finger, worse at night, while the little finger is unaffected. Which structure is compressed, and where? The answer is the median nerve, beneath the flexor retinaculum at the wrist, and the ulnar nerve lies outside that tunnel.

Question 9264. True or false: the ulnar nerve enters the hand by passing through the carpal tunnel. The answer is false. It passes into the palm on the little finger side, superficial to the flexor retinaculum, and only the median nerve and the tendons of the flexors pass through the tunnel.

Question 9265. Which muscle of the hand still works after the median nerve is divided at the wrist: the abductor pollicis brevis, the opponens pollicis, the adductor pollicis, or the superficial head of the flexor pollicis brevis? The answer is the adductor pollicis, which is supplied by the ulnar nerve. The short muscles that draw the thumb away from the palm are lost.

Question 9266. Which three short muscles form the fleshy mass at the base of the thumb, and which of them rolls the first metacarpal toward the palm? The answer is the abductor pollicis brevis, the flexor pollicis brevis and the opponens pollicis, and the opponens rolls the bone. The abductor lies most superficially of the three.

Question 9267. Name two tendons that can be felt at the front of the wrist and say where each lies, spoken as a list. The answer is the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis and the tendon of the palmaris longus. The first lies just medial to the radial artery, and the second lies in the midline over the flexor retinaculum.

Question 9268. Which tendons lie in the first compartment under the extensor retinaculum on the back of the wrist: the extensor pollicis longus, the abductor pollicis longus with the extensor pollicis brevis, the extensor indicis, or the extensor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the abductor pollicis longus with the extensor pollicis brevis. The extensor pollicis longus lies in the third compartment and the extensor indicis in the fourth.

Question 9269. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan shows the scaphoid and the lunate side by side, with the head of the capitate between them. Why is the scaphoid the carpal bone most often broken? The answer is that it bridges the two rows of carpal bones, and its blood supply enters at its far end, so the fragment nearest the forearm may lose its supply.

Question 9270. Which structures lie superficial to the flexor retinaculum and therefore escape compression within the carpal tunnel? The answer is the ulnar nerve, the ulnar artery and the tendon of the palmaris longus, which cross the front of the retinaculum on their way into the hand.

Question 9271. Which arch of arteries lies just deep to the palmar aponeurosis and is formed mainly by the continuation of the ulnar artery? The answer is the superficial palmar arch, which gives off the palmar digital arteries that run to the fingers. The deep palmar arch lies against the metacarpals and is formed mainly by the radial artery.

Question 9272. Which vessel completes the superficial palmar arch on its outer side: the deep branch of the ulnar artery, the radial artery, the anterior interosseous artery, or the posterior interosseous artery? The answer is the radial artery. The deep palmar arch is completed on its inner side by the deep branch of the ulnar artery.

Question 9273. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the first dorsal interosseous with drawing the index finger toward the middle finger, the palmar interossei with spreading the fingers apart, the lumbricals with straightening the joints beyond the knuckles, or the palmaris brevis with rolling the fifth metacarpal toward the palm? The answer is the lumbricals with straightening the joints beyond the knuckles.

Question 9274. A described illustration: an older person has thick cords running down the palm from the wrist to the bases of the ring finger and the little finger, and those two fingers are drawn into the palm. Which structure has thickened? The answer is the palmar aponeurosis, whose cords tether the skin and the flexor sheaths of those fingers.

Question 9275. A described dissection of the palm, after the aponeurosis and the tendons are turned aside, shows a space on the inner side of the middle of the palm bounded by fibrous septa and filled with loose tissue. Which space is shown, and where does it extend? The answer is the midpalmar space, which lies between the middle and the ring metacarpals and behind the flexor tendons.

Question 9276. Which of these muscles forms the rounded pad at the base of the little finger: the abductor digiti minimi, the first dorsal interosseous, the palmaris brevis, or the opponens pollicis? The answer is the abductor digiti minimi, which lies most superficially of the three hypothenar muscles. The flexor digiti minimi brevis lies beside it and the opponens lies deepest.

Question 9277. Which muscle fills the first web space and is tested by spreading the index finger away from the middle finger against resistance: the first palmar interosseous, the first dorsal interosseous, the first lumbrical, or the adductor pollicis? The answer is the first dorsal interosseous, which arises from the sides of the first two metacarpals that face each other.

Question 9278. Which nerve lies immediately deep to the adductor pollicis muscle, and which two groups of muscles does it supply in the deepest plane of the palm? The answer is the deep branch of the ulnar nerve, which supplies the adductor pollicis and the interossei. The nerve curves around the hook of the hamate with the deep palmar arch.

Question 9279. True or false: the lumbrical muscles bend the knuckles and at the same time straighten the joints between the knuckles and the fingertips. The answer is true. Each lumbrical arises from a tendon of the flexor digitorum profundus and ends in the extensor expansion.

Question 9280. A described cut at the base of the thumb divides a small branch curving around the flexor retinaculum. Which branch is it: the deep branch of the ulnar nerve, the recurrent branch of the median nerve, the palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve, or the superficial branch of the radial nerve? The answer is the recurrent branch, and bringing the thumb across the palm is lost.

Question 9281. Which large vein runs up the outer side of the arm and lies in the groove between the deltoid and the pectoralis major? The answer is the cephalic vein. It reaches the deltopectoral groove after passing in front of the elbow on the thumb side, and it ends in the axillary vein.

Question 9282. Which vein crosses the front of the elbow just under the skin and connects the cephalic vein to the basilic vein: the basilic vein, the brachial vein, the median cubital vein, or the cephalic vein? The answer is the median cubital vein, which lies in the roof of the cubital fossa over the bicipital aponeurosis.

Question 9283. Which vein runs up the inner side of the arm and pierces the deep fascia about the middle of the arm to join the brachial veins? The answer is the basilic vein. It begins on the little finger side of the back of the hand and passes in front of the elbow before turning deep.

Question 9284. A described photograph of the back of a hand shows a network of veins over the knuckles, with one vessel running up the thumb side of the wrist and another up the little finger side. Which two veins begin from that network? The answer is the cephalic vein on the thumb side and the basilic vein on the little finger side.

Question 9285. A described illustration: a small, tender swelling is felt in the groove just above and behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus, on the inner side of the elbow. Which group of nodes is enlarged, and which region does it drain? The answer is the epitrochlear nodes, which drain the little finger side of the hand and the inner side of the forearm.

Question 9286. Which five groups make up the axillary lymph nodes? The answer is the apical, the central, the lateral, the pectoral and the posterior groups. They lie in the fat of the axilla around the axillary vein, and most lymph from the upper limb passes through them.

Question 9287. How are the axillary nodes divided into three levels: by their relation to the axillary artery, by their relation to the pectoralis minor muscle, by their relation to the clavicle, or by their relation to the serratus anterior? The answer is by their relation to the pectoralis minor. Those below the muscle form the first level and those above it the third.

Question 9288. A described illustration: with the arm held overhead for a long time, a person notices aching and numbness along the inner side of the forearm and the little finger, and the pulse at the wrist becomes faint. Which structures are compressed at the root of the neck? The answer is the lower trunk of the brachial plexus and the subclavian artery on the first rib.

Question 9289. A described MRI of the root of the neck shows a bundle of nerves passing between two muscles that run from the neck to the first and second ribs, with an artery lying below and in front of the bundle. Which muscles and which space are shown? The answer is the scalenus anterior and the scalenus medius, with the brachial plexus and the subclavian artery between them.

Question 9290. Which statement about the structures that cross the first rib into the axilla is correct: the axillary vein lies in front of the artery, the axillary artery lies behind the brachial plexus, the brachial plexus lies below the vein, or the artery lies in front of the subclavius muscle? The answer is that the axillary vein lies in front of the artery between the clavicle and the first rib.

Question 9291. Which spinal nerve roots form the brachial plexus? The answer is the fifth, sixth, seventh and eighth cervical roots together with the first thoracic root. They emerge between the scalenus anterior and the scalenus medius and join above the clavicle to form three trunks.

Question 9292. Which of these four pairs belongs together: the axillary nerve with the skin over the deltoid, the radial nerve with the little finger, the median nerve with the back of the forearm, or the ulnar nerve with the thumb? The answer is the axillary nerve with the skin over the deltoid. The little finger belongs to the ulnar nerve.

Question 9293. In a difficult delivery a described newborn holds one arm pressed to the side and rotated inward, with the elbow straight and the wrist bent. Which part of the brachial plexus is injured? The answer is the upper trunk, formed by the fifth and sixth cervical roots, so the shoulder and the front of the arm are paralyzed while the hand keeps its power.

Question 9294. A described specimen of a hand shows the fleshy pad at the base of the thumb flattened and hollow, with the thumb lying in the same plane as the palm. Which nerve was injured? The answer is the median nerve, injured at the wrist, so the short muscles of the thumb waste while the little finger keeps its power.

Question 9295. A constructed illustration: an older person falls on the outstretched hand, and the lower end of the radius is broken and driven backward, so the wrist looks like a fork. Which nerve is most often bruised by the displaced fragment, and which wrist bone may break in the same fall? The answer is the median nerve, and the scaphoid may break as well.

Question 9296. When the axillary artery is blocked, blood reaches the arm through which set of connections: the anastomosis around the scapula, the palmar arches, the intercostal arteries, or the interosseous arteries? The answer is the anastomosis around the scapula, where the suprascapular, the dorsal scapular and the circumflex scapular arteries meet.

Question 9297. Which two sheets of deep fascia pass from the humerus to the deep fascia of the arm and divide the limb into an anterior and a posterior compartment? The answer is the medial and the lateral intermuscular septa. The anterior compartment holds the flexors, and the posterior compartment holds the triceps.

Question 9298. Which arteries take part in the anastomosis around the elbow: the suprascapular and the circumflex scapular, the interosseous and the intercostal, the collateral branches of the brachial artery with the recurrent branches of the radial and ulnar arteries, or the dorsal scapular with the lateral thoracic? The answer is the collateral branches of the brachial artery with the recurrent branches of the radial and ulnar arteries.

Question 9299. A described radiograph of the elbow of a young child shows only one small rounded center of bone in the lower end of the humerus, with the shaft and the two forearm bones still cartilaginous at their ends. Which center is it, and which one appears next? The answer is the capitulum, which appears first, followed by the head of the radius.

Question 9300. Which artery supplies the thumb and the thumb side of the index finger: the superficial palmar arch, the deep palmar arch, the principal artery of the thumb from the radial artery, or the anterior interosseous artery? The answer is the principal artery of the thumb, which arises from the radial artery as it turns from the back of the hand into the palm.

To summarize, the upper limb is tied to the trunk by the clavicle alone, and the scapula rests on the chest wall at an oblique angle, so abduction at the shoulder needs the scapula to rotate upward by the trapezius and serratus anterior acting together. The rotator cuff is the supraspinatus, the infraspinatus, the teres minor and the subscapularis, and the axillary nerve winds around the surgical neck with the posterior circumflex humeral artery, while the long thoracic nerve lies exposed on the serratus anterior, so its division leaves the scapula winging. The axillary artery is described in three parts by its relation to the pectoralis minor, and the cords of the brachial plexus are named from their position around its second part. In the arm the radial nerve occupies the spiral groove, where the branches to the triceps arise before it leaves, and the musculocutaneous nerve pierces the coracobrachialis and ends as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm. The median nerve passes through the carpal tunnel beneath the flexor retinaculum, while the ulnar nerve and artery cross over it on the little finger side, and the anterior interosseous branch of the median nerve supplies the deep flexors of the thumb.
