# Chapter 71 — Gallbladder and Biliary System

This chapter covers the gallbladder and the biliary tree first, and then the pancreas, moving from the surface of each organ to its ducts, its blood supply and its neighborhood. It holds one hundred questions, opening with the gallbladder and its drainage and closing with the development of the pancreas. Each question states its answer immediately, before the next question begins, so nothing has to be carried in mind. The two organs are neighbors in the upper abdomen, and the reason to hear them together is that their ducts share one opening into the bowel. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7001. The gallbladder is a pear-shaped sac that lies on the under surface of the liver and stores bile. Which of its parts projects below the sharp lower border of the liver? The answer is the fundus. The fundus is the wide blind end, turning downward and forward to touch the anterior abdominal wall, while the body and the neck lie further back against the liver.

Question 7002. Which part of the gallbladder is the narrow funnel that turns downward and backward to continue as the cystic duct? The answer is the neck. The neck lies in the gallbladder fossa and curves in a gentle S shape before it becomes the duct. A small bulge on its right side is Hartmann's pouch.

Question 7003. Which of these lists gives the parts of the gallbladder in order from the closed end to the duct: body, fundus, neck; fundus, body, neck; neck, fundus, body; or fundus, neck, body? The answer is fundus, body, neck. Bile leaves the organ by the neck, so the fundus is the closed end.

Question 7004. Which duct drains the gallbladder and joins the common hepatic duct to form the common bile duct? The answer is the cystic duct. It is about 3 to 4 centimeters long and runs backward, downward and to the left from the neck. Its lining carries a spiral fold that helps to keep the duct open.

Question 7005. On the visceral surface of the liver, between which two lobes does the gallbladder fossa lie, and what sits within it? The answer is the right lobe and the quadrate lobe, with the gallbladder lying in the fossa. The fossa is a shallow depression on the under surface, in front of the porta hepatis.

Question 7006. Which of these statements about the common hepatic duct is correct: it is formed by the union of the right and left hepatic ducts, it is formed by the cystic duct and the left hepatic duct, it drains only the right lobe of the liver, or it opens directly into the stomach? The answer is that it is formed by the union of the right and left hepatic ducts at the porta hepatis.

Question 7007. What is the name of the duct formed where the cystic duct joins the common hepatic duct, and roughly how long is it? The answer is the common bile duct, about 8 centimeters long. It runs down in the free edge of the lesser omentum and then passes behind the first part of the duodenum.

Question 7008. A described magnetic resonance image of the biliary tree shows a long duct running down behind the duodenum and ending in a swelling that joins a second duct before both enter the bowel wall. What is that swelling? The answer is the hepatopancreatic ampulla. It lies within the wall of the second part of the duodenum.

Question 7009. A constructed illustration: a person has severe pain in the right upper abdomen after a fatty meal, and the pain also reaches the right shoulder. Which organ is the source, and which nerve carries the shoulder pain? The answer is the gallbladder, with the phrenic nerve carrying the pain from the diaphragm.

Question 7010. What is the name of the small pouch on the right side of the neck of the gallbladder in which a stone may become stuck? The answer is Hartmann's pouch. It is a dilatation of the neck, and a stone lodged there presses on the cystic duct. The gallbladder then distends behind the block.

Question 7011. Which of these sets bounds the triangle in which the cystic artery is found: the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the under surface of the liver; the common bile duct, the duodenum and the pancreas; the portal vein, the aorta and the stomach; or the hepatic ducts, the spleen and the diaphragm? The answer is the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the under surface of the liver.

Question 7012. Which artery usually supplies the gallbladder, and from which artery does it arise? The answer is the cystic artery, usually from the right hepatic artery. The right hepatic artery comes from the proper hepatic artery. The cystic artery runs through the triangle bounded by the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the liver.

Question 7013. True or false: the wall of the gallbladder contains a muscularis mucosae and a true submucosa. The answer is false. The wall has a mucosa resting on a lamina propria, a muscle coat arranged in no fixed layers, and a perimuscular coat, but it has no submucosa and no muscularis mucosae.

Question 7014. Into which part of the small intestine does the common bile duct open, and on which wall of it? The answer is the second part of the duodenum, on its posteromedial wall. The opening is the major duodenal papilla, about 8 to 10 centimeters below the pylorus. The descending duodenum wraps around the head of the pancreas.

Question 7015. Name, as a short answer, the two ducts that unite to form the common bile duct. The answer is the common hepatic duct and the cystic duct. The common hepatic duct brings bile down from the liver, the cystic duct brings bile from the gallbladder, and their union marks the beginning of the common bile duct.

Question 7016. Which of these structures guards the entry of bile and pancreatic juice into the duodenum: the pyloric sphincter, the sphincter of Oddi, the ileocecal valve, or the internal anal sphincter? The answer is the sphincter of Oddi. It surrounds the hepatopancreatic ampulla and keeps the contents of the bowel out of the ducts between meals.

Question 7017. A described computed tomography scan of the upper abdomen shows a rounded fluid-filled sac lying between the right lobe of the liver and the duodenum, with dilated ducts beside it. Which organ is distended, and what does the picture suggest? The answer is the gallbladder, with an obstruction lower in the biliary tree.

Question 7018. In the free edge of the lesser omentum, which of the bile duct, the hepatic artery and the portal vein lies most posterior, and which lies furthest to the right? The answer is the portal vein posteriorly and the bile duct furthest to the right. The hepatic artery lies in front and to the left of the vein.

Question 7019. Which fold of mucosa winds along the inside of the cystic duct and helps to keep its lumen open? The answer is the spiral valve. It runs along the duct rather than closing it, so it does not act as a true valve. It also makes the duct easy for a surgeon to recognize.

Question 7020. A constructed illustration: a stone leaves the gallbladder, travels down the cystic duct, and becomes stuck where the duct is narrowest. Where has it most likely stopped? The answer is the lower end of the common bile duct, as it passes through the duodenal wall. The duct dilates above the block.

Question 7021. Which of these organs makes bile: the gallbladder, the liver, the pancreas, or the spleen? The answer is the liver. The gallbladder stores and concentrates bile but does not make it. The liver produces about 600 to 1000 milliliters of bile each day, and bile salts within that fluid help to digest fat.

Question 7022. What is the name of the small outpouchings of gallbladder mucosa that push into the muscle layer and become prominent in adenomyomatosis? The answer is the Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses. They are normal diverticula of the mucosal surface. When the wall thickens, they are seen as tiny pockets within it.

Question 7023. Which of these pairs belongs together: the fundus with the blind lower end, the neck with the spiral valve, the common bile duct with the cystic artery, or the cystic duct with the hepatopancreatic ampulla? The answer is the fundus with the blind lower end. The spiral valve lies in the cystic duct, and the ampulla ends the common bile duct.

Question 7024. A described cholangiogram shows dye filling two ducts that join at the hilum of the liver, and a third duct joining them from below and to the right. Which three ducts are described? The answer is the right hepatic duct, the left hepatic duct and the cystic duct. A stone would appear as a gap in that column of dye.

Question 7025. Which parts of the bowel lie against the body and the neck of the gallbladder? The answer is the transverse colon and the first and second parts of the duodenum. The body rests upon the transverse colon, and the duodenum lies against the neck. The fundus faces the abdominal wall instead.

Question 7026. What happens to bile while it is stored in the gallbladder: is it diluted, is it concentrated, is it mixed with pancreatic juice, or is it absorbed completely? The answer is that it is concentrated. Water and salts are absorbed across the wall, while bile salts, pigments, cholesterol and phospholipids stay behind. It may become five to ten times stronger.

Question 7027. A described transverse section of the upper abdomen shows a rounded structure on the visceral surface of the liver, to the right of the quadrate lobe. In which plane is the section taken, and in which direction does the lower end of the structure point? The answer is a transverse plane, with the lower end pointing downward and forward.

Question 7028. What is the name of the small opening on the inner wall of the duodenum through which bile enters the bowel? The answer is the major duodenal papilla. It lies on the posteromedial wall of the second part of the duodenum, and the pancreatic duct usually shares the same opening.

Question 7029. A constructed illustration: during an operation in the triangle bounded by the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the liver, an artery is divided close to the liver. Which artery has probably been injured, and what does it supply? The answer is the right hepatic artery, which supplies the right lobe and gives off the cystic artery.

Question 7030. Which of these statements about the blood supply of the gallbladder is correct: the cystic artery usually comes from the right hepatic artery, it usually comes from the left hepatic artery, it comes from the gastroduodenal artery, or the gallbladder has no named artery? The answer is that the cystic artery usually comes from the right hepatic artery, though it may arise elsewhere.

Question 7031. True or false: the gallbladder stores bile and empties it into the duodenum after a meal. The answer is true. Between meals the sphincter at the lower end of the bile duct stays closed, so bile backs up the cystic duct into the gallbladder. After a meal the gallbladder contracts and the sphincter relaxes.

Question 7032. A described ultrasound image of the upper abdomen shows an elongated sac with a bright wall lying beneath the liver, dark fluid inside it, and a bright band at its lower end. What is the structure, and what does the bright band most likely represent? The answer is the gallbladder, with a stone at its neck.

Question 7033. Which lymph node lies beside the cystic duct and receives lymph from the gallbladder? The answer is the cystic node. Lymph then passes to the nodes around the portal vein and onward to the coeliac nodes. Some lymph from the fundus drains directly into the liver instead.

Question 7034. Name, as a short answer, the three structures that make up the portal triad in the free edge of the lesser omentum. The answer is the bile duct, the hepatic artery and the portal vein. They run together from the porta hepatis down to the duodenum, with the vein lying behind the other two.

Question 7035. Which structure lies immediately behind the common bile duct in the free edge of the lesser omentum, and which lies to its left? The answer is the portal vein behind and the hepatic artery to the left. The duct is therefore the most anterior and the most rightward of the three, which makes it easy to reach from the front.

Question 7036. Which hormone makes the gallbladder contract and relaxes the sphincter at the end of the bile duct: secretin, cholecystokinin, gastrin, or insulin? The answer is cholecystokinin. It is released by cells in the duodenal wall when fat and protein enter the bowel. Its companion, secretin, makes the pancreas pour out a watery alkaline juice.

Question 7037. A constructed illustration: a person with inflammation around the gallbladder reports pain at the tip of the right shoulder as well as in the upper abdomen. Which nerve explains the shoulder pain, and by which segments does it reach the skin? The answer is the phrenic nerve, carrying fibers from the third, fourth and fifth cervical segments, which also supply the skin over the shoulder.

Question 7038. From which embryonic structure does the gallbladder develop, and at about which week does it appear? The answer is the hepatic diverticulum of the foregut, in the fourth week. That diverticulum grows into the septum transversum and divides, its cranial part forming the liver and its ducts and its caudal part forming the gallbladder and the cystic duct.

Question 7039. Which of these pairs belongs together: the cystic duct with the gallbladder, the common bile duct with the liver, the pancreatic duct with the spleen, or the hepatic duct with the duodenum? The answer is the cystic duct with the gallbladder. The common hepatic duct comes from the liver, and the pancreatic duct comes from the pancreas.

Question 7040. A described operative photograph shows a small artery crossing the triangle formed by the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the liver, with a lymph node beside it. Which artery and which node are shown? The answer is the cystic artery and the cystic node. Because a variant artery in this triangle may be the only supply of the right lobe, the anatomy is defined before anything is divided.

Question 7041. Which feature of the gallbladder wall allows the organ to distend: a thick submucosa, muscle bundles arranged in no fixed layers, a complete ring of cartilage, or a lining of stratified squamous cells? The answer is muscle bundles arranged in no fixed layers. The muscle coat has no orderly inner circular and outer longitudinal arrangement, so the wall can stretch.

Question 7042. At which level does the fundus of the gallbladder lie, and where can it be felt on the abdominal wall? The answer is the transpyloric plane, at the tip of the ninth right costal cartilage. That point lies where the lateral edge of the rectus sheath meets the costal margin. The same plane passes through the pylorus and the neck of the pancreas.

Question 7043. Which part of the gallbladder is the widest, and which part is the closed end? The answer is the body, which is the widest part, and the fundus, which is the closed end. The neck is the narrow part that opens into the cystic duct, and the body lies between the fundus and the neck.

Question 7044. A constructed illustration: a person has pale stools, dark urine and yellow skin, and imaging shows dilated ducts within the liver but a gallbladder of normal size. Where is the obstruction, and why is the gallbladder not enlarged? The answer is the common hepatic duct above the cystic duct, so bile never reaches the gallbladder.

Question 7045. Which of these statements about the lining of the gallbladder is correct: it is a single layer of columnar cells with microvilli, it is stratified squamous epithelium, it is ciliated throughout, or it is transitional epithelium? The answer is a single layer of columnar cells with microvilli. These cells absorb water and salts from the stored bile, and they rest on a lamina propria.

Question 7046. A described radiograph taken after dye is injected into the bile ducts shows a normal duct system with a small sac hanging from it below and to the right. Which structure is the sac, and which duct connects it to the system? The answer is the gallbladder and the cystic duct. A stone would appear as a gap in the dye.

Question 7047. What is the name of the muscle that surrounds the lower end of the common bile duct alone, above the ampulla: the sphincter of the bile duct, the pyloric sphincter, the sphincter of the pancreatic duct, or the internal anal sphincter? The answer is the sphincter of the bile duct. It lies within the duodenal wall and, with its neighbors, forms the sphincter of Oddi.

Question 7048. A described specimen shows a gallbladder whose fundus is folded back on itself like a cap. What is this variation called, and is it important? The answer is a Phrygian cap. It is a harmless fold of the fundus found in a small minority of people, and on a radiograph it may mimic a stone or a fold of the wall.

Question 7049. Which statement best explains why the gallbladder fills with bile between meals: the liver pumps bile at high pressure, the sphincter at the duodenal end of the duct is closed, the cystic duct has a valve that pulls bile in, or the gallbladder draws bile inward? The answer is that the sphincter at the duodenal end of the duct is closed, so bile takes the path of least resistance up the cystic duct.

Question 7050. A constructed illustration: after a long illness in which a person took nothing by mouth, stones are found in a gallbladder that never emptied. Which property of the gallbladder best explains this? The answer is that it keeps concentrating the bile that the liver continues to make. Water is absorbed while the bile sits, so its cholesterol and salts may crystallize.

Question 7051. The pancreas lies against the posterior abdominal wall, and peritoneum covers only its front. Which word describes this position? The answer is retroperitoneal. The gland cannot be lifted forward, because its posterior surface is attached to the tissues behind it. Only the tail, lying in a fold of peritoneum, has any freedom.

Question 7052. Which part of the pancreas lies within the curve of the duodenum? The answer is the head. It is the broad right-hand part of the gland, and the second part of the duodenum wraps around its right border. The uncinate process hangs from its lower and left side.

Question 7053. Which of these statements about the position of the pancreas is correct: it lies across the first and second lumbar vertebrae, it lies in the pelvis, it lies in front of the stomach, or it lies within the lesser sac? The answer is that it lies across the first and second lumbar vertebrae. Its head is lower than its tail.

Question 7054. Which part of the pancreas hooks backward and to the left behind the superior mesenteric vessels? The answer is the uncinate process. It arises from the lower part of the head and passes behind the superior mesenteric vein and artery, so those vessels lie in front of it.

Question 7055. The portal vein is formed behind which part of the pancreas, and which two veins unite there? The answer is the neck of the pancreas, where the splenic vein meets the superior mesenteric vein. The new vein then runs upward in the free edge of the lesser omentum toward the liver.

Question 7056. Which part of the pancreas lies directly in front of the portal vein: the head, the neck, the body, or the tail? The answer is the neck. The vein is formed behind it, and the neck is the narrow part of the gland between the head and the body. The superior mesenteric vessels pass in the groove beneath it.

Question 7057. Into which organ does the tail of the pancreas project, and along which vessels does it run? The answer is the spleen, along the splenic vessels. The tail reaches the hilum of the spleen. It is the only part of the gland that lies free within a peritoneal fold, the splenorenal ligament.

Question 7058. Into which part of the bowel does the main pancreatic duct usually open, and with which duct does it share that opening? The answer is the second part of the duodenum, sharing the major duodenal papilla with the common bile duct. The two ducts join first in the hepatopancreatic ampulla.

Question 7059. A described computed tomography scan of the upper abdomen shows a long glandular organ lying across the midline behind the stomach, its head wrapped by the duodenum and its tail reaching the spleen. Which organ is described, and which large vein lies behind its neck? The answer is the pancreas, with the portal vein behind its neck.

Question 7060. A described transverse section of the upper abdomen shows the stomach in front and a long gland behind it, with a narrow space between the two. What is that space, and what is the gland? The answer is the lesser sac, with the pancreas behind it. The spleen lies to the left in the same section.

Question 7061. Which fault in development produces a ring of pancreatic tissue around the second part of the duodenum: failure of the dorsal bud to appear, division of the ventral bud with migration in two directions, failure of the two ducts to fuse, or absence of the uncinate process? The answer is division of the ventral bud with migration in two directions, so that it encircles the bowel.

Question 7062. True or false: the pancreas is a retroperitoneal organ, and peritoneum covers only its anterior surface. The answer is true, with one small exception. The head, neck and body lie against the posterior wall, while the tail runs into the splenorenal ligament and is covered on both sides.

Question 7063. What is the name of the smaller pancreatic duct that usually opens above the main duct at a separate small papilla? The answer is the accessory pancreatic duct. It drains the upper part of the head of the gland. It usually joins the main duct, but in some people it remains separate.

Question 7064. Which of these does the pancreas secrete: only digestive enzymes, only hormones, both digestive enzymes and hormones, or neither? The answer is both. The acinar cells make enzymes and a watery alkaline fluid, and the islets of Langerhans make insulin, glucagon and other hormones. The endocrine part is only a small fraction of the gland.

Question 7065. A constructed illustration: a person has deep pain in the upper abdomen that bores through to the back and eases when sitting forward. Which organ lies against the structures that produce that back pain? The answer is the pancreas. It lies against the aorta and the coeliac plexus, so its pain is felt deeply and centrally.

Question 7066. Which pair of arteries supplies the head of the pancreas: the superior pancreaticoduodenal and the inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries, the splenic and the left gastric arteries, the cystic and the right hepatic arteries, or the left and right gastroepiploic arteries? The answer is the superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries. The superior one comes from the gastroduodenal artery.

Question 7067. Which structures lie immediately behind the body of the pancreas? The answer is the aorta, the superior mesenteric artery, the left kidney and the splenic vein. The splenic vein runs along the back of the gland. The left crus of the diaphragm and the left renal vessels also lie behind it.

Question 7068. Which groove on the neck of the pancreas carries the superior mesenteric vessels, and what lies behind those vessels? The answer is the groove on the neck, with the uncinate process behind them. The vein and the artery pass in front of the uncinate process, so the gland partly encircles the vessels.

Question 7069. Which of these statements about the control of pancreatic secretion is correct: secretin makes the gland pour a watery alkaline juice, secretin makes the gland pour enzymes, cholecystokinin makes the gland pour bicarbonate, or both hormones come from the stomach? The answer is that secretin makes the gland pour a watery alkaline juice. Cholecystokinin makes the acinar cells pour enzymes.

Question 7070. Which of these pairs belongs together: the head of the pancreas with the duodenum, the tail of the pancreas with the liver, the neck of the pancreas with the spleen, or the body of the pancreas with the rectum? The answer is the head of the pancreas with the duodenum. The tail lies against the spleen, and the body lies behind the stomach.

Question 7071. Which of these best describes pancreatic juice: about 1500 milliliters a day and rich in bicarbonate, about 150 milliliters a day and rich in acid, about 1500 milliliters a day and free of enzymes, or about 5 liters a day and rich in bile salts? The answer is about 1500 milliliters a day and rich in bicarbonate. Its enzymes work best in that alkaline setting.

Question 7072. A described magnetic resonance image of the pancreatic ducts shows a short duct joining the bile duct and a longer duct running the length of the gland and opening separately above it. What pattern is described? The answer is that the dorsal and the ventral ducts have failed to fuse, a pattern called pancreas divisum, in which the long duct carries most of the juice.

Question 7073. A described ultrasound image shows a long narrow gland lying across the midline behind the stomach, with a bright line running along its middle. What is the gland, and what is the bright line? The answer is the pancreas, with the main pancreatic duct. Gas in the bowel in front often hides the gland.

Question 7074. A described transverse section shows a gland whose head lies to the right of the midline and whose tail lies to the left, with a duct running through its middle. Which gland is shown, and in which direction does the duct run? The answer is the pancreas, with its duct running from the tail on the left toward the head on the right.

Question 7075. Name, as a short answer, the two ducts of the pancreas, and say where each usually opens. The answer is the main pancreatic duct, which opens at the major duodenal papilla, and the accessory pancreatic duct, which opens at the minor papilla above it. The main duct joins the common bile duct first.

Question 7076. What is the name of the swelling where the pancreatic duct and the common bile duct join before they enter the duodenum: the pyloric canal, the hepatopancreatic ampulla, the cystic duct, or the pancreatic notch? The answer is the hepatopancreatic ampulla. It lies within the wall of the second part of the duodenum.

Question 7077. A constructed illustration: a growth in the head of the pancreas presses on the structures running through it. Which two ducts are most likely to be blocked, and which vein behind the head may also be compressed? The answer is the common bile duct and the pancreatic duct, with the portal vein behind the head. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7078. True or false: the tail of the pancreas lies within the splenorenal ligament and moves with the spleen. The answer is true. The tail is the only part of the gland that is not fixed to the posterior abdominal wall, and it reaches the hilum of the spleen along with the splenic vessels.

Question 7079. Which statement best explains why pancreas divisum leaves most of the pancreatic juice draining through a small opening: the ventral duct never forms, the dorsal and the ventral ducts fail to fuse, the sphincter of Oddi closes the main duct, or the bile duct drains the whole gland? The answer is that the two ducts fail to fuse, so the dorsal duct carries most of the juice to the smaller minor papilla.

Question 7080. Which kidney lies immediately behind the body and the tail of the pancreas, and which vessels run between them? The answer is the left kidney, with the left renal vessels. The splenic vein also runs along the back of the gland. The right kidney lies further away, behind the head and the duodenum.

Question 7081. Which cells of the pancreas make insulin, and in which part of the gland are they found? The answer is the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans, scattered through the gland and most numerous in the body and the tail. The acinar cells around them make the digestive enzymes.

Question 7082. Which of these statements about the veins of the pancreas is correct: the head drains into the portal vein and the body and tail into the splenic vein, all pancreatic veins drain into the inferior vena cava, the gland has no veins of its own, or all its veins drain into the left renal vein? The answer is the first of these. The splenic vein then carries that blood to the portal vein.

Question 7083. A described computed tomography scan of a person with long-standing pancreatic disease shows a widened duct running the length of the gland with several bright deposits along it. What do the bright deposits represent, and what do they suggest? The answer is calcium within the duct, suggesting chronic inflammation of the gland. The duct is widened behind them.

Question 7084. The root of the transverse mesocolon crosses which of these organs: the pancreas, the spleen, the right kidney, or the gallbladder? The answer is the pancreas, along its anterior border. From that line the two layers of the mesocolon pass down onto the transverse colon. The attachment marks the lower limit of the lesser sac.

Question 7085. Which of these pairs belongs together: the beta cell with insulin, the acinar cell with bile, the alpha cell with bicarbonate, or the duct cell with glucagon? The answer is the beta cell with insulin. The alpha cells make glucagon, the delta cells make somatostatin, and the acinar cells make the digestive enzymes.

Question 7086. Which statement best explains why pancreatic pain is felt deeply in the upper abdomen and the back, while gallbladder pain is felt in the right upper abdomen: the pancreas lies closer to the skin, the two organs send pain fibers to different spinal segments, the gallbladder has no pain fibers, or the pancreas is supplied by the phrenic nerve? The answer is that the two organs send pain fibers to different spinal segments.

Question 7087. A constructed illustration: a newborn vomits after every feed, and imaging shows a dilated first part of the duodenum with a ring of tissue around the second part. Which fault in development is present? The answer is an annular pancreas, formed when the ventral bud migrates in two directions and encircles the duodenum. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7088. Which parts of the pancreas develop from the ventral bud, and which from the dorsal bud? The answer is that the ventral bud forms the lower part of the head and the uncinate process, while the dorsal bud forms the upper head, the body and the tail. The ventral bud rotates around the duodenum before the two fuse.

Question 7089. Which artery supplies the body and the tail of the pancreas, and from which vessel does it arise? The answer is the great pancreatic artery and the other pancreatic branches of the splenic artery. That artery runs along the upper border of the gland and gives off these branches on its way to the spleen.

Question 7090. A described specimen shows a pale lobulated gland lying on a tray, with a narrow end on the left and a broad end on the right. Which end lies nearest the duodenum, and which end lies nearest the spleen? The answer is that the broad head lies nearest the duodenum and the narrow tail nearest the spleen.

Question 7091. A described scan shows a long duct running through the whole gland and a shorter duct above it, the two opening into the duodenum at separate points. Which duct carries most of the juice, and why does the arrangement matter? The answer is the long main duct. The accessory duct drains only the upper head, so its opening is smaller.

Question 7092. True or false: small patches of pancreatic tissue are sometimes found in the wall of the stomach or the small intestine, and they are normal variations. The answer is true. Such patches may contain islets and usually cause no trouble. They are found most often in the stomach and the upper small intestine.

Question 7093. A constructed illustration: a person develops painless yellow skin and a gallbladder that can be felt below the liver, and imaging shows a mass in the head of the pancreas. Which duct is blocked, and why is the gallbladder enlarged? The answer is the common bile duct, blocked below the cystic duct, so bile still reaches and fills the gallbladder. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7094. Which of these pairs belongs together: the splenic artery with the body and tail of the pancreas, the gastroduodenal artery with the tail, the superior mesenteric artery with the tail, or the left gastric artery with the head? The answer is the splenic artery with the body and the tail. The head is supplied by the pancreaticoduodenal arteries.

Question 7095. Which structures lie between the tail of the pancreas and the spleen at the hilum: the splenic vessels and the splenorenal ligament, the bile duct and the portal vein, the aorta and the left crus, or the transverse mesocolon and the left colic flexure? The answer is the splenic vessels and the splenorenal ligament. The tail runs with the splenic artery and vein into the hilum.

Question 7096. The uncinate process lies between which two large vessels, and what lies immediately behind it? The answer is the superior mesenteric vessels in front and the aorta behind. The portal vein forms above and to its right. Because of these relations the process is often hidden behind the mesenteric vessels.

Question 7097. A described transverse section of the upper abdomen at the level of the first lumbar vertebra shows the pancreas in the midline, the aorta behind it, the superior mesenteric artery arising in front of it, and the left kidney behind and to the left. Which structure lies immediately in front of the pancreas in that section? The answer is the stomach.

Question 7098. A constructed illustration: a deep wound in the upper abdomen divides the neck of the pancreas and the vessels passing behind it. Which large vein is most likely to be injured, and what does it drain? The answer is the portal vein, which drains blood from the gut, the spleen and the pancreas to the liver. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7099. A described computed tomography scan shows a rounded collection of fluid lying behind the stomach with the pancreas thickened beside it. Which structure does the fluid lie against, and what does the collection suggest? The answer is the pancreas, suggesting a collection that formed after inflammation of the gland. The lesser sac lies in front of the pancreas.

Question 7100. Which statement best explains why the head and the tail of the pancreas have different blood supplies: the two parts develop from different buds, the head shares the supply of the duodenum and the tail that of the spleen, the head lies in front and the tail behind, or the tail has no artery? The answer is that the head shares the supply of the duodenum and the tail that of the spleen.

To summarize, the gallbladder lies in the gallbladder fossa between the right lobe and the quadrate lobe of the liver, stores and concentrates bile, and empties through the cystic duct, which joins the common hepatic duct to form the common bile duct. That duct runs in the free edge of the lesser omentum, where the portal vein lies behind it and the hepatic artery to its left, and it ends with the pancreatic duct at the hepatopancreatic ampulla in the second part of the duodenum. The pancreas is a retroperitoneal gland lying across the first and second lumbar vertebrae, with its head in the curve of the duodenum, its neck over the portal vein, and its tail reaching the hilum of the spleen. The uncinate process lies behind the superior mesenteric vessels, while the aorta, the left kidney and the splenic vein lie behind the body of the gland. The ventral pancreatic bud forms the lower part of the head and the uncinate process, and the dorsal bud forms the rest, and their ducts usually fuse into one main channel.
