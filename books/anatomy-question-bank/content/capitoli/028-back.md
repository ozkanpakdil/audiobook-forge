# Chapter 28 — Back

The back carries the weight of the trunk and moves it, and this chapter examines the muscles that do that work. It begins with the superficial muscles, the broad sheets that tie the shoulder girdle and the arm to the spine, then turns to the deep muscles that span one vertebra to the next, and it closes with the erector spinae, the long mass that holds the trunk upright. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer at once and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind from one item to the next. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 2701. A described dissection of the back shows a broad diamond-shaped sheet of muscle running from the base of the skull and the lower part of the neck to the middle of the back, with fibers that converge on the shoulder girdle. Which muscle is described? The answer is the trapezius. It takes its name from its shape, and it moves the shoulder blade rather than the arm itself.

Question 2702. Which of these movements does the trapezius produce: lifting the shoulder, drawing the arm down and back, bending the trunk to one side, or turning the head? The answer is lifting the shoulder. The upper fibers raise the shoulder girdle, while the middle and the lower fibers draw the shoulder blade toward the spine and steady it.

Question 2703. A described dissection of the upper back shows one flat muscle lying over another. The outer one reaches the outer end of the collarbone, and the inner one reaches the inner border of the shoulder blade. Which two muscles are shown? The answer is the trapezius over the rhomboid major. The rhomboid muscles lie against the chest wall, deep to the trapezius.

Question 2704. Which broad muscle of the back arises from the lower spine, the hip bone, and the lower ribs, and then twists into a narrow tendon that reaches the front of the upper arm? The answer is the latissimus dorsi. It forms the back wall of the armpit and gives that hollow its posterior fold.

Question 2705. Which nerve supplies the latissimus dorsi: the dorsal scapular nerve, the thoracodorsal nerve, the long thoracic nerve, or the spinal accessory nerve? The answer is the thoracodorsal nerve. It comes from the brachial plexus and runs down the back wall of the armpit to reach the deep surface of the muscle.

Question 2706. Which muscle lies immediately superficial to the rhomboid major: the trapezius, the latissimus dorsi, the serratus posterior superior, or the splenius capitis? The answer is the trapezius. The rhomboid muscles lie against the chest wall deep to the trapezius, and the serratus posterior superior lies deep to them again.

Question 2707. Which of these muscles arises from the transverse processes of the upper cervical vertebrae and runs down to lift the top corner of the shoulder blade: the levator scapulae, the rhomboid major, the serratus posterior superior, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the levator scapulae. It raises the shoulder blade and tilts its lower angle toward the spine.

Question 2708. True or false: the trapezius is supplied by the spinal accessory nerve alone. The answer is false. The spinal accessory nerve carries the motor fibers, but small branches of the cervical plexus join the muscle to carry proprioceptive fibers, so it has two sources of nerve fibers.

Question 2709. A constructed illustration: during an operation in the back of the neck, a nerve that crosses the posterior triangle and lies on the surface of the trapezius is divided, and afterwards the shoulder droops. Which nerve was divided? The answer is the spinal accessory nerve. The trapezius loses its motor supply and can no longer hold the shoulder blade in place.

Question 2710. Which movement do the rhomboid major and the rhomboid minor produce: drawing the shoulder blade forward around the chest, drawing it back toward the spine, lifting the arm above the head, or turning the palm to face the floor? The answer is drawing the shoulder blade back toward the spine. They also hold that bone flat against the chest wall.

Question 2711. Which thin muscle lies deep to the rhomboid muscles, arises from the lower part of the neck and the upper thoracic spines, and runs down and out to the upper ribs? The answer is the serratus posterior superior. It takes its nerve supply from the intercostal nerves rather than from a posterior ramus.

Question 2712. Which of these forms the upper border of the triangle of auscultation: the trapezius, the latissimus dorsi, the spine of the shoulder blade, or the twelfth rib? The answer is the trapezius. The latissimus dorsi forms the lower border and the medial border of the shoulder blade the lateral border, and the floor of the gap is thin.

Question 2713. A described dissection of the lower back outlines a small gap whose sides are the latissimus dorsi, the external abdominal oblique, and the hip bone. What is this gap called, and what forms its floor? The answer is the lumbar triangle, with the internal abdominal oblique forming its floor. A loop of bowel can push through it and make a lumbar hernia.

Question 2714. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the thoracolumbar fascia in the lower back: the erector spinae, the latissimus dorsi, the serratus posterior inferior, or the trapezius? The answer is the erector spinae. The fascia wraps the long muscle mass of the back, and the latissimus dorsi attaches to the same sheet from the outside.

Question 2715. Which muscle attaches to the spine of the shoulder blade: the trapezius, the latissimus dorsi, the serratus posterior inferior, or the splenius capitis? The answer is the trapezius. Its middle fibers reach the acromion and the spine of the shoulder blade, and its upper fibers reach the outer third of the collarbone.

Question 2716. Which of these pairs belongs together: the latissimus dorsi with the front of the upper arm, the trapezius with the back of the upper arm, the rhomboid major with the front of the thigh, or the levator scapulae with the forearm? The answer is the latissimus dorsi with the front of the upper arm. Its tendon twists around the teres major and enters the groove in front of the shoulder joint.

Question 2717. Which deep muscle of the neck arises from the ligamentum nuchae and the upper thoracic spines and runs up and out to the mastoid process behind the ear? The answer is the splenius capitis. It lies in the first layer of the deep muscles and extends and turns the head.

Question 2718. A constructed illustration: after a long period of holding the neck in extension, a person has a burning pain that begins at the back of the head and spreads over the top of the skull. Which nerve is irritated, and where does it become superficial? The answer is the greater occipital nerve, which becomes superficial below the obliquus capitis inferior. It is the posterior ramus of the second cervical spinal nerve.

Question 2719. Which structures lie within the suboccipital triangle: the vertebral artery and the suboccipital nerve, the greater occipital nerve and the occipital artery, the spinal accessory nerve and the vertebral vein, or the second cervical nerve and the occipital artery? The answer is the vertebral artery and the suboccipital nerve, which is the posterior ramus of the first cervical spinal nerve.

Question 2720. Name two muscles of the back that attach to the mastoid process behind the ear. The answer is the splenius capitis and the longissimus capitis. The sternocleidomastoid from the front of the neck also reaches that same small bump.

Question 2721. Which group forms the long mass that runs up each side of the spine: the transversospinalis group, the erector spinae, the suboccipital group, or the levatores costarum? The answer is the erector spinae. It is the second layer of the deep muscles of the back and consists of the iliocostalis, the longissimus, and the spinalis.

Question 2722. A described dissection peels the superficial muscles from the back and shows a deep layer of short fibers that run upward and medially from one vertebra to the one above it. Which group is this? The answer is the transversospinalis group. It contains the semispinalis, the multifidus, and the rotatores.

Question 2723. Which part of the erector spinae lies most laterally and is named for the ribs and the hip bone it reaches? The answer is the iliocostalis. It runs from the hip bone and the lower ribs up to the angles of the upper ribs, and its highest part reaches the neck.

Question 2724. Which muscles lie immediately deep to the semispinalis capitis: the suboccipital muscles, the trapezius, the splenius capitis, or the levator scapulae? The answer is the suboccipital muscles. The semispinalis capitis is the largest of the deep muscles of the neck and covers the four short muscles at the base of the skull almost completely.

Question 2725. Which muscle is built of short bundles that spring from the transverse process of one vertebra and reach the spine of a vertebra two to four levels above it? The answer is the multifidus. Its bundles are thickest in the lower back, where they steady the joints between the vertebrae one at a time.

Question 2726. A constructed illustration: a person lifts a heavy box with the back bent forward and feels a sudden tearing pain beside the lower spine. Which muscle mass is most likely strained at that moment? The answer is the erector spinae. It must resist the forward bend of the trunk, and the strain is felt in the long mass beside the spine.

Question 2727. Which small muscles lie between the spines of neighboring vertebrae: the interspinales, the rotatores, the levatores costarum, or the intertransversarii? The answer is the interspinales. They are best developed in the neck and the lower back, and each one crosses only a single joint.

Question 2728. True or false: the deep muscles of the back are supplied by the posterior rami of the spinal nerves. The answer is true. Those rami pass backward between the transverse processes and give branches to the deep muscles and to the skin of the back, while the superficial muscles of the back are supplied by anterior rami.

Question 2729. A described specimen shows a column of muscle on each side of the lumbar spine, wrapped in a thick fascial sheath and marked by faint grooves into separate strips. Which strips are these? The answer is the iliocostalis, the longissimus, and the spinalis, the three columns of the erector spinae.

Question 2730. Which part of the erector spinae lies closest to the spines of the vertebrae: the iliocostalis, the longissimus, the spinalis, or the levator costarum? The answer is the spinalis. It is the most medial column of the erector spinae and is best developed in the upper thoracic region, where it spans several levels.

Question 2731. Which thick muscle arises from the transverse processes of the upper thoracic and lower cervical vertebrae and reaches the occipital bone between the two nuchal lines? The answer is the semispinalis capitis. It is the largest muscle of the semispinalis group and extends and turns the head.

Question 2732. Which small deep muscles run from the transverse processes of the thoracic vertebrae down and out to the rib below: the levatores costarum, the rotatores, the intertransversarii, or the serratus posterior inferior? The answer is the levatores costarum. They lie in the deepest layer beside the spine and can lift the ribs in inspiration.

Question 2733. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the trapezius but superficial to the serratus posterior superior: the rhomboid major, the latissimus dorsi, the splenius cervicis, or the semispinalis thoracis? The answer is the rhomboid major. From the surface inward at that point come the skin, the trapezius, the rhomboid muscles, and then the serratus posterior superior over the ribs.

Question 2734. A described computed tomography section of the lower back shows the spine in the middle and, behind it, two large oval masses of muscle separated by a fat-filled cleft. Which muscles are those masses? The answer is the erector spinae. One mass lies on each side in the fascial compartment behind the spine, and fat separates them at the midline.

Question 2735. From which of these does the erector spinae arise as a single mass: the sacrum and the hip bone, the ribs alone, the occipital bone, or the shoulder blade? The answer is the sacrum and the hip bone. It also takes fibers from the spines of the lower lumbar vertebrae and from the thoracolumbar fascia, and it splits above into its three columns.

Question 2736. A constructed illustration: a sharp instrument enters the back just below the tip of the shoulder blade, close to the spine, and the lung on that side later collapses. Through which gap did it most likely pass into the chest? The answer is the triangle of auscultation. Its floor is thin, and it is bounded by the trapezius, the latissimus dorsi, and the shoulder blade.

Question 2737. Which short muscle bounds the suboccipital triangle on its medial side and runs from the spine of the second cervical vertebra to the inferior nuchal line? The answer is the rectus capitis posterior major. It turns the head to the same side and is one of the four short muscles at the base of the skull.

Question 2738. How does the rhomboid minor differ from the rhomboid major: it lies below the major, it arises from the spine of the seventh cervical vertebra and the first thoracic vertebra, it inserts on the humerus, or it is supplied by the thoracodorsal nerve? The answer is that it arises from the spine of the seventh cervical vertebra and the first thoracic vertebra. The major arises from the second to the fifth thoracic spines.

Question 2739. Which of these pairs belongs together: the trapezius with the spinal accessory nerve, the latissimus dorsi with the dorsal scapular nerve, the rhomboid major with the thoracodorsal nerve, or the levator scapulae with the long thoracic nerve? The answer is the trapezius with the spinal accessory nerve. The rhomboid muscles and the levator scapulae take the dorsal scapular nerve, and the latissimus dorsi takes the thoracodorsal nerve.

Question 2740. A described photograph of a standing person shows a rounded prominence at the base of the neck, a midline furrow down the back, and two flat triangles of muscle sloping out to the shoulder blades. Which muscles are shown? The answer is the trapezius muscles, with the spine of the seventh cervical vertebra forming the prominence at the base of the neck.

Question 2741. Which movement does the latissimus dorsi produce at the shoulder: raising the arm forward, drawing the arm downward and backward and turning it inward, lifting the shoulder blade, or straightening the elbow? The answer is drawing the arm downward and backward and turning it inward. When the arms are fixed, the same muscle pulls the trunk upward.

Question 2742. Which deep muscles are made of short bundles that run from the transverse process of one vertebra to the lamina of the vertebra above and are best developed in the thoracic region? The answer is the rotatores. Each bundle spans one or two levels, and they are the deepest members of the transversospinalis group.

Question 2743. A described dissection of the back of the neck reflects the trapezius and the semispinalis capitis and exposes a small triangle at the base of the skull. What lies inside that triangle, and what are its three borders? The answer is the vertebral artery and the suboccipital nerve, bordered by the rectus capitis posterior major, the obliquus capitis superior, and the obliquus capitis inferior.

Question 2744. Through which muscle does the greater occipital nerve pass on its way to the skin of the scalp: the obliquus capitis inferior, the semispinalis capitis, the splenius cervicis, or the rectus capitis posterior minor? The answer is the semispinalis capitis. That nerve is the posterior ramus of the second cervical spinal nerve, and it also pierces the trapezius before it reaches the skin.

Question 2745. Which of these muscles belongs to the superficial group of the back: the trapezius, the multifidus, the rotatores, or the interspinales? The answer is the trapezius. The superficial muscles connect the shoulder girdle and the arm to the trunk, while the multifidus, the rotatores, and the interspinales are deep muscles of the back.

Question 2746. A constructed illustration: a described procedure on the back of the neck passes close to the upper surface of the first cervical vertebra, and afterwards the patient is unsteady and the back of the brain loses part of its blood supply. Which vessel lies in that position? The answer is the vertebral artery, whose third part lies in the suboccipital triangle above the posterior arch of the first cervical vertebra.

Question 2747. Which statement about the longissimus is correct: it lies between the iliocostalis and the spinalis, it lies medial to the spinalis, it lies superficial to the trapezius, or it belongs to the transversospinalis group? The answer is that it lies between the iliocostalis and the spinalis. It is the middle column of the erector spinae, and its capitis part reaches the mastoid process.

Question 2748. True or false: the upper fibers of the trapezius and its lower fibers pull the shoulder blade in opposite directions. The answer is true. The upper fibers lift the shoulder girdle while the lower fibers depress it, and when both act with the middle fibers they help to turn the shoulder blade upward for raising the arm.

Question 2749. Which slender muscle of the erector spinae runs from the upper thoracic transverse processes to the mastoid process and turns the head to the same side? The answer is the longissimus capitis. It lies deep to the splenius capitis in the neck and is often the smallest part of the longissimus.

Question 2750. Which thin muscle arises from the lower thoracic and upper lumbar spines and runs upward and outward to the lower four ribs? The answer is the serratus posterior inferior. Its fibers point upward, the reverse of the serratus posterior superior above it, and it is supplied by the lower intercostal nerves.

Question 2751. Which muscle arises from the upper thoracic spines and reaches the transverse processes of the upper cervical vertebrae: the splenius capitis, the splenius cervicis, the semispinalis cervicis, or the levator scapulae? The answer is the splenius cervicis. It lies below the splenius capitis and turns the neck to the same side.

Question 2752. Which small muscles lie between the transverse processes of neighboring vertebrae and are best developed in the cervical and lumbar regions? The answer is the intertransversarii. They bend the vertebral column to one side, and in the neck they are rich in proprioceptive fibers.

Question 2753. Which structure lies immediately lateral to the erector spinae in the lower chest and receives the lowest fibers of that muscle: the twelfth rib, the eleventh rib, the hip bone, or the peritoneum? The answer is the twelfth rib. The lowest fibers of the iliocostalis and the longissimus run to it, and it is short and easily felt in the back.

Question 2754. A described specimen shows four short muscles arranged around a triangle at the base of the skull, with one running from the spine of the second cervical vertebra to the transverse process of the first. Which muscle is that, and which border of the triangle does it form? The answer is the obliquus capitis inferior, forming the lower border.

Question 2755. Which action does the erector spinae perform when both sides contract together: bending the trunk forward, straightening the trunk and holding it upright, turning the head to one side only, or lifting the ribs? The answer is straightening the trunk and holding it upright. When only one side contracts, the trunk bends to that same side.

Question 2756. A constructed illustration: a described needle passes between the spines of the lower lumbar vertebrae and meets first a tough band, then a softer one, before the fluid is reached. Which two structures does it cross in that order? The answer is the supraspinous ligament and then the interspinous ligament. The strong yellow ligament lies deep to both.

Question 2757. Which muscle of the erector spinae runs from the spines of the lower thoracic and upper lumbar vertebrae up to the spines of the upper thoracic vertebrae? The answer is the spinalis thoracis. It is the medial column of the erector spinae and is best seen in the upper part of the back.

Question 2758. Which of these both lifts the shoulder blade and helps to turn it upward when the arm is raised: the levator scapulae alone, the upper fibers of the trapezius, the rhomboid minor, or the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the upper fibers of the trapezius. They work with the serratus anterior to turn the shoulder blade upward.

Question 2759. When a standing person bends the trunk to the left, in which plane does the movement take place, and what happens to the erector spinae on the right? The answer is the coronal plane, with the right erector spinae lengthening under control. The trunk moves from side to side in that plane while the spine turns about an axis running from front to back.

Question 2760. A described magnetic resonance imaging study of the lower back shows fat between the skin and the long muscles of the back, and a thin dark band wrapped around those muscles. Which structure is the dark band, and what does it enclose? The answer is the thoracolumbar fascia, which encloses the erector spinae.

Question 2761. Which of these pairs belongs together: the obliquus capitis inferior with turning the head to the same side, the rectus capitis posterior minor with turning the head, the obliquus capitis superior with lifting the shoulder, or the rectus capitis posterior major with bending the neck the other way? The answer is the obliquus capitis inferior with turning the head to the same side. The two rectus muscles extend the head.

Question 2762. Which of these lies deepest: the semispinalis capitis, the splenius capitis, the trapezius, or the skin of the back of the neck? The answer is the semispinalis capitis. The layers from the surface inward are the skin, the trapezius, the splenius capitis, and then the semispinalis capitis over the suboccipital muscles.

Question 2763. Which muscle runs from the spine of the second cervical vertebra to the transverse process of the first and turns the head to the same side? The answer is the obliquus capitis inferior. It is the only suboccipital muscle that does not attach to the skull, and it forms the lower border of the suboccipital triangle.

Question 2764. Which vessel lies in a groove on the upper surface of the posterior arch of the first cervical vertebra: the vertebral artery, the internal carotid artery, the occipital artery, or the external jugular vein? The answer is the vertebral artery. It turns inward and upward there to enter the skull through the foramen magnum.

Question 2765. What does the multifidus do when only the bundles of one segment contract: it extends the whole trunk, it steadies that single joint between the vertebrae, it lifts the ribs, or it turns the head? The answer is that it steadies that single joint. The short bundles give the vertebral column local control, while the long erector spinae supplies the power.

Question 2766. A constructed illustration: a person holds the head turned and tilted back for a long time, and afterwards has a deep ache at the base of the skull with tenderness over one small muscle there. Which suboccipital muscle is tender, and which nerve lies beside it? The answer is the obliquus capitis inferior, with the greater occipital nerve beside it.

Question 2767. A described dissection shows a thick sheet of fascia attached to the spines of the vertebrae and splitting to enclose a long muscle mass, with the latissimus dorsi attached to its outer surface. Which sheet is described, and which muscle does it enclose? The answer is the thoracolumbar fascia, enclosing the erector spinae.

Question 2768. Which of these small muscles lies between the transverse processes of two neighboring vertebrae rather than between their spines: the interspinales, the intertransversarii, the rotatores, or the multifidus? The answer is the intertransversarii. They are best developed in the neck and the lower back and bend the vertebral column to one side.

Question 2769. Which part of the erector spinae arises from the lower thoracic and lumbar region and inserts on the ribs and the transverse processes of the thoracic vertebrae? The answer is the longissimus thoracis. It is the largest part of the longissimus and lies between the iliocostalis and the spinalis.

Question 2770. Which statement separates the iliocostalis from the longissimus: the iliocostalis attaches to the angles of the ribs, the longissimus lies lateral to the iliocostalis, the iliocostalis reaches the mastoid process, or the longissimus attaches to the hip bone only? The answer is that the iliocostalis attaches to the angles of the ribs. The longissimus lies medial to it, and its capitis part reaches the mastoid process.

Question 2771. A described cross section at the middle of the chest shows the rib cage as a ring of bone, the vertebral body behind, and three columns of muscle between the transverse processes and the skin. What are those columns, from lateral to medial? The answer is the iliocostalis, the longissimus, and the spinalis. The multifidus lies deep to the spinalis.

Question 2772. Which nerve supplies the four suboccipital muscles: the posterior ramus of the first cervical spinal nerve, the posterior ramus of the second cervical spinal nerve, the spinal accessory nerve, or the dorsal scapular nerve? The answer is the posterior ramus of the first cervical spinal nerve, also called the suboccipital nerve. It has no branch to the skin.

Question 2773. Which part of the trapezius arises from the spines of the lower thoracic vertebrae and inserts on the spine of the shoulder blade? The answer is the lower fibers. They pull the shoulder blade downward and, with the upper fibers, help to turn it upward when the arm is raised.

Question 2774. Which muscle forms the lower border of the triangle of auscultation and the posterior border of the lumbar triangle at the same time? The answer is the latissimus dorsi. It forms one border of both gaps because it crosses the back on its way from the spine to the arm.

Question 2775. Which of these muscles is supplied by the posterior rami of the spinal nerves: the trapezius, the rhomboid major, the levator scapulae, or the multifidus? The answer is the multifidus. The first three are superficial muscles of the back and take anterior rami, while all the deep muscles of the back take posterior rami.

Question 2776. A constructed illustration: a person has pain in the lower back that worsens on standing and eases on lying down, and the tenderness lies over the thick fascial sheet beside the spine. Which structures are involved in this described picture? The answer is the thoracolumbar fascia and the erector spinae that it encloses. That fascia anchors muscle to the spine and the hip bone.

Question 2777. Which muscle covers the widest area of the back and gives the armpit its posterior fold: the trapezius, the latissimus dorsi, the rhomboid major, or the serratus posterior inferior? The answer is the latissimus dorsi. It runs from the lower spine, the hip bone, and the lower ribs up to the front of the upper arm.

Question 2778. A described specimen of the lower back shows a thick sheet of fascia with a flat muscle attached to its outer surface and a small triangular gap just below that muscle at the edge of the back. What is the gap, and which muscle borders it above? The answer is the lumbar triangle, with the latissimus dorsi forming its upper and posterior border.

Question 2779. Which muscle of the erector spinae group is the longest, running from the sacrum and the hip bone as far up as the mastoid process? The answer is the longissimus. Its capitis part reaches the mastoid process, and its thoracis part is the largest of the three columns.

Question 2780. True or false: when the erector spinae on one side contracts alone, the trunk bends to the opposite side. The answer is false. The trunk bends to the same side as the contracting muscle, and when both columns work together they straighten the back and hold it upright against gravity.

Question 2781. Which of these pairs belongs together: the rectus capitis posterior minor with the posterior tubercle of the first cervical vertebra, the rectus capitis posterior major with the spine of the third cervical vertebra, the obliquus capitis superior with the spine of the second cervical vertebra, or the obliquus capitis inferior with the occipital bone? The answer is the rectus capitis posterior minor with the posterior tubercle of the first cervical vertebra. The major arises from the spine of the second.

Question 2782. Which statement about the deep muscles of the back is correct: the multifidus is supplied by the anterior rami, the rotatores span three or four levels each, the semispinalis capitis is supplied by the second cervical nerve, or the suboccipital muscles are supplied by the first cervical nerve? The answer is that the suboccipital muscles are supplied by the first cervical nerve. All the deep muscles of the back take posterior rami.

Question 2783. Which muscle of the transversospinalis group arises from the transverse processes of the lower thoracic vertebrae and reaches the spines of the upper thoracic and lower cervical vertebrae? The answer is the semispinalis thoracis. Its fibers cross four to six levels, more than the bundles of the multifidus below it.

Question 2784. A described section through the lower back shows the twelfth rib as a short bar of bone beside the spine, with muscle fibers running up to it from below and a large muscle mass on each side of the midline. Which muscle reaches that rib, and which mass lies beside the spine? The answer is the iliocostalis, beside the erector spinae.

Question 2785. A constructed illustration: a wound in the lower back, above the hip bone and behind the edge of the latissimus dorsi, allows a loop of bowel to appear beneath the skin. Which gap has it passed through, and what forms the floor of that gap? The answer is the lumbar triangle, whose floor is the internal abdominal oblique.

Question 2786. Which structure lies immediately below the vertebral artery within the suboccipital triangle: the suboccipital nerve, the greater occipital nerve, the spinal accessory nerve, or the occipital artery? The answer is the suboccipital nerve. It is the posterior ramus of the first cervical spinal nerve and runs between the artery and the posterior arch of the first cervical vertebra.

Question 2787. Which muscle arises from the spines of the seventh cervical and the first thoracic vertebrae and inserts on the medial border of the shoulder blade at the level of its spine? The answer is the rhomboid minor. The rhomboid major lies below it and reaches further down the same border.

Question 2788. Which muscle arises from the lower thoracic and upper lumbar spines and runs upward and outward to the lower ribs: the serratus posterior superior, the serratus posterior inferior, the levatores costarum, or the iliocostalis? The answer is the serratus posterior inferior. Its fibers point upward, and it takes its nerves from the lower intercostal nerves.

Question 2789. Name the three columns of the erector spinae, spoken as a list from lateral to medial. The answer is the iliocostalis, the longissimus, and the spinalis. Each column is named again for the region it occupies, as in iliocostalis lumborum or longissimus capitis.

Question 2790. Which of these lies in the deepest layer of the muscles of the back: the levatores costarum, the multifidus, the semispinalis thoracis, or the splenius capitis? The answer is the levatores costarum. The deepest layer also holds the interspinales and the intertransversarii, while the splenius lies in the first layer.

Question 2791. A described dissection of the side of the neck shows a thin muscle running from the upper thoracic spines up and out to the transverse processes of the upper cervical vertebrae, lying deep to the larger muscle that reaches the mastoid process. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the splenius cervicis, deep to the splenius capitis.

Question 2792. Which muscle forms the lateral border of the suboccipital triangle and runs from the transverse process of the first cervical vertebra up to the occipital bone? The answer is the obliquus capitis superior. It extends the head and bends it to the same side.

Question 2793. The greater occipital nerve lies immediately below which muscle, and between which two bones does that muscle run? The answer is the obliquus capitis inferior, which runs between the spine of the second cervical vertebra and the transverse process of the first. The nerve turns around the lower border of that muscle on its way to the scalp.

Question 2794. A constructed illustration: a surgeon plans to move a broad muscle of the back to repair a defect on the chest wall and must keep its nerve intact. Which nerve must be preserved, and where does it run? The answer is the thoracodorsal nerve, which runs down the back wall of the armpit to the deep surface of the latissimus dorsi.

Question 2795. The bundles of the multifidus run upward and medially from one vertebra to another. Which way do they face in a transverse section, and do they cross the midline? The answer is that they lie in the groove beside the spine and never cross the midline. Each bundle spans two to four vertebrae in the lower back.

Question 2796. Which of these pairs belongs together: the trapezius with the eleventh cranial nerve, the latissimus dorsi with the dorsal scapular nerve, the multifidus with an anterior ramus, or the levator scapulae with the thoracodorsal nerve? The answer is the trapezius with the eleventh cranial nerve. The multifidus takes a posterior ramus, and the latissimus dorsi takes the thoracodorsal nerve.

Question 2797. Which muscle of the back is attached to the base of the skull, the ligamentum nuchae, and the spines of the lower cervical and all the thoracic vertebrae? The answer is the trapezius. Its fibers converge on the outer third of the collarbone, the acromion, and the spine of the shoulder blade.

Question 2798. A described image of the back of the neck, made with magnetic resonance imaging, shows a small nerve passing around the lower edge of a short muscle that runs from the spine of the second cervical vertebra to the first. Which muscle and which nerve are shown? The answer is the obliquus capitis inferior and the greater occipital nerve, which then pierces the semispinalis capitis.

Question 2799. Name two muscles of the deep group of the back that extend the head at the joint between the skull and the first cervical vertebra. The answer is the semispinalis capitis and the splenius capitis. The rectus capitis posterior major and minor and the longissimus capitis also extend the head.

Question 2800. True or false: when the arms are fixed, the latissimus dorsi can raise the trunk. The answer is true. In climbing or on crutches, the muscle pulls the trunk up toward the fixed arm, and it is one of the strongest muscles of the back.

To summarize, the superficial muscles of the back connect the shoulder girdle and the arm to the trunk and are supplied by anterior rami: the trapezius by the eleventh cranial nerve, the latissimus dorsi by the thoracodorsal nerve, and the rhomboid muscles and the levator scapulae by the dorsal scapular nerve. The deep muscles of the back are supplied by the posterior rami of the spinal nerves and are arranged in layers, the splenius muscles first, then the erector spinae, then the transversospinalis group of semispinalis, multifidus and rotatores, and last the deepest layer of levatores costarum, interspinales and intertransversarii. The erector spinae rises from the sacrum, the hip bone, and the thoracolumbar fascia as one mass and splits into the iliocostalis, the longissimus, and the spinalis, which straighten the trunk and bend it to the same side. At the base of the skull the four suboccipital muscles bound the suboccipital triangle, which holds the vertebral artery and the suboccipital nerve, while the greater occipital nerve winds below the obliquus capitis inferior. The triangle of auscultation and the lumbar triangle are the two named gaps of the back, and each is defined by muscles met in this chapter.
