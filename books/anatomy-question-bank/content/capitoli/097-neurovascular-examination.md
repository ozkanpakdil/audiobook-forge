# Chapter 97 — Neurovascular Examination

This chapter joins two subjects that meet at the bedside: the examination that maps nerves and vessels onto the living body, and the bony injuries that so often damage them. The first half covers the pulses, the named nerves, the reflexes and the areas of skin a clinician tests by hand, and the second half covers the fractures and dislocations of the limbs, the girdles and the spine. It holds one hundred questions, and every one answers immediately, then gives the reason. This is educational material, and it does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9601. Which artery is felt on the thumb side of the front of the wrist, where the pulse is usually taken? The answer is the radial artery. It lies just lateral to the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis, covered only by skin and fascia. The ulnar artery lies on the little finger side and is harder to feel.

Question 9602. Which vessel is pressed against the shaft of the humerus to control bleeding from a wound of the upper limb: the axillary artery, the brachial artery, the radial artery, or the ulnar artery? The answer is the brachial artery, which runs down the medial side of the arm close to the bone. The radial and the ulnar arteries lie below the elbow.

Question 9603. A described recording shows a strong pulse behind the medial malleolus and a weaker one on the top of the foot. Which vessels are these? The answer is the posterior tibial artery behind the malleolus and the dorsalis pedis artery on the top of the foot, which lies beside the tendon of the extensor hallucis longus. The dorsalis pedis pulse is absent in some healthy people.

Question 9604. Which nerve lies in a shallow groove behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus, where a knock sends tingling into the little finger? The answer is the ulnar nerve. Only skin and fascia cover it there, which is why the spot is called the funny bone. The tingling is felt in the little finger and half of the ring finger.

Question 9605. Which nerve passes through the carpal tunnel deep to the flexor retinaculum, and which muscles of the thumb does it supply? The answer is the median nerve, which supplies the thenar muscles that move the thumb away from the palm and oppose it. It also supplies the first two lumbricals. The ulnar nerve enters the hand outside the tunnel.

Question 9606. Which nerve supplies the deltoid muscle and the skin over the lower shoulder: the axillary nerve, the radial nerve, the suprascapular nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the axillary nerve, which winds around the surgical neck of the humerus and also supplies the teres minor. The suprascapular nerve supplies the supraspinatus and the infraspinatus.

Question 9607. A constructed illustration: after a described fracture of the middle of the humerus, the wrist hangs, the fingers cannot be straightened, and the back of the hand is numb. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the radial nerve, which lies in the spiral groove against the bone and supplies the muscles that straighten the wrist and the fingers.

Question 9608. True or false: the ankle reflex tests the first sacral segment of the spinal cord. The answer is true. Tapping the tendon behind the ankle makes the calf muscles contract, and the impulse travels through the tibial nerve and the first sacral root. Loss of that reflex is an early sign of pressure on the root.

Question 9609. Which nerve winds around the neck of the fibula, where it can be felt as a firm cord just under the skin? The answer is the common fibular nerve. It divides into superficial and deep branches that supply the outer side and the front of the leg. Injury there causes a foot drop.

Question 9610. A described dissection of the back of the knee shows a nerve on the medial side, another nerve on the lateral side, and a large vessel deepest of all. Which three structures are shown in that order? The answer is the tibial nerve medially, the common fibular nerve laterally and the popliteal artery deepest, with the popliteal vein between the artery and the nerves.

Question 9611. Which of these structures lies deepest in the back of the knee: the tibial nerve, the popliteal artery, the small saphenous vein, or the common fibular nerve? The answer is the popliteal artery, which lies against the bone and the back of the joint. The two nerves lie nearer the surface, and the small saphenous vein runs in the fat under the skin.

Question 9612. Which structure lies immediately behind the medial malleolus, in company with the tibial nerve and the tendons of the deep muscles of the leg? The answer is the posterior tibial artery. It lies between the tendon of the flexor digitorum longus and the tendon of the flexor hallucis longus. The order from the medial side is tendon, tendon, artery, nerve, tendon.

Question 9613. A constructed illustration: a person describes tingling in the thumb, the index finger and the middle finger that wakes them at night, while the little finger is spared. Which nerve is compressed, and where? The answer is the median nerve, at the carpal tunnel beneath the flexor retinaculum. The little finger is spared because the ulnar nerve supplies it.

Question 9614. Which nerve lies lateral to the femoral artery in the femoral triangle and supplies the quadriceps muscle? The answer is the femoral nerve, which enters the thigh behind the inguinal ligament and also carries feeling from the front of the thigh. One of its branches, the saphenous nerve, runs down the medial side of the leg.

Question 9615. Which nerve carries the knee reflex: the femoral nerve, the sciatic nerve, the obturator nerve, or the tibial nerve? The answer is the femoral nerve, which carries the impulse through the third and fourth lumbar segments. Tapping the tendon below the kneecap makes the quadriceps contract. The ankle reflex travels through the tibial nerve instead.

Question 9616. Name the two nerves that supply the small muscles of the hand, and say which fingers each one serves. The answer is the median nerve and the ulnar nerve. The median nerve supplies the thenar muscles and the first two lumbricals and carries feeling from the thumb, the index finger, the middle finger and half of the ring finger, while the ulnar nerve supplies the rest.

Question 9617. A described dissection of the front of the forearm shows one nerve running down the middle between the two groups of muscles and another running down the medial side beside an artery. Which nerves are shown? The answer is the median nerve in the middle and the ulnar nerve medially, with the ulnar artery beside it. The median nerve lies deep to the flexor carpi radialis.

Question 9618. A constructed illustration: a deep wound over the front of the elbow divides one nerve, and the person can no longer bring the tips of the thumb and the index finger together. Which nerve is divided? The answer is the median nerve, through its anterior interosseous branch, which supplies the flexor pollicis longus and part of the flexor digitorum profundus.

Question 9619. Which nerve leaves the pelvis below the piriformis muscle and runs down the back of the thigh? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It is the largest nerve in the body, and it divides into the tibial and the common fibular nerves. It supplies the muscles of the back of the thigh before that division.

Question 9620. Which of these pairs belongs together: the biceps reflex with the radial nerve, the triceps reflex with the musculocutaneous nerve, the knee reflex with the femoral nerve, or the ankle reflex with the obturator nerve? The answer is the knee reflex with the femoral nerve. The biceps reflex runs through the musculocutaneous nerve and the triceps reflex through the radial nerve.

Question 9621. Which nerve supplies the diaphragm: the phrenic nerve, the vagus nerve, an intercostal nerve, or the sympathetic trunk? The answer is the phrenic nerve, which arises from the third, fourth and fifth cervical segments and runs down through the neck and the chest. The intercostal nerves supply the muscles between the ribs.

Question 9622. In a described cross section of the middle of the arm, the humerus lies at the center with a nerve in a groove on its back and the main artery on its medial side. Which nerve and which artery are those? The answer is the radial nerve in the spiral groove and the brachial artery medial to the bone. The nerve passes between the two heads of the triceps.

Question 9623. Which nerve lies immediately medial to the brachial artery in the cubital fossa, after crossing in front of the artery higher in the arm? The answer is the median nerve, which enters the forearm between the two heads of the pronator teres. The brachial artery divides into the radial and the ulnar arteries just below that point.

Question 9624. Which nerve supplies the adductor muscles of the thigh: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the inferior gluteal nerve? The answer is the obturator nerve, which comes from the second, third and fourth lumbar segments and leaves the pelvis through the obturator canal. It also carries feeling from the inner side of the thigh.

Question 9625. Which of these muscles is supplied by the ulnar nerve: the abductor pollicis brevis, the opponens pollicis, the first dorsal interosseous, or the superficial head of the flexor pollicis brevis? The answer is the first dorsal interosseous. The ulnar nerve supplies most of the small muscles of the hand, while the thenar muscles are supplied mainly by the median nerve.

Question 9626. A described study of the back of the leg shows a nerve lying against the tibia deep to the soleus muscle, alongside the posterior tibial vessels. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the tibial nerve, which supplies the muscles of the back of the leg. It passes behind the medial malleolus and divides into the medial and lateral plantar nerves.

Question 9627. A constructed illustration: a wound on the little finger side of the wrist divides one nerve, and the person can no longer grip a sheet of paper between the fingers. Which nerve is divided? The answer is the ulnar nerve, which supplies the interosseous muscles that hold the fingers together and the muscle that pulls the thumb across the palm.

Question 9628. True or false: in the hand, the superficial branch of the radial nerve carries feeling but supplies no muscle. The answer is true. It supplies the back of the hand and the first web space. The deep branch is the one that supplies the extensor muscles of the forearm.

Question 9629. Which nerve pierces the coracobrachialis muscle and then runs between the biceps and the brachialis to supply both of them? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve, which continues below the elbow as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm. It arises from the lateral cord of the brachial plexus.

Question 9630. In a described examination the triceps reflex is lost and the wrist extensors are weak, while the biceps reflex is normal. Which spinal segment is involved: the fifth cervical, the sixth cervical, the seventh cervical, or the eighth cervical? The answer is the seventh cervical segment, which serves the triceps reflex and the wrist extensors. The biceps reflex belongs mainly to the fifth and sixth segments.

Question 9631. The phrenic nerve runs down the front of the scalenus anterior muscle, while the trunks of the brachial plexus and the subclavian artery pass behind it. Which lies in front and which behind? The answer is the phrenic nerve in front and the plexus with the artery behind. The subclavian vein crosses in front of the same muscle, lower down.

Question 9632. Which of these pairs belongs together: the axillary nerve with winging of the scapula, the long thoracic nerve with the deltoid, the spinal accessory nerve with the trapezius, or the suprascapular nerve with the rhomboids? The answer is the spinal accessory nerve with the trapezius. The long thoracic nerve supplies the serratus anterior, and its injury produces winging of the scapula.

Question 9633. Name three nerves that are compressed where they lie against bone, one at the elbow, one at the wrist and one at the neck of the fibula. The answer is the ulnar nerve at the medial epicondyle of the humerus, the median nerve at the carpal tunnel and the common fibular nerve at the neck of the fibula. Each lies just under the skin against a hard surface.

Question 9634. Which nerve carries feeling from the front of the thigh and continues down the medial side of the leg as the saphenous nerve? The answer is the femoral nerve, which comes from the second, third and fourth lumbar segments and enters the thigh lateral to the femoral artery. Its saphenous branch reaches almost to the great toe.

Question 9635. Which of these tests examines the first sacral nerve root: the knee reflex, the ankle reflex, the biceps reflex, or the abdominal reflex? The answer is the ankle reflex, which is lost when the first sacral root is compressed, often before any weakness appears. The reflex travels through the tibial nerve.

Question 9636. A described photograph of a person pushing both arms forward shows the right scapula lifting away from the chest wall like a wing. Which nerve is paralyzed? The answer is the long thoracic nerve, which supplies the serratus anterior, the muscle that holds the scapula flat against the ribs. The spinal accessory nerve supplies the trapezius instead.

Question 9637. Which nerve carries feeling from the little finger, the medial half of the ring finger and the medial border of the hand? The answer is the ulnar nerve, which passes behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus and then through a channel at the wrist outside the carpal tunnel. Its superficial branch divides into palmar and dorsal branches for those fingers.

Question 9638. A described examination finds that the elbow can be bent but not straightened against resistance, the triceps reflex is absent, and feeling is reduced over the back of the forearm. Which nerve is affected: the musculocutaneous, the radial, the median, or the ulnar nerve? The answer is the radial nerve, which supplies the triceps and the skin of the back of the forearm.

Question 9639. A constructed illustration: a child falls with the arm outstretched and breaks the bone just above the elbow, and the hand is pale and the fingers hurt when they are straightened. Which artery is at risk, and which nerve is usually injured with it? The answer is the brachial artery, with the median nerve, because both lie in front of the lower humerus.

Question 9640. Within the carotid sheath, which structure lies lateral to the common carotid artery, and which lies between and behind the artery and the vein? The answer is the internal jugular vein laterally and the vagus nerve between and behind them. The sympathetic trunk lies behind the sheath, not inside it.

Question 9641. Which nerve supplies the gluteus maximus muscle and the skin over it? The answer is the inferior gluteal nerve, which comes from the fifth lumbar and the first and second sacral segments. It leaves the pelvis below the piriformis muscle and below the sciatic nerve, and it supplies that one muscle, which straightens the hip.

Question 9642. A described examination finds that the person cannot spread the fingers apart or pull the little finger across the palm, but the thumb can still be opposed. Which nerve is affected: the median, the ulnar, the radial, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the ulnar nerve. Spreading the fingers is the work of the interosseous muscles, which the ulnar nerve supplies.

Question 9643. A described dissection at the root of the neck shows five nerve roots emerging between two muscles and joining into three trunks above the collarbone. Which plexus is shown? The answer is the brachial plexus, and its roots pass between the scalenus anterior and the scalenus medius. The upper two roots join first, and the lower three join together.

Question 9644. True or false: the radial nerve supplies the triceps muscle and also carries feeling from the skin over the back of the hand. The answer is true. In the arm it supplies the triceps and the other muscles that straighten the elbow, and its superficial branch supplies the back of the hand.

Question 9645. Which statement about the arteries of the hand is correct: the superficial palmar arch is formed mainly by the ulnar artery, the deep palmar arch is formed mainly by the ulnar artery, both arches are formed by the radial artery, or both arches lie at the same level? The answer is that the superficial arch is formed mainly by the ulnar artery, while the deep arch is formed mainly by the radial artery.

Question 9646. Which muscle is tested by asking a person to stand on tiptoe: the tibialis anterior, the gastrocnemius, the quadriceps, or the gluteus medius? The answer is the gastrocnemius, which is supplied by the tibial nerve through the first and second sacral segments. Standing on the heels tests the tibialis anterior instead.

Question 9647. Which artery is felt deep in the back of the knee, where it lies against the popliteal surface of the femur? The answer is the popliteal artery, the continuation of the femoral artery, which divides below the knee into the anterior and the posterior tibial arteries. Its pulse is easiest to feel with the knee slightly bent.

Question 9648. A described examination maps loss of pain sensation over the index finger and the middle finger. Which spinal segment is most likely involved: the sixth cervical, the seventh cervical, the eighth cervical, or the first thoracic? The answer is the seventh cervical segment, whose area of skin includes the middle finger. The little finger marks the eighth cervical segment.

Question 9649. A described examination finds that the elbow cannot be straightened, the triceps reflex is absent, and the wrist and fingers are weak. Which lesion explains this: a radial nerve lesion in the armpit, a posterior interosseous nerve lesion, an ulnar nerve lesion, or a median nerve lesion? The answer is a radial lesion in the armpit, because it lies above the branches to the triceps and the extensors.

Question 9650. Which finding best distinguishes a lesion of the median nerve at the wrist from one higher in the arm: weakness of the thumb muscles alone, weakness of the thumb muscles with weakness of the long muscles of the forearm, loss of feeling in the little finger, or weakness of the muscles that straighten the fingers? The answer is the second finding, because a lesion above the elbow also weakens the forearm muscles.

Question 9651. Which fracture of the lower end of the radius drives the distal fragment backward and produces a dinner fork shape when the wrist is seen from the side? The answer is the Colles fracture, whose break lies about 2 to 3 centimeters above the wrist joint. The radius carries the hand at the wrist, so the deformity follows its displacement.

Question 9652. Which structure is most often injured with a fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus: the axillary nerve, the radial nerve, the median nerve, or the ulnar nerve? The answer is the axillary nerve, which winds around the surgical neck close to the bone on its way to the deltoid. The radial nerve lies against the shaft instead.

Question 9653. A described radiograph of the wrist shows a break across the waist of a small bone at the base of the thumb. Which bone is broken, and why does that matter? The answer is the scaphoid. Its blood supply enters at the far end, so a break through the waist can cut off the supply to the nearer fragment.

Question 9654. Which artery is at risk in a fracture of the neck of the femur, and which part of the bone does it supply? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral artery, which supplies most of the head of the femur. Its branches run along the neck in folds of the capsule and are stretched when the neck is displaced, so the bone can die.

Question 9655. Which injury of the ankle is produced by forcible turning of the foot outward, with a break of the lateral malleolus together with a break of the medial malleolus or a tear of the ligament on the inner side? The answer is a bimalleolar fracture, also called a Pott fracture. The talus is driven against the malleoli, and the foot is displaced outward.

Question 9656. Which of these fractures is most likely to injure the common fibular nerve: a fracture of the neck of the fibula, a fracture of the shaft of the tibia, a fracture of the medial malleolus, or a fracture of the calcaneus? The answer is a fracture of the neck of the fibula, where the nerve lies just under the skin. Injury there causes a foot drop.

Question 9657. A constructed illustration: a described dislocation of the knee shows the tibia displaced backward on the femur, and no pulse can be felt at the ankle. Which structure is most at risk? The answer is the popliteal artery, which is tethered between the opening in the adductor muscle above and the arch of the soleus below, so it tears when the joint is displaced.

Question 9658. Which of these pairs belongs together: the Colles fracture with the lower end of the radius, the Smith fracture with the neck of the femur, the Bennett fracture with the distal phalanx of the thumb, or the Jones fracture with the first metatarsal? The answer is the Colles fracture with the lower end of the radius. The Smith fracture is also a break of the radius but drives the fragment forward.

Question 9659. Which bone is the most commonly broken in the body, and which way does its inner fragment move when it breaks in the middle? The answer is the clavicle, whose inner fragment is pulled upward by the sternocleidomastoid muscle. The outer fragment drops with the weight of the arm, and the break usually lies in the middle third.

Question 9660. Which fracture endangers the artery that supplies the body of the talus: a fracture of the neck of the talus, a fracture of the calcaneus, a fracture of the fifth metatarsal, or a fracture of the first metatarsal? The answer is a fracture of the neck of the talus. The artery of the tarsal canal enters from below and behind, so a displaced break can cause death of the bone.

Question 9661. A described radiograph of the foot shows a break across the fifth metatarsal just beyond the bony prominence at its base. Which fracture is shown, and why is it slow to heal? The answer is a Jones fracture, at the junction of the base and the shaft, where the blood supply is poor. The tendon of the peroneus brevis pulls on the base.

Question 9662. Which structures lie immediately in front of the hip joint and can be injured by a dislocation that drives the head of the femur forward? The answer is the femoral nerve and the femoral artery, which lie in the femoral triangle on the front of the joint. A posterior dislocation threatens the sciatic nerve behind the joint instead.

Question 9663. Which pelvic injury is most likely to tear the membranous part of the urethra: a break of the iliac wing, a break of the pubic rami with separation of the symphysis, a break of the sacrum, or a break of the acetabulum? The answer is a break of the pubic rami with separation of the symphysis. The membranous urethra is fixed to the pubic bone and is torn by the displacement.

Question 9664. Which fracture of the first cervical vertebra results from a blow on the top of the head and splits the ring into several parts? The answer is the Jefferson fracture. The force drives the occipital condyles down between the two lateral masses and bursts the ring outward, and the cord is often spared because the canal is wide there.

Question 9665. A constructed illustration: a person who fell from a height has a break through the pedicles of the second cervical vertebra on both sides, with the body displaced forward on the third. Which fracture is described? The answer is a hangman fracture. The displacement narrows the canal and can injure the cord.

Question 9666. True or false: a break through the peg of bone that rises from the body of the second cervical vertebra is stable, because the ring of the first vertebra holds the fragment in place. The answer is false. The fragment stays with the first vertebra while the rest of the second moves away, and the cord lies close behind it.

Question 9667. Which fracture runs horizontally through the body of a vertebra and through its posterior elements, as happens in a sudden stop when a seat belt acts as a fulcrum: a Chance fracture, a burst fracture, a wedge fracture, or a Jefferson fracture? The answer is a Chance fracture. The spine is pulled apart from behind and crushed in front.

Question 9668. Which of these pairs belongs together: a fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus with the axillary nerve, a supracondylar fracture with the radial nerve, a fracture of the medial epicondyle with the median nerve, or a fracture of the calcaneus with the femoral nerve? The answer is the surgical neck with the axillary nerve. A supracondylar fracture endangers the median nerve.

Question 9669. Which injury combines a fracture of the proximal third of the ulna with a dislocation of the head of the radius? The answer is the Monteggia injury. The head of the radius dislocates forward in most cases, and if only the ulna is treated the dislocation is missed.

Question 9670. Which injury combines a fracture of the distal third of the radius with a dislocation of the joint between the two bones of the forearm: a Galeazzi injury, a Monteggia injury, a Colles fracture, or a Smith fracture? The answer is a Galeazzi injury. The radial fragment shortens and the lower end of the ulna rides up beside it.

Question 9671. A described radiograph of the elbow of a child shows a small piece of bone inside the joint on the inner side, and the medial epicondyle is missing from its place. Which injury is shown, and which nerve is at risk? The answer is a displaced medial epicondyle trapped in the joint, with the ulnar nerve, which lies against its back.

Question 9672. In a described burst fracture of a vertebral body, which structure lies immediately behind the bone and is compressed when fragments are driven backward? The answer is the spinal cord. In the cervical region the canal is wide, so the cord may escape, but in the thoracic region the canal is narrow and the cord is compressed more easily.

Question 9673. A constructed illustration: after a fracture of the shaft of the tibia a person has severe pain in the front of the leg that worsens when the toes are stretched, and the leg feels tense. Which compartment is involved, and which nerve runs through it? The answer is the anterior compartment and the deep branch of the common fibular nerve.

Question 9674. Which fracture of the heel bone is produced by a fall onto the foot, and which part of the bone is crushed? The answer is a crush fracture of the body of the calcaneus, which is driven up between the talus above and the ground below. The angle between the long axis of the bone and its upper surface is flattened.

Question 9675. Which statement about the blood supply of the head of the femur is correct: the artery inside the ligament of the head supplies most of it, the medial circumflex femoral artery supplies most of it, the lateral circumflex femoral artery supplies most of it, or the obturator artery supplies all of it? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral artery.

Question 9676. A described radiograph of the shoulder shows the head of the humerus lying below and medial to the empty socket. Which dislocation is shown, and which nerve is most often injured with it? The answer is an anterior dislocation, with the axillary nerve, which winds around the surgical neck and is stretched by the displacement.

Question 9677. Which of these findings on a described radiograph suggests a posterior dislocation of the shoulder: the head lies below and medial to the socket, the head lies behind the socket and the arm cannot be rotated outward, the shaft of the humerus is broken, or the coracoid process is displaced? The answer is that the head lies behind the socket and the arm cannot be rotated outward.

Question 9678. Which structure is torn in the common athletic dislocation of the shoulder that leaves the joint unstable at the front, and what is the injury to the rim of the socket called? The answer is the glenoid labrum, the fibrous rim of the socket, which is torn away from the bone at the front. The head of the humerus is often dented at the back as well.

Question 9679. A constructed illustration: a fall onto the point of the shoulder leaves a step at the outer end of the clavicle, which rides above the spine of the scapula. Which joint is injured? The answer is the acromioclavicular joint, whose ligaments are torn. If the ligaments between the clavicle and the coracoid process tear as well, the displacement is greater.

Question 9680. Which of these pairs belongs together: the dinner fork shape with the Colles fracture, the dinner fork shape with the Smith fracture, the garden spade shape with the Colles fracture, or the pistol grip shape with the Smith fracture? The answer is the dinner fork shape with the Colles fracture. The Smith fracture drives the fragment forward instead of backward.

Question 9681. In a described radiograph of the elbow, a thin dark line in front of the lower humerus is lifted away from the bone by a small collection of fluid. What does that mean, and which injury does it suggest? The answer is that fluid in the joint has pushed the fat pad forward, which suggests an undisplaced fracture of the lower humerus.

Question 9682. Which of these bones is dislocated when a described radiograph of the wrist shows it turned over, with the capitate no longer sitting on it: the scaphoid, the lunate, the triquetrum, or the hamate? The answer is the lunate, which is driven forward and loses contact with the capitate. The displaced bone often presses on the median nerve.

Question 9683. Which structures pass immediately behind the middle of the clavicle and can be injured by a fracture at the inner end of that bone? The answer is the subclavian artery and the trunks of the brachial plexus, which pass between the clavicle and the first rib. A break of the middle third usually spares them, because the muscles hold the fragments apart.

Question 9684. Which injury breaks the fibula high above the ankle while the tibia remains whole, together with a tear of the ligaments between the two bones? The answer is a Maisonneuve injury, which follows a twisting force that turns the foot outward. The whole length of the fibula must be examined, because the break lies far from the joint that hurts.

Question 9685. Which of these fractures is most likely to be missed on a first radiograph: a fracture of the waist of the scaphoid, a fracture of the shaft of the femur, a fracture of the lower end of the radius, or a fracture of the clavicle? The answer is a fracture of the waist of the scaphoid, because the bone overlaps its neighbors and the break may be invisible for days.

Question 9686. A described radiograph of the lumbar spine shows a vertebral body that is narrower at the front than at the back, with the end plates pressed together. Which fracture is shown, and which part of the vertebra is spared? The answer is a wedge compression fracture of the body. The posterior elements are spared, so the canal is not narrowed.

Question 9687. A constructed illustration: the knee strikes the dashboard in a sudden stop, the hip is dislocated backward, the foot is weak and feeling is lost over the back of the thigh. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the sciatic nerve, which lies immediately behind the hip joint and supplies all the muscles below the knee.

Question 9688. Which fracture of the upper femur lies between the neck and the greater trochanter and heals better than a break of the neck? The answer is an intertrochanteric fracture, which lies outside the capsule of the joint in bone with a good blood supply. A break of the neck lies inside the capsule, and the vessels along it are often torn.

Question 9689. Which fracture of the humerus is most likely to injure the radial nerve: a fracture of the head, a fracture of the shaft, a fracture of the medial epicondyle, or a fracture of the capitulum? The answer is a fracture of the shaft, because the radial nerve runs in the spiral groove against the bone. The medial epicondyle threatens the ulnar nerve instead.

Question 9690. Which fracture of the hand is produced by striking a hard object with a closed fist, and which bone is broken? The answer is a fracture of the neck of the fifth metacarpal, whose head is driven forward and whose finger is shortened. The knuckle at the base of the little finger is lost when the fist is closed.

Question 9691. Which fracture of the thumb runs through the base of the first metacarpal and into the joint, leaving a small fragment in place while the shaft is pulled away: a Bennett fracture, a Rolando fracture, a Jones fracture, or a gamekeeper thumb? The answer is a Bennett fracture. The small inner fragment stays with the trapezium because a ligament holds it.

Question 9692. Which artery passes through the opening in the membrane between the tibia and the fibula just below the knee and then runs down the front of the leg? The answer is the anterior tibial artery, which a fracture of the upper shaft of the tibia can injure. It continues onto the top of the foot as the dorsalis pedis artery.

Question 9693. A constructed illustration: a break through the neck of the talus is treated, and months later the person still has pain and the body of the bone looks dense on a described radiograph. Which vessel was interrupted? The answer is the artery of the tarsal canal, which enters the bone from below and behind.

Question 9694. Which bone of the leg carries the weight of the body, so that a break of it is a serious injury, and which bone lies beside it? The answer is the tibia, with the fibula beside it. The tibia is the thicker and more medial bone and forms the ankle joint with the talus, while the fibula carries little weight.

Question 9695. Which of these dislocations most often damages the artery that supplies the head of the femur: a posterior dislocation of the hip, an anterior dislocation of the shoulder, a dislocation of the kneecap, or a dislocation of the head of the radius? The answer is a posterior dislocation of the hip, which stretches the medial circumflex femoral artery.

Question 9696. A described radiograph of the wrist of a child shows a break that runs through the growth plate and out through the shaft of the bone. Which structure is involved, and what does that mean for growth? The answer is the growth plate of the lower radius. A break that crosses the plate can disturb growth and leave the arm short.

Question 9697. Which of these complications follows a fracture of the neck of the femur more often than a break between the trochanters: death of the head of the femur, infection of the bone, shortening of the leg, or injury to the sciatic nerve? The answer is death of the head of the femur, because the break lies inside the capsule and tears the vessels along the neck.

Question 9698. Which of these statements about the displacement in a Colles fracture is correct: the distal fragment is displaced backward and carries the hand with it, the distal fragment is displaced forward, the proximal fragment is displaced backward, or the fragments do not displace? The answer is that the distal fragment is displaced backward, which produces the dinner fork shape.

Question 9699. Which of these injuries of the knee is most often accompanied by injury to the popliteal artery: a dislocation of the knee, a tear of the medial meniscus, a tear of the anterior cruciate ligament, or a dislocation of the kneecap? The answer is a dislocation of the knee. The artery is fixed above and below the joint, so it tears when the joint is displaced.

Question 9700. Which structure lies immediately behind the body of a lumbar vertebra and is at risk from a burst fracture at that level? The answer is the cauda equina, the bundle of nerve roots that fills the canal below the first lumbar vertebra. Above that level the same space holds the spinal cord.

To summarize, the radial pulse is felt on the thumb side of the wrist, and the ulnar nerve lies behind the medial epicondyle, the median nerve in the carpal tunnel, the common fibular nerve at the neck of the fibula and the axillary nerve around the surgical neck of the humerus. Reflexes map onto segments, with the knee reflex through the femoral nerve and the ankle reflex through the tibial nerve in the first sacral segment. Fractures endanger whatever rests against the bone, so the neck of the femur threatens the medial circumflex femoral artery, the shaft of the humerus the radial nerve and a knee dislocation the popliteal artery. The Colles fracture drives the distal radius backward and the Smith fracture forward, while the Monteggia injury pairs a break of the ulna with a dislocated radial head.
