# Chapter 92 — Pelvis and Perineum

The pelvis is the bony ring that carries the weight of the trunk to the lower limbs, and the perineum is the shallow diamond of soft tissue that closes it below. This chapter covers the bones and joints of the pelvic ring, the inlet, the midplane and the outlet, the muscles of the pelvic wall and the pelvic floor, the bladder, the rectum, the internal reproductive organs and the fascia that holds them, the vessels and nerves that cross the pelvic brim, and the two triangles of the perineum with the perineal body between them. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The relations described here are the ones a clinician uses at the bedside, in the operating room and at delivery. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9101. Which three bones fuse during childhood to form each hip bone, and where do they meet? The answer is the ilium, the ischium and the pubis, which meet at the acetabulum. A Y-shaped plate of cartilage joins them there until the middle teens. The ilium forms the upper part, the ischium the lower and posterior part, and the pubis the front.

Question 9102. Which of these landmarks is the front upper corner of the hip bone: the ischial spine, the anterior superior iliac spine, the pubic tubercle, or the sacral promontory? The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine. It is the front end of the iliac crest and the point where the inguinal ligament begins. The pubic tubercle lies much lower and nearer the midline.

Question 9103. Which small pointed projection of the hip bone separates the greater sciatic notch above from the lesser sciatic notch below? The answer is the ischial spine. It gives attachment to the sacrospinous ligament and lies at the level of the pelvic floor.

Question 9104. A described radiograph of an adult pelvis shows a smooth ridge running forward and downward across the inner surface of the hip bone from the sacroiliac joint to the pubis. Which ridge is shown? The answer is the arcuate line of the ilium. It continues forward as the pectineal line and forms the lateral part of the pelvic brim.

Question 9105. Which part of the hip bone bears the weight of the body when a person sits: the iliac crest, the ischial tuberosity, the acetabular rim, or the superior pubic ramus? The answer is the ischial tuberosity. It is the rough lower end of the ischium and lies just under the skin of the buttock. The iliac crest and the pubis lie too high to take that weight.

Question 9106. Which of these lies on the upper border of the body of the pubis, a little to the side of the midline, and gives attachment to the medial end of the inguinal ligament: the pubic tubercle, the ischial spine, the iliopubic eminence, or the lesser trochanter? The answer is the pubic tubercle. The inguinal ligament stretches from it to the anterior superior iliac spine.

Question 9107. Which of these is the iliac fossa: the socket of the hip joint, the smooth concave surface on the inner side of the ilium, the groove for the obturator nerve, or the rough area between the two gluteal lines? The answer is the smooth concave surface on the inner side of the ilium. It forms part of the false pelvis and gives origin to the iliacus muscle.

Question 9108. A described dried hip bone shows a deep notch on its posterior border, bounded below by a sharp spine, with a smooth surface above where a muscle leaves the pelvis. Which notch is shown? The answer is the greater sciatic notch. The piriformis muscle passes through it.

Question 9109. Which of these marks the middle of the pelvic brim at the back and is the point from which the obstetric conjugate is measured: the sacral promontory, the ischial spine, the pubic tubercle, or the arcuate line? The answer is the sacral promontory. It is the upper front edge of the first sacral vertebra, at the junction with the fifth lumbar vertebra.

Question 9110. Which of these is true of the sacrum: it is formed by five fused vertebrae, it has three pairs of sacral foramina, it articulates with the femur, or it forms the whole of the pelvic floor? The answer is that it is formed by five fused vertebrae. Four pairs of sacral foramina carry the branches of the sacral spinal nerves.

Question 9111. Which bone forms the lowest part of the vertebral column and the posterior point of the diamond of the perineum? The answer is the coccyx. It is made of three to five small vertebrae fused into a curved triangle.

Question 9112. The obturator foramen lies below and in front of the acetabulum. Which structures pass through the small canal at its upper part? The answer is the obturator nerve with the obturator artery above it and the obturator vein below it. The canal is the gap between the obturator membrane and the superior border of the foramen.

Question 9113. Which structures together form the boundary of the pelvic inlet? The answer is the sacral promontory, the alae of the sacrum, the arcuate lines of the ilia, the pectineal lines, the pubic crests and the upper border of the pubic symphysis. Together they form the pelvic brim.

Question 9114. Which of these is the shortest front to back diameter of the pelvic inlet: the obstetric conjugate, the diagonal conjugate, the transverse diameter, or an oblique diameter? The answer is the obstetric conjugate. It runs from the sacral promontory to the upper posterior margin of the pubic symphysis and measures about 11 centimeters in the average pelvis.

Question 9115. Which plane of the pelvic cavity is the narrowest, and which two landmarks define its width? The answer is the midplane, defined by the two ischial spines, about 10 to 10.5 centimeters apart. It is the narrowest fixed plane that a fetal head must pass.

Question 9116. True or false: the ischial spines form part of the bony boundary of the pelvic outlet. The answer is false. The outlet is bounded by the pubic symphysis, the ischiopubic rami, the ischial tuberosities, the sacrotuberous ligaments and the coccyx. The spines lie above and within that ring and mark the midplane instead.

Question 9117. Which of these statements about the false pelvis is correct: it lies below the pelvic brim, it contains the urinary bladder, it lies above the pelvic brim and holds coils of small intestine, or it is closed below by the pelvic diaphragm? The answer is that it lies above the pelvic brim and holds coils of small intestine. It is part of the abdominal cavity.

Question 9118. The plane of the pelvic inlet is described in a standing person. In which kind of plane does it lie, and how is it tilted? The answer is an oblique plane, tilted so that its posterior border at the sacral promontory lies higher than the pubic symphysis. The inlet therefore faces upward and forward.

Question 9119. Which of these best describes the sacroiliac joint: a fibrous suture, a primary cartilaginous joint, a synovial joint reinforced by very strong posterior ligaments, or a freely mobile ball and socket joint? The answer is a synovial joint reinforced by very strong posterior ligaments. Its stability comes from those ligaments and from the wedge shape of the sacrum.

Question 9120. Which ligament runs from the ischial spine to the side of the sacrum and coccyx, and which opening does it help to close? The answer is the sacrospinous ligament, which helps to close the lesser sciatic foramen. It lies in front of the sacrotuberous ligament.

Question 9121. True or false: the sacrotuberous ligament runs from the ischial tuberosity to the sacrum and helps to form the lesser sciatic foramen. The answer is true. It is a strong band felt as the lower edge of the buttock. With the sacrospinous ligament it closes the lesser sciatic foramen.

Question 9122. Which of these pairs belongs together: the inguinal ligament with the pubic symphysis, the sacrospinous ligament with the ischial tuberosity, the sacrotuberous ligament with the ischial tuberosity, or the lacunar ligament with the iliac crest? The answer is the sacrotuberous ligament with the ischial tuberosity. The sacrospinous ligament runs to the ischial spine instead.

Question 9123. The inguinal ligament is a thickening of which structure, and between which two bony points does it stretch? The answer is the lower border of the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle, stretching from the anterior superior iliac spine to the pubic tubercle. It forms the floor of the inguinal canal. The femoral vessels pass beneath its middle.

Question 9124. Which structure lies immediately deep to the inguinal ligament at the midpoint between the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic tubercle? The answer is the femoral nerve, which lies lateral to the femoral artery. The artery lies next, and the femoral vein lies medial to the artery.

Question 9125. Which of these boundaries belongs to the femoral ring: the inguinal ligament in front, the femoral vein behind, the ischial tuberosity medially, or the sciatic nerve laterally? The answer is the inguinal ligament in front. The ring is bounded behind by the pectineal ligament, medially by the lacunar ligament and laterally by the femoral vein. A femoral hernia passes through it into the femoral canal.

Question 9126. A described dissection of the lateral wall of the pelvis shows a flat muscle arising from the inner surface of the obturator membrane and converging toward the lesser sciatic foramen below the spine. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the obturator internus. Its tendon leaves through that foramen to reach the greater trochanter. Its fascia thickens into the tendinous arch of the pelvic floor.

Question 9127. A described specimen of the posterior pelvic wall shows a pear-shaped muscle arising from the front of the sacrum and passing out of the greater sciatic foramen above a large nerve. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the piriformis. It inserts on the upper border of the greater trochanter. It turns the extended thigh outward and steadies the head of the femur.

Question 9128. Which of these muscles belong to the levator ani: the obturator internus, the pubococcygeus, the coccygeus, or the piriformis? The answer is the pubococcygeus. The levator ani is made of the pubococcygeus, the puborectalis and the iliococcygeus. The coccygeus lies behind it and is a separate muscle.

Question 9129. Which part of the levator ani forms a sling behind the anorectal junction and maintains the anorectal angle? The answer is the puborectalis. Its fibers run from the back of the pubis on each side, around the bowel, and pull it forward. Loss of its tone contributes to fecal incontinence.

Question 9130. Which of these muscles lies immediately behind the levator ani, running from the ischial spine to the side of the coccyx: the coccygeus, the obturator internus, the piriformis, or the puborectalis? The answer is the coccygeus. It helps to support the pelvic viscera and to steady the coccyx. It forms the posterior part of the pelvic diaphragm.

Question 9131. Which of these nerves supplies the levator ani: the obturator nerve, the nerve to levator ani from the sacral plexus, the femoral nerve, or the ilioinguinal nerve? The answer is the nerve to levator ani from the sacral plexus. It arises from the third and fourth sacral spinal nerves and runs on the upper surface of the muscle. The pudendal nerve supplies the external anal sphincter instead, though small branches pass between the two.

Question 9132. A described cross section through the pelvis at the level of the hip joints shows a muscular sheet sloping down and back from the pubis to the coccyx, with fat-filled spaces below it on each side of the anal canal. Which sheet is shown? The answer is the pelvic diaphragm. It separates the pelvic cavity above from the perineum below.

Question 9133. Name the two muscles that form the pelvic diaphragm, spoken as a list. The answer is the levator ani and the coccygeus. The levator ani is the larger and forms a sling around the urethra, the vagina and the anorectal junction. The coccygeus lies behind it, running from the ischial spine to the coccyx.

Question 9134. What is the tendinous arch of the pelvic floor, and which muscle arises from it? The answer is a thickened line of fascia on the obturator internus, from which the levator ani arises. It runs from the back of the pubis to the ischial spine.

Question 9135. What is the name of the smooth triangular area on the inner surface of the bladder, marked by two ureteric openings above and the internal urethral opening below? The answer is the trigone. Its mucosa is smooth and firmly attached to the muscle, so it does not wrinkle when the bladder empties.

Question 9136. Which of these structures is the remnant of the urachus and runs from the apex of the bladder to the umbilicus: the median umbilical ligament, the medial umbilical ligament, the round ligament, or the urogenital mesentery? The answer is the median umbilical ligament. It is a single midline fold in the adult. The medial umbilical ligaments are paired and are the remnants of the umbilical arteries.

Question 9137. Which muscle of the bladder wall contracts to empty the bladder during urination? The answer is the detrusor muscle. It is a mesh of smooth muscle in the wall of the bladder that squeezes urine into the urethra.

Question 9138. Which structure lies immediately in front of the bifurcation of the common iliac artery as it crosses the pelvic brim? The answer is the ureter. It crosses the brim just in front of the division of the artery into its external and internal iliac branches. This is one of the three places where a urinary stone may lodge.

Question 9139. Which of these sets names the three places where the ureter is normally narrowed: the renal pelvis, the pelvic brim and the bladder wall; the aorta, the rectum and the urethra; the diaphragm, the sacrum and the anal canal; or the kidney, the colon and the prostate? The answer is the renal pelvis, the pelvic brim and the bladder wall. Stones lodge at these three points.

Question 9140. A constructed illustration: during an operation to remove the uterus, the surgeon ties the uterine artery close to the cervix and the ureter is injured. Why does the ureter lie at risk at that point? The answer is that the ureter passes below and behind the uterine artery about 2 centimeters from the cervix. The vessel crosses above it, which is why the relation is remembered as water under the bridge. This is an illustration and not a real case.

Question 9141. Which of these statements about the urethral sphincters is correct: the internal urethral sphincter is skeletal muscle and voluntary, the external urethral sphincter is smooth muscle and involuntary, the external urethral sphincter is skeletal muscle and voluntary, or neither sphincter lies in the perineum? The answer is that the external urethral sphincter is skeletal muscle and voluntary. It surrounds the membranous urethra in the deep perineal pouch. The internal sphincter is smooth muscle at the neck of the bladder.

Question 9142. A described contrast study of the bladder shows a smooth rounded outline with a small notch at its lowest point. Which part of the bladder is the notch, and what does it mark? The answer is the neck of the bladder, marking the internal urethral opening. The trigone lies just above it.

Question 9143. What is the name of the dilated lower part of the rectum that rests on the pelvic floor and stores feces? The answer is the rectal ampulla. It lies above the anorectal junction and below the sacral flexure of the rectum. It has no sacculations and no taeniae coli.

Question 9144. Which of these is the arrangement of the transverse folds of the rectum: three folds with the largest near the middle, two folds one on each side, four folds in a spiral, or no folds at all? The answer is three folds with the largest and most constant near the middle. They are also called the rectal valves.

Question 9145. True or false: the internal anal sphincter is a voluntary muscle supplied by the pudendal nerve. The answer is false. The internal anal sphincter is smooth muscle, a thickening of the circular muscle of the rectum, and it is involuntary. The external anal sphincter is the voluntary one and is supplied by the inferior rectal branch of the pudendal nerve.

Question 9146. A described sagittal section through the rectum and anal canal shows the bowel curving back over the coccyx, then turning forward and down to open at the anus, with a thickening of the circular muscle around the upper part of the canal. Which two structures are shown? The answer is the anorectal flexure and the internal anal sphincter. The flexure is produced by the puborectalis sling.

Question 9147. Which structure lies immediately in front of the rectum in the female, and which bony structures lie behind it? The answer is the uterus and the vagina in front, and the sacrum with the coccyx behind. In the male the prostate and the seminal vesicles lie in front instead.

Question 9148. What is the name of the wavy line in the anal canal that marks the junction of the visceral part above and the somatic part below? The answer is the pectinate line, also called the dentate line. Above it the lining is columnar and the vessels follow the rectal supply. Below it the lining is stratified squamous and drains with the perineum.

Question 9149. A described dissection of the male pelvis shows a firm gland wrapped around the first part of the urethra, lying below the neck of the bladder and in front of the rectal ampulla. Which gland is shown? The answer is the prostate. Its base is against the bladder and its apex rests on the pelvic floor. The seminal vesicles lie above its base.

Question 9150. On which part of the male urethra does the seminal colliculus lie: the prostatic part, the membranous part, the spongy part, or the navicular fossa? The answer is the prostatic part. The two ejaculatory ducts open on the colliculus, one on each side.

Question 9151. A described midline image of a male pelvis shows the bladder above, a rounded gland below it, the rectum behind, and a thin dark line marking the perineal membrane under the gland. Which gland is shown? The answer is the prostate. The seminal vesicles lie above its base and against the bladder.

Question 9152. What is the name of the lobe of the prostate that lies between the ejaculatory ducts and the urethra, and what does its enlargement obstruct? The answer is the median lobe, and it obstructs the prostatic urethra. It lies behind the urethra and projects upward into the base of the bladder. Its enlargement lifts the trigone and produces the uvula of the bladder.

Question 9153. Which of these veins drains the prostatic venous plexus: the inferior vena cava directly, the internal iliac veins, the portal vein, or the superficial external pudendal vein? The answer is the internal iliac veins. The plexus also communicates with the internal vertebral venous plexus, which has no valves. That connection is one route by which tumor cells from the prostate reach the bones of the spine.

Question 9154. A constructed illustration: an older man cannot pass urine, and a firm, enlarged gland is felt through the front wall of the rectum. Which part of the gland is most likely compressing the urethra? The answer is the median lobe of the prostate. The clinician is feeling the posterior surface of the gland through the rectal wall. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9155. Which of these parts of the uterus lies above the openings of the uterine tubes and forms its domed upper end: the fundus, the cervix, the isthmus, or the posterior fornix? The answer is the fundus. It is the part of the uterus above the level at which the tubes enter.

Question 9156. Which of these describes the usual position of the uterus: anteverted and anteflexed, retroverted and retroflexed, anteverted and retroflexed, or held backward against the sacrum? The answer is anteverted and anteflexed. Anteversion means that the cervix tilts forward on the vagina, and anteflexion means that the body bends forward on the cervix. A retroverted uterus is a common normal variant.

Question 9157. Which structure lies immediately below and behind the uterine artery about 2 centimeters from the cervix? The answer is the ureter. The artery crosses above the ureter and then runs along the side of the uterus. This crossing is the reason the ureter is at risk during pelvic operations.

Question 9158. What is the name of the band of fascia that runs from the cervix and the upper vagina to the lateral wall of the pelvis and helps to hold the cervix in place? The answer is the cardinal ligament, also called the transverse cervical ligament. It is a thickening of the pelvic fascia at the base of the broad ligament. The uterosacral ligaments run backward from the cervix to the sacrum instead.

Question 9159. Which of these is the lowest point of the peritoneal cavity in a woman, lying between the uterus and the rectum: the vesicouterine pouch, the rectouterine pouch, the paravesical fossa, or the rectovesical pouch? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, also called the pouch of Douglas. It lies behind the uterus and above the posterior vaginal fornix. Fluid in the abdomen collects there and can be reached through that fornix.

Question 9160. A constructed illustration: a woman with a missed period has pain and bleeding, and a pregnancy is found in the widest part of a uterine tube. Which part is involved, and why is it the usual site? The answer is the ampulla, which is the widest and longest part of the tube. It is where fertilization normally occurs.

Question 9161. A constructed illustration: a person with a full bladder takes a blow to the lower abdomen and the bladder ruptures at its weakest point. Which part of the bladder has torn, and where does the urine collect? The answer is the superior surface, which is covered by peritoneum, and the urine collects in the peritoneal cavity. A tear below the peritoneum instead fills the retropubic space. This is an illustration and not a real case.

Question 9162. Into which two divisions does the internal iliac artery branch, and how are they named? The answer is the anterior and posterior divisions. The posterior division gives the iliolumbar, the lateral sacral and the superior gluteal arteries. The anterior division gives the visceral branches together with the obturator, internal pudendal and inferior gluteal arteries.

Question 9163. Which of these nerves leaves the pelvis through the obturator canal: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the pudendal nerve? The answer is the obturator nerve. It arises from the second, third and fourth lumbar spinal nerves and supplies the medial muscles of the thigh.

Question 9164. What is the perineum, and where does it lie? The answer is the diamond-shaped region below the pelvic diaphragm. It is bounded in front by the pubic symphysis, on each side by the ischiopubic rami and the ischial tuberosities, and behind by the sacrotuberous ligaments and the coccyx.

Question 9165. Which of these structures forms the front point of the diamond of the perineum: the coccyx, the pubic symphysis, the ischial tuberosity, or the sacral promontory? The answer is the pubic symphysis. The two ischial tuberosities form the lateral corners of the diamond. The coccyx forms the posterior point.

Question 9166. A described dissection of the perineum shows a diamond of skin and fat divided into two triangles by an imaginary line joining the two ischial tuberosities. Which structures lie in front of that line and which behind it? The answer is the urogenital triangle in front and the anal triangle behind. The anterior triangle contains the external genitalia and the openings of the urinary tract.

Question 9167. Which triangle of the perineum lies immediately in front of the line between the ischial tuberosities, and which muscle sheet lies above it? The answer is the urogenital triangle, with the pelvic diaphragm above it. The perineal membrane stretches across the triangle below that diaphragm. The anal triangle lies behind the same line.

Question 9168. Which of these structures lies in the anal triangle: the bulb of the penis, the greater vestibular gland, the anal canal, or the clitoris? The answer is the anal canal. The triangle also contains the ischioanal fossae and the external anal sphincter.

Question 9169. What is the ischioanal fossa, and where is it found? The answer is a wedge-shaped space filled with fat on each side of the anal canal. It lies below the pelvic diaphragm and medial to the obturator internus and the ischial tuberosity.

Question 9170. Which structures lie immediately medial to the ischioanal fossa, and which lie lateral to it? The answer is the levator ani and the external anal sphincter medially, and the obturator internus with the ischial tuberosity laterally. The fossa narrows to an apex where those two walls meet. Its anterior wall is the posterior border of the perineal membrane.

Question 9171. A described transverse section through the anal canal shows a fat-filled space on each side, a tunnel in the lateral wall of each space carrying a nerve and vessels, and a muscle sheet sloping down toward the midline. Which structures are shown? The answer is the ischioanal fossae, the pudendal canals and the levator ani. The canals lie in the lateral wall beside the obturator internus.

Question 9172. A constructed illustration: an infection in the fat of the perineum on one side of the anal canal later appears on the other side. Through which route has it spread? The answer is the communication between the two ischioanal fossae behind the anal canal, deep to the anococcygeal ligament. Infection can also track forward into the urogenital triangle. This is an illustration and not a real case.

Question 9173. What is the pudendal canal, and what does it contain? The answer is a tunnel in the fascia on the medial side of the obturator internus, carrying the pudendal nerve and the internal pudendal vessels. It lies in the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa.

Question 9174. Which spinal nerve roots give rise to the pudendal nerve, and what territory does it supply? The answer is the second, third and fourth sacral spinal nerves, and it supplies the muscles and skin of the perineum. Its branches include the inferior rectal nerve and the dorsal nerve of the penis or the clitoris.

Question 9175. A constructed illustration: a cyclist has burning pain in the perineum and numbness of the skin of the scrotum after long rides. Which nerve is compressed, and where? The answer is the pudendal nerve, compressed in the pudendal canal against the obturator internus. Pressure from the saddle is transmitted to the canal.

Question 9176. A described dissection shows a nerve and an artery winding around the sacrospinous ligament just medial to the ischial spine, then turning forward into a canal in the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa. Which two structures are shown? The answer is the pudendal nerve and the internal pudendal artery. They leave the pelvis below the piriformis and return through the lesser sciatic foramen. Both then run forward in the pudendal canal.

Question 9177. What is the perineal membrane, and between which structures does it stretch? The answer is a tough triangular sheet of fascia stretched between the two ischiopubic rami. It forms the floor of the deep perineal pouch and the roof of the superficial perineal pouch. The urethra passes through it in the male, and the urethra and the vagina pass through it in the female.

Question 9178. Which pouch lies immediately above the perineal membrane, and which lies immediately below it? The answer is the deep perineal pouch above and the superficial perineal pouch below. The membrane is the partition between them.

Question 9179. Which of these structures lies in the deep perineal pouch: the bulb of the penis, the external urethral sphincter, the greater vestibular gland, or the crura of the penis? The answer is the external urethral sphincter. The pouch also contains the deep transverse perineal muscle and, in the male, the bulbourethral glands. The bulb and the crura lie below the perineal membrane.

Question 9180. A constructed illustration: a clinician plans a pudendal nerve block for a procedure on the perineum and must find the nerve where it is most accessible. Which bony landmark guides the needle, and which structure does the nerve lie against there? The answer is the ischial spine, against which the nerve lies as it crosses the sacrospinous ligament. The block is placed just medial and posterior to the spine. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9181. Which of these structures lies in the superficial perineal pouch of the male: the bulb of the penis, the prostate, the seminal vesicle, or the membranous urethra? The answer is the bulb of the penis. The pouch also holds the crura of the penis, the ischiocavernosus, the bulbospongiosus and the superficial transverse perineal muscles.

Question 9182. What is the fascia that encloses the superficial perineal pouch and is continuous with the dartos of the scrotum and the membranous layer of the abdominal wall? The answer is the superficial perineal fascia, also called the fascia of Colles. It attaches to the ischiopubic rami on each side and to the posterior border of the perineal membrane. That attachment keeps fluid in the pouch from reaching the thigh.

Question 9183. A constructed illustration: urine leaks from a torn urethra below the perineal membrane and collects in the scrotum, the penis and the lower abdominal wall, but not in the thighs. Why does it spare the thighs? The answer is that the superficial perineal fascia is attached to the ischiopubic rami and to the posterior border of the perineal membrane. Fluid therefore follows the loose tissue upward into the abdominal wall instead.

Question 9184. A described dissection of the superficial perineal pouch in the male shows three pairs of muscles, a midline bulb pierced by the urethra, and two crura fixed to the ischiopubic rami. Which structures are shown? The answer is the bulbospongiosus over the bulb, the ischiocavernosus over each crus, and the superficial transverse perineal muscles. The bulb is the expanded end of the corpus spongiosum.

Question 9185. Which of these muscles covers the bulb of the penis: the ischiocavernosus, the bulbospongiosus, the superficial transverse perineal, or the external anal sphincter? The answer is the bulbospongiosus. It compresses the bulb and the spongy urethra and helps to expel the last drops of urine. The ischiocavernosus covers each crus of the penis instead.

Question 9186. Which structure lies immediately below the perineal membrane in the male: the membranous urethra, the bulb of the penis, the prostate, or the bladder? The answer is the bulb of the penis. It lies in the superficial perineal pouch and contains the first part of the spongy urethra. The membranous urethra lies above the membrane, in the deep pouch.

Question 9187. A described dissection of the female perineum shows two small glands at the posterior end of the bulbs of the vestibule, each with a duct opening beside the vaginal opening. Which glands are shown? The answer is the greater vestibular glands. They lie in the superficial perineal pouch and their ducts are easily blocked.

Question 9188. Which of these structures belongs to the superficial perineal pouch of the female: the bulbs of the vestibule, the external urethral sphincter, the deep transverse perineal muscle, or the membranous urethra? The answer is the bulbs of the vestibule. The pouch also holds the crura of the clitoris, the greater vestibular glands and the superficial transverse perineal muscles. The other structures lie in the deep perineal pouch.

Question 9189. A described view of the female external genitalia shows a midline cleft between two folds of skin, a small erectile organ in front of it, and two openings within the cleft. Which structures are shown? The answer is the labia majora, the glans of the clitoris, and the external urethral and vaginal openings. The labia minora lie between the two larger folds.

Question 9190. The perineum is described as the floor of the pelvis. In which plane does it lie relative to the pelvic diaphragm, and what separates the two? The answer is that the perineum lies below the pelvic diaphragm and is separated from the pelvic cavity by it. The perineal membrane lies in the urogenital triangle below the diaphragm.

Question 9191. Which of these pairs belongs together: the bulbospongiosus with the crus of the penis, the ischiocavernosus with the bulb of the penis, the bulbospongiosus with the bulb of the penis, or the external anal sphincter with the clitoris? The answer is the bulbospongiosus with the bulb of the penis. The ischiocavernosus covers the crus. The external anal sphincter encircles the anal canal.

Question 9192. What is the perineal body? The answer is a fibromuscular node in the midline between the anal canal behind and the vagina or the bulb of the penis in front. It lies at the center of the perineum, just below the perineal membrane.

Question 9193. Which muscles are attached to the perineal body? The answer is the bulbospongiosus, the superficial and deep transverse perineal muscles, the external anal sphincter and part of the levator ani. The posterior wall of the vagina is attached there in the female.

Question 9194. A constructed illustration: a woman tears the tissue between the vaginal opening and the anus during a difficult delivery. Which structure has been injured, and which attachments have been lost? The answer is the perineal body, with loss of the attachments of the bulbospongiosus, the transverse perineal muscles and the external anal sphincter. The result is a weakened pelvic floor. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9195. True or false: in the female the perineal body lies between the vagina and the anal canal. The answer is true. It lies in the midline of the perineum below the perineal membrane. In the male the same body lies between the bulb of the penis and the anal canal.

Question 9196. Which of these statements about lymphatic drainage in the male is correct: the testis drains to the superficial inguinal nodes, the skin of the scrotum drains to the lumbar nodes, both drain to the superficial inguinal nodes, or the testis drains to the lumbar nodes while the scrotal skin drains to the superficial inguinal nodes? The answer is that the testis drains to the lumbar nodes while the scrotal skin drains to the superficial inguinal nodes. The testis follows the testicular vessels from the abdomen. The scrotal skin follows the external pudendal vessels.

Question 9197. Into which venous plexus does the deep dorsal vein of the penis drain? The answer is the prostatic venous plexus, which lies behind the pubic symphysis. The superficial dorsal vein drains instead into the superficial external pudendal vein. The prostatic plexus communicates with the internal vertebral venous plexus.

Question 9198. Which of these pairs belongs together: the pudendal nerve with the pelvic diaphragm, the inferior rectal nerve with the skin around the anus, the obturator nerve with the perineum, or the femoral nerve with the anal canal? The answer is the inferior rectal nerve with the skin around the anus. It is a branch of the pudendal nerve. The pudendal nerve also gives the perineal nerve and the dorsal nerve of the penis or the clitoris.

Question 9199. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superior rectal artery with the internal pudendal artery, the inferior rectal artery with the internal pudendal artery, the middle rectal artery with the inferior mesenteric artery, or the superior rectal vein with the internal iliac vein? The answer is the inferior rectal artery with the internal pudendal artery. The superior rectal artery comes from the inferior mesenteric artery. The superior rectal vein drains into the portal vein.

Question 9200. Name the two triangular divisions of the perineum, spoken as a list. The answer is the urogenital triangle and the anal triangle. They are separated by the line joining the two ischial tuberosities.

To summarize, the pelvic ring is built from the two hip bones and the sacrum, and its inlet, midplane and outlet are the three planes that matter, with the ischial spines marking the narrowest of them. The sacroiliac joint is a synovial joint held by very strong posterior ligaments, and the sacrospinous and sacrotuberous ligaments convert the sciatic notches into the greater and lesser sciatic foramina. The pelvic diaphragm, formed by the levator ani and the coccygeus, separates the pelvic cavity from the perineum, and the urethra, the vagina and the anal canal pass through it. The ureter crosses the pelvic brim in front of the common iliac bifurcation, runs below the uterine artery in the female and is crossed by the ductus deferens in the male, and it is narrowed at the renal pelvis, the pelvic brim and the bladder wall. The bladder, the rectum, the prostate and the uterus keep fixed relations that can be felt and imaged, and the pectinate line divides the anal canal into a visceral part above and a somatic part below. The perineum is a diamond split into a urogenital triangle and an anal triangle, with the perineal body at its center, and the membranous urethra and the fascia of Colles explain where extravasated urine can travel.
