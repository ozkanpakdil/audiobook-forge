# Chapter 110 — Orthopedic Anatomy

Orthopedic anatomy is the anatomy that decides how a limb carries weight, where a joint gives way, and what lies in the path of an operation. This chapter works through the bones, the joints, the ligaments, the tendons and the muscles of the limbs and the spine as the orthopedic clinician meets them: the structure of bone and the way it heals, the named fracture patterns, the compartments of the limbs, the stabilizers of the shoulder, the elbow, the hip, the knee and the ankle, and the surface landmarks that guide an examination. It holds one hundred questions, and every question is answered immediately after it is asked and then explained, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10901. Which tendon of the rotator cuff passes beneath the acromion and the coracoacromial arch and is the one most often torn? The answer is the supraspinatus tendon. It runs from the fossa above the spine of the scapula to the top of the greater tubercle, and the arch presses on it whenever the arm is raised.

Question 10902. Which word describes a break in which the bone is shattered into several separate pieces: transverse, comminuted, spiral, or greenstick? The answer is comminuted. A comminuted break has more than two fragments, while a transverse break runs straight across the shaft and a spiral break twists along it.

Question 10903. A described radiograph of the wrist shows a break at the far end of the radius, with the hand and the small distal fragment carried backward so that the forearm takes on a dinner fork outline. Which fracture is described? The answer is a Colles fracture. The break lies in the last part of the radius, and the backward tilt of the fragment gives the shape.

Question 10904. A break of the surgical neck of the humerus endangers a nerve that lies immediately behind the bone at that level. Which nerve lies in that position? The answer is the axillary nerve. It passes through the quadrangular space with the posterior circumflex humeral vessels and supplies the deltoid and the skin over it.

Question 10905. A constructed illustration: a young person falls onto an outstretched hand and afterwards has tenderness in the hollow at the base of the thumb. Which carpal bone is likely injured, and why is the injury easily missed? The answer is the scaphoid. A break across its waist may not be seen at once, and the part beyond can lose its blood supply.

Question 10906. True or false: the periosteum covers the whole surface of a long bone, including the parts that lie inside a joint. The answer is false. The periosteum stops at the edge of the articular cartilage, and that bare cartilage is what lets the joint surfaces glide on one another.

Question 10907. Which of these muscles is not part of the rotator cuff: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, or teres major? The answer is teres major. The cuff is formed by supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor and subscapularis, whose tendons blend into the capsule, while teres major passes below the shoulder and does not join it.

Question 10908. Which tendon is the thickest and strongest in the body, and where does it attach? The answer is the Achilles tendon, which attaches to the back of the calcaneus. It is formed where the gastrocnemius and the soleus come together above the heel, and it lifts the heel at every step.

Question 10909. The anterior talofibular ligament runs forward from the front edge of the lateral malleolus to the neck of the talus. On which side of the ankle joint does it lie, and in which direction does it run? The answer is the lateral side, running forward and slightly downward. It is the weakest of the lateral ligaments and the first to give way when the foot rolls inward.

Question 10910. A described radiograph of the hip shows a break across the neck of the femur, with the shaft riding upward while the head stays in the socket. Why is the blood supply of the head at risk? The answer is that the vessels run along the neck inside the capsule. A break there tears them, and the head beyond may lose its supply.

Question 10911. Which of these pairs belongs together: the patella with the quadriceps tendon, the patella with the Achilles tendon, the fabella with the triceps tendon, or the sesamoid of the great toe with the plantar fascia? The answer is the patella with the quadriceps tendon. The patella is a sesamoid bone inside that tendon, and the fabella lies in the tendon of the gastrocnemius instead.

Question 10912. Name two of the structures that pass through the carpal tunnel beneath the flexor retinaculum, spoken as a list. The answer is the median nerve and the tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis, along with those of the flexor digitorum profundus and the flexor pollicis longus. The ulnar nerve crosses the wrist outside the tunnel, on the medial side.

Question 10913. Which of these structures is torn from the front rim of the glenoid when the shoulder dislocates forward: the acromion, the labrum, the coracoacromial ligament, or the coracoid process? The answer is the labrum, and such a tear is called a Bankart lesion. The head may also dent the back of its own head, which is a Hill-Sachs lesion.

Question 10914. Which meniscus of the knee is held more firmly to the deep surface of the joint capsule, and which ligament shares that attachment? The answer is the medial meniscus, which is attached to the deep part of the tibial collateral ligament. Because of that attachment it moves less than the lateral one and is torn more often.

Question 10915. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the front of the leg the skin is intact but the front of the shin is tightly swollen, and the person cannot lift the foot. Which compartment is involved, and which nerve is affected? The answer is the anterior compartment, with the deep peroneal nerve. That nerve runs down against the interosseous membrane and supplies the muscles that lift the foot.

Question 10916. Which ligament connects the laminae of neighboring vertebrae, contains a high proportion of elastic fibers, and lies immediately in front of the spinal cord? The answer is the ligamentum flavum. Its elastic tissue lets it stretch when the neck bends back without pinching the canal.

Question 10917. A described radiograph of a knee after a twisting injury shows a small oval fragment torn from the outer edge of the tibial plateau below the joint line. Which structure has been avulsed: the tibial collateral ligament, the anterolateral ligament, the posterior cruciate ligament, or the patellar tendon? The answer is the anterolateral ligament with part of the lateral capsule. That small avulsion is strongly associated with a tear of the anterior cruciate ligament.

Question 10918. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the knee shows the joint filled with fluid and a dark band in the middle that no longer runs in a straight line. Which ligament is torn? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament. It normally runs upward and backward from the front of the tibia to the back of the femur.

Question 10919. True or false: the ulnar nerve passes behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus in a groove where it can be felt as a cord. The answer is true. That groove is the cubital tunnel, and because the nerve lies just under the skin a knock there sends tingling into the little finger.

Question 10920. Which of these carpal bones bridges the two rows of the wrist and is the one most often broken: the lunate, the scaphoid, the capitate, or the pisiform? The answer is the scaphoid. Blood enters it near the thumb and flows backward toward the forearm, so a break across its waist leaves the proximal part poorly supplied.

Question 10921. Which ligament wraps around the head of the radius and holds it against the radial notch of the ulna? The answer is the annular ligament of the radius. It forms about four fifths of a ring, and the notch completes it, so the head of the radius can turn inside it when the palm is turned over.

Question 10922. Which nerve lies against the back of the shaft of the humerus in the spiral groove, between the origins of the two heads of the triceps? The answer is the radial nerve, with the profunda brachii artery beside it. A break of the shaft can injure it, and the muscles that straighten the wrist and fingers then fail.

Question 10923. Which ligament is the first to be torn when the foot rolls inward under the ankle: the deltoid ligament, the anterior talofibular ligament, the calcaneofibular ligament, or the posterior talofibular ligament? The answer is the anterior talofibular ligament. It is the weakest of the lateral ligaments, while the deltoid ligament lies on the medial side.

Question 10924. Which carpal bone lies at the outer end of the proximal row of the wrist, between the radius above and the trapezium below? The answer is the scaphoid. It is the largest bone of the proximal row and the one that bridges the two rows, and a fall on the outstretched hand drives the radius against it.

Question 10925. A constructed illustration: after a break in the middle of the shaft of the humerus, the person cannot straighten the wrist or the fingers and the back of the hand feels numb. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the radial nerve. It lies in the spiral groove against the bone at that level.

Question 10926. In the classification of injuries to the growth plate, which type runs along the plate and then out through a corner of the metaphysis: the first, the second, the third, or the fourth type? The answer is the second type, which is the commonest of the group. The third and fourth types cross the joint surface or the plate itself.

Question 10927. Which vessels, running in the reflected fold of the capsule, provide most of the blood to the adult head of the femur? The answer is the retinacular branches of the medial circumflex femoral artery. They creep along the neck beneath the capsule, and a displaced break of the neck tears them and leaves the head with only the small vessel in the ligament of the head.

Question 10928. A described computed tomography scan of the pelvis shows a gap at the front between the two pubic bones and a step at the back where the sacrum meets the ilium. What has happened to the ring of the pelvis? The answer is that the ring is broken in two places and is unstable. A ring of bone cannot break at one point alone without giving way somewhere else.

Question 10929. Which nerve is most often injured when the head of the femur leaves the socket through the back of the hip joint: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the superior gluteal nerve? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen and passes immediately behind the joint.

Question 10930. Which nerve winds around the neck of the fibula just under the skin, where it can be rolled against the bone? The answer is the common peroneal nerve. It lies at the lateral side of the knee, and because it is so superficial a blow there or a tight bandage can injure it.

Question 10931. Which tendon carries the pull of the quadriceps from the lower pole of the patella to the tibial tuberosity? The answer is the patellar tendon. It is the continuation of the quadriceps tendon below the kneecap, and a sudden pull can tear it in an older person.

Question 10932. A constructed illustration: after a fall the elbow is dislocated backward, the pulse at the wrist is lost, and the hand is pale. Which vessel is in danger, and why does its position explain the injury? The answer is the brachial artery. It lies in front of the joint, so a backward dislocation stretches it across the front of the elbow.

Question 10933. Which of these bones forms the medial malleolus at the ankle: the fibula, the tibia, the talus, or the calcaneus? The answer is the tibia. Its lower end is broad, and its medial corner hangs down as the medial malleolus, while the fibula forms the lateral malleolus on the outer side.

Question 10934. A described computed tomography scan of the lower spine shows a vertebral body broken in several directions, with a piece of its back wall pushed into the canal. Which pattern of injury is this? The answer is a burst fracture. The whole body is broken rather than just its front, and the fragment carried backward can press on the cord.

Question 10935. Which muscle forms the rounded contour of the shoulder, is supplied by the same nerve as the skin over it, and is the chief abductor of the arm once the movement has begun? The answer is the deltoid. It arises from the clavicle, the acromion and the spine of the scapula, and the axillary nerve supplies both it and the shoulder skin.

Question 10936. Which tendon forms the border of the hollow at the base of the thumb on the little finger side: the abductor pollicis longus, the extensor pollicis brevis, the extensor pollicis longus, or the extensor carpi radialis longus? The answer is the extensor pollicis longus. It hooks around the lower end of the radius to reach the thumb.

Question 10937. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior compartment of the leg with the tibial nerve, the posterior compartment with the tibial nerve, the anterior compartment with the sural nerve, or the lateral compartment with the femoral nerve? The answer is the posterior compartment with the tibial nerve. It runs down the back of the leg, and the anterior compartment takes the deep peroneal nerve.

Question 10938. Which muscle arises just below the lateral condyle of the femur, inside the capsule of the knee, and passes backward and downward to the tibia? The answer is the popliteus. Its tendon lies between the lateral meniscus and the capsule, and it unlocks the knee by turning the tibia inward at the start of bending.

Question 10939. Which structure is the chief restraint against a blow that drives the knee inward and opens the joint on the outer side: the anterior cruciate ligament, the tibial collateral ligament, the fibular collateral ligament, or the patellar tendon? The answer is the tibial collateral ligament on the inner side, which is often torn together with the medial meniscus.

Question 10940. Which muscle arises from the outer surface of the ilium between the two gluteal lines and keeps the pelvis level when the opposite foot is lifted? The answer is the gluteus medius. It is supplied by the superior gluteal nerve, and when it is weak the pelvis drops on the unsupported side.

Question 10941. A described radiograph of the ankle shows the space between the tibia and the fibula widened, with a break high in the fibula near the knee and no break of either malleolus. Which injury is this? The answer is a Maisonneuve fracture. The force tears the syndesmosis and then breaks the fibula far above the ankle.

Question 10942. A constructed illustration: a young person twists a straight leg, and the kneecap slips outward over the ridge of the femur before returning. Which soft tissue structure on the inner side of the knee is torn? The answer is the medial patellofemoral ligament, the chief check against sideways displacement of the kneecap.

Question 10943. Which structures lie immediately behind the sternoclavicular joint and are therefore at risk when the inner end of the clavicle is driven backward: the trachea alone, the great veins and the arch of the aorta, the phrenic nerve alone, or the esophagus alone? The answer is the great veins and the arch of the aorta, with the trachea and the esophagus behind them. That is why a backward dislocation at this joint is taken seriously.

Question 10944. Which thick band of fibrous tissue stretches from the front of the calcaneus to the bases of the toes and holds up the arch of the foot? The answer is the plantar fascia. It runs forward beneath the sole in several slips, and pain at its attachment under the heel is common after long standing.

Question 10945. A described radiograph of the foot shows a small fragment pulled away from the outer side of the base of the fifth metatarsal bone. Which tendon has pulled it off? The answer is the tendon of the peroneus brevis, which attaches there. A twisted foot can produce a similar appearance.

Question 10946. Which nerve leaves the pelvis below the piriformis muscle and then runs down the back of the thigh? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It is the largest nerve in the body, and below the hip it divides into the tibial and common peroneal nerves.

Question 10947. Which of these muscles does not join the group whose tendons attach to the inner surface of the upper tibia as the pes anserinus: the sartorius, the gracilis, the semitendinosus, or the semimembranosus? The answer is the semimembranosus, which attaches behind the inner condyle instead. The three tendons of the pes anserinus spread out like a goose foot.

Question 10948. Name the two ligaments that cross one another in the middle of the knee joint, and say what each one checks. The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament and the posterior cruciate ligament. The anterior one stops the tibia from sliding forward on the femur and the posterior one stops it from sliding backward.

Question 10949. Which bony prominence on the front of the upper tibia receives the patellar tendon and can be felt just below the kneecap? The answer is the tibial tuberosity. In a growing child it has its own center of ossification, and a sudden pull can separate it from the bone.

Question 10950. True or false: the fibula carries the weight of the body from the knee down to the ankle. The answer is false. The tibia carries the weight, while the fibula serves mainly for muscle attachment and as the outer wall of the ankle.

Question 10951. A described view inside the knee shows a C-shaped rim of fibrocartilage lying on the flat upper surface of the tibia, thicker at its outer edge and fixed at both ends. Which structure is shown? The answer is the medial meniscus. It is attached to the deep surface of the capsule, which is why it is torn more often than the lateral one.

Question 10952. Which nerve is at risk during an approach to the hip from the outer side, where the gluteus medius is split above the greater trochanter: the femoral nerve, the sciatic nerve, the superior gluteal nerve, or the obturator nerve? The answer is the superior gluteal nerve. It enters the buttock above the piriformis and runs between the gluteus medius and minimus.

Question 10953. A constructed illustration: after a twisting injury the knee swells and then locks so that it cannot be straightened, and a click is felt when it finally moves. Which structure is most likely caught between the bones? The answer is a torn meniscus. A displaced part of the cartilage slips between the femur and the tibia and blocks the movement.

Question 10954. Which large quadrilateral prominence can be felt on the outer side of the upper thigh and gives attachment to the gluteus medius and minimus? The answer is the greater trochanter of the femur. It lies at the junction of the neck and the shaft on the outer side, and in the adult its tip is level with the pubic tubercle.

Question 10955. Which structures lie immediately behind the posterior cruciate ligament, between it and the skin at the back of the knee? The answer is the posterior part of the capsule, with the oblique popliteal ligament behind it, and then the popliteal vessels. The artery is the deepest structure in the hollow of the knee.

Question 10956. A described radiograph of a child's elbow taken from the side shows a thin dark shadow in front of the lower humerus and another behind it, with no visible break. What does the posterior shadow mean? The answer is that fluid in the joint has pushed a fat pad backward, which points to an undisplaced fracture above the condyles.

Question 10957. Which nerve passes through the carpal tunnel with the flexor tendons and is compressed in the condition that causes tingling in the first three fingers: the ulnar nerve, the median nerve, the radial nerve, or the musculocutaneous nerve? The answer is the median nerve. It lies just beneath the flexor retinaculum, and the ulnar nerve crosses the wrist outside the tunnel.

Question 10958. Which structure holds the dens of the axis against the back of the front arch of the atlas and so keeps the spinal cord from being pressed when the head turns? The answer is the transverse ligament of the atlas. It stretches between the two lateral masses, and if it gives way the dens can move backward against the cord.

Question 10959. Which ligament resists a force that turns the sole of the foot outward: the anterior talofibular ligament, the calcaneofibular ligament, the deltoid ligament, or the plantar fascia? The answer is the deltoid ligament on the medial side of the ankle. It is a strong triangular band, and the lateral ligaments resist the opposite movement.

Question 10960. Which of these bones forms the heel and takes the weight of the body first as the foot meets the ground: the talus, the calcaneus, the navicular, or the cuboid? The answer is the calcaneus, the largest bone of the foot. It lies below and behind the talus, which passes the weight on to it.

Question 10961. Which of these pairs belongs together: a break of the surgical neck of the humerus with the axillary nerve, a break of the shaft with the axillary nerve, a break of the shaft with the radial nerve, or a break of the medial epicondyle with the radial nerve? The answer is the surgical neck with the axillary nerve. The shaft break endangers the radial nerve, and the medial epicondyle break the ulnar nerve.

Question 10962. Which structure forms the shallow socket into which the head of the humerus fits: the acromion, the glenoid cavity, the coracoid process, or the greater tubercle? The answer is the glenoid cavity on the lateral border of the scapula. It is deepened only a little by the ring of fibrocartilage at its edge, and that shallowness allows the wide movement of the shoulder.

Question 10963. Which tendon runs in the groove on the front of the upper end of the humerus, held there by the transverse humeral ligament? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps brachii. It leaves the shoulder joint through the groove and runs down inside the sheath of the muscle.

Question 10964. A constructed illustration: a child injures the inner side of the lower humerus, and years later the elbow tilts outward at an increased angle and the little finger side of the hand is weak. Which part of the bone was damaged, and which nerve suffers late? The answer is the medial part of the growth plate, with a late palsy of the ulnar nerve. The inner side stops growing while the outer side continues, so the elbow tilts outward.

Question 10965. Within the femoral triangle, which of these lies most lateral: the femoral vein, the femoral nerve, the femoral artery, or the lymph nodes? The answer is the femoral nerve. From lateral to medial the order is nerve, artery, vein and then the lymph nodes, and the nerve lies outside the sheath of the vessels.

Question 10966. A described radiograph of the hand shows a break across the neck of the fifth metacarpal bone with the head tilted forward toward the palm. Which injury is this? The answer is a boxer's fracture. The neck gives way when the closed fist strikes a hard surface, and the pull of the muscles makes the finger droop.

Question 10967. True or false: the anterior cruciate ligament stops the tibia from sliding forward on the femur. The answer is true. It runs upward and backward from the front of the tibia to the back of the femur, and it also resists twisting and hyperextension of the knee.

Question 10968. Which sheet of fibrous tissue joins the shafts of the radius and the ulna and carries the force of the hand up to the arm? The answer is the interosseous membrane of the forearm. Its fibers run downward and medially from the radius to the ulna, and it also gives attachment to muscles.

Question 10969. Which structure is the chief restraint against the tibia being driven backward on the femur: the anterior cruciate ligament, the posterior cruciate ligament, the tibial collateral ligament, or the iliotibial band? The answer is the posterior cruciate ligament. It is the stronger of the cruciate pair and is injured when a bent knee is struck from the front.

Question 10970. At the wrist, which structure lies immediately medial to the ulnar artery, between the artery and the tendon of the flexor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the ulnar nerve. It crosses the front of the wrist just outside the carpal tunnel, so a cut there endangers both the nerve and the artery.

Question 10971. A described radiograph of the neck shows a break through the narrow bridge of bone between the upper and lower articular surfaces of the second cervical vertebra, with its body slipped forward on the vertebra below. Which injury is this? The answer is a break through the pars interarticularis of the axis. Both sides give way, and the body of the vertebra moves forward.

Question 10972. Which nerve leaves the pelvis through the obturator foramen and supplies the muscles that pull the thigh inward: the femoral nerve, the sciatic nerve, the obturator nerve, or the pudendal nerve? The answer is the obturator nerve. It comes from the lumbar plexus and passes through the upper part of the foramen with the obturator vessels.

Question 10973. Which long flat band of fibrous tissue runs down the outer side of the thigh from the iliac crest to the outer condyle of the tibia? The answer is the iliotibial band. It is the thickened part of the fascia lata, it receives the insertion of the gluteus maximus, and it helps steady the knee.

Question 10974. To which bony point does the Achilles tendon attach: the navicular bone, the calcaneal tuberosity, the lateral malleolus, or the head of the talus? The answer is the calcaneal tuberosity on the back of the heel. The tendon is the thickest in the body and lies just under the skin.

Question 10975. A described radiograph of the forearm shows a break of the ulna just below the elbow with the head of the radius displaced forward out of its ring. Which injury is this? The answer is a Monteggia fracture. The radial head must be looked for in every radiograph of a broken forearm.

Question 10976. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lateral compartment of the leg with the superficial peroneal nerve, the lateral compartment with the deep peroneal nerve, the anterior compartment with the superficial peroneal nerve, or the posterior compartment with a peroneal nerve? The answer is the lateral compartment with the superficial peroneal nerve. That compartment holds the peroneus longus and brevis, and the deep peroneal nerve belongs to the front of the leg.

Question 10977. Name two structures, one nerve and one artery, that pass into the sole of the foot behind the medial malleolus. The answer is the tibial nerve and the posterior tibial artery. Both run down the back of the leg and turn forward under the flexor retinaculum beside the tendons of the deep muscles.

Question 10978. Which roughened area on the upper radius receives the tendon of the biceps brachii? The answer is the radial tuberosity. It lies on the medial side of the bone just below the neck, and the tendon turns as it reaches it so that the muscle can both bend the elbow and turn the palm upward.

Question 10979. Which bones form the floor of the hollow at the base of the thumb: the lunate and the triquetrum, the scaphoid and the trapezium, the capitate and the hamate, or the pisiform and the hamate? The answer is the scaphoid and the trapezium. The hollow is bounded by the tendons of the thumb, and tenderness in its floor after a fall points to the scaphoid.

Question 10980. The neck of the femur runs upward and inward from the shaft to the head. In which of the three reference planes does that line lie, and what is the angle between the neck and the shaft called? The answer is the frontal plane, and the angle is the angle of inclination. In the adult it is a little over one hundred and twenty degrees.

Question 10981. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the wrist, taken months after a fall, shows the part of the scaphoid nearest the forearm as dark and without signal while the part near the thumb looks normal. What has happened? The answer is that the proximal part has lost its blood supply and has died. Blood enters the bone near the thumb and flows backward toward the forearm.

Question 10982. Which artery gives the branches that creep up the back of the neck of the femur inside the capsule, and where do those branches lie? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral artery, whose branches run in the reflected fold of the capsule along the neck. The ligament of the head carries only a small vessel of its own.

Question 10983. A constructed illustration: after the head of the humerus has left its socket downward, the shoulder looks squared and the skin over its outer side is numb. Which nerve is injured, and what movement will be weak? The answer is the axillary nerve, and abduction of the arm will be weak. The nerve winds around the surgical neck, close to the lower part of the capsule.

Question 10984. Which of these structures is not found behind the medial malleolus: the tibial nerve, the posterior tibial artery, the tendon of the flexor hallucis longus, or the sural nerve? The answer is the sural nerve, which runs down the back of the leg and along the outer border of the foot. The others pass under the flexor retinaculum into the sole.

Question 10985. Which ligament of the hip joint arises from the ischium, spirals upward and outward over the back of the neck of the femur, and becomes tight when the hip is fully flexed? The answer is the ischiofemoral ligament. It checks the last part of flexion and inward rotation and helps to hold the head in its socket.

Question 10986. True or false: the medial meniscus is more firmly fixed to the capsule of the knee than the lateral meniscus. The answer is true. The medial meniscus is attached along its outer edge to the deep part of the tibial collateral ligament, while the lateral one is more mobile.

Question 10987. A described specimen of the hip shows a ring of fibrocartilage around the rim of the socket, with a tear in its front and upper part. What is the ring? The answer is the acetabular labrum. It deepens the socket and helps to seal the joint, and its front and upper part is the part most often torn.

Question 10988. Which of these structures forms the roof over the head of the humerus and the tendon of the supraspinatus: the acromion alone, the coracoacromial arch, the coracoid process alone, or the spine of the scapula? The answer is the coracoacromial arch, formed by the acromion, the coracoid process and the ligament between them. The subacromial bursa lies beneath it.

Question 10989. Which rough elevation on the outer surface of the middle of the humerus marks the insertion of the muscle that forms the contour of the shoulder? The answer is the deltoid tuberosity. It lies about halfway down the shaft on the outer side, and the deltoid pulls on it to lift the arm away from the body.

Question 10990. A constructed illustration: a middle-aged person develops a painful, flat foot, and the tendon felt just behind the medial malleolus is no longer strong. Which tendon has failed, and what does it normally do? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior. It attaches mainly to the navicular bone and holds up the arch of the foot.

Question 10991. Which of these pairs belongs together: the gluteus medius with the inferior gluteal nerve, the gluteus medius with the superior gluteal nerve, the gluteus maximus with the superior gluteal nerve, or the tensor of the fascia lata with the sciatic nerve? The answer is the gluteus medius with the superior gluteal nerve. The gluteus maximus takes the inferior gluteal nerve.

Question 10992. Which structure passes out of the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen: the tendon of the obturator internus, the sciatic nerve, the tendon of the piriformis, or the obturator externus? The answer is the tendon of the obturator internus. It turns around the spine of the ischium, while the sciatic nerve and the piriformis pass through the greater sciatic foramen.

Question 10993. A described radiograph of the foot shows that the base of the second metatarsal bone has moved away from the bone beside it and the front of the foot is shifted outward. Which joint has given way? The answer is the tarsometatarsal joint, and the injury is a Lisfranc disruption. The second metatarsal is the keystone of that line of joints.

Question 10994. Which structure lies immediately deep to the flexor retinaculum in the middle of the wrist, superficial to the tendons of the flexor digitorum? The answer is the median nerve. It runs down the middle of the front of the wrist, and the long flexor tendons lie behind it and to either side.

Question 10995. Which structure lies immediately behind the dens of the axis and is at risk when the dens is broken at its base: the vertebral artery, the spinal cord, the esophagus, or the recurrent laryngeal nerve? The answer is the spinal cord. It lies in the canal just behind the dens, separated from it only by the transverse ligament.

Question 10996. Which of these is the largest sesamoid bone in the body: the pisiform, the patella, the fabella, or the sesamoid of the great toe? The answer is the patella. It develops inside the tendon of the quadriceps and lies in front of the knee joint, and a sesamoid bone is one that lies within a tendon.

Question 10997. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the elbow, the little finger and the ring finger are bent and cannot be straightened, the spaces between the fingers are hollowed, and the inner side of the hand is numb. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the ulnar nerve, which lies behind the medial epicondyle and supplies most of the small muscles of the hand.

Question 10998. Which structure is the chief check against hyperextension of the knee: the anterior cruciate ligament, the patellar tendon, the iliotibial band, or the oblique popliteal ligament? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament. It is taut when the knee is straight, and it also limits inward rotation of the tibia.

Question 10999. Which structure forms the flat upper surface of the lower end of the tibia that carries the weight of the body onto the talus? The answer is the tibial plafond. It is the roof of the ankle joint, and because it carries the whole weight a break through it disturbs the joint surface.

Question 11000. Which part of which bone forms the knuckle of a finger: the base of the proximal phalanx, the head of the metacarpal, the neck of the metacarpal, or the base of the metacarpal? The answer is the head of the metacarpal. It is the rounded far end of the bone and joins the first bone of the finger.

To summarize, bone carries load along its length, its periosteum stops at the articular cartilage, and the growth plate is the weak point of a child's skeleton. The named breaks each fix a bone, a level and a direction, from the Colles fracture of the radius and the scaphoid waist to the boxer's fracture of the fifth metacarpal, the Monteggia and Galeazzi pairs, the Segond avulsion and the Maisonneuve break high in the fibula. Blood enters several bones from one end, so the head of the femur, the proximal part of the scaphoid and the talus are at risk after a break near the neck. The ligaments hold each joint in a fixed order, with the cruciate ligaments inside the knee, the tibial and fibular collateral ligaments at its sides, the deltoid ligament against eversion at the ankle and the anterior talofibular ligament against inversion, while the nerves lie where the bone is thinnest, the axillary nerve at the surgical neck, the radial nerve in the spiral groove, the ulnar nerve behind the medial epicondyle and the common peroneal nerve at the fibular neck.
