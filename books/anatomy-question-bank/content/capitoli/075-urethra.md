# Chapter 75 — Urethra

Every drop of urine that leaves the body passes through the urethra, and this chapter follows that tube from the urinary bladder to the exterior. The first twenty-five questions deal with the bladder, its parts, its muscle, its sphincters and its relations, and the remaining seventy-five deal with the urethra itself, the four named parts of the male urethra and the much shorter female urethra. The chapter holds one hundred questions, and every one of them gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The urethra is the only part of the urinary tract that differs markedly between the sexes, and that difference runs through the questions that follow. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7401. Which narrow tube carries urine from the urinary bladder to the exterior of the body? The answer is the urethra. In the male it passes through the prostate gland, the perineal membrane and the penis, while in the female it is a short straight tube that opens into the vestibule. It is the final part of the urinary tract.

Question 7402. The urinary bladder is described in four named parts. Which one is the rounded part that points forward and upward behind the pubic symphysis: the fundus, the apex, the neck, or the trigone? The answer is the apex. The fundus is the base behind, the neck is the lowest part, and the trigone is a smooth area inside the base.

Question 7403. Which fibrous cord runs from the apex of the bladder up the front of the abdominal wall to the umbilicus? The answer is the median umbilical ligament. It is the remains of the urachus, the fetal tube that drained the bladder into the umbilical cord. After birth the tube closes and becomes this cord.

Question 7404. The smooth triangular area inside the bladder has three corners. Which openings form its two upper corners and its lower corner? The answer is the two ureteric orifices above and the internal urethral orifice below. The two orifices above are joined by a raised ridge, the interureteric crest.

Question 7405. Which muscle forms the main wall of the urinary bladder: the detrusor, the sphincter urethrae, the levator ani, or the rectus abdominis? The answer is the detrusor. Its fibers run in several directions and contract as a whole to expel urine. The other three muscles belong to the pelvic floor or the abdominal wall.

Question 7406. Which of these pairs belongs together: the fundus with the median umbilical ligament, the apex with the ureteric orifices, the neck with the internal urethral orifice, or the trigone with the prostate gland? The answer is the neck with the internal urethral orifice. The neck is the lowest and most fixed part of the bladder, and the urethra leaves the organ from it.

Question 7407. Which part of the inside of the bladder is a smooth triangle whose mucosa is firmly fixed to the muscle and never thrown into folds? The answer is the trigone. Its corners are the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice. Because the mucosa is adherent, the area stays smooth when the bladder empties.

Question 7408. A described view through a cystoscope shows a pale smooth triangle with a raised ridge running between two small openings. Which structure is the ridge? The answer is the interureteric crest. It joins the two ureteric orifices across the base of the bladder and is a useful guide when the base is examined from inside.

Question 7409. Which statement about the capacity of the adult urinary bladder is correct: it holds about 300 to 500 milliliters, it holds about 50 milliliters, it holds about 2 liters, or its capacity never changes? The answer is that it holds about 300 to 500 milliliters. When it fills, it rises out of the pelvis into the abdomen.

Question 7410. In the male, which structure lies immediately behind the urinary bladder and which lies immediately in front of it? The answer is the rectum behind and the pubic symphysis in front. Above, the bladder is covered by peritoneum, and below it rests upon the prostate gland.

Question 7411. True or false: the peritoneum covers only the superior surface of the urinary bladder, while the rest of the organ lies outside the peritoneal cavity. The answer is true. The peritoneum is reflected from the bladder onto the abdominal wall and onto the rectum, so the organ is described as extraperitoneal.

Question 7412. A constructed illustration: a person cannot pass urine and a rounded swelling is felt above the pubic symphysis. Which organ is distended, and how does it displace the peritoneum? The answer is the urinary bladder, which rises into the abdomen and pushes the peritoneum upward ahead of it. The swelling lies in the midline and is dull to percussion.

Question 7413. Which ring of muscle lies at the neck of the bladder, surrounds the beginning of the urethra, and is made of smooth muscle? The answer is the internal urethral sphincter. It is well developed in the male and poorly developed in the female. Being smooth muscle, it works without voluntary control.

Question 7414. Which nerves carry the parasympathetic fibers that empty the bladder: the pelvic splanchnic nerves, the pudendal nerves, the obturator nerves, or the genitofemoral nerves? The answer is the pelvic splanchnic nerves. They arise from the second to the fourth sacral segments, and their fibers make the detrusor contract.

Question 7415. Which part of the autonomic nervous system reaches the bladder through the hypogastric plexus, and what does it do there? The answer is the sympathetic part, from the eleventh thoracic to the second lumbar segments. It relaxes the detrusor and, in the male, tightens the internal urethral sphincter.

Question 7416. A described computed tomography scan of the pelvis shows the bladder filled with contrast, with a ureter entering its base on each side. Which structure joins the two entry points across the base? The answer is the interureteric crest. It marks the upper border of the trigone and shows where the two ureters open.

Question 7417. Which structure lies immediately in front of the empty urinary bladder in the male: the pubic symphysis, the rectum, the seminal vesicle, or the prostate gland? The answer is the pubic symphysis. The prostate lies below the bladder, and the rectum and the seminal vesicles lie behind it, so only the symphysis is truly anterior.

Question 7418. Name, as a list in one sentence, the two structures that open into the urinary bladder and the single tube that leaves it. The answer is the two ureters and the urethra. The ureters open at the two upper corners of the trigone, and the urethra leaves from its lower corner.

Question 7419. Which small rounded elevation lies just behind the internal urethral orifice in the male bladder, formed by the middle lobe of the prostate gland? The answer is the uvula vesicae. It projects into the bladder at the lower end of the trigone. Enlargement of that lobe raises the uvula and can hinder the outflow of urine.

Question 7420. Which artery is the main supply of the base of the bladder and of the prostate gland: the superior vesical, the inferior vesical, the obturator, or the internal pudendal? The answer is the inferior vesical artery. The superior vesical artery supplies the upper part and the fundus, and the other two reach the bladder only by small branches.

Question 7421. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the lower abdomen with the bladder full, free fluid is found inside the peritoneal cavity. Which part of the bladder is likely torn, and why there? The answer is the superior surface, which is the only part covered by peritoneum. A rupture there spills urine into the peritoneal cavity.

Question 7422. A described specimen of the bladder is cut open to show its inner surface. Over most of the surface the mucosa is thrown into folds, but one small area is smooth. Which area is it, and why is it smooth? The answer is the trigone, because its mucosa is firmly bound to the muscle beneath.

Question 7423. Which statement explains why the mucosa over the trigone stays smooth when the bladder empties: it is firmly attached to the muscular wall, it has no epithelium, it is covered by peritoneum, or it lies outside the bladder? The answer is that it is firmly attached to the muscular wall, so it cannot be thrown into folds.

Question 7424. In a standing person the empty bladder lies wholly inside the pelvis, behind the pubic symphysis. In which direction does the full bladder expand, and what does it push ahead of it? The answer is upward into the abdomen, pushing the peritoneum ahead of it. The bladder becomes an abdominal organ as it fills.

Question 7425. Which statement about the position of the bladder in a newborn infant is correct: it lies mostly in the abdomen, it lies wholly within the pelvis, its neck lies at the pubic symphysis, or it lies behind the rectum? The answer is that it lies mostly in the abdomen. The pelvis is small at birth, and the bladder descends as the pelvis grows during childhood.

Question 7426. In the female, which tube is about 4 centimeters long, runs downward and forward, and opens into the vestibule? The answer is the urethra. It lies in front of the vagina and behind the pubic symphysis, and it is much shorter and straighter than the male urethra.

Question 7427. Which of these lists the parts of the male urethra in order from the bladder outward: prostatic, membranous, spongy; spongy, prostatic, membranous; membranous, spongy, prostatic; or prostatic, spongy, membranous? The answer is prostatic, membranous, spongy. A short preprostatic segment lies within the bladder wall before the prostatic part begins, and the whole tube is about 18 to 20 centimeters long.

Question 7428. In the male, the membranous part of the urethra lies below and behind the inferior border of the pubic symphysis. Which vein lies in front of it, between it and the arcuate pubic ligament? The answer is the deep dorsal vein of the penis. That vein passes beneath the symphysis to reach the prostatic plexus, and the urethra lies behind it.

Question 7429. A described radiograph is made by injecting contrast into the external urethral opening so that the whole male urethra is outlined. Which part appears as a short narrow segment between the prostate gland and the bulb of the penis? The answer is the membranous urethra. It is the shortest part of the tube and the least distensible.

Question 7430. Which part of the male urethra lies within the prostate gland, is the widest and most dilatable part of the tube, and receives the ejaculatory ducts and the prostatic ducts? The answer is the prostatic urethra. It is about 3 to 4 centimeters long and runs obliquely through the gland from above downward and forward.

Question 7431. Which part of the male urethra is the longest, beginning at the bulb of the penis and ending at the external urethral opening: the prostatic part, the membranous part, the spongy part, or the preprostatic part? The answer is the spongy part, which is about 15 centimeters long and lies within the corpus spongiosum.

Question 7432. Which short segment of the male urethra is about 1 to 1.5 centimeters long and passes obliquely through the wall of the bladder before the prostate gland is reached? The answer is the preprostatic part, also called the intramural part. Its oblique course helps to close the tube as the bladder fills.

Question 7433. Which of these pairs belongs together: the prostatic urethra with the corpus spongiosum, the membranous urethra with the external urethral sphincter, the spongy urethra with the prostate gland, or the preprostatic urethra with the perineal membrane? The answer is the membranous urethra with the external urethral sphincter. That sphincter surrounds the membranous part in the deep perineal pouch.

Question 7434. A described midsagittal magnetic resonance image of the male pelvis shows a single tube passing through the prostate gland above and piercing a firm membrane below on its way to the bulb. Which tube and which parts are shown? The answer is the urethra, shown in its prostatic, membranous and bulbar parts.

Question 7435. In the male, which part of the urethra is the widest and most dilatable, and which part is the least distensible? The answer is that the prostatic part is the widest and the membranous part the least distensible. The external urethral orifice is the narrowest point of the whole tube.

Question 7436. The prostatic urethra carries a midline ridge on its posterior wall. What is the name of the rounded elevation on that ridge, and what lies on either side of it? The answer is the seminal colliculus, flanked by the prostatic sinuses. Into those sinuses the ducts of the prostate gland open.

Question 7437. Which rounded elevation on the posterior wall of the prostatic urethra is the landmark that surgeons and endoscopists call the verumontanum? The answer is the seminal colliculus. It lies in the middle of the prostatic urethra. The two ejaculatory ducts and the prostatic utricle open upon it.

Question 7438. Name, spoken as a list, the two kinds of opening that are found on the seminal colliculus of the prostatic urethra. The answer is the two ejaculatory ducts and the prostatic utricle. The utricle is a small blind pouch in the midline, and the ducts open one on each side of it.

Question 7439. Two small slit-like openings lie on the seminal colliculus, one on each side of a small blind pouch. Which ducts end at those openings? The answer is the two ejaculatory ducts. Each is formed by the union of the ductus deferens with the duct of the seminal vesicle behind the bladder.

Question 7440. Into which part of the prostatic urethra do the many small ducts of the prostate gland open: the urethral crest, the prostatic sinuses, the seminal colliculus, or the prostatic utricle? The answer is the prostatic sinuses. These are the grooves on either side of the urethral crest, and about twenty prostatic ducts open into them.

Question 7441. A described endoscopic view of the prostatic urethra shows a midline ridge with a rounded elevation upon it and small openings in the grooves on either side. Which structure is the ridge, and which are the grooves? The answer is the urethral crest with the seminal colliculus upon it, and the prostatic sinuses on either side.

Question 7442. True or false: the prostatic urethra is the widest and most dilatable part of the male urethra. The answer is true. It is also the part that receives the ejaculatory ducts and the prostatic ducts, and it is about 3 to 4 centimeters long.

Question 7443. A constructed illustration: an older man reports difficulty in starting the flow of urine and a weak stream. Which part of the urethra is compressed, and by what? The answer is the prostatic urethra, compressed by the enlarging prostate gland. The gland surrounds that part of the tube. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7444. Which gland lies immediately below the urinary bladder, surrounds the first part of the urethra, and is pierced by the two ejaculatory ducts? The answer is the prostate gland. It is described in lobes and is separated from the rectum behind it by a fascial septum. The urethra leaves the bladder through its substance.

Question 7445. Which part of the male urethra is about 1 to 2 centimeters long, pierces the perineal membrane, and is surrounded by the external urethral sphincter: the prostatic, the membranous, the spongy, or the preprostatic part? The answer is the membranous part. It is the shortest part of the tube and the least distensible.

Question 7446. Which part of the male urethra is the shortest and the least able to be stretched, and how long is it? The answer is the membranous part, which is about 1 to 2 centimeters long. It lies in the deep perineal pouch between the prostate gland above and the bulb of the penis below.

Question 7447. Which muscle surrounds the membranous part of the urethra, lies in the deep perineal pouch, and provides voluntary control of the flow of urine? The answer is the external urethral sphincter, also called the sphincter urethrae. It is made of striated muscle and is supplied by the pudendal nerve.

Question 7448. A constructed illustration: a person has a fracture of the pelvis, and blood appears at the external urethral opening. Which part of the urethra is torn, and why is that part at risk? The answer is the membranous part, because it is short, it is fixed to the perineal membrane and it lies just below the prostate gland.

Question 7449. A described magnetic resonance image of the male pelvis follows the urethra from the bladder to the tip of the penis. Which two segments are surrounded by muscle, and which segment lies within erectile tissue? The answer is that the preprostatic and prostatic segments are surrounded by the internal sphincter and the prostate, the membranous segment by the external sphincter, and the spongy segment lies within the corpus spongiosum.

Question 7450. Into which part of the urethra do the ducts of the bulbourethral glands open: the prostatic part, the membranous part, the bulbar part of the spongy urethra, or the navicular fossa? The answer is the bulbar part of the spongy urethra. The ducts are long and open about 2.5 centimeters below the perineal membrane.

Question 7451. In which direction does the membranous part of the urethra run as it passes from the prostate gland to the bulb of the penis? The answer is downward and forward. It pierces the perineal membrane on that oblique course, and the change of direction at the bulb forms one of the two curves of the male urethra.

Question 7452. Which of these pairs belongs together: the internal urethral sphincter with striated voluntary muscle, the external urethral sphincter with smooth involuntary muscle, the internal urethral sphincter with a ring of smooth muscle at the bladder neck, or the detrusor with the pelvic diaphragm? The answer is the internal urethral sphincter with a ring of smooth muscle at the bladder neck.

Question 7453. Which sphincter of the urethra is under voluntary control and can be tightened at will: the internal urethral sphincter, the external urethral sphincter, the detrusor, or the uvula vesicae? The answer is the external urethral sphincter. It is made of striated muscle and is supplied by the pudendal nerve.

Question 7454. True or false: the external urethral sphincter is made of striated muscle and is under voluntary control. The answer is true. It surrounds the membranous part of the urethra in the deep perineal pouch, and the internal sphincter, which is smooth muscle, lies at the bladder neck.

Question 7455. A constructed illustration: a woman leaks urine when she coughs, and the support of her urethra is being assessed. Which muscles close the female urethra, and in which part of the perineum does the middle of the urethra lie? The answer is the sphincter urethrae and the compressor urethrae, with the urethra in the deep perineal pouch.

Question 7456. Which of these structures lies within the deep perineal pouch of the male: the prostate gland, the bulb of the penis, the bulbourethral glands, or the seminal vesicles? The answer is the bulbourethral glands. They lie one on each side of the membranous urethra, within the sphincter urethrae, and their ducts pass downward to the bulb.

Question 7457. Which part of the male urethra lies within the corpus spongiosum and is surrounded along its length by erectile tissue? The answer is the spongy part, also called the penile part. It begins at the bulb, runs forward within the penis and ends at the external urethral opening. It is the longest part of the tube.

Question 7458. Which part of the male urethra begins in the bulb of the penis, runs forward within the corpus spongiosum, and is about 15 centimeters long? The answer is the spongy part. It is the longest part of the urethra, and it ends at the external urethral opening on the glans.

Question 7459. Which dilatation of the spongy urethra lies within the glans penis and is named for its boat-like shape? The answer is the navicular fossa. It lies just behind the external urethral opening. The mucosa of the fossa is stratified squamous, unlike the rest of the spongy urethra.

Question 7460. Which statement about the narrow points of the male urethra is correct: the external urethral orifice is the narrowest point, the prostatic urethra is the narrowest, the spongy urethra is the narrowest, or all parts are equally wide? The answer is that the external urethral orifice is the narrowest point. The internal urethral orifice and the membranous part are the other narrow points.

Question 7461. A described radiograph made with contrast injected into the urethra shows a short narrowed segment in the region just below the perineal membrane. Which part of the urethra is narrowed, and what is the common cause of narrowing there? The answer is the bulbar part of the spongy urethra. It is a common site of narrowing after inflammation because of its shape and its mucous glands.

Question 7462. Which small mucous glands open along the spongy urethra, and which of them is the largest, lying on the roof of the navicular fossa? The answer is the urethral glands of Littre, and the largest is the lacuna magna. Their openings face backward, so an instrument passed forward may catch in them.

Question 7463. Which statement about the lining of the male urethra is correct: the prostatic part is lined by transitional epithelium, the spongy part by transitional epithelium, the membranous part by stratified squamous epithelium, or the navicular fossa by columnar epithelium? The answer is that the prostatic part is lined by transitional epithelium, as the bladder is.

Question 7464. A described coronal section through the penis shows three columns of erectile tissue, and the urethra lies within the one on the ventral side. Which column contains the urethra? The answer is the corpus spongiosum. It lies below the two corpora cavernosa, and it alone carries the urethra.

Question 7465. A constructed illustration: a cyclist falls astride the crossbar and has bruising of the perineum with blood at the external urethral opening. Which part of the urethra is most likely torn? The answer is the bulbar part of the spongy urethra. It is fixed below the perineal membrane and is crushed against the pubic arch.

Question 7466. A constructed illustration: after an injury to the urethra, urine tracks into the scrotum, the penis and the lower abdominal wall but not into the thighs. Which layer limits it, and why does it spare the thigh? The answer is the membranous layer of the superficial perineal fascia, which is attached behind to the perineal membrane and on each side to the deep fascia of the thigh.

Question 7467. A described computed tomography image of the perineum after an injury shows a collection of fluid in the superficial perineal pouch. Which layer forms the deep boundary of that pouch, and where is it attached? The answer is the membranous layer of the superficial perineal fascia, and it is attached behind to the perineal membrane.

Question 7468. Which statement about the external urethral opening of the male urethra is correct: it is a vertical slit at the tip of the glans, it lies within the prostate gland, it is the widest part of the tube, or it lies below the perineal membrane? The answer is that it is a vertical slit at the tip of the glans. It is the narrowest point of the whole urethra.

Question 7469. True or false: the male urethra has two curvatures, one fixed around the lower border of the pubic symphysis and one mobile in front of it. The answer is true. The fixed curve lies below the symphysis, and the mobile part is created by the penis itself and can be straightened.

Question 7470. A catheter is passed along the male urethra while the penis is lifted upward. Which of the two curvatures does that lift straighten, and which one remains? The answer is that the mobile curve in front of the pubic symphysis is straightened, and the fixed curve below the symphysis remains. The tip must therefore be directed upward at that point.

Question 7471. How long is the female urethra, and what is its usual width: about 4 centimeters long and 6 millimeters wide, about 15 centimeters long and 6 millimeters wide, about 4 centimeters long and 2 centimeters wide, or about 20 centimeters long and 1 centimeter wide? The answer is about 4 centimeters long and 6 millimeters wide.

Question 7472. Which tube in the female is embedded in the anterior wall of the vagina, opens into the vestibule, and lies behind the pubic symphysis? The answer is the urethra. It is about 4 centimeters long, and its wall is fused with that of the vagina along most of its length.

Question 7473. In the female, the external urethral opening lies in the vestibule. Which structure lies immediately in front of that opening, and which opening lies immediately behind it? The answer is the glans of the clitoris in front and the opening of the vagina behind. The urethral opening lies about 2.5 centimeters behind the glans.

Question 7474. Which small glands lie beside the female urethra and open by short ducts on either side of the external urethral opening? The answer is the paraurethral glands, also called the glands of Skene. They are the female counterpart of the prostate gland and lie in the wall of the urethra.

Question 7475. Which statement about the sphincters of the female urethra is correct: the internal urethral sphincter is a thick ring of smooth muscle, the external urethral sphincter is the main voluntary sphincter in the deep perineal pouch, the urethra has no sphincter at all, or the internal sphincter is striated? The answer is that the external urethral sphincter is the main voluntary sphincter.

Question 7476. A described midsagittal magnetic resonance image of the female pelvis shows a short tube lying between the bladder above, the vagina behind and the pubic symphysis in front. Which structure is shown, and which part of the pelvic floor does it pierce? The answer is the urethra, which pierces the pelvic diaphragm and then the perineal membrane.

Question 7477. A constructed illustration: a woman has recurrent urinary infection, and an image shows a small pocket lying beside the urethra within the anterior wall of the vagina. Which structure is the pocket? The answer is a urethral diverticulum. It lies beside the urethra and may be felt through the anterior vaginal wall. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 7478. Name, spoken as a list of two, the muscles that compress the female urethra as it passes through the deep perineal pouch. The answer is the sphincter urethrae and the compressor urethrae. Both are striated muscles of the deep perineal pouch, and they help to keep the urethra closed.

Question 7479. The whole of the female urethra and the lower part of the male urethra develop from which structure: the mesonephric duct, the urogenital sinus, the paramesonephric duct, or the metanephric blastema? The answer is the urogenital sinus. The upper part of the male urethra forms from the vesicourethral canal, which is the upper part of the same sinus.

Question 7480. A described transverse section through the female perineum shows the urethra in the midline, the vagina behind it and erectile tissue on either side. Which structure lies immediately behind the urethra, and with which structure is its wall fused? The answer is the vagina, whose anterior wall is fused with the urethra.

Question 7481. A constructed illustration: a woman has a fracture of the pelvis, and blood appears at the external urethral opening. Where along the urethra is the injury most likely, and why? The answer is where the urethra passes through the pelvic floor near the neck of the bladder, because the short tube is fixed there against the bone.

Question 7482. Which artery is the main supply of the spongy part of the urethra: the internal pudendal artery, the superior vesical artery, the obturator artery, or the inferior epigastric artery? The answer is the internal pudendal artery. Its bulbar and dorsal branches reach the bulb and the penile urethra, and its branches also supply the perineal muscles.

Question 7483. Which statement about the lymphatic drainage of the urethra is correct: the whole tube drains to the superficial inguinal nodes, the distal part to the superficial inguinal nodes and the proximal part to the internal iliac nodes, it drains to the para-aortic nodes, or not at all? The answer is that the distal part drains to the superficial inguinal nodes and the proximal part to the internal iliac nodes.

Question 7484. Which nerve carries sensation from the spongy part of the urethra, and from which main nerve does it arise? The answer is the dorsal nerve of the penis, which arises from the pudendal nerve. It runs along the dorsum of the penis beside the deep dorsal vein and the dorsal artery.

Question 7485. Which nerve plexus supplies the prostatic part of the urethra: the pudendal nerve, the prostatic nerve plexus, the obturator nerve, or the sciatic nerve? The answer is the prostatic nerve plexus. It is formed mainly by branches of the inferior hypogastric plexus, which carry both sympathetic and parasympathetic fibers.

Question 7486. A described image shows a small rounded gland lying beside the membranous urethra, above the bulb and below the perineal membrane, with a long duct running down to open into the bulb. Which gland is shown? The answer is the bulbourethral gland. It lies in the deep perineal pouch, and its duct opens about 2.5 centimeters below the membrane.

Question 7487. A described image made while an infant boy passes urine shows a dilated posterior urethra with a thin oblique line below it. Which part of the urethra is dilated, and what forms the line? The answer is the prostatic urethra, dilated behind a posterior urethral valve that arises from the urethral crest.

Question 7488. Which statement explains why the urethral opening lies on the dorsal surface of the penis in epispadias: the urethral folds failed to fuse, the genital tubercle developed abnormally so that the urethral groove formed on the dorsal surface, the prostate gland is enlarged, or the bulbourethral glands are absent? The answer is that the genital tubercle developed abnormally, placing the groove on the dorsal surface.

Question 7489. A constructed illustration: an infant boy has a urethral opening on the ventral surface of the penis, some distance behind the glans. Which structures failed to fuse during development? The answer is the urethral folds. They normally meet in the midline to form the spongy urethra, and the opening may lie anywhere along the ventral surface.

Question 7490. Which statement about the urethra in the two sexes is correct: the male urethra serves both urine and semen, the female urethra carries semen, the two are of the same length, or the male urethra opens into the vestibule? The answer is that the male urethra serves both urine and semen. The female urethra carries urine only.

Question 7491. Which kind of epithelium lines the prostatic urethra, and which kind lines the navicular fossa at the far end of the tube? The answer is transitional epithelium in the prostatic part and stratified squamous epithelium in the navicular fossa. The membranous and spongy parts are lined mainly by stratified columnar epithelium.

Question 7492. Which of these pairs belongs together: the navicular fossa with transitional epithelium, the prostatic urethra with stratified squamous epithelium, the spongy urethra with stratified columnar epithelium, or the membranous urethra with transitional epithelium? The answer is the spongy urethra with stratified columnar epithelium, which also lines the membranous part.

Question 7493. In the male, the spongy urethra lies in the corpus spongiosum. Which two structures lie immediately above it within the penis, and which fascia wraps all three columns together? The answer is the two corpora cavernosa above it, with the deep fascia of the penis around all three.

Question 7494. True or false: the female urethra is longer than the male urethra. The answer is false. The female urethra is about 4 centimeters long, while the male urethra is about 18 to 20 centimeters long. The female tube is also straighter, and it runs almost directly from the neck of the bladder to the vestibule.

Question 7495. On which wall of the female urethra does the urethral crest lie: the anterior wall, the posterior wall, the lateral wall, or the floor of the vestibule? The answer is the posterior wall. It is a low longitudinal ridge, and it is the female counterpart of the urethral crest of the prostatic urethra.

Question 7496. A described coronal section of the male perineum shows the urethra in the midline, surrounded by a ring of striated muscle, with two small glands beside it and the bulb of the penis below. Which part of the urethra is shown? The answer is the membranous part, lying in the deep perineal pouch between the membrane and the bulb.

Question 7497. Which of these pairs belongs together: the dorsal nerve of the penis with the prostatic urethra, the pudendal nerve with the spongy urethra, the pelvic splanchnic nerves with the external urethral sphincter, or the obturator nerve with the bladder? The answer is the pudendal nerve with the spongy urethra, from which its dorsal branch carries sensation.

Question 7498. Which structure forms the immediate posterior relation of the female urethra along its length, and through which opening in the pelvic floor does the urethra pass? The answer is the anterior wall of the vagina, and the urethra passes through the urogenital hiatus of the pelvic diaphragm before piercing the perineal membrane.

Question 7499. A catheter is passed along the male urethra with the penis held upward. From the bulb, in which direction does the tube run to the perineal membrane, and in which direction does it then continue through the prostate into the bladder? The answer is upward and backward in both segments, with the change of direction at the bulb.

Question 7500. Which of these structures does not open directly into the urethra: the ejaculatory ducts, the prostatic ducts, the duct of the bulbourethral gland, or the duct of the seminal vesicle? The answer is the duct of the seminal vesicle. It joins the ductus deferens to form the ejaculatory duct, and only that duct opens into the urethra.

To summarize, the urinary bladder has an apex joined to the umbilicus by the median umbilical ligament, a fundus, a neck and a smooth trigone whose corners are the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice. Its wall is the detrusor, it is emptied by the pelvic splanchnic nerves and it is held closed by the internal and external urethral sphincters, and only its superior surface carries peritoneum. The male urethra is about 18 to 20 centimeters long and is described in four parts, the preprostatic, prostatic, membranous and spongy parts, of which the prostatic part is the widest and most dilatable and the membranous part the shortest and least distensible, while the external urethral orifice is the narrowest point of the tube. The membranous part pierces the perineal membrane within the deep perineal pouch, surrounded by the external urethral sphincter and flanked by the bulbourethral glands, and the spongy part runs through the corpus spongiosum to dilate as the navicular fossa within the glans. The female urethra is about 4 centimeters long, lies in front of the vagina and behind the pubic symphysis, and is closed by the sphincter urethrae and the compressor urethrae. Rupture of the urethra lets urine into the superficial perineal pouch or the pelvis, and the distal urethra drains to the superficial inguinal lymph nodes while the proximal urethra drains to the internal iliac nodes.
