# Chapter 34 — Pelvis and Hip

This chapter moves through the muscles that build the pelvis and move the hip, and then through the gluteal region and the three compartments of the thigh. It covers the pelvic floor and the pelvic walls, the iliopsoas and the deep lateral rotators of the hip, the gluteal muscles with their nerves, and the muscles, boundaries and actions of the anterior, medial and posterior compartments of the thigh. It holds one hundred questions, and every question states its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The pelvis carries the weight of the trunk into the lower limb, and the muscles described here control that transfer. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3301. Which muscle is formed by the psoas major and the iliacus joining together, and where does it insert? The answer is the iliopsoas, which inserts on the lesser trochanter of the femur. The psoas major arises from the lumbar vertebral bodies and the iliacus from the iliac fossa. Together they form the strongest flexor of the hip.

Question 3302. Which of these muscles is a flexor of the hip: the gluteus maximus, the iliopsoas, the adductor magnus, or the piriformis? The answer is the iliopsoas. It crosses the front of the hip joint and pulls the thigh up toward the trunk. The gluteus maximus and the adductor magnus extend the hip instead.

Question 3303. A described radiograph of a hip shows a small fragment of bone pulled away from the medial side of the upper femur, just below the neck. Which muscle is acting on that fragment? The answer is the iliopsoas. It inserts on the lesser trochanter, the small projection on the posteromedial side of the upper femur. A sudden strong contraction can pull that prominence away.

Question 3304. Which single muscle both flexes the hip and extends the knee, so that it acts on two joints at once: the sartorius, the rectus femoris, the adductor longus, or the pectineus? The answer is the rectus femoris. It is one of the four parts of the quadriceps and it crosses both the hip and the knee. The sartorius also crosses both joints, but it flexes the knee.

Question 3305. Which muscle leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen and inserts on the upper border of the greater trochanter? The answer is the piriformis. It arises from the front of the sacrum and passes out of the pelvis behind the sciatic nerve in most people. It turns the extended thigh outward.

Question 3306. Which muscle lines the inner surface of the obturator membrane and leaves the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen: the obturator externus, the obturator internus, the quadratus femoris, or the coccygeus? The answer is the obturator internus. Its tendon turns through a right angle around the ischial spine to reach the greater trochanter. The obturator externus lies on the outer surface of the membrane instead.

Question 3307. Which two small muscles lie immediately above and below the tendon of the obturator internus as it crosses the ischium? The answer is the superior gemellus above and the inferior gemellus below. They arise from the ischial spine and the ischial tuberosity and join that tendon. The three act together as lateral rotators of the hip.

Question 3308. Which of these muscles turns the thigh outward and lies between the inferior gemellus and the adductor magnus: the quadratus femoris, the piriformis, the gluteus minimus, or the pectineus? The answer is the quadratus femoris. It runs from the ischial tuberosity to the quadrate tubercle on the back of the femur. The piriformis lies above it and the adductor magnus below.

Question 3309. Which broad muscle forms the main part of the pelvic floor, stretching from the pubis in front to the coccyx behind? The answer is the levator ani. It has three named parts: the pubococcygeus, the iliococcygeus and the puborectalis. It supports the pelvic organs and helps to control the openings that pass through it.

Question 3310. True or false: the pelvic diaphragm is pierced by the urethra, by the vagina in the female, and by the anal canal. The answer is true. These three passages cross the floor between the pubis and the coccyx. The levator ani surrounds each of them and helps to keep them closed.

Question 3311. Which part of the levator ani forms a sling behind the anal canal: the puborectalis, the iliococcygeus, the coccygeus, or the obturator internus? The answer is the puborectalis. Its fibers run from the pubis on each side around the back of the junction between the rectum and the anal canal. The coccygeus lies behind the levator ani and is not part of it.

Question 3312. A described dissection of the perineum shows a small midline mass of fibrous tissue between the anal canal behind and the urogenital opening in front, with several muscles meeting in it. Which structure is described? The answer is the perineal body. It is the point where the bulbospongiosus, the external anal sphincter and the levator ani meet. It gives the pelvic floor a firm anchor in the midline.

Question 3313. Which small triangular muscle lies behind the levator ani, running from the ischial spine to the coccyx? The answer is the coccygeus. It forms the posterior part of the pelvic floor together with the levator ani. It supports the pelvic organs and pulls the coccyx forward.

Question 3314. Which nerve supplies the levator ani: the pudendal nerve, the inferior gluteal nerve, the nerve to the obturator internus, or a branch of the sacral plexus that runs on its upper surface? The answer is the branch of the sacral plexus that runs on its upper surface. It carries fibers from the third and fourth sacral nerves. The pudendal nerve supplies the external anal sphincter below the floor.

Question 3315. A constructed illustration: a person who has had several vaginal deliveries describes a dragging sensation and a bulge at the outlet of the pelvis. Which structure has lost its support? The answer is the pelvic floor, chiefly the levator ani. Its fibers surround the vagina and the rectum and hold the pelvic organs above the outlet. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3316. Which muscle holds the junction of the rectum and the anal canal forward, and what does the angle it creates achieve: the puborectalis, the external anal sphincter, the coccygeus, or the obturator internus? The answer is the puborectalis. Its sling maintains the angle between the rectum and the anal canal. That angle helps to keep the contents of the rectum above the outlet until defecation.

Question 3317. Which muscle surrounds the anal canal just below the pelvic floor and closes the canal by its steady contraction? The answer is the external anal sphincter. It is a ring of skeletal muscle supplied by the pudendal nerve. The internal anal sphincter above it is smooth muscle and is not under voluntary control.

Question 3318. Which muscle covers most of the lateral wall of the pelvis below the pelvic brim: the piriformis, the obturator internus, the levator ani, or the quadratus femoris? The answer is the obturator internus. It arises from the inner surface of the obturator membrane and the surrounding bone. Its fascia thickens into a band that gives origin to part of the levator ani.

Question 3319. A described section through the pelvis shows a muscular sheet sloping downward and backward from the pubis to the coccyx, with the urethra, the vagina and the rectum passing through it. Which structure is the sheet? The answer is the pelvic diaphragm, formed by the levator ani and the coccygeus. It separates the pelvic cavity above from the perineum below.

Question 3320. Which bursa lies between the tendon of the iliopsoas and the front of the hip joint: the iliopectineal bursa, the trochanteric bursa, the ischial bursa, or the prepatellar bursa? The answer is the iliopectineal bursa, which reduces friction where the tendon crosses the joint. It often communicates with the joint cavity.

Question 3321. Which long ribbon-like muscle runs obliquely across the front of the thigh from the front of the hip bone to the medial side of the knee? The answer is the sartorius. It is the longest muscle in the body, and it forms the lateral boundary of the femoral triangle. It flexes both the hip and the knee.

Question 3322. Which movement of the hip carries the limb through the shape of a cone rather than in one plane: flexion, extension, abduction, or circumduction? The answer is circumduction. It combines flexion, abduction, extension and adduction in that order. A movement that stays in a single plane is not circumduction.

Question 3323. Which of these pairs belongs together: the piriformis with the first and second sacral nerves, the piriformis with the obturator nerve, the quadratus femoris with the femoral nerve, or the iliopsoas with the sciatic nerve? The answer is the piriformis with the first and second sacral nerves. The obturator internus has its own nerve from the sacral plexus.

Question 3324. Which statement about the psoas minor is correct: it is present in every person, it is absent in many people, it inserts on the greater trochanter, or it is a lateral rotator of the hip? The answer is that it is absent in many people, which makes it a normal variant rather than a rule. When present it is a slender muscle in front of the psoas major.

Question 3325. A described dissection of the posterior abdominal wall shows a long spindle-shaped muscle running down beside the lumbar vertebrae, with a plexus of nerves emerging through its substance. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the psoas major. It arises from the sides of the lumbar vertebral bodies and the discs between them. The lumbar plexus lies within it.

Question 3326. Which muscle arises from the outer surface of the obturator membrane and inserts on the trochanteric fossa on the medial side of the greater trochanter? The answer is the obturator externus. It turns the thigh outward. Unlike the obturator internus, it does not leave the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen.

Question 3327. Which group of muscles pulls the thigh toward the midline: the abductors, the adductors, the lateral rotators, or the extensors? The answer is the adductors. They occupy the medial compartment of the thigh and pull the limb toward the opposite side. The abductors move the limb away from the midline instead.

Question 3328. Which structure emerges from the lateral border of the psoas major and lies in the groove between the psoas and the iliacus? The answer is the femoral nerve. It is formed within the substance of the psoas major from the lumbar plexus. The obturator nerve emerges from the medial border of that muscle.

Question 3329. Which muscle does the sciatic nerve pass beneath as it leaves the pelvis, and what is the common variant? The answer is the piriformis, and in most people the nerve emerges below it. In a minority the nerve pierces the muscle or passes above it, which is a normal variation.

Question 3330. Which nerve arises from the sacral plexus, leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, and then re-enters through the lesser sciatic foramen to supply a muscle on the pelvic wall? The answer is the nerve to the obturator internus. It supplies the obturator internus and the superior gemellus. The pudendal nerve follows a similar course and continues into the perineum.

Question 3331. Which structures leave the pelvis above the piriformis through the greater sciatic foramen: the superior gluteal nerve and vessels, the sciatic nerve, the inferior gluteal nerve and vessels, or the pudendal nerve? The answer is the superior gluteal nerve and vessels. The other three leave below the piriformis. The muscle therefore divides the greater sciatic foramen into two parts.

Question 3332. A described section through the upper thigh shows a bulky muscle lying against the front of the hip joint and, medial to it, a fan-shaped muscle running from the pubis to the back of the femur. Which two muscles are in front of the joint? The answer is the iliopsoas, which is the bulky one, and the pectineus, medial to it. The pectineus flexes and adducts the hip.

Question 3333. A constructed illustration: a person lies with the hip held slightly flexed and reports pain when the hip is passively extended. Which muscle, lying behind the peritoneum and crossing the front of the joint, is the likely source? The answer is the psoas major. Irritation of it produces pain on extension of the hip. Any further assessment belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3334. Which pair of muscles forms the pelvic diaphragm: the levator ani and the coccygeus, the obturator internus and the piriformis, the iliopsoas and the pectineus, or the quadratus femoris and the gemelli? The answer is the levator ani and the coccygeus. Together they form the muscular floor of the pelvis. The obturator internus and the piriformis belong to the pelvic wall.

Question 3335. Which part of the levator ani runs from the pubis backward past the prostate or the vagina and forms a sling around the urethra? The answer is the pubococcygeus. Its fibers form the anterior part of the pelvic floor and support the bladder and the urethra. The puborectalis forms the sling behind the anorectal junction instead.

Question 3336. About which axis does flexion of the hip take place, and in which plane is the movement seen: a longitudinal axis in the coronal plane, a transverse axis in the sagittal plane, an anteroposterior axis in the coronal plane, or a transverse axis in the transverse plane? The answer is a transverse axis in the sagittal plane. Flexion carries the thigh forward toward the trunk.

Question 3337. A described specimen of a hip joint opened from the front shows a strong band of fibrous tissue running from the front of the hip bone down to the line between the two trochanters, with a bursa beneath it. Which structure lies deep to that band? The answer is the tendon of the iliopsoas, which lies in a groove under the band. The bursa beneath the tendon is the iliopectineal bursa.

Question 3338. Which of these muscles inserts on the lesser trochanter of the femur: the iliopsoas, the gluteus medius, the piriformis, or the quadratus femoris? The answer is the iliopsoas. The other three insert on or near the greater trochanter. The lesser trochanter is the small projection on the posteromedial side of the upper femur.

Question 3339. Which thickened band of fascia on the pelvic wall gives origin to part of the levator ani and covers the obturator internus? The answer is the tendinous arch of the pelvic fascia. It runs from the back of the pubis to the ischial spine. It is formed where the fascia over the obturator internus thickens into a line.

Question 3340. Which structures pass through the obturator canal, the gap at the upper part of the obturator foramen: the femoral nerve and vessels, the obturator nerve and vessels, the sciatic nerve, or the pudendal nerve and vessels? The answer is the obturator nerve and the obturator vessels. They travel from the pelvis into the medial compartment of the thigh.

Question 3341. Which structure lies immediately posterior to the inguinal ligament and lateral to the femoral vessels as it enters the thigh? The answer is the iliopsoas, with the femoral nerve on its surface. The psoas and the iliacus pass under the lateral part of the ligament to reach the lesser trochanter. The femoral artery lies medial to the nerve.

Question 3342. Which of these muscles turns the thigh outward at the hip: the obturator internus, the adductor longus, the rectus femoris, or the sartorius? The answer is the obturator internus. The muscles of the gluteal region that lie below the piriformis share this action. Adduction and flexion are different movements about different axes.

Question 3343. Which ligament lies immediately behind the coccygeus muscle, running from the ischial spine to the sacrum? The answer is the sacrospinous ligament. With the sacrotuberous ligament below it, it converts the sciatic notches into the greater and lesser sciatic foramina. The coccygeus lies on its pelvic surface.

Question 3344. Why can the iliopsoas turn the thigh outward in one position and inward in another: because it inserts on the greater trochanter, because its pull crosses in front of and medial to the hip axis, because the obturator nerve supplies it, or because it lies behind the joint? The answer is that its pull crosses in front of and medial to the hip axis. With the hip extended it turns the thigh outward, and with the hip flexed it turns the thigh inward.

Question 3345. A described dissection of the pelvis from above shows a funnel of muscle sloping down to the midline, with the urethra and the rectum passing through its front and back. Which muscle forms the funnel? The answer is the levator ani, with the puborectalis forming the sling behind the rectum. The iliococcygeus forms the sloping posterior part of the floor.

Question 3346. Which nerve leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, hooks around the ischial spine, and re-enters through the lesser sciatic foramen: the sciatic nerve, the pudendal nerve, the obturator nerve, or the femoral nerve? The answer is the pudendal nerve. It passes between the sacrospinous and the sacrotuberous ligaments on its way to the perineum. The obturator nerve leaves through the obturator canal instead.

Question 3347. Which two small muscles arise from the ischial spine and the ischial tuberosity and insert on the tendon of the obturator internus? The answer is the superior gemellus and the inferior gemellus. They straddle that tendon as it crosses the ischium. Both turn the thigh outward.

Question 3348. Which of these pairs belongs together: the levator ani with support of the pelvic organs, the piriformis with flexion of the hip, the coccygeus with abduction of the hip, or the obturator internus with extension of the knee? The answer is the levator ani with support of the pelvic organs. The piriformis and the obturator internus both turn the thigh outward.

Question 3349. Which statement is correct: the pelvic diaphragm and the perineal membrane are the same muscle, the pelvic diaphragm lies above the perineal membrane and the urethra crosses both, the perineal membrane contains the levator ani, or the pelvic diaphragm lies below the perineal membrane? The answer is that the pelvic diaphragm lies above and the perineal membrane below, with the urethra crossing both. The perineal membrane is fibrous tissue.

Question 3350. A constructed illustration: a person lies flat and the lower back arches because the thigh cannot lie flat on the couch and the hip cannot be fully extended. Which muscle, lying in front of the hip joint, is most likely shortened? The answer is the iliopsoas. A fixed flexion posture of the hip is described as a contracture of that muscle until it is proven otherwise. Care belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3351. A described dissection of the buttock shows a thick quadrilateral muscle running downward and laterally from the back of the hip bone and the sacrum to the back of the femur and the iliotibial tract. Which muscle is shown? The answer is the gluteus maximus. It is the largest and most superficial muscle of the gluteal region. It extends and laterally rotates the hip.

Question 3352. Which muscle lies under cover of the gluteus maximus above and behind, arises from the outer surface of the ilium between the anterior and the posterior gluteal lines, and inserts on the lateral surface of the greater trochanter? The answer is the gluteus medius. It is the main abductor of the hip. It steadies the pelvis when the opposite foot is off the ground.

Question 3353. Below the piriformis, which structures leave the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen? The answer is the sciatic nerve, the inferior gluteal nerve and vessels, the pudendal nerve, and the nerve to the obturator internus. The superior gluteal nerve and vessels leave above the piriformis. The muscle therefore divides the foramen into two named parts.

Question 3354. A constructed illustration: a person standing on one leg tips the opposite side of the pelvis downward and leans the trunk toward the standing limb. Which muscle is weak? The answer is the gluteus medius of the standing limb. The gluteus minimus acts with it, and the superior gluteal nerve supplies both.

Question 3355. Name the muscles supplied by the superior gluteal nerve, spoken as a list. The answer is the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus and the tensor fasciae latae. All three are abductors of the hip and all three steady the pelvis. The nerve enters the gluteal region above the piriformis, beside the superior gluteal vessels.

Question 3356. Which nerve supplies the gluteus maximus: the superior gluteal nerve, the inferior gluteal nerve, the sciatic nerve, or the pudendal nerve? The answer is the inferior gluteal nerve. It leaves the pelvis below the piriformis and enters the deep surface of the muscle. The superior gluteal nerve supplies the other gluteal muscles instead.

Question 3357. A described section through the buttock at the level of the hip shows a large superficial muscle behind the joint, a smaller one lateral to it, and a thick nerve lying between the superficial muscle and the deeper muscles. Which structures are described? The answer is the gluteus maximus, the gluteus medius and the sciatic nerve. The nerve lies in the plane between the gluteus maximus and the deeper muscles of the region.

Question 3358. Which gluteal muscle lies immediately deep to the gluteus medius and is also supplied by the superior gluteal nerve? The answer is the gluteus minimus. It arises from the outer surface of the ilium between the anterior and the inferior gluteal lines. It inserts on the front of the greater trochanter.

Question 3359. True or false: the gluteus maximus is the main abductor of the hip. The answer is false. It extends and laterally rotates the hip, and it works hardest in rising from a chair or climbing stairs. Abduction belongs to the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus and the tensor fasciae latae.

Question 3360. Which of these pairs belongs together: the gluteus maximus with the inferior gluteal nerve, the gluteus medius with the inferior gluteal nerve, the gluteus minimus with the sciatic nerve, or the piriformis with the superior gluteal nerve? The answer is the gluteus maximus with the inferior gluteal nerve. The gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus are supplied by the superior gluteal nerve.

Question 3361. A constructed illustration: a person reports pain running down the back of the thigh after sitting a long time on a hard bench, and the buttock is tender midway between the ischial tuberosity and the greater trochanter. Which nerve is compressed, and where? The answer is the sciatic nerve, compressed as it crosses the ischial tuberosity deep to the gluteus maximus.

Question 3362. Which muscle forms the lateral boundary of the femoral triangle at the front of the upper thigh: the sartorius, the adductor longus, the inguinal ligament, or the rectus femoris? The answer is the sartorius. The inguinal ligament forms the base above and the adductor longus forms the medial boundary. The femoral nerve, artery and vein lie within the triangle.

Question 3363. A described dissection of the front of the thigh shows four muscles that share a common tendon below and enclose the femur, with the largest of them lying between the other two on the front. Which group is shown? The answer is the quadriceps femoris. Its four parts are the rectus femoris and the three vasti, and all four join the quadriceps tendon.

Question 3364. Which muscle forms the floor of the adductor canal in its lower part, and through which opening do the femoral vessels leave the canal? The answer is the adductor magnus, with the vessels passing through the adductor hiatus in that muscle. The canal also contains the saphenous nerve and the nerve to the vastus medialis.

Question 3365. Which muscle of the medial compartment of the thigh arises from the body of the pubis just below the pubic crest and inserts on the middle third of the linea aspera? The answer is the adductor longus. It forms the medial boundary of the femoral triangle. It adducts the thigh and helps to flex it.

Question 3366. A described section through the middle of the thigh shows three muscles of the medial compartment lying one behind another from front to back. Which muscle lies most posteriorly? The answer is the adductor magnus, which lies behind the adductor brevis and the adductor longus. It reaches from the pubis and the ischium to the linea aspera and the adductor tubercle.

Question 3367. True or false: the gracilis is the only muscle of the medial compartment of the thigh that crosses the knee joint. The answer is true. It runs from the body of the pubis to the upper part of the medial surface of the tibia. Because it crosses the knee, it flexes the leg as well as adducting the thigh.

Question 3368. Which muscle of the medial compartment has a double nerve supply, receiving fibers from the obturator nerve and from the tibial part of the sciatic nerve: the adductor longus, the adductor brevis, the adductor magnus, or the gracilis? The answer is the adductor magnus. Its adductor part comes from the obturator nerve and its ischial part from the sciatic nerve.

Question 3369. Which structure enters the medial compartment of the thigh through the obturator canal and divides into an anterior and a posterior branch around the adductor brevis? The answer is the obturator nerve. Its anterior branch supplies the adductor longus and the gracilis, and its posterior branch supplies the adductor magnus and the obturator externus.

Question 3370. A described section through the middle of the thigh shows a thick band of deep fascia around the limb, three groups of muscles separated by sheets of fascia, and the femur in the middle. Which group lies in the posterior compartment, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the hamstrings, supplied by the sciatic nerve. The anterior compartment holds the quadriceps, and the medial compartment holds the adductors.

Question 3371. Which of these pairs belongs together: the vastus lateralis with the anterior compartment, the biceps femoris with the medial compartment, the gracilis with the posterior compartment, or the adductor magnus with the anterior compartment? The answer is the vastus lateralis with the anterior compartment. The biceps femoris belongs to the posterior compartment and the gracilis to the medial one.

Question 3372. A described dissection of the lateral side of the thigh shows a broad band of fibrous tissue running from the front of the hip down to the outer side of the knee, with a small muscle pulling on its upper end. Which structures are described? The answer is the iliotibial tract and the tensor fasciae latae. The muscle arises from the front of the iliac crest and tightens the band.

Question 3373. Which opening in a muscle of the medial compartment of the thigh transmits the femoral artery and vein from the front of the thigh to the back of the knee? The answer is the adductor hiatus. It is a gap in the lowest part of the adductor magnus, between its adductor part and its ischial part. Above it the vessels lie in the adductor canal.

Question 3374. On which muscle does the sciatic nerve rest in the back of the thigh, and which muscle lies across it laterally? The answer is the adductor magnus, with the long head of the biceps femoris lying across it laterally. The semitendinosus and the semimembranosus lie medial to the nerve. The nerve divides into its two branches above the knee.

Question 3375. A constructed illustration: a person cannot straighten the knee against resistance and the knee gives way when walking downstairs, while hip flexion is preserved. Which muscle group is weak? The answer is the quadriceps femoris, supplied by the femoral nerve. Its four parts extend the knee, and the rectus femoris also flexes the hip.

Question 3376. Which hamstring muscle inserts on the upper part of the medial surface of the tibia, deep to the sartorius and the gracilis: the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus, the semimembranosus, or the popliteus? The answer is the semitendinosus, whose long tendon forms the lower part of the medial boundary of the popliteal fossa. The biceps femoris inserts on the head of the fibula.

Question 3377. Name the three muscles that insert together on the upper medial surface of the tibia, spoken as a list. The answer is the sartorius, the gracilis and the semitendinosus. Their combined tendon is the pes anserinus. All three flex the knee, and the sartorius and the gracilis also flex and adduct the hip.

Question 3378. A described dissection of the back of the thigh shows three muscles running from the ischial tuberosity toward the knee. Which group is shown? The answer is the hamstrings: the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus. They extend the hip and flex the knee. The biceps femoris lies laterally and has two heads.

Question 3379. Which muscle of the posterior compartment of the thigh has two heads, arises from the ischial tuberosity and the linea aspera, and inserts on the head of the fibula? The answer is the biceps femoris. It extends the hip and flexes the knee, and it rotates the flexed leg laterally. It forms the lateral boundary of the popliteal fossa above.

Question 3380. Which statement about the femoral sheath is correct: the femoral nerve lies within it, the femoral artery lies in its lateral compartment, the femoral canal lies lateral to the artery, or the sheath is a continuation of the transversalis fascia only? The answer is that the femoral artery lies in the lateral compartment of the sheath. The femoral vein lies in the intermediate compartment and the femoral canal in the medial one. The femoral nerve lies outside the sheath, lateral to it.

Question 3381. Which structure lies immediately lateral to the femoral artery at the level of the inguinal ligament? The answer is the femoral nerve, which lies outside the femoral sheath in the groove between the iliopsoas and the pectineus. The femoral vein lies medial to the artery. The order from lateral to medial is nerve, artery, vein.

Question 3382. A constructed illustration: a person reports aching pain on the inner side of the knee after a long walk, with tenderness over the adductor canal. Which nerve in that canal is the likely source? The answer is the saphenous nerve, which runs with the femoral vessels and emerges below the knee. Any diagnosis or treatment belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3383. Which of these muscles extends the knee: the biceps femoris, the vastus medialis, the semimembranosus, or the gracilis? The answer is the vastus medialis, one of the four parts of the quadriceps femoris. The other three muscles named all flex the knee. The quadriceps as a whole is the only extensor of the knee.

Question 3384. True or false: the femoral nerve continues down the thigh inside the adductor canal and emerges below the knee as the saphenous nerve. The answer is false. The femoral nerve ends in the thigh by dividing into branches to the quadriceps and to the skin. One of those branches is the saphenous nerve, and it is that branch which travels in the canal.

Question 3385. A described dissection of the middle third of the thigh shows a tunnel lying deep to a long ribbon-like muscle, with a nerve and two vessels inside it and a roof of tough fascia. Which space is described, and which muscle forms its roof? The answer is the adductor canal, roofed by the sartorius. The femoral artery and vein and the saphenous nerve lie within it, and it opens below through the adductor hiatus.

Question 3386. Which long slender muscle runs down the medial side of the thigh from the body of the pubis to the upper part of the medial surface of the tibia, and is the most superficial muscle of the medial compartment? The answer is the gracilis. It is the only adductor that crosses the knee joint. It flexes the knee and rotates the flexed leg medially.

Question 3387. A described section at the level of the hip joint shows the femoral vessels lying on the front of the thigh, one lateral to the other, with a thick nerve beside them. In what order do the nerve, the artery and the vein lie from lateral to medial? The answer is the nerve laterally, then the artery, then the vein medially. Within the femoral sheath the artery lies in the lateral compartment.

Question 3388. Which statement about the iliotibial tract is correct: it is tightened by the tensor fasciae latae and the gluteus maximus, it is a thickening of the superficial fascia, it inserts on the head of the fibula, or it bounds the femoral triangle medially? The answer is that it is tightened by the tensor fasciae latae and the gluteus maximus. Both muscles gain an insertion through it on the lateral side of the knee. The tract is a thickening of the deep fascia.

Question 3389. Which of these pairs belongs together: the adductor longus with the obturator nerve, the rectus femoris with the sciatic nerve, the semitendinosus with the femoral nerve, or the gluteus medius with the inferior gluteal nerve? The answer is the adductor longus with the obturator nerve. The rectus femoris is supplied by the femoral nerve and the semitendinosus by the sciatic nerve.

Question 3390. A described dissection of the inner side of the thigh shows three muscles lying in order from front to back, the front one arising from the pubis and the back one reaching from the pubis and the ischium to the linea aspera. Which compartment is shown, and which muscle is the largest? The answer is the medial compartment, and the adductor magnus is the largest of the three. The adductor longus lies in front.

Question 3391. True or false: the hamstring muscles extend the hip and flex the knee. The answer is true. They arise from the ischial tuberosity and cross both joints. The biceps femoris lies laterally and the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus lie medially. All three are supplied by the sciatic nerve.

Question 3392. Which muscle lies immediately deep to the adductor longus and superficial to the adductor magnus in the medial compartment of the thigh? The answer is the adductor brevis. The anterior branch of the obturator nerve lies in front of it and the posterior branch behind it. That arrangement explains why the two branches are separated by the muscle.

Question 3393. Which small muscle forms the floor of the popliteal fossa, arises from the lateral condyle of the femur, and rotates the tibia medially to unlock the extended knee? The answer is the popliteus. Its tendon passes into the knee joint and it is supplied by the tibial nerve. It flexes the knee slightly as it unlocks it.

Question 3394. A constructed illustration: a person feels a sudden sharp pain in the groin while pushing off during a sprint, and there is tenderness over the pubic origin of a muscle. Which muscle is injured, and what does it normally do? The answer is the adductor longus, which pulls the thigh toward the midline. Any assessment and care belong to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3395. Which muscle of the upper thigh arises from the pecten of the pubis, lies in the floor of the femoral triangle, and both flexes and adducts the hip? The answer is the pectineus. It is unusual in receiving fibers from both the femoral nerve and the obturator nerve. It lies in front of the adductor brevis.

Question 3396. Name two muscles that flex the knee and rotate the flexed leg medially, spoken as a list. The answer is the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus, and the gracilis also does so. The biceps femoris rotates the flexed leg laterally instead. Medial rotation of the flexed leg is therefore the work of the medial hamstrings.

Question 3397. Which branch of the femoral nerve runs in the adductor canal and supplies the skin of the medial side of the leg: the nerve to the vastus medialis, the saphenous nerve, the nerve to the sartorius, or the medial cutaneous nerve of the thigh? The answer is the saphenous nerve, the longest branch of the femoral nerve. The nerve to the vastus medialis also travels in the canal but ends in the muscle.

Question 3398. A constructed illustration: after an operation on the hip, a person cannot abduct the limb and the pelvis drops when the person stands on that side. Which nerve has most likely been injured? The answer is the superior gluteal nerve, which supplies the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus and the tensor fasciae latae. Care belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 3399. Which part of the quadriceps femoris lies directly on the front of the femur, deep to the rectus femoris and between the vastus lateralis and the vastus medialis? The answer is the vastus intermedius. It arises from the front and lateral surfaces of the shaft of the femur. It extends the knee, as do the other three parts.

Question 3400. A constructed illustration: a person cannot extend the knee and the front of the thigh is numb after a deep wound in the groin, while adduction of the thigh and flexion of the knee are normal. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the femoral nerve. It supplies the quadriceps and the skin of the front of the thigh. Adduction and knee flexion are preserved because the obturator and the sciatic nerves are intact.

To summarize, the pelvis is closed below by the pelvic diaphragm, formed by the levator ani and the coccygeus, which supports the pelvic organs and surrounds the urethra, the vagina and the anal canal, while the puborectalis holds the anorectal junction forward. The hip is flexed most strongly by the iliopsoas, which inserts on the lesser trochanter, extended and laterally rotated by the gluteus maximus, abducted by the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus, and turned outward by the piriformis, the obturator internus and the gemelli. The sciatic nerve leaves the pelvis below the piriformis and lies between the gluteus maximus and the deeper muscles on its way down the back of the thigh, resting on the adductor magnus. The thigh is then divided into an anterior compartment supplied by the femoral nerve, a medial compartment supplied mainly by the obturator nerve, and a posterior compartment supplied by the sciatic nerve. The femoral triangle, the adductor canal and the adductor hiatus give those muscles their named relations, and the superior gluteal nerve supplies the abductors while the inferior gluteal nerve supplies the gluteus maximus, a distinction that explains the two patterns of weakness after injury.
