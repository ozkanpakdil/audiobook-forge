# Chapter 48 — General Nervous System Anatomy

This chapter maps the general plan of the nervous system, from the brain and the spinal cord that form the central nervous system to the cranial and spinal nerves that form the peripheral nervous system. It sets out the gray matter that holds the cell bodies, the white matter that carries the tracts, and the neurons and supporting cells from which both are built. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. Every answer names its structure aloud rather than pointing to a picture. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 4701. Which two organs make up the central nervous system, and where does each lie? The answer is the brain and the spinal cord. The brain lies in the cranial cavity, wrapped by the meninges and protected by the bones of the skull. The spinal cord runs down inside the vertebral canal. Every other nerve belongs to the peripheral nervous system.

Question 4702. Which three membranes wrap the brain and the spinal cord, spoken in order from the outside inward? The answer is the dura mater, the arachnoid mater and the pia mater. The dura is the tough outer coat, the arachnoid lies against it, and the pia follows every fold of the nervous tissue. All three continue around the spinal cord.

Question 4703. Which of these fluids fills the ventricles of the brain and the space around the spinal cord: blood, cerebrospinal fluid, lymph, or synovial fluid? The answer is cerebrospinal fluid. It is made within the ventricles, and it cushions the brain and the cord. Lymph travels in lymphatic vessels, and synovial fluid lies only inside joint cavities.

Question 4704. Which structure inside the ventricles produces most of the cerebrospinal fluid, using a fringe of small blood vessels covered by ependymal cells? The answer is the choroid plexus. It hangs in the lateral ventricles, the third ventricle and the fourth ventricle. The ependymal cells take fluid from the blood and release it into the ventricle.

Question 4705. Through which narrow channel does cerebrospinal fluid pass from the third ventricle into the fourth ventricle? The answer is the cerebral aqueduct, a small tunnel through the midbrain. It is the only route between those two chambers. A block there holds the fluid back in the lateral and the third ventricles and dilates them.

Question 4706. A described section of the brain cut in the midline shows a curved band of white matter whose fibers sweep forward and backward between the two hemispheres. Which structure is shown? The answer is the corpus callosum. Its fibers cross the midline and join matching regions of the hemispheres. It has a rostrum, a genu, a trunk and a splenium.

Question 4707. Which part of the brain lies in the posterior cranial fossa, has two hemispheres joined by a narrow vermis, and is folded into narrow ridges called folia? The answer is the cerebellum. It smooths and coordinates movement that the cortex begins, and it helps to keep balance and posture. It lies below the tentorium cerebelli.

Question 4708. Which of these is not part of the brainstem: the midbrain, the pons, the medulla oblongata, or the thalamus? The answer is the thalamus. The brainstem joins the brain to the spinal cord and is made of the midbrain, the pons and the medulla oblongata. The thalamus belongs to the diencephalon, above the brainstem.

Question 4709. Which part of the brainstem holds the centers for the heart, the blood vessels and breathing, so that a small injury there can be fatal? The answer is the medulla oblongata. It lies lowest in the brainstem, just above the foramen magnum. All the long tracts that run between the brain and the spinal cord pass through it.

Question 4710. Which structure lies immediately superior to the midbrain: the pons, the medulla oblongata, the thalamus, or the spinal cord? The answer is the thalamus. The midbrain is the uppermost part of the brainstem, and the thalamus sits above it as part of the diencephalon. The pons lies below the midbrain and the medulla below the pons.

Question 4711. A described horizontal section through the brain shows a large egg-shaped mass of gray matter on each side of a narrow midline cavity, with a band of white matter just lateral to it. Which structure is the gray mass? The answer is the thalamus. Each thalamus relays sensory information to the cerebral cortex. The band beside it is the internal capsule.

Question 4712. Which two arteries climb the front of the brainstem, join to form a single midline vessel, and so help to complete the arterial ring at the base of the brain? The answer is the two vertebral arteries. They unite as the basilar artery, which divides into the posterior cerebral arteries. The internal carotid arteries complete the front of the ring.

Question 4713. Which fold of the dura mater hangs in the midline between the two cerebral hemispheres and carries the superior sagittal sinus in its upper edge? The answer is the falx cerebri. It runs from the vault of the skull down to the corpus callosum. The tentorium cerebelli lies below it and forms a roof over the cerebellum.

Question 4714. Which of these folds of the dura mater forms a roof over the cerebellum and separates it from the occipital lobes: the falx cerebri, the tentorium cerebelli, the falx cerebelli, or the diaphragma sellae? The answer is the tentorium cerebelli. It stretches across the posterior cranial fossa, and its free edge bounds the opening for the brainstem.

Question 4715. Which space lies between the dura mater and the arachnoid mater and normally holds only a thin film of fluid? The answer is the subdural space. It is only a potential space, so it opens when blood or fluid collects there. The subarachnoid space lies deeper and holds the cerebrospinal fluid, and the epidural space lies outside the dura.

Question 4716. A constructed illustration: a person falls and bleeds from the small veins that run from the surface of the brain to a dural venous sinus. Which space fills with blood? The answer is the subdural space. Those bridging veins cross it on their way to the superior sagittal sinus. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 4717. A described magnetic resonance image of the head shows a narrow midline cavity filled with fluid, with a larger cavity on each side draining into it. Which part of the ventricular system is the midline cavity? The answer is the third ventricle. The lateral ventricles open into it through the interventricular foramina, and it drains backward through the cerebral aqueduct.

Question 4718. Which statement about the lower end of the spinal cord in the adult is correct: it ends at the twelfth thoracic vertebra, it reaches the sacrum, it ends at the first or second lumbar vertebra, or it fills the whole vertebral canal? The answer is that it ends at the first or second lumbar vertebra. Below that level the canal holds only the roots of the cauda equina.

Question 4719. A constructed illustration: fluid is taken from the lumbar cistern, and the needle is placed low in the back so that the cord is not injured. Why is that level chosen? The answer is that the spinal cord ends above it, so only the roots of the cauda equina lie there. They float in the fluid and move aside.

Question 4720. Which of these openings leads from the third ventricle into the fourth ventricle: the interventricular foramen, the cerebral aqueduct, the central canal, or the median aperture? The answer is the cerebral aqueduct, a narrow channel through the midbrain. The interventricular foramen joins each lateral ventricle to the third, and the median aperture opens the fourth to the subarachnoid space.

Question 4721. Which statement about the arteries of the brain is correct: the middle cerebral artery supplies the occipital lobe, the vertebral arteries supply the frontal lobes, the anterior cerebral arteries supply the medial surface, or the internal carotid arteries supply only the cerebellum? The answer is that the anterior cerebral arteries supply the medial surface of each hemisphere. The occipital lobe belongs to the posterior cerebral artery.

Question 4722. A described specimen shows the base of the brain with a ring of vessels around a central structure that has been removed. Which vessel completes the front of that ring on each side after the internal carotid artery divides? The answer is the anterior cerebral artery, joined across the midline by the anterior communicating artery. The ring is the cerebral arterial circle.

Question 4723. A constructed illustration: a person has a sudden severe headache and a stiff neck, and blood is found in the cerebrospinal fluid. A small pouch of the arachnoid mater has ballooned into a vein at the base of the brain. Which space has bled? The answer is the subarachnoid space, which carries the cerebrospinal fluid. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 4724. True or false: the epidural space around the spinal cord lies between the dura mater and the periosteum of the vertebral canal, and it holds fat and a plexus of thin-walled veins. The answer is true. Inside the skull the dura lies against the bone, so no such space exists there.

Question 4725. Which of these pairs belongs together: the falx cerebri with the superior sagittal sinus, the tentorium cerebelli with the inferior sagittal sinus, the falx cerebelli with the straight sinus, or the diaphragma sellae with the cavernous sinus? The answer is the falx cerebri with the superior sagittal sinus, which runs in its upper edge. The diaphragma sellae covers the pituitary gland.

Question 4726. How many pairs of cranial nerves leave the brain, and how many pairs of spinal nerves leave the spinal cord? The answer is 12 pairs of cranial nerves and 31 pairs of spinal nerves. The cranial nerves leave through openings in the skull, and the spinal nerves leave through the intervertebral foramina, one on each side at every segment.

Question 4727. Which of these nerves is a cranial nerve: the sciatic nerve, the optic nerve, the median nerve, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the optic nerve, the second cranial nerve. It carries the fibers of sight from the retina to the brain. The sciatic and median nerves belong to the limbs, and the phrenic nerve comes from the cervical spinal nerves.

Question 4728. Which root of a spinal nerve carries the sensory fibers toward the spinal cord, and where do the cell bodies of those fibers lie? The answer is the posterior root, with its cell bodies in the posterior root ganglion. The anterior root carries the motor fibers outward. The two roots join beyond the ganglion to form the trunk of the nerve.

Question 4729. Which of these nerves is the largest in the body and runs from the pelvis down the back of the thigh: the femoral nerve, the sciatic nerve, the obturator nerve, or the tibial nerve? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It comes from the sacral plexus and leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen. The tibial nerve is one of its divisions.

Question 4730. A described dissection of the back shows a nerve emerging from an opening between two vertebrae and at once dividing into a small branch that turns backward and a larger branch that continues forward. What emerges from the opening? The answer is the trunk of a spinal nerve. Its posterior ramus supplies the deep muscles and the skin of the back.

Question 4731. Where does the trunk of a spinal nerve form, and what are the two branches it gives? The answer is that it forms in the intervertebral foramen, where the posterior and the anterior roots join, and it divides into a posterior ramus and an anterior ramus. The posterior ramus turns backward toward the muscles and the skin of the back.

Question 4732. True or false: the anterior rami of the spinal nerves supply the limbs and the front and sides of the trunk. The answer is true. Most anterior rami join one another to form the cervical, brachial, lumbar and sacral plexuses. The posterior rami stay segmental and supply the deep muscles and the skin of the back.

Question 4733. Which plexus is formed by the anterior rami of the four lower cervical spinal nerves and the first thoracic spinal nerve, and gives branches to the upper limb? The answer is the brachial plexus. It lies in the lower part of the neck and behind the clavicle, and its cords surround the axillary artery. The median, ulnar and radial nerves are among its branches.

Question 4734. Which spinal nerves give rise to the phrenic nerve, which supplies the diaphragm: the first and second thoracic, the fifth to the eighth cervical, the first and second lumbar, or the third, fourth and fifth cervical? The answer is the third, fourth and fifth cervical spinal nerves. The nerve then descends through the thorax to the diaphragm.

Question 4735. A described dissection of the neck shows a long nerve lying on the front of the scalenus anterior muscle and passing down into the thorax between the subclavian artery and the subclavian vein. Which nerve is shown? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It runs from lateral to medial across that muscle and continues down to the diaphragm.

Question 4736. Which nerve passes behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus, where it can be struck against the bone, and what does injury there weaken? The answer is the ulnar nerve, and injury weakens the small muscles of the hand. The nerve lies in a shallow groove on the back of the epicondyle, close to the surface.

Question 4737. Which nerve carries parasympathetic fibers to the heart, the lungs and most of the abdominal organs: the phrenic nerve, the sciatic nerve, the vagus nerve, or the facial nerve? The answer is the vagus nerve, the tenth cranial nerve. It descends through the neck and thorax into the abdomen. The facial nerve reaches only glands of the head.

Question 4738. A constructed illustration: a person cannot wrinkle the forehead or close one eye, and the corner of the mouth droops on that side. Which nerve is affected? The answer is the facial nerve, the seventh cranial nerve. It supplies the muscles of facial expression on the same side. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 4739. Which nerve is formed in the axilla by two heads that embrace the axillary artery, and later passes through the carpal tunnel to reach the hand? The answer is the median nerve. It runs lateral to the brachial artery in the upper arm and crosses to the medial side lower down. It supplies most muscles that move the thumb.

Question 4740. Which statement about the autonomic nervous system is correct: the parasympathetic fibers arise only from the brainstem, the sympathetic trunk lies on each side of the vertebral column, the sympathetic fibers arise only from the sacral cord, or the parasympathetic fibers reach the limbs? The answer is that the sympathetic trunk lies on each side of the vertebral column from the neck to the sacrum.

Question 4741. A described specimen shows a chain of small swellings joined by a thin cord, lying on the sides of the vertebral bodies behind the pleura. Which structure is shown? The answer is the sympathetic trunk with its chain of ganglia, part of the autonomic nervous system. Each ganglion receives a small branch from a nearby spinal nerve.

Question 4742. A constructed illustration: a person has a fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus and cannot lift the arm sideways, with numbness over the outer part of the shoulder. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the axillary nerve. It winds around the surgical neck of the humerus, so it is at risk in that fracture.

Question 4743. Which of these pairs belongs together: the phrenic nerve with the diaphragm, the sciatic nerve with the serratus anterior, the femoral nerve with the calf muscles, or the radial nerve with the muscles of the face? The answer is the phrenic nerve with the diaphragm. The sciatic nerve supplies the back of the thigh, the femoral nerve the front of the thigh, and the radial nerve the back of the arm.

Question 4744. Name two things that a spinal nerve supplies through its anterior ramus, spoken as a list. The answer is the muscles of a limb and the skin over the front and sides of the trunk. The anterior ramus is much larger than the posterior ramus, and the anterior rami join to form the plexuses.

Question 4745. Which statement about the dermatomes of the trunk is correct: the bands belong to the autonomic nerves, one band covers the whole chest, each band comes from a cranial nerve, or each spinal nerve supplies a band of skin around the trunk? The answer is that each spinal nerve supplies a band of skin around the trunk. Adjacent bands overlap.

Question 4746. Which part of the nervous tissue is called gray matter, and what does it contain? The answer is the part that holds the cell bodies, the dendrites and the unmyelinated fibers. It forms the cortex of the cerebrum and the cerebellum, and it lies deep in the spinal cord. The white matter around it holds the myelinated fibers.

Question 4747. Which of these structures is made of gray matter: the corpus callosum, the internal capsule, the cerebral cortex, or the pyramidal tract in the medulla oblongata? The answer is the cerebral cortex. It is a folded sheet of gray matter on the surface of each hemisphere. The corpus callosum, the internal capsule and the pyramids are all bundles of white matter.

Question 4748. A described section across the spinal cord shows a butterfly-shaped area of darker tissue in the center with a paler rim around it. Which part is the gray matter, and which horn holds the cell bodies of the motor neurons? The answer is the butterfly-shaped center, with those cell bodies in the anterior horn.

Question 4749. True or false: in the spinal cord the gray matter lies on the outside and the white matter lies in the center. The answer is false. In the spinal cord the gray matter forms a central butterfly shape around the central canal, and the white matter surrounds it. In the cerebrum the arrangement is the opposite.

Question 4750. What name is given to the deep masses of gray matter inside each cerebral hemisphere, which include the caudate nucleus, the putamen and the globus pallidus? The answer is the basal nuclei, also called the basal ganglia. They help to start and to smooth voluntary movement. The internal capsule separates them from the thalamus.

Question 4751. Which of these horns of the spinal gray matter holds the cell bodies of the motor neurons that leave by the anterior root: the posterior horn, the anterior horn, the lateral horn, or the commissure? The answer is the anterior horn, which is broad and rounded. The lateral horn holds the sympathetic neurons of the thoracic segments.

Question 4752. Which horn of the spinal gray matter lies nearest the posterior root, and what information does it receive? The answer is the posterior horn, which receives the sensory fibers that enter by the posterior root. It lies at the back of the cord, nearest the vertebral spines. Fibers from its cells cross to the opposite side or run up the cord.

Question 4753. Which layers make up the cerebellar cortex, in order from the surface inward: the pyramidal, the granular and the white; the molecular, the granular and the Purkinje; the granular, the Purkinje and the molecular; or the molecular, the Purkinje and the granular? The answer is the molecular layer, then the Purkinje cell layer, then the granular layer. The Purkinje cells alone leave the cerebellar cortex.

Question 4754. A described stained section of the cerebral cortex shows a band of large triangular cells in one layer and a dense layer of small round cells in another. Which are the large triangular cells, and where do their axons go? The answer is the pyramidal cells, and their axons leave the cortex to join the white matter. The small round cells are the granular cells.

Question 4755. Which group of cells in the midbrain contains a dark pigment, sends fibers to the basal nuclei, and is damaged in the disorder that causes tremor and rigidity? The answer is the cells of the substantia nigra. Their fibers release dopamine, which helps to control movement. Loss of those cells is the anatomical basis of Parkinson disease.

Question 4756. Which band of white matter separates the thalamus and the caudate nucleus on its medial side from the lentiform nucleus on its lateral side? The answer is the internal capsule. Its fibers are packed tightly, so a small lesion there can cause a large weakness on the opposite side. The caudate nucleus lies medial to it.

Question 4757. Which statement about the lateral horn of the spinal gray matter is correct: it lies in every segment of the cord, it holds the sensory fibers from the skin, it is present in the thoracic and upper lumbar segments, or it lies at the back of the cord? The answer is that it is present in the thoracic and upper lumbar segments, and it holds sympathetic motor neurons.

Question 4758. A constructed illustration: a small stroke destroys a patch of gray matter deep in one cerebral hemisphere, and the movements of the opposite hand become slow and jerky. Which gray masses lie there? The answer is the basal nuclei, which include the caudate nucleus, the putamen and the globus pallidus. They help to smooth movement.

Question 4759. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior horn with the motor neurons, the posterior horn with the motor neurons, the lateral horn with the sensory fibers, or the central canal with the basal nuclei? The answer is the anterior horn with the motor neurons. The posterior horn receives sensory fibers, and the lateral horn holds sympathetic neurons.

Question 4760. Which statement about the gray matter of the brainstem is correct: it lies only on the surface, the columns of cells are broken into separate nuclei by the fibers that cross it, it is absent below the midbrain, or it forms the cerebellar cortex? The answer is that the columns are broken into separate nuclei by the crossing fibers.

Question 4761. Which tissue of the nervous system is called white matter, and why is it pale? The answer is the tissue made mostly of myelinated axons, whose fatty sheaths give it its pale color. It forms the tracts that carry information from one region to another. In the spinal cord it lies on the outside.

Question 4762. Which of these names is given to a bundle of fibers that runs from one part of the central nervous system to another: a nerve, a tract, a ganglion, or a plexus? The answer is a tract. In the peripheral nervous system such a bundle is called a nerve instead, and a ganglion is a collection of cell bodies.

Question 4763. True or false: the corpus callosum is a band of white matter that joins matching areas of the two cerebral hemispheres. The answer is true. Its fibers cross the midline and are called commissural fibers. In a section cut from front to back, its thick body forms a curved roof over the lateral ventricles.

Question 4764. A described horizontal section of the brain shows a broad band of white matter sweeping across the midline and fanning out into the hemisphere on each side. What kind of fibers are shown, and what is this band called? The answer is commissural fibers, and this band is the corpus callosum, which joins the two hemispheres.

Question 4765. Which three groups are used to classify the fibers of the cerebral white matter, according to where they run? The answer is association fibers, which join parts of the same hemisphere; commissural fibers, which cross to the other hemisphere; and projection fibers, which run up to the cortex or down from it. The internal capsule is made of projection fibers.

Question 4766. Which of these fiber groups carries information from the cortex down to the spinal cord: association fibers, commissural fibers, projection fibers, or the fibers of the posterior root? The answer is projection fibers, which include the corticospinal tract. Association fibers link nearby parts of one hemisphere, and commissural fibers cross the midline between the hemispheres.

Question 4767. Which tract lies between the thalamus and the lentiform nucleus, and which structures form its medial boundary? The answer is the internal capsule, and the thalamus and the caudate nucleus form its medial boundary. On its lateral side lies the lentiform nucleus. Its fibers run in a tight bundle from the cortex to the cord.

Question 4768. Through which part of the internal capsule do the fibers of the corticospinal tract pass on their way down from the cortex: the anterior limb, the genu, the posterior limb, or the part behind the lentiform nucleus? The answer is the posterior limb. The fibers of the face pass through the genu, and the anterior limb links the frontal lobe with the thalamus.

Question 4769. A described section of the medulla oblongata shows that most of the fibers of the descending tract cross to the opposite side and then continue downward. What is that crossing called, and where does the crossed tract lie? The answer is the pyramidal decussation, and the crossed fibers form the lateral corticospinal tract in the lateral column of the cord.

Question 4770. Which tract carries the sensations of pain and temperature from the body to the thalamus, and on which side does it cross? The answer is the spinothalamic tract, which crosses within 1 or 2 segments of the level where it enters the spinal cord. Its fibers then ascend in the anterior and lateral columns to the thalamus.

Question 4771. Which tract carries the sense of vibration and of fine touch from the leg, and where do its fibers cross the midline? The answer is the posterior column tract, whose fibers ascend on the same side of the cord and cross in the medulla oblongata. They then run as the medial lemniscus to the thalamus. Fibers from the leg lie nearest the midline.

Question 4772. Which statement about the tracts of the spinal cord is correct: both cross in the spinal cord, both cross in the medulla oblongata, neither crosses, or the spinothalamic fibers cross in the spinal cord and the posterior column fibers cross in the medulla? The answer is that the spinothalamic fibers cross in the cord and the posterior column fibers in the medulla.

Question 4773. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lateral corticospinal tract with the movement of the limbs, the posterior column with pain and temperature, the spinothalamic tract with vibration, or the medial lemniscus with the sense of smell? The answer is the lateral corticospinal tract with the movement of the limbs. The posterior column carries vibration and fine touch.

Question 4774. A constructed illustration: a person has patchy loss of myelin in the white matter of the brain and the spinal cord, with weakness and numbness that come and go. Which tissue is attacked? The answer is the myelin sheath of the white matter tracts, so signals travel slowly or fail. The gray matter is largely spared.

Question 4775. A constructed illustration: a small hemorrhage destroys the posterior limb of the internal capsule on one side. Which fibers are interrupted, and what follows? The answer is the projection fibers that run between the cortex and the brainstem or the spinal cord. Movement and sensation fail on the opposite side of the body. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 4776. Which three main parts make up a neuron, and which part carries the impulse away from the cell body? The answer is the cell body, the dendrites and the axon, and the axon carries the impulse away. The dendrites bring information toward the cell body. The axon ends in small terminals that pass the message to the next cell.

Question 4777. Which of these names is given to the short branches that carry information toward the cell body of a neuron: the axons, the dendrites, the myelin sheaths, or the nodes of Ranvier? The answer is the dendrites. They form a branching tree around the cell body. The axon is the single long process that carries the message away.

Question 4778. Which type of neuron has a single process that leaves the cell body and then divides into one branch going to the periphery and one going to the spinal cord? The answer is the unipolar neuron, also called the pseudounipolar neuron. The cell bodies of this type lie in the posterior root ganglia of the spinal nerves.

Question 4779. Which of these neurons carries impulses from a sensory receptor toward the central nervous system: a motor neuron, a sensory neuron, an interneuron, or a neuron of the anterior horn? The answer is a sensory neuron, also called an afferent neuron. A motor neuron carries impulses away to a muscle or a gland.

Question 4780. A described specimen of the spinal cord shows a nerve cell whose body lies in a swelling on the posterior root, with one process leaving the cell body and dividing in the shape of the letter T. Which kind of neuron is shown? The answer is a unipolar sensory neuron, with one branch to the skin and one entering the cord.

Question 4781. True or false: in a chemical synapse the impulse crosses from one neuron to the next by the release of a chemical messenger into a narrow gap. The answer is true. The gap is the synaptic cleft, and the messenger is a neurotransmitter. The impulse crosses only from the presynaptic side to the postsynaptic side, so it travels one way.

Question 4782. Which types of supporting cells form the myelin sheath in the central nervous system and in the peripheral nervous system? The answer is the oligodendrocytes in the central nervous system and the Schwann cells in the peripheral nervous system. One oligodendrocyte can wrap several axons, while each Schwann cell wraps only one segment of one axon.

Question 4783. Which of these cells of the central nervous system helps to form the blood-brain barrier and to repair the tissue after injury: the oligodendrocyte, the astrocyte, the microglial cell, or the ependymal cell? The answer is the astrocyte. Its processes touch the capillaries and the neurons, and it forms a scar in the brain. The microglial cell is the phagocyte.

Question 4784. A described section of nervous tissue shows a large star-shaped cell with many processes, some touching a small blood vessel and others touching the surface of neurons. Which cell is shown? The answer is the astrocyte. It supports the neurons, helps to form the blood-brain barrier and takes part in the repair of injury.

Question 4785. Which supporting cells of the peripheral nervous system wrap the cell bodies in a ganglion and form the myelin around a single axon: the satellite cells and the Schwann cells, the astrocytes and the oligodendrocytes, the ependymal cells and the microglial cells, or the Purkinje cells and the granular cells? The answer is the satellite cells and the Schwann cells.

Question 4786. Which of these cells lines the ventricles of the brain and helps to make cerebrospinal fluid: the astrocyte, the ependymal cell, the microglial cell, or the oligodendrocyte? The answer is the ependymal cell. A sheet of these cells covers the choroid plexus, where most of the fluid is made.

Question 4787. Name two chemical messengers of the nervous system and say where each is released, spoken as a list. The answer is acetylcholine, released at the neuromuscular junction and by parasympathetic endings, and norepinephrine, released by most sympathetic endings. Others include dopamine, serotonin and gamma-aminobutyric acid, which is the main inhibitory messenger of the brain.

Question 4788. Which of these events happens first when an impulse reaches the end of an axon: the neurotransmitter crosses the synaptic cleft, calcium enters the presynaptic terminal, the postsynaptic cell fires, or the vesicles refill? The answer is that calcium enters the presynaptic terminal. It makes the vesicles of neurotransmitter fuse and release their contents into the synaptic cleft.

Question 4789. A described drawing of a synapse shows a small swelling at the end of an axon filled with tiny round sacs, facing a thicker membrane across a narrow gap. What are the sacs, and what is the gap? The answer is that the sacs are the synaptic vesicles, which hold the neurotransmitter, and the gap is the synaptic cleft.

Question 4790. A described section of the spinal cord is cut straight across its long axis. In which direction do the long tracts of the white matter run relative to that cut, and in which column does the lateral corticospinal tract lie? The answer is that the tracts run at right angles to the cut, and that tract lies in the lateral column.

Question 4791. Which part of the neuron is the short region where the axon leaves the cell body, and why is it important in the start of an impulse? The answer is the axon hillock, where the impulse is generated. The cell body and the dendrites add up the messages they receive, and when the sum reaches a threshold the hillock fires.

Question 4792. A constructed illustration: a cut in the forearm divides a nerve, and the part of the axon separated from its cell body swells and then breaks up while its sheath remains. What is that change called? The answer is Wallerian degeneration. Fibers of the peripheral nervous system can regrow along the sheath, but those of the central nervous system cannot.

Question 4793. Which of these statements about the speed of an impulse is correct: all fibers carry it at the same speed, a thin unmyelinated fiber carries it faster, a thick myelinated fiber carries it faster than a thin unmyelinated fiber, or the speed depends only on the length of the axon? The answer is that a thick myelinated fiber is faster. The myelin lets the impulse jump from node to node.

Question 4794. Which of these pairs belongs together: the node of Ranvier with the gap between two myelin segments, the axon hillock with the receiving tip of a dendrite, the synaptic vesicle with the myelin sheath, or the ependymal cell with the movement of a limb? The answer is the node of Ranvier with the gap between two myelin segments, where the impulse is renewed.

Question 4795. A described section through the cerebral hemisphere passes through the midline and shows both lateral ventricles with the corpus callosum above them. In which plane was the brain cut, and does the cut pass through both hemispheres? The answer is a horizontal plane, and it does pass through both hemispheres. A sagittal cut would show only one hemisphere.

Question 4796. A described electron micrograph shows a chain of cells wrapped many times around a single axon, with short bare gaps between the wrappings. Which cells are shown, and what happens at the gaps? The answer is the Schwann cells of a peripheral nerve, and the gaps are the nodes of Ranvier, where the impulse is renewed.

Question 4797. Which of these statements about the regeneration of a divided nerve fiber is correct: a fiber of the central nervous system regrows faster, both regrow equally well, neither regrows at all, or a fiber of the peripheral nervous system can regrow along its sheath? The answer is that a peripheral fiber can regrow along the sheath left by the Schwann cells.

Question 4798. A constructed illustration: a wound divides the median nerve at the wrist, and after it heals the thumb still cannot be opposed. Which cells guide the regrowth of the divided fibers? The answer is the Schwann cells, whose sheath remains as a tube along which the new fiber grows. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 4799. Which statement about the resting membrane potential of a neuron is correct: the inside is positive relative to the outside, the inside of the cell is negative relative to the outside, the two sides are equal, or the potential depends only on the size of the cell? The answer is that the inside is negative, largely because the membrane lets potassium leak out.

Question 4800. Name two structures that meet at a synapse, spoken as a list. The answer is the presynaptic terminal of one neuron and the postsynaptic membrane of the next. The impulse reaches the presynaptic terminal, a neurotransmitter crosses the narrow synaptic cleft, and the postsynaptic membrane responds. The message passes in one direction only.

To summarize, the central nervous system is the brain and the spinal cord, wrapped in the dura mater, the arachnoid mater and the pia mater and cushioned by cerebrospinal fluid made in the choroid plexus. The peripheral nervous system is the 12 pairs of cranial nerves and the 31 pairs of spinal nerves, each spinal nerve formed by a posterior sensory root and an anterior motor root in the intervertebral foramen. Gray matter holds the cell bodies and dendrites, in the cerebral cortex, the basal nuclei, the cerebellar cortex or the horns of the spinal cord, and white matter holds the myelinated axons grouped into association, commissural and projection fibers. The great tracts cross the midline according to their function, the corticospinal tract in the medulla oblongata, the spinothalamic tract in the spinal cord and the posterior column tract in the medulla. Neurons come in sensory, motor and interneuron forms, supported within the central nervous system by astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglial cells and ependymal cells, and outside it by Schwann cells and satellite cells.
