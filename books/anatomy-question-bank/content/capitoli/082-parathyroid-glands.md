# Chapter 82 — Parathyroid Glands

This chapter turns to three sets of endocrine glands that are read together because they lie close to one another and work as partners. It begins with the thyroid gland, its capsule, its blood supply, its development and its relations in the front of the neck, then takes the four small parathyroid glands that sit on the posterior surface of the thyroid and control the calcium of the body fluids, and it closes with the paired adrenal glands that cap the kidneys. It holds one hundred questions, and every question states its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind from one item to the next. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 8101. Which gland lies in the front of the neck, wrapped around the upper trachea and joined across the midline by a narrow bridge of tissue? The answer is the thyroid gland. Its two lobes lie on either side of the airway and the isthmus crosses in front of it. The gland rises when the person swallows, because the pretracheal fascia binds it to the larynx.

Question 8102. Which artery is the first branch of the external carotid artery and runs down to the upper pole of the thyroid gland: the superior thyroid artery, the inferior thyroid artery, the lingual artery, or the facial artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery. It leaves the external carotid just above the carotid bifurcation and reaches the upper pole. The inferior thyroid artery comes from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery.

Question 8103. Which nerve lies in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus, immediately posterior to the lateral lobe of the thyroid gland? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It climbs in that groove to reach the larynx, where it supplies every laryngeal muscle except the cricothyroid. The lateral lobe of the gland lies directly in front of it.

Question 8104. Which layer lies immediately deep to the strap muscles and forms a false capsule around the thyroid gland: the investing layer of deep cervical fascia, the pretracheal fascia, the prevertebral fascia, or the carotid sheath? The answer is the pretracheal fascia. It encloses the gland and binds it to the larynx, which is why the gland moves with swallowing. The strap muscles lie superficial to it.

Question 8105. What is the name of the narrow band of thyroid tissue that crosses the midline in front of the second, third and fourth tracheal rings? The answer is the isthmus. It joins the two lobes across the front of the airway and lies within the pretracheal fascia. A small pyramidal lobe may rise from it as a remnant of the thyroglossal duct.

Question 8106. A constructed illustration: after an operation on the front of the neck the voice is hoarse and one vocal cord does not move. Which nerve has most likely been injured? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It runs in the tracheoesophageal groove behind the thyroid lobe, where it is easily caught or stretched. The external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve lies higher and weakens the cricothyroid instead.

Question 8107. Which nerve runs with the superior thyroid artery toward the upper pole and is at risk when that artery is tied very high: the recurrent laryngeal nerve, the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, or the ansa cervicalis? The answer is the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. It supplies the cricothyroid muscle, so its injury lowers the pitch of the voice.

Question 8108. A described transverse section of the neck shows a gland wrapped across the front of the airway, with a shield-shaped cartilage above it and the trachea behind. Which gland is shown, and which cartilage lies above it? The answer is the thyroid gland, with the thyroid cartilage above it. The cricoid cartilage lies just below the thyroid cartilage.

Question 8109. True or false: the thyroid gland has a true fibrous capsule that sends septa into its substance, and a looser false capsule formed by the pretracheal fascia. The answer is true. The thin true capsule adheres closely to the gland. The pretracheal fascia lies outside it, separated from it by a small amount of loose tissue.

Question 8110. Which artery arises from the thyrocervical trunk and supplies the lower pole of the thyroid gland: the inferior thyroid artery, the superior thyroid artery, the thyroidea ima artery, or the ascending pharyngeal artery? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery. It passes behind the carotid sheath and then turns medially to reach the gland. It also supplies the parathyroid glands.

Question 8111. Which veins drain the lower part of the thyroid gland directly into the brachiocephalic vein, without joining the internal jugular vein? The answer is the inferior thyroid veins. They descend in front of the trachea in the midline. The superior and middle thyroid veins drain into the internal jugular vein instead.

Question 8112. The inferior thyroid artery passes behind the carotid sheath and then turns medially toward the gland. Which nerve does it meet on that course, and why does the crossing matter? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve, which may pass in front of the artery or behind it. That variable crossing makes the nerve easy to injure when the artery is tied.

Question 8113. A constructed illustration: a child has a smooth swelling in the midline of the neck that moves upward both when swallowing and when the tongue is pushed forward. From which structure does the swelling arise? The answer is a remnant of the thyroglossal duct. The duct descends from the foramen cecum of the tongue to the thyroid gland, and a cyst may form anywhere along that path.

Question 8114. Which hormone is secreted by the parafollicular cells of the thyroid gland: calcitonin, thyroxine, triiodothyronine, or parathyroid hormone? The answer is calcitonin. These cells, also called C cells, lie between the follicles and lower the level of calcium in the blood. Thyroxine and triiodothyronine come from the follicular cells.

Question 8115. A described microscopic section of an endocrine gland shows many rounded sacs lined by a single layer of cuboidal cells and filled with a pink, glassy material. Which gland is shown, and what is the material inside the sacs? The answer is the thyroid gland, with colloid inside its follicles. The colloid stores the precursor of the thyroid hormones.

Question 8116. Which pair belongs together: the superior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery, the superior thyroid artery with the subclavian artery, the inferior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery, or the inferior thyroid artery with the facial artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery. The inferior thyroid artery arises from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery.

Question 8117. Which small artery, present in only a minority of people, may ascend in the midline in front of the trachea to reach the isthmus of the thyroid gland: the thyroidea ima artery, the ascending cervical artery, the transverse cervical artery, or the suprascapular artery? The answer is the thyroidea ima artery. It usually arises from the brachiocephalic trunk and is a hazard in midline surgery of the neck.

Question 8118. Which ring of cartilage lies immediately above the first tracheal ring, is the only complete ring of the airway, and sits just above the isthmus of the thyroid gland? The answer is the cricoid cartilage. It is narrow at the front and broad at the back, where the arytenoid cartilages sit. The thyroid cartilage lies above it.

Question 8119. Name two arteries that supply the thyroid gland, spoken as a list. The answer is the superior thyroid artery and the inferior thyroid artery. The superior artery supplies the upper pole and comes from the external carotid. The inferior artery supplies the lower pole and the parathyroid glands as well.

Question 8120. Which vein drains the middle of the thyroid gland and opens into the internal jugular vein: the superior thyroid vein, the middle thyroid vein, the inferior thyroid vein, or the anterior jugular vein? The answer is the middle thyroid vein. It is short and runs from the lateral surface of the lobe to the internal jugular vein. The inferior thyroid veins drain into the brachiocephalic vein.

Question 8121. A constructed illustration: a person has a bleeding mass at the base of the tongue, and no thyroid tissue can be found in the usual place in the neck. What is the mass, and why is it in the tongue? The answer is a lingual thyroid, formed when the thyroglossal duct fails to descend. The gland begins at the foramen cecum and travels downward, so arrest leaves it high.

Question 8122. A described sagittal section of the neck shows a narrow tract running from the back of the tongue downward, passing in front of the hyoid bone and the laryngeal cartilages. What is this tract, and what does it become in the adult? The answer is the thyroglossal duct, which normally disappears. Its only remnant in the adult is the foramen cecum at the back of the tongue.

Question 8123. Which structure lies immediately lateral to the thyroid gland and contains the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve? The answer is the carotid sheath. It lies posterolateral to the lobe, separated from it by the pretracheal fascia. The recurrent laryngeal nerve lies between the gland and the trachea instead.

Question 8124. Which posterolateral projection of the thyroid lobe is a condensation of connective tissue that acts as a landmark for the recurrent laryngeal nerve at operation: the pyramidal lobe, the tubercle of Zuckerkandl, the isthmus, or the ligament of Berry? The answer is the tubercle of Zuckerkandl. When it is present, the nerve usually lies just medial to it.

Question 8125. In a transverse section of the neck, where does the isthmus of the thyroid gland lie relative to the trachea, and which way do the lobes extend from it? The answer is that the isthmus lies in front of the trachea, and the lobes extend backward and laterally on each side. The gland therefore wraps around the front and the sides of the airway.

Question 8126. Which small glands, usually four in number, sit on the posterior surface of the thyroid gland and produce parathyroid hormone? The answer is the parathyroid glands. There are normally two superior and two inferior glands, each about the size of a small pea. They are brownish yellow and lie within the capsule of the thyroid gland.

Question 8127. The superior parathyroid glands develop from which pharyngeal pouch: the third, the fourth, the second, or the first? The answer is the fourth pharyngeal pouch. The third pouch gives rise to the inferior parathyroid glands and to the thymus, which is why an inferior gland may be found in the chest.

Question 8128. A described posterior view of the thyroid gland shows four small brownish bodies, two near the upper poles and two near the lower poles, each lying within the capsule. Which glands are shown? The answer is the parathyroid glands. Their position on the posterior border keeps them close to the inferior thyroid artery and its branches.

Question 8129. Which structure must be identified and preserved when an inferior parathyroid gland is removed, because it runs in the tracheoesophageal groove immediately medial to the gland? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. It lies in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus, behind the lobe of the thyroid gland. Injury to it paralyses the muscles of the larynx.

Question 8130. Which parathyroid gland is more constant in position, and where does it usually lie? The answer is the superior gland, which usually lies near the middle of the posterior border of the thyroid lobe. It sits about one centimeter above the point where the inferior thyroid artery enters the gland.

Question 8131. Which artery provides the main blood supply to the parathyroid glands: the superior thyroid artery, the inferior thyroid artery, the thyroidea ima artery, or the ascending pharyngeal artery? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery. It supplies both pairs of glands in most people and also supplies the lower pole of the thyroid gland.

Question 8132. A constructed illustration: some days after an operation on the front of the neck, a person reports tingling around the mouth and in the hands. Which glands were most likely disturbed? The answer is the parathyroid glands, with a fall in the level of calcium in the blood. They lie within the thyroid capsule and depend on the inferior thyroid artery.

Question 8133. Which cells of the parathyroid gland are the most numerous and secrete parathyroid hormone? The answer is the chief cells, also called principal cells. They are small and pale with dark nuclei, and they lie in cords among the capillaries. The larger oxyphil cells are fewer and appear only after childhood.

Question 8134. Parathyroid hormone raises the level of which substance in the blood: calcium, phosphate, potassium, or sodium? The answer is calcium. The hormone also lowers the level of phosphate and acts on bone, on the kidney and on the gut. Its release is controlled by the calcium level in the blood itself.

Question 8135. True or false: every person has exactly four parathyroid glands. The answer is false. Four is the usual number, but five or more glands occur, and fewer than four is also possible. The glands are small, so an extra one is easily missed at operation.

Question 8136. Which pair belongs together: the superior parathyroid glands with the fourth pharyngeal pouch, the inferior parathyroid glands with the fourth pharyngeal pouch, the superior parathyroid glands with the third pharyngeal pouch, or the inferior parathyroid glands with the second pharyngeal pouch? The answer is the superior glands with the fourth pouch. The third pouch gives the inferior glands and the thymus.

Question 8137. A described microscopic section of a small gland shows cords of pale cells with dark nuclei and a rich network of capillaries, with no rounded follicles and no colloid. Which gland is shown? The answer is a parathyroid gland. Its solid cords and its store of fat cells distinguish it from the thyroid gland.

Question 8138. The inferior parathyroid gland lies near the lower pole of the thyroid gland. Which two structures are its closest important neighbors, and why does its position vary so much? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve and the inferior thyroid artery. The gland develops from the third pouch with the thymus and descends far, so it may be carried into the chest.

Question 8139. Why are the inferior parathyroid glands more variable in position than the superior glands: they descend further with the thymus, they develop from the fourth pouch, they lack a blood supply, or they lie inside the thyroid substance? The answer is that they descend further with the thymus. The superior glands come from the fourth pouch and move a shorter distance.

Question 8140. Which cells of the parathyroid gland are larger, stain more deeply, and appear only after childhood? The answer is the oxyphil cells. They are thought to be inactive or degenerating chief cells and have no known separate secretion. They increase in number as a person grows older.

Question 8141. A constructed illustration: while a parathyroid gland is being removed from the tracheoesophageal groove, the surgeon works close to a nerve that supplies the larynx. Which nerve is it, and what would injury cause? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. Injury causes hoarseness and weakness of the vocal cord on that side.

Question 8142. Parathyroid hormone acts on which organ to increase the reabsorption of calcium: the kidney, the liver, the spleen, or the pancreas? The answer is the kidney. There it also increases the excretion of phosphate and helps to produce the active form of vitamin D, which raises the absorption of calcium from the gut.

Question 8143. A described computed tomography section of the neck shows a rounded soft tissue mass below the lower pole of the left thyroid lobe, separate from the gland, with the esophagus medial to it. Which gland is the most likely source of the mass? The answer is an inferior parathyroid gland, which may lie below the lower pole. Its long descent with the thymus explains this position.

Question 8144. Name the two arteries that may supply a parathyroid gland, spoken as a list. The answer is the inferior thyroid artery and the superior thyroid artery. The inferior artery is the main supply in most people, and the two vessels anastomose with one another along the posterior border of the thyroid lobe.

Question 8145. Which structure lies immediately lateral to the parathyroid glands when they occupy their usual place on the posterior border of the thyroid lobe? The answer is the carotid sheath, containing the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve. The tracheoesophageal groove lies on the medial side of the gland.

Question 8146. About how large is a normal parathyroid gland, and what color is it when it is seen at operation? The answer is about 5 millimeters long, the size of a grain of rice, and brownish yellow. Its small size and its color make it easy to confuse with a lobule of fat.

Question 8147. Which statement about the blood supply of the parathyroid glands is correct: the inferior thyroid artery supplies both pairs in most people, the superior thyroid artery is the only supply, the glands are supplied by the thyroid veins, or each gland has a single end artery? The answer is that the inferior thyroid artery supplies both pairs. Fine branches also come from the superior thyroid artery, and together they form a plexus.

Question 8148. A constructed illustration: a person has a high calcium level that persists after one enlarged parathyroid gland has been removed, and no second gland can be found in the neck. Where might that gland lie? The answer is in the mediastinum, within or beside the thymus. An inferior gland develops from the third pouch with the thymus and may descend into the chest.

Question 8149. A described microscopic section of an enlarged parathyroid gland shows sheets of chief cells with almost no fat, while a normal gland has fat cells throughout its stroma. What does the loss of fat indicate? The answer is an overactive gland. Normal parathyroid tissue contains much fat, which is replaced when the gland becomes hyperplastic or adenomatous.

Question 8150. Into which vessels do the parathyroid veins drain: the thyroid veins, the internal jugular vein directly, the azygos vein, or the vertebral veins? The answer is the thyroid veins. The parathyroid glands lie within the capsule of the thyroid gland, so their small veins join the venous plexus of the thyroid gland and follow the thyroid veins.

Question 8151. From which pharyngeal pouch do the inferior parathyroid glands and the thymus arise together? The answer is the third pharyngeal pouch. The two structures travel downward together, and the thymus drags the parathyroid gland with it. That shared journey explains why an inferior gland may be found in the chest.

Question 8152. True or false: the parathyroid glands lie outside the fibrous capsule of the thyroid gland and can be lifted away without opening that capsule. The answer is false. They usually lie within the capsule on the posterior surface, although they may lie outside it in some people. The capsule must be opened to reach them.

Question 8153. Which pair belongs together: parathyroid hormone with a raised calcium level, parathyroid hormone with a lowered calcium level, calcitonin with a raised calcium level, or calcitonin with a raised phosphate level? The answer is parathyroid hormone with a raised calcium level. Calcitonin does the opposite and lowers the level of calcium in the blood.

Question 8154. A described posterior view of the neck shows a small gland behind the upper pole of the thyroid lobe near the cricothyroid junction, and another gland below the lower pole near the thymus. Which glands are shown, and which one is more likely to lie in the chest? The answer is the parathyroid glands, and the lower one is the more likely.

Question 8155. Which chain of ganglia lies behind the carotid sheath, posterior to the thyroid and parathyroid glands, and may be injured in a deep dissection of the posterior border? The answer is the sympathetic trunk. Injury to it on one side causes a drooping eyelid and a constricted pupil. It lies against the prevertebral muscles.

Question 8156. Which feature distinguishes a parathyroid gland from a thyroid gland in a section: the parathyroid has follicles filled with colloid, the parathyroid has cords of cells and much fat, the parathyroid is lined by ciliated cells, or the parathyroid contains thyroid follicles? The answer is cords of cells and much fat. The thyroid gland has follicles filled with colloid.

Question 8157. Which statement about parathyroid hormone is correct: it raises the calcium level by acting on bone and kidney, it lowers the calcium level by acting on bone, it is secreted by the thyroid follicles, or it is stored in colloid? The answer is that it raises the calcium level by acting on bone and kidney. It is not stored, so it is released as it is made.

Question 8158. What is the name of the hormone secreted by the chief cells of the parathyroid gland? The answer is parathyroid hormone. It is the main regulator of the level of calcium in the blood and of the balance of calcium in bone. Its secretion rises when the calcium level in the blood falls.

Question 8159. In a transverse section of the neck at the level of the thyroid isthmus, place these structures from front to back: the parathyroid gland, the trachea, the prevertebral fascia, and the esophagus. The answer is the trachea in front, with the parathyroid gland behind and lateral to it, the esophagus behind the trachea, and the prevertebral fascia behind them all.

Question 8160. Which change in the blood does parathyroid hormone produce: it raises calcium and lowers phosphate, it raises both calcium and phosphate, it lowers calcium and raises phosphate, or it lowers both? The answer is that it raises calcium and lowers phosphate. Those two movements together keep the product of the two from rising.

Question 8161. A described section of an enlarged parathyroid gland shows two cell types, one small and pale and one large and deeply staining, with a rim of compressed normal tissue at the edge. What does that rim most likely represent? The answer is normal gland compressed by an adenoma. A rim of normal tissue favors an adenoma rather than hyperplasia.

Question 8162. Which gland lies deep to the pretracheal fascia on the posterior border of the thyroid lobe, in the space bounded by the carotid sheath on the lateral side and the tracheoesophageal groove on the medial side? The answer is a parathyroid gland. It lies within the thyroid capsule, which is why it moves with the thyroid gland when the person swallows.

Question 8163. Which two pairs of small glands lie on the posterior surface of the thyroid gland? The answer is the superior parathyroid glands and the inferior parathyroid glands. There are usually four in all, and each pair sits within the capsule on the posterior border of a lobe.

Question 8164. Which statement about the position of the superior parathyroid gland is correct: it lies near the cricothyroid junction, it lies below the inferior thyroid artery, it lies within the thymus, or it lies in the carotid sheath? The answer is that it lies near the cricothyroid junction, about a centimeter above the point where the inferior thyroid artery enters the gland.

Question 8165. A constructed illustration: a person has muscle spasm and tingling after an operation on the thyroid gland, and the level of calcium in the blood is low. Which structures were injured or deprived of their blood supply? The answer is the parathyroid glands. They lie within the thyroid capsule and depend on the inferior thyroid artery, so they are at risk in that operation.

Question 8166. A described operative view of the posterior surface of the thyroid lobe shows a small brown gland with a vessel entering its hilum from below and the recurrent laryngeal nerve crossing deep to it. Which gland is shown? The answer is a parathyroid gland. Its main supply is the inferior thyroid artery, which enters from below.

Question 8167. Why does removal of the thyroid gland put the parathyroid glands at risk even when the surgeon stays outside the thyroid capsule: the glands may lie outside the capsule, the glands lie in the carotid sheath, the glands are supplied by the thyroid veins, or the glands lie in the mediastinum in most people? The answer is that the glands may lie outside the capsule, and their artery is at risk as well.

Question 8168. Which pair belongs together: calcitonin with a lowered calcium level, calcitonin with a raised calcium level, parathyroid hormone with a lowered calcium level, or parathyroid hormone with a lowered phosphate level? The answer is calcitonin with a lowered calcium level. Calcitonin comes from the thyroid gland, while parathyroid hormone raises calcium and lowers phosphate.

Question 8169. Which parathyroid glands lie at the lower poles of the thyroid gland and are supplied mainly by the inferior thyroid artery? The answer is the inferior parathyroid glands. They are the more variable pair, because they develop from the third pharyngeal pouch and descend with the thymus into the upper chest.

Question 8170. Which statement about the parathyroid glands is correct: they are usually four in number and lie on the posterior surface of the thyroid gland, they lie in front of the thyroid gland, they are part of the thyroid gland, or they lie within the thymus in every adult? The answer is that they are usually four and lie on the posterior surface.

Question 8171. A described computed tomography section of the upper chest shows a small rounded soft tissue mass beside the arch of the aorta, below the lower pole of the thyroid gland. Which gland might this be, and why is it there? The answer is an inferior parathyroid gland that descended with the thymus into the mediastinum during development.

Question 8172. Which cell does parathyroid hormone act on first in bone, and how does that raise the level of calcium: it stimulates the osteoblast, which then activates the osteoclast, it stimulates the osteoclast directly, it inhibits the osteoblast only, or it acts on cartilage cells? The answer is that it stimulates the osteoblast, which then activates the osteoclast.

Question 8173. Which description of the actions of parathyroid hormone is correct: it raises blood calcium, lowers blood phosphate and increases the active form of vitamin D, it lowers blood calcium and raises phosphate, it acts only on bone, or it acts only on the gut? The answer is the first description. The hormone works on bone, on the kidney and on the gut together.

Question 8174. True or false: each parathyroid gland is supplied by a single end artery, so that tying one small vessel always destroys the gland. The answer is false. The glands are supplied by a fine plexus fed by the inferior and superior thyroid arteries, so flow may continue through another branch.

Question 8175. Which hormone lowers the level of calcium in the blood and comes from the thyroid gland rather than from the parathyroid glands? The answer is calcitonin, from the parafollicular cells. It inhibits the osteoclasts and so reduces the release of calcium from bone. Parathyroid hormone has the opposite effect.

Question 8176. Which paired glands sit on the upper poles of the kidneys, one on each side, in the fatty tissue behind the peritoneum? The answer is the adrenal glands, also called the suprarenal glands. Each is enclosed in the same fatty capsule and renal fascia as the kidney, so it moves with the kidney when that organ descends.

Question 8177. Which vein drains the right adrenal gland: the right renal vein, the inferior vena cava, the right hepatic vein, or the azygos vein? The answer is the inferior vena cava. The right adrenal vein is very short and enters the vena cava directly, which makes it easy to tear. The left vein drains into the left renal vein.

Question 8178. Which large vein lies immediately in front of and medial to the right adrenal gland? The answer is the inferior vena cava. The right gland is squeezed between the liver above and the vena cava on its medial side, and that pressure gives it its triangular shape. The left gland lies beside the aorta instead.

Question 8179. A described transverse section of the abdomen shows a triangular gland against the back wall, with the liver in front of it and a large vein at its medial edge. Which gland is shown, and on which side of the body does it lie? The answer is the right adrenal gland, which lies on the right side.

Question 8180. Which three zones make up the cortex of the adrenal gland, from the outside inward? The answer is the zona glomerulosa, the zona fasciculata and the zona reticularis. The outer zone makes mineralocorticoids, the middle zone makes glucocorticoids, and the inner zone makes androgens.

Question 8181. A constructed illustration: a person loses weight, has low blood pressure and notices darkening of the skin, and both adrenal glands are found to be destroyed. Which part of the gland is failing, and which hormones are lacking? The answer is the cortex of the gland, with cortisol and aldosterone lacking. The management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8182. The superior suprarenal artery is a branch of which vessel: the inferior phrenic artery, the aorta, the renal artery, or the celiac trunk? The answer is the inferior phrenic artery. The middle suprarenal artery comes from the aorta, and the inferior suprarenal artery comes from the renal artery.

Question 8183. Which cells of the adrenal medulla secrete epinephrine and norepinephrine? The answer is the chromaffin cells. They lie in clusters around the medullary veins and release their hormones into the blood when the sympathetic nerves fire. The cortex around them makes steroid hormones instead.

Question 8184. True or false: the adrenal medulla develops from the same tissue as the sympathetic ganglia. The answer is true. Both come from neural crest cells, which is why the medulla behaves like a modified sympathetic ganglion. The cortex develops from a different layer altogether.

Question 8185. The cortex of the adrenal gland develops from which tissue: intermediate mesoderm, neural crest, endoderm of the gut, or ectoderm of the skin? The answer is intermediate mesoderm. The medulla comes from neural crest cells instead, so the two parts of the gland have separate origins and separate functions.

Question 8186. A described microscopic section of an adrenal gland shows an outer layer of rounded clusters, a wide middle layer of straight cords, and an inner layer of branching cords around a central mass of pale cells. Which layers are shown? The answer is the three zones of the cortex, surrounding the medulla.

Question 8187. Which structures lie immediately in front of the left adrenal gland, and what separates the gland from the peritoneal cavity? The answer is the pancreas and the spleen, with the stomach above them. The peritoneum covers the front of the gland, and the pancreas lies directly against it.

Question 8188. A constructed illustration: a person has episodes of high blood pressure and palpitations, and a tumor of the adrenal medulla is found. Which cells are involved, and where do they lie? The answer is the chromaffin cells of the medulla, in the center of the gland. They release epinephrine and norepinephrine into the blood.

Question 8189. The left adrenal vein drains into which vessel: the inferior vena cava, the left renal vein, the left gonadal vein, or the splenic vein? The answer is the left renal vein. The left vein is longer than the right one and joins the renal vein before that vessel crosses in front of the aorta.

Question 8190. Which pair belongs together: the zona glomerulosa with mineralocorticoids, the zona fasciculata with mineralocorticoids, the zona reticularis with glucocorticoids, or the medulla with androgens? The answer is the zona glomerulosa with mineralocorticoids. The fasciculata makes glucocorticoids, the reticularis makes androgens, and the medulla makes catecholamines.

Question 8191. A described computed tomography section of the upper abdomen shows a small triangular gland on the right, between the liver and the inferior vena cava, and a larger crescent-shaped gland on the left, medial to the spleen. Which glands are shown? The answer is the right and left adrenal glands.

Question 8192. Name two arteries that supply the adrenal gland apart from the superior suprarenal artery, spoken as a list. The answer is the middle suprarenal artery from the aorta and the inferior suprarenal artery from the renal artery. The three arteries anastomose, so the gland has no end arteries.

Question 8193. Which zone of the adrenal cortex lies immediately deep to the capsule and secretes aldosterone? The answer is the zona glomerulosa. It is the outermost and narrowest zone, arranged in rounded clusters. It is the only source of aldosterone, which controls the balance of sodium and potassium.

Question 8194. Why does the right adrenal gland have a pyramidal shape while the left is crescent-shaped: the right gland is compressed between the liver and the inferior vena cava, the left gland contains more medulla, the right gland develops from different tissue, or the left gland is covered by peritoneum? The answer is that the right gland is compressed between the liver and the vena cava.

Question 8195. A constructed illustration: during an operation on the left kidney, bleeding comes from a short vein that joins the renal vein near the hilum. Which vessel is it likely to be, and where does it drain? The answer is the left suprarenal vein. It joins the left renal vein, which then crosses in front of the aorta.

Question 8196. Which layer of fat surrounds the adrenal gland and the kidney, and which fascia encloses both of them? The answer is the perirenal fat, enclosed by the renal fascia. The adrenal gland lies within that fascia and moves with the kidney when the kidney descends toward the pelvis.

Question 8197. A described magnetic resonance imaging section shows a gland sitting on the upper pole of the left kidney, behind the pancreas and medial to the spleen. Which gland is shown, and which vein will drain it? The answer is the left adrenal gland, drained by the left suprarenal vein into the left renal vein.

Question 8198. Which zone of the adrenal cortex lies deepest, next to the medulla, and produces androgens? The answer is the zona reticularis. It is the innermost zone, arranged in branching cords around the medulla. Its androgens are weak compared with those produced by the testis.

Question 8199. Which statement about the arterial supply of the adrenal gland is correct: it is supplied by three arteries on each side, it has a single end artery, it is supplied only by the middle suprarenal artery, or it is supplied by the renal vein? The answer is that it is supplied by three arteries on each side. They anastomose freely, so tying one does not usually cause infarction.

Question 8200. A small mass of adrenal tissue is found at operation along the course of the gonadal vessels in the pelvis, separate from the gland. Which of these is it most likely to be: an adrenal rest, a parathyroid rest, a thyroid rest, or a thymic rest? The answer is an adrenal rest, which is cortical tissue carried along with the descending gonad.

To summarize, the thyroid gland lies in front of and beside the upper trachea, joined by an isthmus that crosses in front of the second to fourth tracheal rings, and it is held to the larynx by the pretracheal fascia, so it rises with swallowing. Its blood comes from the superior thyroid artery of the external carotid and the inferior thyroid artery of the thyrocervical trunk, and its veins drain to the internal jugular and brachiocephalic veins, while the recurrent laryngeal nerve runs in the tracheoesophageal groove immediately behind each lobe. The four parathyroid glands lie within the capsule on the posterior surface of the thyroid lobes, the superior pair from the fourth pharyngeal pouch and the inferior pair from the third pouch with the thymus, which is why an inferior gland may be found in the mediastinum. They are supplied mainly by the inferior thyroid artery, their chief cells secrete parathyroid hormone, and that hormone raises the level of calcium and lowers the level of phosphate by acting on bone, kidney and gut. The adrenal glands cap the upper poles of the kidneys behind the peritoneum, each with a cortex of three zones and a medulla of chromaffin cells that arises from neural crest, and while three arteries supply each gland, the right vein drains to the inferior vena cava and the left vein drains to the left renal vein.
