# Chapter 90 — Thorax, Part Two

This chapter continues the regional anatomy of the trunk, taking the thorax first and the abdomen second. The first half works through the mediastinum and its divisions, the heart and its coverings, the great vessels, the trachea and the bronchi, the lungs and the pleura, and the thoracic wall and the diaphragm. The second half follows the peritoneum and its folds, the foregut organs, the midgut and the hindgut, the posterior abdominal wall with its vessels, nerves and kidneys, the inguinal region, and the surface landmarks of the abdomen. It holds one hundred questions, and every one of them gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The questions are written to be answered out loud from the relations of each structure rather than from a picture. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 8901. A described section of the chest is cut across the trunk through the disc between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae. Which plane is this, and what does it separate? The answer is the transverse thoracic plane, which separates the superior mediastinum above from the inferior mediastinum below. The sternal angle marks it in front and the same disc marks it behind.

Question 8902. What name is given to the central compartment of the chest that lies between the two pleural cavities and holds the heart and the great vessels? The answer is the mediastinum. It extends from the superior thoracic aperture above to the diaphragm below. The lungs and their pleural coverings lie on either side of it.

Question 8903. Which part of the mediastinum contains the heart and the pericardium: the superior mediastinum, the anterior mediastinum, the middle mediastinum, or the posterior mediastinum? The answer is the middle mediastinum. It lies between the anterior mediastinum in front and the posterior mediastinum behind. The superior mediastinum lies above the heart.

Question 8904. Which subdivision of the mediastinum lies immediately behind the sternum and in front of the pericardium, and holds loose fat and, in the child, the thymus? The answer is the anterior mediastinum. It is the narrowest of the divisions. In the adult it holds little more than connective tissue.

Question 8905. Which structures lie in the posterior mediastinum, immediately in front of the bodies of the thoracic vertebrae? The answer is the esophagus, the descending thoracic aorta and the thoracic duct, with the azygos vein on the right. The esophagus lies against the vertebral column, and the thoracic duct runs behind it.

Question 8906. A described frontal radiograph of the chest shows the heart as a shadow in the middle of the thorax. Which chamber forms most of the right border of that shadow? The answer is the right atrium. The left ventricle forms the left border and the apex.

Question 8907. Which statement about the fibrous pericardium is correct: it is a single layer of serous membrane, a tough sac fused below to the central tendon of the diaphragm, the lining of the lungs, or the membrane that secretes pleural fluid? The answer is the tough sac fused below to the central tendon of the diaphragm. It is inelastic, so fluid collecting inside it can compress the heart.

Question 8908. What is the name of the smooth serous membrane that lines the inside of the fibrous pericardium and is reflected onto the surface of the heart? The answer is the serous pericardium. Its parietal layer lines the fibrous sac, and its visceral layer, the epicardium, covers the heart.

Question 8909. Which nerve runs downward in front of the root of the lung, between the fibrous pericardium and the mediastinal pleura, and supplies the diaphragm? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It is joined on its course by the pericardiophrenic vessels. The vagus nerve lies behind the root of the lung instead.

Question 8910. A constructed illustration: after a wound to the left side of the chest, the heart sounds become muffled and the neck veins are distended. Which space has filled with blood, and what limits its expansion? The answer is the pericardial cavity, limited by the tough fibrous pericardium, which prevents the heart from filling. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8911. The transverse pericardial sinus is a passage in the pericardial cavity. Between which structures does it lie: between the aorta and the pulmonary trunk in front and the superior vena cava behind, between the two atria, between the two ventricles, or between the heart and the lungs? The answer is between the aorta and the pulmonary trunk in front and the superior vena cava behind. A ligature passed through it controls the great vessels.

Question 8912. A described computed tomography section through the chest at the level of the heart shows the four chambers in one plane. Which chamber lies most anterior, immediately behind the sternum? The answer is the right ventricle. The right atrium lies to its right and the left atrium behind it.

Question 8913. True or false: the left atrium is the most posterior chamber of the heart and lies immediately in front of the esophagus. The answer is true. An enlarged left atrium can press on the esophagus and make swallowing difficult. On a described barium swallow the atrium makes a smooth impression on its front.

Question 8914. Which vein runs in the posterior part of the coronary sulcus, collects most of the blood from the heart muscle, and opens into the right atrium just above the opening of the inferior vena cava? The answer is the coronary sinus. The great cardiac vein is its largest tributary.

Question 8915. Which artery arises from the left aortic sinus, passes between the left atrium and the pulmonary trunk, and divides into the anterior interventricular and circumflex branches: the right coronary artery, the left coronary artery, the circumflex artery alone, or the posterior interventricular artery? The answer is the left coronary artery. The right coronary artery arises from the right aortic sinus and runs in the coronary sulcus.

Question 8916. Which structure lies immediately behind the manubrium and in front of the trachea, and is large in the child but regresses in the adult? The answer is the thymus. In the adult it is replaced largely by fat and connective tissue. It lies in the anterior part of the superior mediastinum.

Question 8917. Which of these pairs belongs together: the right atrium with the opening of the coronary sinus, the left atrium with the opening of the superior vena cava, the right ventricle with the mitral valve, or the left ventricle with the tricuspid valve? The answer is the right atrium with the opening of the coronary sinus. The mitral valve guards the left atrioventricular opening instead.

Question 8918. Which great vessel arises from the right ventricle and lies immediately in front of and to the left of the ascending aorta: the pulmonary trunk, the superior vena cava, the arch of the aorta, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the pulmonary trunk. It carries venous blood to the lungs.

Question 8919. A constructed illustration: a young person has high blood pressure in the arms and weak pulses in the legs. Which short segment of the aorta is narrowed, and which remnant marks the site? The answer is the aortic isthmus, just distal to the left subclavian artery, at the ligamentum arteriosum. Blood reaches the lower body through collateral channels.

Question 8920. Which fibrous remnant connects the left pulmonary artery to the under surface of the arch of the aorta, and which fetal vessel did it carry blood through? The answer is the ligamentum arteriosum, the remains of the ductus arteriosus. In the fetus that vessel carried blood from the pulmonary trunk to the aorta.

Question 8921. A described computed tomography image of the superior mediastinum shows a vessel arching over the right main bronchus and joining the superior vena cava. Which vein is described? The answer is the azygos vein. It ascends on the right of the vertebral column and drains the posterior intercostal veins.

Question 8922. How many branches arise from the arch of the aorta, and which one is given off first: one, the left subclavian artery; two, the left common carotid artery; three, the brachiocephalic trunk; or four, the right subclavian artery? The answer is three, the brachiocephalic trunk first. Then follow the left common carotid and the left subclavian arteries.

Question 8923. Which tube begins at the lower border of the larynx, lies in the midline of the neck and the superior mediastinum, and divides into two main bronchi? The answer is the trachea. It ends at the carina, at the level of the sternal angle. Its wall is reinforced by C-shaped rings of cartilage.

Question 8924. In the superior mediastinum, which structure lies immediately in front of the trachea, and which lies immediately behind it? The answer is the arch of the aorta in front and the esophagus behind. The arch crosses from right to left in front of the windpipe.

Question 8925. Name two structures that lie immediately in front of the trachea in the superior mediastinum. The answer is the arch of the aorta and the left brachiocephalic vein, with the thymus in front of both in the child. The brachiocephalic vein crosses the midline from left to right.

Question 8926. Which main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical, so that an inhaled foreign body is more likely to enter it: the right, the left, both equally, or the trachea itself? The answer is the right main bronchus. The left is longer and more horizontal because it passes under the arch of the aorta.

Question 8927. A described frontal radiograph of the chest shows a dark space in the lower left corner, with the lung edge retracted and the diaphragm blunted. Which space has filled with air? The answer is the pleural cavity, between the visceral and the parietal pleura. The two layers are normally held together by surface tension.

Question 8928. Which structure lies most posteriorly in the root of each lung, behind the pulmonary artery and the pulmonary veins? The answer is the main bronchus. On the right side the eparterial bronchus lies above the pulmonary artery. The bronchus is the most posterior structure of the root.

Question 8929. How many bronchopulmonary segments are usually found in the right lung and in the left: ten in each, ten on the right and eight or nine on the left, eight on the right and ten on the left, or twelve in each? The answer is ten on the right and eight or nine on the left. Each segment has its own bronchus and its own artery. The left lung loses one or two segments to the lingula.

Question 8930. A constructed illustration: after a fracture of a rib, a person has sudden pain and breathlessness, and the described radiograph shows the lung edge separated from the chest wall. Which space has filled with air, and what has been breached? The answer is the pleural cavity, breached through the parietal pleura and the chest wall, so that the lung recoils toward the hilum.

Question 8931. Which fissure of the lung runs obliquely from the upper part of the posterior border down to the lower part of the anterior border, and is present on both sides? The answer is the oblique fissure. It separates the upper from the lower lobe on the left, and the upper and middle from the lower lobe on the right.

Question 8932. Which pair belongs together: the right lung with two fissures, the left lung with three lobes, the horizontal fissure with the left lung, or the cardiac notch with the right lung? The answer is the right lung with two fissures, the oblique and the horizontal. The left lung has two lobes and no horizontal fissure.

Question 8933. The tongue-shaped projection of lung tissue that lies below the cardiac notch, and corresponds to the middle lobe of the other lung, is named what: the lingula, the eparterial bronchus, the apex, or the hilum? The answer is the lingula, which belongs to the upper lobe of the left lung. It is made of two bronchopulmonary segments.

Question 8934. A described computed tomography section of the chest at the level of the carina shows a dark air-filled tube dividing into two. Which structure lies immediately behind the carina and behind the left atrium? The answer is the esophagus. It runs down the posterior mediastinum in front of the descending aorta.

Question 8935. What is the name of the dome of pleura that rises above the medial third of the clavicle into the root of the neck, and is covered by a fibrous membrane? The answer is the cupola, or cervical pleura. The suprapleural membrane covers it, and the apex of the lung lies inside it.

Question 8936. Within an intercostal space, in what order do the intercostal vein, artery and nerve lie from above downward, and where do they run? The answer is vein, artery and nerve, in the costal groove on the under surface of the rib above. They are protected there from a needle passed above the rib below.

Question 8937. The internal thoracic artery descends behind the costal cartilages beside the sternum and ends by dividing into which two arteries: the superior epigastric and the musculophrenic, the inferior epigastric and the superficial epigastric, the pericardiophrenic and the phrenic, or the intercostal and the subcostal? The answer is the superior epigastric and the musculophrenic. It also gives pericardiophrenic and anterior intercostal branches.

Question 8938. A described frontal radiograph of the chest shows the right dome of the diaphragm higher than the left, with a dark space between the base of the lung and the diaphragm. What is the name of that space, and which layers of pleura bound it? The answer is the costodiaphragmatic recess, bounded by the costal pleura above and the diaphragmatic pleura below.

Question 8939. What is the name of the small gap between the costal and the sternal origins of the diaphragm, through which the superior epigastric vessels and lymphatics pass? The answer is the sternocostal triangle. It lies between the two muscular slips near the lower end of the sternum, and tissue can herniate through it.

Question 8940. Which structures pass through the aortic hiatus of the diaphragm at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra: the aorta and the thoracic duct, the inferior vena cava, the esophagus and the vagus nerves, or the phrenic nerves? The answer is the aorta and the thoracic duct, with the azygos vein. The inferior vena cava has its own opening higher up.

Question 8941. A constructed illustration: a person has numbness along the inner border of the forearm and a pulse that weakens when the arm is raised above the head. Between which two structures does the lower trunk of the brachial plexus pass? The answer is between the first rib and the clavicle, over the first rib, where the subclavian vessels also cross.

Question 8942. A described computed tomography image of the chest shows a vein crossing the midline in front of the trachea, running from left to right to join another vein. Which vessel is described? The answer is the left brachiocephalic vein. It joins the right brachiocephalic vein to form the superior vena cava.

Question 8943. Which structure lies lateral to the bodies of the thoracic vertebrae, deep to the parietal pleura, and receives white and gray rami communicantes from the spinal nerves: the sympathetic trunk, the vagus nerve, the thoracic duct, or the azygos vein? The answer is the sympathetic trunk. It runs down the posterior chest wall in front of the heads of the ribs.

Question 8944. The opening for the inferior vena cava in the diaphragm lies at which vertebral level: the eighth thoracic, the tenth thoracic, the twelfth thoracic, or the fourth thoracic? The answer is the eighth thoracic. It lies in the central tendon. The esophageal opening is at the tenth level and the aortic hiatus at the twelfth.

Question 8945. True or false: the esophagus passes through the diaphragm at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra, together with the two vagus nerves. The answer is true. The esophageal opening lies in the muscular part of the diaphragm. The vagus nerves re-form below it as the anterior and posterior vagal trunks.

Question 8946. Which of these pairs belongs together: the caval opening with the eighth thoracic vertebra, the aortic hiatus with the tenth thoracic vertebra, the esophageal opening with the twelfth thoracic vertebra, or the caval opening with the twelfth thoracic vertebra? The answer is the caval opening with the eighth thoracic vertebra. The esophageal opening lies at the tenth and the aortic hiatus at the twelfth.

Question 8947. Which of these ribs is a typical rib, sharing the same features as the third to the ninth: the first, the second, the sixth, or the eleventh? The answer is the sixth. A typical rib has a head, a neck and a tubercle, and its shaft is curved and twisted. The first and the eleventh are atypical.

Question 8948. A constructed illustration: a wound at the root of the neck on the left is followed by a raised left dome of the diaphragm on the described radiograph. Which nerve has been injured, and where does it lie within the chest? The answer is the left phrenic nerve, which runs in front of the root of the lung between the pericardium and the mediastinal pleura.

Question 8949. The recurrent laryngeal nerve hooks around a different structure on each side. Which two: the left around the aortic arch and the right around the right subclavian artery, the left around the left subclavian artery and the right around the arch, both around the pulmonary arteries, or both around the carotid arteries? The answer is the left around the aortic arch and the right around the right subclavian artery, because the sixth aortic arch persists on the left.

Question 8950. A described radiograph of the chest shows the trachea pushed to the right, a dense white shadow filling the lower left chest, and the left border of the heart lost. What has collected in the pleural cavity? The answer is a large collection of fluid, which pushes the mediastinum toward the opposite side. Collapse of a lung pulls it toward the same side instead.

Question 8951. What is the name of the thin serous membrane that lines the wall of the abdominal cavity and covers the surfaces of the organs within it? The answer is the peritoneum. Its parietal layer lines the wall and its visceral layer covers the organs. The narrow space between them is the peritoneal cavity.

Question 8952. Which part of the peritoneal cavity lies behind the stomach and the lesser omentum, and communicates with the rest through a small opening: the greater sac, the lesser sac, the pleural cavity, or the retroperitoneal space? The answer is the lesser sac, also called the omental bursa. It communicates with the greater sac through the epiploic foramen.

Question 8953. Which structure forms the anterior boundary of the epiploic foramen, and which forms the posterior boundary? The answer is the free edge of the lesser omentum, containing the portal vein, in front, and the inferior vena cava behind. The foramen is the doorway between the greater and the lesser sac.

Question 8954. What is the name of the fan-shaped double layer of peritoneum that suspends the jejunum and the ileum from the posterior abdominal wall and carries their vessels? The answer is the mesentery. Its root runs obliquely from the left of the second lumbar vertebra to the right sacroiliac joint. It carries the superior mesenteric vessels.

Question 8955. A described computed tomography section of the upper abdomen shows a large organ on the right with vessels entering a deep transverse groove on its inferior surface. Which organ is shown, and what is the name of that groove? The answer is the liver, and the groove is the porta hepatis, through which the portal vein, the hepatic artery and the bile duct pass.

Question 8956. Which part of the stomach lies above the level of the cardiac opening, fills with gas when a person stands, and appears as a bubble on a described radiograph: the fundus, the body, the pyloric antrum, or the pyloric canal? The answer is the fundus, the highest part of the stomach, lying under the left dome of the diaphragm.

Question 8957. Which ring of smooth muscle guards the outlet of the stomach and controls the passage of its contents into the duodenum? The answer is the pyloric sphincter. It lies where the stomach joins the first part of the duodenum. Its thickened circular muscle forms a palpable ring.

Question 8958. A constructed illustration: a person has burning pain in the upper abdomen that wakes them at night, and the described radiograph shows a rounded projection from the first part of the duodenum. Which part of the gut is involved? The answer is the duodenal cap, the commonest site of a duodenal ulcer. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8959. The greater curvature of the stomach is supplied by which pair of arteries: the right and left gastric arteries, the right and left gastroepiploic arteries, the short gastric and the left gastric arteries, or the splenic and the hepatic arteries alone? The answer is the right and left gastroepiploic arteries, which join one another along the curvature. The gastric arteries run along the lesser curvature.

Question 8960. Which large vessels lie immediately behind the third part of the duodenum as it crosses the midline from right to left? The answer is the abdominal aorta and the inferior vena cava. The third part also crosses in front of the right ureter and the psoas muscle. The superior mesenteric artery crosses in front of the duodenum above it.

Question 8961. Which part of the pancreas lies immediately in front of the portal vein, is grooved by the superior mesenteric vessels, and joins the head to the body? The answer is the neck of the pancreas. It lies in front of the portal vein, which is formed behind it. The uncinate process hooks behind the superior mesenteric vessels.

Question 8962. A described section of the upper abdomen shows a long gland lying across the midline in front of two large vessels, with its tail reaching an organ on the left. Which gland is shown? The answer is the pancreas, whose tail reaches the spleen at its hilum. The gland lies behind the stomach.

Question 8963. True or false: the spleen lies in the left upper quadrant of the abdomen, between the fundus of the stomach and the left kidney, and its long axis follows the direction of the tenth rib. The answer is true. It lies below the left dome of the diaphragm, and it is covered by peritoneum except at its hilum.

Question 8964. Which lobe of the liver lies immediately to the left of the groove for the inferior vena cava and to the right of the fissure for the ligamentum venosum: the right lobe, the left lobe, the caudate lobe, or the quadrate lobe? The answer is the caudate lobe, on the posterior part of the inferior surface. The quadrate lobe lies further forward, between the gallbladder and the round ligament.

Question 8965. What is the name of the transverse fissure on the inferior surface of the liver through which the portal vein, the hepatic artery and the bile duct enter and leave? The answer is the porta hepatis. It lies between the caudate lobe behind and the quadrate lobe in front. All the vessels and ducts of the liver pass through it.

Question 8966. In which direction does the long axis of each kidney run, and which pole lies nearer the midline? The answer is that the long axis runs downward and laterally, so the upper pole lies nearer the midline. The right kidney lies a little lower than the left because the liver lies above it.

Question 8967. From the surface inward, which is the correct order of the coverings of the kidney: renal fascia, fat, fibrous capsule; fibrous capsule, fat, renal fascia; fat, fibrous capsule, renal fascia; or renal fascia, fibrous capsule, fat? The answer is renal fascia, then fat, then the fibrous capsule, which lies directly on the organ.

Question 8968. Which organs lie immediately in front of the right kidney, and which gland caps its upper pole? The answer is the liver above, with the second part of the duodenum and the ascending colon lower down, and the right adrenal gland capping the upper pole. The peritoneum covers the front of the kidney.

Question 8969. What is the name of the muscular tube that carries urine from the kidney to the urinary bladder and crosses in front of the psoas muscle? The answer is the ureter. It begins at the hilum of the kidney and runs downward and medially on the posterior abdominal wall. It crosses the pelvic brim in front of the common iliac artery.

Question 8970. A described radiograph of the abdomen shows a narrow tube running down from the hilum of each kidney, crossing the tips of the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae before turning into the pelvis. Which structure is shown? The answer is the ureter. It lies in front of the psoas muscle and behind the peritoneum, and it narrows at three points.

Question 8971. Which pair belongs together: the superior mesenteric artery with the foregut, the inferior mesenteric artery with the hindgut, the celiac trunk with the midgut, or the inferior mesenteric artery with the foregut? The answer is the inferior mesenteric artery with the hindgut, from the distal transverse colon to the upper rectum. The celiac trunk supplies the foregut.

Question 8972. Which three arteries arise from the celiac trunk: the left gastric, the splenic and the common hepatic; the superior mesenteric, the inferior mesenteric and the splenic; the right gastric, the right gastroepiploic and the gastroduodenal; or the phrenic, the suprarenal and the renal? The answer is the left gastric, the splenic and the common hepatic. They supply the foregut.

Question 8973. Which blind tube arises from the posteromedial aspect of the cecum, below the junction with the ileum, and is suspended by a small mesentery containing its artery? The answer is the vermiform appendix. Its base lies at the meeting point of the three taeniae of the cecum. Its position varies, and it is retrocecal in many people.

Question 8974. Which structure lies immediately above and lateral to the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, and into which canal does it open? The answer is the deep inguinal ring, which opens into the inguinal canal. The inferior epigastric artery lies medial to it and separates it from the superficial ring.

Question 8975. Name the three structures that pass through the porta hepatis. The answer is the portal vein, the proper hepatic artery and the hepatic ducts. The portal vein lies behind the other two. They are wrapped together in the free edge of the lesser omentum.

Question 8976. Blood from the abdominal part of the digestive tract, the spleen and the pancreas reaches the liver by which vein: the portal vein, the inferior vena cava, the hepatic veins, or the azygos vein? The answer is the portal vein, which carries blood rich in absorbed nutrients to the liver. The blood then leaves by the hepatic veins.

Question 8977. A described section of the upper abdomen shows a large vein running upward behind the neck of the pancreas, formed where two tributaries join. Which vein is described, and where does it end? The answer is the portal vein, formed by the splenic and the superior mesenteric veins, and it ends at the porta hepatis.

Question 8978. A constructed illustration: a swelling in the groin descends into the scrotum when the person coughs. Through which two openings does it pass, and which artery lies medial to the second of them? The answer is the deep inguinal ring and then the superficial inguinal ring, with the inferior epigastric artery medial to the deep ring.

Question 8979. What is the name of the passage about 4 centimeters long that runs obliquely through the lower part of the anterior abdominal wall, from the deep ring to the superficial ring? The answer is the inguinal canal. In the male it transmits the spermatic cord and in the female the round ligament of the uterus.

Question 8980. The anterior wall of the inguinal canal is formed mainly by which structure: the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle, the internal oblique muscle, the transversus abdominis muscle, or the rectus abdominis muscle? The answer is the aponeurosis of the external oblique. The internal oblique reinforces the lateral part of that wall.

Question 8981. True or false: the femoral canal lies medial to the femoral vein and is the site at which a femoral hernia appears. The answer is true. The canal is the small medial compartment of the femoral sheath, and it contains a lymph node. It is wider in women, so a femoral hernia is commoner in them.

Question 8982. Which pair belongs together: the ileum with abundant lymphoid aggregates, the jejunum with abundant lymphoid aggregates, the ileum with thick prominent circular folds, or the jejunum with a wall that is thin and poorly supplied with blood? The answer is the ileum with abundant lymphoid aggregates, the Peyer patches. The jejunum has the thicker wall and the taller folds.

Question 8983. Which part of the large intestine lies behind the peritoneum and has no mesentery of its own: the transverse colon, the sigmoid colon, the ascending colon, or the appendix? The answer is the ascending colon. The transverse and sigmoid parts have mesenteries and are mobile. The ascending part lies against the posterior abdominal wall.

Question 8984. What name is given to the three narrow bands of longitudinal muscle that run along the wall of the colon and gather at the base of the appendix? The answer is the taeniae coli. The sacculations between them are the haustra. The fatty tags along the colon are the appendices epiploicae.

Question 8985. A described computed tomography scan of the abdomen shows a thick-walled loop of bowel in the left iliac fossa, with fat inflammation around it and small pouches along its wall. Which part of the bowel is shown? The answer is the sigmoid colon, with diverticula. It has a mesentery and lies in the left iliac fossa.

Question 8986. A constructed illustration: discomfort begins around the navel and later settles in the right iliac fossa, where pressing the wall is most tender at a point on the line between the umbilicus and the right anterior superior iliac spine. Which structure is inflamed? The answer is the vermiform appendix, and the tender point is McBurney point. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8987. Which of these structures lies in the free edge of the lesser omentum, within the hepatoduodenal ligament: the portal vein, the splenic artery, the left gastric artery, or the inferior vena cava? The answer is the portal vein, with the proper hepatic artery and the bile duct beside it. The splenic and left gastric arteries run along the pancreas.

Question 8988. Name two organs that lie behind the peritoneum and are therefore described as retroperitoneal. The answer is the kidneys and the pancreas, and the duodenum and the great vessels are also retroperitoneal. Such organs lie against the posterior abdominal wall and are covered by peritoneum only on their front.

Question 8989. Which muscle of the posterior abdominal wall lies immediately lateral to the psoas major, medial to the twelfth rib, and behind the kidney? The answer is the quadratus lumborum, which extends and laterally flexes the trunk. The psoas major lies in front of it and flexes the hip.

Question 8990. Which nerve of the lumbar plexus emerges from the lateral border of the psoas major and supplies the skin and muscles of the front of the thigh: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the genitofemoral nerve, or the ilioinguinal nerve? The answer is the femoral nerve. The obturator nerve emerges from the medial border instead.

Question 8991. Which structures cross immediately in front of the abdominal inferior vena cava as it ascends to the liver? The answer is the head of the pancreas and the third part of the duodenum above, with the root of the mesentery below. The inferior vena cava lies to the right of the aorta and in front of the vertebral column.

Question 8992. A described transverse section of the abdomen at the level of the third lumbar vertebra shows a vertebral body in the middle, two kidneys behind, and two large vessels in front of it. Which of those vessels lies to the left of the midline? The answer is the abdominal aorta. The inferior vena cava lies to its right.

Question 8993. At which vertebral level does the abdominal aorta end by dividing into the two common iliac arteries: the third lumbar, the fourth lumbar, the fifth lumbar, or the twelfth thoracic? The answer is the fourth lumbar vertebra, a little to the left of the midline. The common iliac arteries divide at the pelvic brim.

Question 8994. Which structure crosses the midline in front of the aorta and the inferior vena cava: the third part of the duodenum, the second part of the duodenum, the head of the pancreas, or the ascending colon? The answer is the third part of the duodenum. The second part descends on the right of the midline.

Question 8995. A constructed illustration: after a fall, a person has bruising over the left lower ribs and blood in the urine, and the described scan shows a collection behind the peritoneum on that side. Which organ is most likely injured? The answer is the left kidney, which lies behind the peritoneum under the eleventh and twelfth ribs. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8996. True or false: the spleen lies entirely behind the peritoneum and is held in place by the renal fascia. The answer is false. The spleen is covered by peritoneum except at its hilum and is held by the gastrosplenic and splenorenal ligaments. It lies against the left kidney but is not fixed by the renal fascia.

Question 8997. Which ligament contains the splenic vessels and the tail of the pancreas, and connects the spleen to the left kidney: the gastrosplenic ligament, the splenorenal ligament, the falciform ligament, or the phrenicocolic ligament? The answer is the splenorenal ligament. The gastrosplenic ligament connects the spleen to the stomach and carries the short gastric arteries.

Question 8998. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the upper abdomen shows a rounded organ on the left, behind the stomach and in front of the left kidney, with vessels entering its hilum. Which organ is shown? The answer is the spleen, and the vessels enter at its hilum. It lies below the left dome of the diaphragm.

Question 8999. The left renal vein crosses the midline in front of which structure: the abdominal aorta, the inferior vena cava, the left kidney, or the superior mesenteric artery? The answer is the abdominal aorta. It then passes behind the superior mesenteric artery to reach the inferior vena cava, and that crossing explains why it can be compressed.

Question 9000. A constructed illustration: a person has fractures of the left ninth, tenth and eleventh ribs and becomes pale and faint, and the described scan shows blood around the spleen and in the peritoneal cavity. Which organ has been injured, and why does the blood enter the peritoneal cavity? The answer is the spleen, which is covered by peritoneum and lies under those ribs. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

To summarize, the mediastinum is divided by the transverse thoracic plane into a superior and an inferior part, and the inferior part into anterior, middle and posterior divisions, with the heart in the middle. The transverse pericardial sinus lies between the aorta and the pulmonary trunk in front and the superior vena cava behind, and the trachea divides at the carina, where the right main bronchus is wider and more vertical. Each lung is divided by fissures into lobes and by bronchi into segments, the pleura forms the costodiaphragmatic recess and the cupola, and the intercostal vein, artery and nerve lie in the costal groove from above downward. The diaphragm has three main openings, the caval opening at the eighth thoracic vertebra, the esophageal opening at the tenth and the aortic hiatus at the twelfth, and the phrenic nerve runs in front of the root of the lung. In the abdomen the peritoneum forms the greater and lesser sacs, the mesentery and the omenta, the foregut is supplied by the celiac trunk, the midgut by the superior mesenteric artery and the hindgut by the inferior mesenteric artery, and blood from the gut, the spleen and the pancreas reaches the liver by the portal vein. The kidneys, the ureters, the pancreas, the duodenum and the great vessels lie behind the peritoneum, the inguinal canal and the femoral canal are the two passages through which a hernia may descend, and this chapter is educational material that does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.
