# Chapter 68 — Stomach

This chapter follows the alimentary canal from the lower end of the esophagus to the first loops of the jejunum. It covers the parts, curvatures, walls and glands of the stomach, the folds of peritoneum that suspend it, the vessels and nerves that reach it, and the organs that lie against its front and its back. It then turns to the four parts of the duodenum and to the jejunum, the first coil of small intestine beyond the duodenojejunal flexure. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer at once, followed by the reason. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 6701. Which rounded part of the stomach rises above the opening of the esophagus and lies under the left dome of the diaphragm? The answer is the fundus. It is the highest part of the organ, and in an upright person it holds a small bubble of swallowed air. The cardia lies below it, where the esophagus enters.

Question 6702. Which of these names the narrow lower part of the stomach that funnels into the duodenum: the fundus, the body, the pyloric antrum, or the cardia? The answer is the pyloric antrum. It narrows into the pyloric canal and ends at the pyloric sphincter. The fundus and the body lie above it.

Question 6703. True or false: the ridges of the gastric mucosa flatten out when the stomach is fully distended. The answer is true. Those ridges are the gastric rugae, folds of mucosa and submucosa that disappear as the wall stretches. They are most obvious in an empty stomach.

Question 6704. How many muscle coats does the stomach have, and which one is found nowhere else in the gut: one, two with a circular and a longitudinal layer, three with an inner oblique layer, or three with an outer oblique layer? The answer is three coats, with an inner oblique layer. The longitudinal and circular coats are the usual pattern of the gut, and the oblique fibers lie inside them.

Question 6705. Which large gland lies immediately posterior of the body of the stomach, with the lesser sac between them? The answer is the pancreas. Its body and tail run across the back of the stomach from right to left. The left kidney, the spleen and the left adrenal gland also lie in the bed of the stomach.

Question 6706. A constructed illustration: a deep ulcer on the lesser curvature of the stomach erodes through the full thickness of the wall. Which artery running along that border is at risk? The answer is the left gastric artery, which runs along the lesser curvature. The right gastric artery lies nearer the pylorus.

Question 6707. A described radiograph taken after a barium meal shows a J-shaped outline in the left upper abdomen, with a bubble of gas above the level of the contrast. Which organ is shown, and which part of it forms the bubble? The answer is the stomach, with the fundus forming the bubble. Gas rises to the highest part of the organ, and the contrast settles below it.

Question 6708. What is the name of the sharp angle on the greater curvature of the stomach, where the esophagus meets the fundus? The answer is the cardiac notch. It lies between the esophagus and the fundus on the left side. The angle helps the cardia to close when the stomach fills.

Question 6709. Name the two curved borders of the stomach, spoken as a list. The answer is the greater curvature and the lesser curvature. The greater curvature is the long convex border, and the greater omentum hangs from it. The lesser curvature is the short concave border, and the lesser omentum is attached to it.

Question 6710. Along the greater curvature of the stomach, which two arteries run toward each other and meet near the middle: the left and right gastric arteries, the left and right gastro-omental arteries, the short gastric arteries, or the splenic artery? The answer is the left and right gastro-omental arteries. The left comes from the splenic artery and the right from the gastroduodenal artery. They meet near the middle of the curvature.

Question 6711. Which artery, the smallest branch of the celiac trunk, runs up to the cardia and then down along the lesser curvature of the stomach? The answer is the left gastric artery. It supplies both surfaces of the stomach along that border. It meets the right gastric artery near the pylorus.

Question 6712. The lesser omentum passes from the liver to the stomach. Which structures run in its free right border, and what lies immediately behind that border? The answer is the portal vein, the hepatic artery and the bile duct, with the epiploic foramen behind them. That foramen is the doorway between the greater sac and the lesser sac.

Question 6713. A constructed illustration: an ulcer on the front wall of the stomach perforates, and its contents spread through the whole abdomen. Why does a perforation of the back wall behave differently? The answer is that the front wall faces the greater sac and the back wall faces the lesser sac. The lesser sac is closed, so material escaping into it is contained at first.

Question 6714. A described computed tomography section through the upper abdomen shows a rounded structure filled with air and fluid on the left, a long apron of fat hanging from its lower border, and a gland behind it. Which organ is shown, and what is the apron? The answer is the stomach, with the greater omentum hanging from its greater curvature. The gland behind it is the pancreas.

Question 6715. Which cells of the gastric glands secrete hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor: the chief cells, the parietal cells, the mucous neck cells, or the enteroendocrine cells? The answer is the parietal cells. They lie mainly in the upper half of the glands of the fundus and the body. The chief cells secrete pepsinogen instead.

Question 6716. What is the name of the thickened ring of circular muscle that guards the outlet of the stomach? The answer is the pyloric sphincter. It lies where the pyloric canal joins the duodenum. Its thickening is why the pylorus feels like a firm ring.

Question 6717. True or false: the pyloric canal lies to the right of the midline and ends at the level of the first lumbar vertebra. The answer is true. The pylorus lies close to the midline and usually a little to its right, at that level. The fundus reaches far to the left and much higher.

Question 6718. Into which system does the venous blood of the stomach drain: the inferior vena cava directly, the portal system, the azygos system, or the pulmonary veins? The answer is the portal system. The gastric and short gastric veins reach the portal vein, most of them by way of the splenic vein. Because that blood crosses the liver first, portal and caval channels can meet at the stomach.

Question 6719. Which fold of peritoneum joins the spleen to the greater curvature of the stomach, and which vessels does it carry? The answer is the gastrosplenic ligament, carrying the short gastric arteries and the left gastro-omental artery. The spleen lies behind the stomach and to its left. The splenorenal ligament joins the spleen to the left kidney instead.

Question 6720. A constructed illustration: a tumor of the stomach has spread to a lymph node above the left clavicle. Which group of nodes is the first station for lymph from the stomach, and why does the spread reach the neck? The answer is the celiac nodes. Lymph follows the arteries to those nodes, and the thoracic duct then carries it to the left venous angle.

Question 6721. A described specimen shows the stomach opened along its greater curvature, with a soft lining thrown into long ridges that run toward the pylorus. What is that lining, and what are the ridges? The answer is the gastric mucosa, with the gastric rugae. They are most prominent near the greater curvature and flatten when the organ fills.

Question 6722. What is the name of the small angular bend on the lesser curvature that marks the boundary between the body and the pyloric part of the stomach? The answer is the angular incisure. It is a more constant landmark than the cardiac notch on the other curvature. It divides the body from the pyloric part.

Question 6723. Which nerve forms the anterior vagal trunk on the front of the stomach: the right vagus nerve, the left vagus nerve, the greater splanchnic nerve, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the left vagus nerve. It turns forward as it passes the lower esophagus, while the right vagus becomes the posterior trunk. The two trunks send branches along both curvatures.

Question 6724. In a person lying on the back, does the fundus of the stomach lie above or below the pylorus, and does it lie to the left or to the right? The answer is above and to the left. The fundus is tucked under the left dome of the diaphragm, while the pylorus lies lower and to the right. The stomach therefore slopes downward and to the right.

Question 6725. From which border of the stomach does the greater omentum hang, and where does it descend: from the lesser curvature to the liver, from the greater curvature over the coils of small intestine, from the fundus to the spleen, or from the pylorus to the pancreas? The answer is from the greater curvature. It hangs down over the coils of small intestine and then turns back up to the transverse colon.

Question 6726. Which cells set the basic electrical rhythm of the stomach and act as its pacemaker: the parietal cells, the chief cells, the enteroendocrine cells, or the interstitial cells of Cajal? The answer is the interstitial cells of Cajal. They lie between the muscle coats and generate the slow waves that spread through the wall.

Question 6727. Name three structures that form the bed on which the stomach rests, spoken as a list. The answer is the pancreas, the spleen and the left kidney. The diaphragm, the left adrenal gland, the transverse mesocolon and the splenic artery also lie in that bed. Most of them lie behind the lesser sac, which separates them from the stomach.

Question 6728. A constructed illustration: a young infant vomits forcefully after feeds, and a firm olive-shaped mass can be felt in the upper abdomen. Which structure is thickened, and what does the thickening obstruct? The answer is the pyloric sphincter, which obstructs the outlet of the stomach. The stomach wall thickens behind it. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6729. A described radiograph taken after a barium meal shows a narrow stream of contrast running along the lesser curvature between two ridges. Which channel is that? The answer is the gastric canal, the groove formed by the rugae along the lesser curvature. Swallowed liquid runs along it straight to the pylorus, while solid food is held back to be mixed.

Question 6730. Which structure lies immediately above the fundus of the stomach: the liver, the left dome of the diaphragm, the pancreas, or the transverse colon? The answer is the left dome of the diaphragm. It separates the fundus from the left lung and the pleura above. The liver lies on the right and the pancreas lies behind.

Question 6731. What is the name of the largest part of the stomach, lying between the fundus above and the pyloric antrum below? The answer is the body. It forms the greater part of the organ and ends at the angular incisure on the lesser curvature. The pyloric part narrows from there toward the duodenum.

Question 6732. Which secretion of the stomach is needed for the absorption of a vitamin in the lower small intestine: hydrochloric acid, pepsinogen, intrinsic factor, or gastrin? The answer is intrinsic factor. The parietal cells release it, and it binds the vitamin so that the ileum can take it in. Without it the vitamin is not absorbed, however much is eaten.

Question 6733. True or false: the short gastric veins carry blood from the fundus of the stomach into the splenic vein. The answer is true. They run in the gastrosplenic ligament from the fundus to the spleen and join the splenic vein there. That vein then helps to form the portal vein.

Question 6734. Which statement about the emptying of the stomach is correct: the fundus stores food and the antrum pumps it out, the body pumps and the antrum stores, the pylorus pushes food back into the esophagus, or the cardia sets the rate? The answer is that the fundus and body store the food and the antrum pumps it out. The pylorus is a valve rather than a pump.

Question 6735. A tumor of the greater curvature of the stomach has grown into the transverse colon. Along which fold of peritoneum did it likely spread: the lesser omentum, the gastrocolic ligament, the gastrosplenic ligament, or the falciform ligament? The answer is the gastrocolic ligament, part of the greater omentum, which joins the greater curvature to the transverse colon. The two organs lie against each other across it.

Question 6736. What is the name of the peritoneal recess that lies behind the stomach and in front of the pancreas? The answer is the lesser sac, also called the omental bursa. It communicates with the greater sac through the epiploic foramen. The stomach forms most of its front wall.

Question 6737. Which structures lie immediately behind the fundus of the stomach on the left side: the spleen and the left kidney, the liver and the gallbladder, the duodenum and the head of the pancreas, or the transverse colon and the rectum? The answer is the spleen and the left kidney. The spleen lies behind the stomach and to its left, and the left kidney lies behind the spleen.

Question 6738. In which part of the stomach are the glands that contain parietal cells most numerous: the cardia, the fundus and body, the pyloric antrum, or the pyloric canal? The answer is the fundus and body. The cardiac and pyloric glands are shorter and contain few parietal cells. This is why acid secretion is greatest in the upper two thirds of the organ.

Question 6739. Which of these pairs belongs together: the left gastric artery with the splenic artery, the right gastric artery with the hepatic artery, the short gastric arteries with the gastroduodenal artery, or the right gastro-omental artery with the left gastric artery? The answer is the right gastric artery with the hepatic artery. It runs along the lesser curvature toward the pylorus.

Question 6740. A described dissection shows a large apron of fat and peritoneum hanging from the greater curvature of the stomach and covering the coils of small intestine below. What is this structure, and how many layers does it have? The answer is the greater omentum, which has four layers, two descending from the stomach and two returning to the transverse colon.

Question 6741. What is the name of the short narrow tube between the pyloric antrum and the duodenum? The answer is the pyloric canal. It lies within the thickened pyloric sphincter and is about 2 to 3 centimeters long. Its muscle controls the exit of food from the stomach.

Question 6742. Which of these is the inactive precursor released by the chief cells of the gastric glands: hydrochloric acid, pepsinogen, intrinsic factor, or gastrin? The answer is pepsinogen. Acid in the stomach converts it into the active enzyme pepsin, which begins the digestion of protein. Gastrin reaches the glands through the blood instead.

Question 6743. Which artery reaches the fundus of the stomach: the left gastric artery, the short gastric arteries from the splenic artery, the right gastro-omental artery, or the gastroduodenal artery? The answer is the short gastric arteries. They pass in the gastrosplenic ligament from the spleen to the fundus. The left gastric artery reaches the cardia and the lesser curvature.

Question 6744. A constructed illustration: an ulcer on the back wall of the stomach penetrates deeply, and the pain is felt in the back. Which organ lies immediately behind the stomach at that point? The answer is the pancreas. The splenic artery runs along its upper border, behind the stomach. The care of such an ulcer belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6745. A described radiograph taken after a barium meal shows a small rounded projection on the lesser curvature, with folds of mucosa radiating toward it from all sides. What is the projection, and what do the radiating folds show? The answer is an ulcer niche, a crater that fills with contrast. The folds converge on it because the mucosa is drawn inward by scarring.

Question 6746. Which structure lies immediately in front of the fundus of the stomach: the diaphragm, the liver, the pancreas, or the transverse colon? The answer is the diaphragm. The fundus lies under the left dome of the diaphragm, which separates it from the left lung and its pleura above. The lower part of the stomach lies behind the abdominal wall.

Question 6747. Which of these pairs belongs together: the fundus with the pyloric sphincter, the pyloric antrum with the glands of the body, the greater curvature with the greater omentum, or the lesser curvature with the short gastric arteries? The answer is the greater curvature with the greater omentum, which hangs from that border. The short gastric arteries run near the fundus instead.

Question 6748. What is the name of the opening through which the lesser sac communicates with the greater sac, and what forms its front wall? The answer is the epiploic foramen, whose front wall is the free border of the lesser omentum. Behind the foramen lies the inferior vena cava, and below it lies the first part of the duodenum.

Question 6749. Which artery runs along the upper border of the pancreas, behind the stomach, and gives off the short gastric arteries? The answer is the splenic artery. It runs a tortuous course from the celiac trunk to the spleen. The stomach lies in front of it at that level, with the lesser sac between the two.

Question 6750. A described horizontal section through the upper abdomen shows the stomach in front, a narrow slit-like space behind it, and the pancreas behind that space. What is the slit, and what bounds it? The answer is the lesser sac, bounded in front by the stomach and behind by the peritoneum over the pancreas. It connects with the greater sac above.

Question 6751. Which part of the duodenum lies immediately beyond the pylorus, and what is its other name? The answer is the superior part, also called the first part or the duodenal cap. It runs upward and backward from the pylorus. Unlike the rest of the duodenum, it is covered by peritoneum on both surfaces and moves freely.

Question 6752. In which order do the four parts of the duodenum run: superior, horizontal, descending, ascending; superior, descending, horizontal, ascending; descending, superior, ascending, horizontal; or horizontal, superior, descending, ascending? The answer is superior, descending, horizontal, ascending. The second part runs down beside the head of the pancreas, and the fourth rises to the duodenojejunal flexure.

Question 6753. Which organ lies immediately behind the descending part of the duodenum, and which part of the pancreas lies against its medial border? The answer is the right kidney behind it, with the head of the pancreas against its medial border. The right ureter and the right renal vessels also lie behind that part. The bile duct runs down behind the medial border.

Question 6754. A described radiograph taken after a barium meal shows a smooth triangular shadow shaped like a cap, filling at once and passing quickly onwards. Which part of the gut is shown? The answer is the superior part of the duodenum, known from its outline as the duodenal cap. It fills and empties quickly because it lies directly beyond the pylorus.

Question 6755. A constructed illustration: an ulcer forms on the first part of the duodenum, just beyond the pylorus. Which feature of that part explains why it is a common site for such an ulcer? The answer is that it receives acid chyme straight from the stomach. The descending part is protected by the bile and pancreatic juice that join it, and the first part is not.

Question 6756. What is the name of the raised opening on the medial wall of the descending duodenum where the bile duct and the main pancreatic duct usually join? The answer is the major duodenal papilla. It lies in the middle of the descending part. A smaller minor papilla may lie above it, where an accessory pancreatic duct opens.

Question 6757. What is the name of the dilation formed where the bile duct and the main pancreatic duct join before opening into the duodenum: the pyloric canal, the hepatopancreatic ampulla, the cystic duct, or the duodenal cap? The answer is the hepatopancreatic ampulla. It lies in the wall of the descending part and opens at the major duodenal papilla.

Question 6758. True or false: of the four parts of the duodenum, only the first is mobile and covered by peritoneum on both surfaces. The answer is true. The remaining three parts lie behind the peritoneum against the posterior abdominal wall. That is why the first part is the one that moves.

Question 6759. Which structure crosses in front of the horizontal part of the duodenum and may compress it against the aorta: the inferior vena cava, the superior mesenteric artery, the splenic artery, or the left renal vein? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery. The third part of the duodenum passes between the aorta behind and that artery in front. The left renal vein crosses the same region, but it lies higher.

Question 6760. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superior part with the right kidney, the descending part with the head of the pancreas, the horizontal part with the left kidney, or the ascending part with the gallbladder? The answer is the descending part with the head of the pancreas. That part curves around the head of the pancreas and lies against it.

Question 6761. What is the name of the band of muscle that suspends the duodenojejunal flexure from the right crus of the diaphragm? The answer is the suspensory muscle of the duodenum, also called the ligament of Treitz. It contains smooth muscle and connective tissue and fixes the flexure. The flexure itself lies to the left of the aorta.

Question 6762. Where does the duodenojejunal flexure lie in relation to the aorta and the vertebral column, and what does it mark? The answer is that it lies to the left of the aorta, in front of the left renal vessels, at the level of the second lumbar vertebra. It marks the junction of the duodenum and the jejunum. It is fixed there by the suspensory muscle.

Question 6763. A described computed tomography section through the lower abdomen shows a thin-walled tube passing between two large vessels, one behind and one in front, with a fan of mesentery below it. Which part of the gut is shown, and which vessels are they? The answer is the horizontal part of the duodenum, with the aorta behind and the superior mesenteric artery in front.

Question 6764. From which two arteries does the duodenum receive its blood: the gastroduodenal and the superior mesenteric, the splenic and the left gastric, the inferior mesenteric and the middle colic, or the hepatic and the cystic? The answer is the gastroduodenal and the superior mesenteric arteries. The inferior pancreaticoduodenal artery comes from the superior mesenteric artery.

Question 6765. A constructed illustration: a thin young person loses weight and vomits after meals, and imaging shows the horizontal part of the duodenum dilated above a narrow point. Which two structures are narrowing it? The answer is the aorta behind and the superior mesenteric artery in front. The third part of the duodenum passes between them, where the angle between the vessels is narrow. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6766. What is the name of the permanent circular folds of the lining of the small intestine that run around the tube and are tallest in the jejunum? The answer is the circular folds, also called the plicae circulares. They run part of the way around the wall and are fixed, unlike the rugae of the stomach. They increase the surface for absorption.

Question 6767. Where do the two arterial supplies of the duodenum meet: in the first part, in the middle of the descending part, in the horizontal part, or in the fourth part? The answer is in the middle of the descending part, where the superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries meet. The upper half of the duodenum belongs to the celiac territory and the lower half to the superior mesenteric territory.

Question 6768. In a horizontal section through the upper abdomen, which parts of the duodenum lie behind the peritoneum against the posterior wall, and which part lies free? The answer is that the descending, horizontal and ascending parts lie behind the peritoneum, and only the superior part lies free. The jejunum, by contrast, hangs on a mesentery and moves.

Question 6769. Into which part of the duodenum do the bile duct and the main pancreatic duct open: the superior part, the descending part, the horizontal part, or the ascending part? The answer is the descending part. They open on its medial wall at the major duodenal papilla, about halfway down. The head of the pancreas lies against that wall.

Question 6770. A described radiograph of a barium meal shows a loop of gut shaped like a letter C, curving around a rounded mass, with a cap-shaped shadow at its upper end. Which structure forms the loop, and what does it curve around? The answer is the duodenum, curving around the head of the pancreas. The cap-shaped shadow is the superior part.

Question 6771. At which named point does the jejunum begin? The answer is the duodenojejunal flexure. It lies to the left of the aorta, at the level of the second lumbar vertebra. The flexure is fixed by the suspensory muscle, and from there the jejunum hangs on its mesentery.

Question 6772. In which part of the abdomen does the jejunum usually lie, and which coils occupy the lower and right parts? The answer is the left upper part of the abdomen, with the ileum in the lower and right parts. The jejunum occupies the umbilical and left iliac regions most often. It has a thicker wall than the ileum.

Question 6773. Which feature helps to identify the jejunum at operation: a wall thinner than that of the ileum, a mesentery loaded with fat, long straight vessels with few arcades, or large collections of lymphoid tissue in its wall? The answer is long straight vessels with few arcades. The jejunum has fewer arterial arcades than the ileum, and its mesentery carries little fat.

Question 6774. True or false: the mesentery of the jejunum carries more fat than the mesentery of the ileum. The answer is false. The mesentery of the jejunum is thin and translucent, while that of the ileum is thick with fat. That difference is one of the signs used to tell the two apart.

Question 6775. A constructed illustration: at operation a loop of small intestine is found with a thick wall, tall folds of mucosa and a thin translucent mesentery carrying long straight vessels. Which part is it? The answer is the jejunum. Its wall is thicker and more vascular than that of the ileum, and its mesentery holds little fat.

Question 6776. What is the name of the long straight arteries that run from the last arterial arcade to the wall of the small intestine? The answer is the vasa recta. They are longer and fewer in the jejunum and shorter and more numerous in the ileum. They reach the wall at right angles to the bowel.

Question 6777. How does the number of arterial arcades differ between the jejunum and the ileum: the jejunum has many more arcades, the jejunum has one or two rows and the ileum several, the ileum has no arcades, or both have the same number? The answer is that the jejunum has one or two rows and the ileum has several. More arcades in the ileum mean shorter straight vessels reaching the wall.

Question 6778. Which of these pairs belongs together: the jejunum with abundant fat in its mesentery, the ileum with long vasa recta, the jejunum with tall circular folds, or the ileum with a thicker wall? The answer is the jejunum with tall circular folds. Its folds are more numerous and taller than those of the ileum. The ileum carries more fat in its mesentery and has a thinner wall.

Question 6779. A described specimen of small intestine, opened along its length, shows tall closely packed circular folds and a thick lining, with very little fat in the attached mesentery. Which part is it? The answer is the jejunum. Its circular folds are tall and closely set, and its wall is thicker than that of the ileum. The ileum has fewer folds and more fat.

Question 6780. Name two features of the jejunum that help to distinguish it from the ileum, spoken as a list. The answer is its thicker wall and its longer straight arteries, with fewer arterial arcades. Its mesentery also carries less fat, and its circular folds are taller. None of these features alone is reliable, so they are used together.

Question 6781. What is the name of the fold of peritoneum that suspends the coils of the small intestine and carries their vessels and nerves? The answer is the mesentery, whose attached edge is the root of the mesentery. The root runs from the duodenojejunal flexure down to the ileocecal junction. It crosses the third part of the duodenum and the aorta.

Question 6782. Which statement about the lengths of the jejunum and the ileum is correct: they are equal, the jejunum is longer, the ileum is longer, or the duodenum is the longest? The answer is that the ileum is longer. The jejunum forms about two fifths of the small intestine beyond the duodenum and the ileum about three fifths. The jejunum lies mainly in the left upper abdomen.

Question 6783. From which point to which point does the root of the mesentery run, and which structures does it cross? The answer is that it runs from the duodenojejunal flexure on the left to the ileocecal junction on the right, crossing the third part of the duodenum, the aorta and the right ureter. Its attachment is only about 15 centimeters long, while the free border is far longer.

Question 6784. A described specimen shows a loop of small intestine with a thin mesentery, translucent in places, and a single row of arterial arcades with long straight vessels reaching the wall. Which part is shown, and what are the long vessels called? The answer is the jejunum, and the long vessels are the vasa recta. The ileum would show several rows of arcades and much more fat.

Question 6785. A constructed illustration: an ulcer on the back wall of the first part of the duodenum bleeds heavily. Which artery is most likely involved, and why is it at risk there? The answer is the gastroduodenal artery or one of its branches, which passes down behind the first part of the duodenum before dividing. The care of such a bleed belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6786. Which part of the duodenum runs downward in front of the right kidney and around the head of the pancreas? The answer is the descending part, the second part. It is about 7 to 10 centimeters long. The bile duct and the main pancreatic duct open into its medial wall.

Question 6787. Which artery supplies the jejunum: the celiac trunk directly, the superior mesenteric artery, the inferior mesenteric artery, or the internal iliac artery? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery. It gives a series of jejunal and ileal branches that form the arcades in the mesentery. The inferior mesenteric artery supplies the hindgut instead.

Question 6788. Where are the circular folds of the small intestine tallest and most closely set, and where are they absent? The answer is that they are tallest in the jejunum and absent from the first part of the duodenum and from the terminal ileum. They are permanent folds of the mucosa and the submucosa. They increase the surface for absorption.

Question 6789. A described section of the wall of the small intestine shows a fold running part of the way around the lumen, with a core of submucosa and a covering of mucosa. What is the fold, and how does it differ from a fold of the stomach? The answer is a circular fold, which is permanent, while the rugae of the stomach flatten when it fills.

Question 6790. Which of these pairs belongs together: the first part of the duodenum with the head of the pancreas, the second part with the right kidney, the third part with the left kidney, or the fourth part with the gallbladder? The answer is the second part with the right kidney, which lies behind it. The head of the pancreas lies against its medial border.

Question 6791. Which part of the duodenum rises on the left of the aorta to reach the duodenojejunal flexure? The answer is the fourth part, the ascending part. It begins at the level of the third lumbar vertebra and ends at the flexure. The suspensory muscle attaches at its junction with the jejunum.

Question 6792. Which statement about the mesentery of the jejunum is correct: it is thick and loaded with fat, it is thin and translucent, it is absent, or it contains the portal vein? The answer is that it is thin and translucent. Less fat is stored in it than in the mesentery of the ileum, so the vessels show through it. The portal vein lies far behind it.

Question 6793. Which structure lies immediately behind the descending part of the duodenum, and which tube descends from it across the same region? The answer is the right kidney, with the right ureter descending from its hilum in front of it. The right renal vessels also lie behind the duodenum. All of them lie behind the peritoneum of the posterior abdominal wall.

Question 6794. A described specimen of small intestine shows a mesentery with several rows of arterial arcades, abundant fat and short straight vessels reaching the wall, and the wall itself is thin with low folds. Which part is shown? The answer is the ileum, which lies below the jejunum. Its circular folds are lower and less numerous than those above it.

Question 6795. A constructed illustration: in a newborn the duodenojejunal flexure lies to the right of the spine. What developmental event has gone wrong? The answer is that the midgut failed to rotate about the superior mesenteric artery, so the flexure never passed to the left. The care of the infant belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6796. What is the name of the sharp bend where the duodenum becomes the jejunum? The answer is the duodenojejunal flexure. It lies to the left of the aorta at the level of the second lumbar vertebra. It marks the end of the fixed duodenum and the start of the mobile jejunum.

Question 6797. Which structure fixes the duodenojejunal flexure in position: the lesser omentum, the suspensory muscle of the duodenum, the root of the mesentery, or the gastrosplenic ligament? The answer is the suspensory muscle of the duodenum, also called the ligament of Treitz. It runs from the right crus of the diaphragm to the flexure. The root of the mesentery begins there but does not fix it.

Question 6798. A described radiograph of the small bowel shows coils with tall feathery folds filling the left upper abdomen, and other coils with fewer folds lying lower. Which parts of the small intestine are shown in the two places? The answer is the jejunum above and the ileum below. The feathery appearance comes from the tall circular folds of the jejunum.

Question 6799. Which statement about the jejunum is correct: it lies mainly in the right lower abdomen, it has a thinner wall than the ileum, it has a richer blood supply than the ileum, or it carries more fat in its mesentery than the ileum? The answer is that it has a richer blood supply than the ileum. Its wall is thicker and its vessels are larger, and its mesentery holds less fat.

Question 6800. Which single feature is the most reliable guide to the upper end of the jejunum at operation: the color of the bowel, the number of arterial arcades, the width of the lumen, or the amount of fat in the mesentery? The answer is the number of arterial arcades, which increase steadily from the jejunum to the ileum. The jejunum has one or two rows and the ileum has four or five.

To summarize, the stomach has a fundus, a body, a pyloric antrum and a pyloric canal, with a greater curvature that carries the greater omentum and a lesser curvature that carries the lesser omentum and the two gastric arteries. Its wall has three muscle coats, its glands in the fundus and body hold the parietal cells that make acid and intrinsic factor, and its blood comes from all three branches of the celiac trunk while its veins drain into the portal system. The duodenum has four parts, and only the first is mobile; the descending part receives the bile and pancreatic ducts at the major duodenal papilla, and the horizontal part passes between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery. The jejunum begins at the duodenojejunal flexure, hangs on a thin mesentery in the left upper abdomen, and is recognized by its thick wall, tall circular folds, few arterial arcades and long straight arteries.
