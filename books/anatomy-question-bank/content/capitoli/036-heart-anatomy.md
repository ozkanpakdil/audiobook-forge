# Chapter 36 — Heart Anatomy

This chapter examines the heart itself: its four chambers, the valves that keep blood moving one way through them, the septa that divide it, and the great vessels that enter and leave its base. It holds one hundred questions, and every question is followed at once by its answer and by the reason that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind from one item to the next. The same three-part shape is used throughout, and the reasons name the relations that make the answer true. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 3501. Which chamber of the heart receives blood from the superior vena cava and the inferior vena cava? The answer is the right atrium. Both of those large veins open into its smooth posterior wall, and the coronary sinus opens into it as well. From there the blood passes through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle.

Question 3502. Which chamber forms the right border of the heart as it is seen on a chest radiograph: the right ventricle, the right atrium, the left atrium, or the left ventricle? The answer is the right atrium. It lies at the right edge of the cardiac shadow, with the superior vena cava above it and the inferior vena cava below.

Question 3503. A described chest radiograph shows a rounded bulge along the upper right border of the cardiac shadow, where a vertical vein joins from above. Which chamber lies at that point? The answer is the right atrium. Its upper part receives the superior vena cava there, and that junction is what produces the slight bulge at the border.

Question 3504. Which chamber of the heart has the thickest muscular wall? The answer is the left ventricle. It pumps blood into the aorta against the resistance of the whole body, so its wall is about three times as thick as that of the right ventricle. The right ventricle pumps only to the lungs, where resistance is far lower.

Question 3505. Which chamber of the heart lies immediately in front of the esophagus and the descending thoracic aorta: the right atrium, the right ventricle, the left atrium, or the left ventricle? The answer is the left atrium. It forms most of the posterior surface of the heart, which is why an enlarged left atrium can press on the esophagus.

Question 3506. Which of these features belongs to the ventricles rather than the atria: pectinate muscles, a crista terminalis, trabeculae carneae, or a fossa ovalis? The answer is trabeculae carneae. These are the irregular muscular ridges of the ventricular wall. Pectinate muscles and the crista terminalis belong to the right atrium, and the fossa ovalis lies in the interatrial septum.

Question 3507. What is the name of the small ear-shaped pouch that projects from the upper part of each atrium and increases its capacity? The answer is the auricle. It is a thin-walled appendage with a narrow cavity. The auricle of the left atrium is a common place for a clot to form when the atrium does not empty properly.

Question 3508. A constructed illustration: a person with a long-standing irregular atrial rhythm is found to have a clot inside a small muscular pouch of the left atrium. Which part of the heart is described? The answer is the left auricle. Its narrow cavity and ridged wall allow blood to stagnate there when the atrium stops emptying in an orderly way.

Question 3509. True or false: the wall of the right ventricle is thicker than the wall of the left ventricle. The answer is false. The left ventricle has the thicker wall because it drives blood around the entire body. The right ventricle has a thinner wall because the lungs offer much less resistance to the flow of blood through them.

Question 3510. A muscular band crosses the cavity of the right ventricle from the interventricular septum to the anterior papillary muscle. Which structure is described: the moderator band, a chorda tendinea, the crista supraventricularis, or a pectinate muscle? The answer is the moderator band. It carries part of the right branch of the conducting system across the cavity so that both sides contract together.

Question 3511. A described transverse section through the heart shows one chamber with a nearly circular cavity and a thick muscular wall, and another chamber whose crescent-shaped cavity wraps around its front. Which chambers are shown? The answer is the left ventricle and the right ventricle. In this section the left ventricle is round and the right ventricle is a crescent against it.

Question 3512. Into which chamber of the heart does the coronary sinus empty? The answer is the right atrium. The coronary sinus is the main vein of the heart's own muscle, and it opens into the right atrium just above the opening of the inferior vena cava. A small fold of tissue guards its opening.

Question 3513. Which chamber of the heart lies most posteriorly, so that its enlargement is felt mainly from behind: the right ventricle, the right atrium, the left ventricle, or the left atrium? The answer is the left atrium. It forms most of the base of the heart and lies against the esophagus and the vertebral column.

Question 3514. Name the two chambers that receive blood and the two that pump it out, spoken as a list. The answer is the right atrium and the left atrium for the receiving chambers, and the right ventricle and the left ventricle for the pumping chambers. The atria are thin-walled and the ventricles thick-walled, which is what their names imply.

Question 3515. Which structure runs down the inner wall of the right atrium from the opening of the superior vena cava to the opening of the inferior vena cava? The answer is the crista terminalis. It is a vertical muscular ridge that separates the smooth posterior part of the atrium from the muscular part in front of it.

Question 3516. What name is given to the parallel muscular ridges that cover the inner surface of the right auricle? The answer is the pectinate muscles. They resemble the teeth of a comb, which is where the name comes from. Their arrangement lets the thin wall of the auricle contract without folding on itself.

Question 3517. A section of the heart is cut in a plane that runs across the body at right angles to its long axis, passing through the openings of the two atrioventricular valves. Which chambers does that section show? The answer is parts of all four chambers. The plane crosses the junction between the atria above and the ventricles below.

Question 3518. A constructed illustration: a penetrating wound enters the chest just to the left of the sternum at the level of the fifth rib and bleeds into the pericardial sac. Which chamber lies closest to that point? The answer is the right ventricle. It forms most of the anterior surface of the heart, directly behind the sternum.

Question 3519. Which chamber of the heart lies most anteriorly, immediately behind the sternum: the left atrium, the left ventricle, the right atrium, or the right ventricle? The answer is the right ventricle. It forms most of the front of the heart, while the right atrium forms the right border and the left ventricle forms the left border and the apex.

Question 3520. A described transverse section of the chest shows the heart with one thin-walled chamber lying immediately behind the sternum, a second chamber to its right, and a third with a much thicker wall behind and to the left. Which chamber is the thin-walled one in front? The answer is the right ventricle.

Question 3521. Which chamber of the heart lies directly behind the body of the sternum and the costal cartilages of the third, fourth and fifth ribs? The answer is the right ventricle. Most of the anterior surface of the heart belongs to it, with the right atrium at the right edge. A wound in this region reaches the right ventricle first.

Question 3522. Which part of the right ventricle is smooth-walled and leads upward to the pulmonary valve: the trabecular part, the conus arteriosus, the sinus of the venae cavae, or the auricle? The answer is the conus arteriosus, also called the infundibulum. It is the outflow tract, and its smooth wall contrasts with the ridged cavity below it.

Question 3523. What is the ordinary thickness of the muscular wall of the right ventricle in an adult heart? The answer is about 3 to 5 millimeters. The wall of the left ventricle in the same heart measures about 8 to 12 millimeters. That difference reflects the much lower resistance of the circulation through the lungs.

Question 3524. A constructed illustration: blood collects rapidly in the pericardial sac after a chest injury, and the thin-walled chambers cannot fill. Which chamber is compressed first, because it has the lowest internal pressure: the left ventricle, the right atrium, the left atrium, or the aortic root? The answer is the right atrium.

Question 3525. In which intercostal space, in the midclavicular line, is the apex beat of the heart normally felt: the fourth, the fifth, the sixth, or the second? The answer is the fifth intercostal space. The apex of the heart is formed by the left ventricle and lies at that point in most adults. It may be displaced in disease.

Question 3526. True or false: the apex of the heart is formed by the left ventricle. The answer is true. The apex is the pointed lower end of the heart, directed downward, forward and to the left. The right ventricle approaches it but does not form it.

Question 3527. What is the name of the small fold that guards the opening of the inferior vena cava into the right atrium? The answer is the valve of the inferior vena cava. It is a remnant of a larger fetal fold that once directed blood toward the interatrial opening. In the adult it is small and often has holes in it.

Question 3528. Which statement about the capacity of the four chambers is correct: the right ventricle holds much more than the left, the two ventricles hold about the same volume, the atria together hold more than the ventricles, or the left atrium is the largest chamber? The answer is that the two ventricles hold about the same volume as each other.

Question 3529. Which structure lies immediately behind the left atrium, separated from it by the pericardium alone, and what follows when the atrium enlarges? The answer is the esophagus. An enlarged left atrium can press on it and cause difficulty in swallowing. The same chamber also lies in front of the descending thoracic aorta and the vertebral column.

Question 3530. A described cross section of the heart shows a crescent-shaped chamber wrapped around a circular one, with a thick muscular septum between them and a valve leaflet hanging into the crescent. Which two chambers are shown? The answer is the right ventricle and the left ventricle. The crescent is the right ventricle, applied to the front of the round left ventricle.

Question 3531. Which valve lies between the right atrium and the right ventricle? The answer is the tricuspid valve. It has three cusps, named anterior, posterior and septal. The septal cusp lies against the interventricular septum, and the other two are attached to the wall of the ventricle.

Question 3532. Which valve of the heart has only two cusps: the tricuspid, the pulmonary, the mitral, or the aortic? The answer is the mitral valve. It lies between the left atrium and the left ventricle and is therefore also called the bicuspid valve. Its two cusps are named anterior and posterior.

Question 3533. A described radiograph of an older heart shows a dense curved line in the region of the left atrioventricular opening. Which valve has become calcified there? The answer is the mitral valve. Calcium in its ring shows as a curved density projecting to the left of the spine. The tricuspid valve would lie nearer the midline.

Question 3534. At which point on the chest wall is the sound of the mitral valve best heard? The answer is at the apex, in the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line. The valve itself lies behind the sternum, but its sound is carried to the apex by the direction of the jet of blood. Each valve is heard where its jet points.

Question 3535. Which structures keep the atrioventricular valves from being pushed back into the atria during contraction of the ventricles: the semilunar cusps, the papillary muscles with their chordae tendineae, the fibrous trigones, or the crista supraventricularis? The answer is the papillary muscles with their chordae tendineae.

Question 3536. Which valve lies between the left ventricle and the aorta? The answer is the aortic valve. It has three semilunar cusps, and two of them are named for the coronary arteries that arise from the sinuses behind them. Its cusps meet in the middle of the opening when the ventricle relaxes.

Question 3537. A constructed illustration: a person who becomes dizzy during heavy exertion is found to have a thickened, narrowed valve at the outlet of the left ventricle. Which valve is affected? The answer is the aortic valve. The left ventricle must generate a much higher pressure to force blood through the narrow opening, and the supply to the brain suffers.

Question 3538. True or false: the pulmonary valve has three semilunar cusps and no chordae tendineae. The answer is true. The pulmonary valve and the aortic valve both have three cusps and no papillary muscles. The tricuspid and mitral valves have cusps that are held by chordae tendineae.

Question 3539. Which cusps of the aortic valve are named for the coronary arteries that arise behind them: the right and left cusps, the anterior and posterior cusps, the septal and mural cusps, or the superior and inferior cusps? The answer is the right and left cusps. The third cusp is the posterior one, and no coronary artery arises behind it.

Question 3540. Which pair belongs together: the mitral valve with the apex in the fifth intercostal space, the aortic valve with the apex, the pulmonary valve with the lower left sternal edge, or the tricuspid valve with the second right intercostal space? The answer is the mitral valve with the apex. The aortic valve is heard at the second right space.

Question 3541. What name is given to the two valves that guard the outflow tracts of the ventricles? The answer is the semilunar valves. The pulmonary valve guards the outflow of the right ventricle and the aortic valve guards that of the left ventricle. Their cusps are shaped like half moons, which is the source of the name.

Question 3542. A described dissection shows a dense plate of fibrous tissue at the base of the heart, with the four valves anchored to it and an insulating plane running through it. What is that structure? The answer is the fibrous skeleton of the heart. It also separates the muscle of the atria from the muscle of the ventricles.

Question 3543. Of the four valves of the heart, which one lies most anteriorly, closest to the chest wall? The answer is the pulmonary valve. It lies at the upper left of the heart and is the most superficial of the four. The aortic valve lies behind it and a little to the right.

Question 3544. A described preparation shows the anterior cusp of the mitral valve hanging directly below the opening of the aorta. What does that continuity mean for the surgeon who must work there: the two rings are separate, the aortic and mitral valves share a fibrous curtain, the tricuspid valve lies between them, or the septum divides them? The answer is that the two share a curtain.

Question 3545. A constructed illustration: a heart examined after a long illness shows the mitral opening narrowed by thick, fused cusps, with shortened chordae. Which valve is described, and which chamber behind it becomes enlarged? The answer is the mitral valve, with enlargement of the left atrium behind it, because that chamber must work harder to fill the ventricle.

Question 3546. How many cusps does the tricuspid valve have? The answer is three. They are named anterior, posterior and septal. Each cusp is tethered by chordae tendineae to papillary muscles in the right ventricle, and the septal cusp lies against the interventricular septum.

Question 3547. Which valve guards the outflow tract of the right ventricle: the aortic valve, the mitral valve, the pulmonary valve, or the tricuspid valve? The answer is the pulmonary valve. It sits at the top of the conus arteriosus and opens into the pulmonary trunk. The aortic valve guards the outflow of the left ventricle.

Question 3548. A described specimen shows a valve with two cusps, each tethered by chordae to two papillary muscles, one in front and one behind. Which valve is it? The answer is the mitral valve. Its two papillary muscles are named anterior and posterior, and they lie in the left ventricle below the two cusps.

Question 3549. A described dissection follows the blood from the right atrium into the lungs. Which two valves does the blood cross on that path, and in which order? The answer is the tricuspid valve first and the pulmonary valve second. The same route is followed by anything that enters the right side of the heart and travels with the stream.

Question 3550. Which pair belongs together: the anterior cusp of the mitral valve with the aortic opening above it, the septal cusp of the tricuspid with the pulmonary opening, the posterior cusp of the aortic valve with the pulmonary trunk, or the anterior cusp of the tricuspid with the aortic opening? The answer is the anterior cusp of the mitral valve with the aortic opening above it.

Question 3551. Name the three structures that keep an atrioventricular valve closed during the contraction of a ventricle, spoken as a list. The answer is the cusps, the chordae tendineae and the papillary muscles. If any one of the three fails, the valve leaks, because the cusps can no longer meet squarely in the middle of the opening.

Question 3552. In an adult heart, roughly what is the diameter of the normal mitral opening when it is fully open: 1 to 2 centimeters, 3 to 4 centimeters, 6 to 7 centimeters, or 8 to 9 centimeters? The answer is 3 to 4 centimeters. The tricuspid opening is a little larger, and the two semilunar openings are smaller than either.

Question 3553. A constructed illustration: a person with a widened aortic root is found to have a valve whose cusps no longer meet in the middle, so blood leaks back into the left ventricle as it relaxes. Which valve is involved, and what is the result? The answer is the aortic valve, and the leak makes the left ventricle enlarge.

Question 3554. True or false: the mitral valve lies between the left atrium and the left ventricle. The answer is true. It is also called the bicuspid valve, because it has two cusps where the valve on the right side has three. The valve between the right atrium and the right ventricle is the tricuspid valve.

Question 3555. What is the name of the fold that guards the opening of the coronary sinus into the right atrium? The answer is the valve of the coronary sinus. It is a thin crescent at the lower end of the opening, close to the opening of the inferior vena cava. It is a normal feature and varies greatly in size.

Question 3556. A described section through the base of the heart shows three pouch-like cusps above a fibrous ring, with a small opening behind one of them. Which valve is shown, and what is the small opening? The answer is the aortic valve, and the opening is the origin of a coronary artery.

Question 3557. Which structure separates the left ventricle from the right ventricle? The answer is the interventricular septum. Its greater part is thick muscle that bulges into the cavity of the right ventricle. Its upper part is a thin membrane, and the two parts meet along a curved line.

Question 3558. Which part of the interventricular septum is thin, lies just below the aortic valve, and is the usual site of a hole between the ventricles: the muscular part, the membranous part, the atrioventricular part, or the infundibular part? The answer is the membranous part. It is a small oval area, and a hole there is the commonest defect of the septum.

Question 3559. A described section through the heart shows a small defect in the upper part of the septum between the ventricles, immediately below the aortic valve and in front of the septal cusp of the tricuspid valve. Which part of the septum is defective? The answer is the membranous part of the interventricular septum.

Question 3560. Which chamber lies immediately to the right of the interatrial septum, and which lies to its left? The answer is the right atrium on the right and the left atrium on the left. The thin upper part of the septum carries the fossa ovalis, which is the remnant of the opening that joined the two atria before birth.

Question 3561. A constructed illustration: a newborn has a small hole in the membranous part of the interventricular septum and a loud murmur, but the skin is not blue. Why is the blood not blue? The answer is that the pressures on the two sides keep the shunt moving from the left ventricle into the right.

Question 3562. True or false: the fossa ovalis lies in the interventricular septum. The answer is false. It lies in the interatrial septum as the remnant of the foramen ovale, and it is seen from the right atrium. The interventricular septum carries no such depression.

Question 3563. What is the name of the muscular ridge that forms the upper border of the fossa ovalis: the crista terminalis, the limbus of the fossa ovalis, the moderator band, or the sinus of the venae cavae? The answer is the limbus of the fossa ovalis. It is the raised rim of the fetal opening, and the floor below it is thin.

Question 3564. What is the name of the oval depression on the right side of the interatrial septum? The answer is the fossa ovalis. It marks the site of the foramen ovale, through which blood passed from the right atrium to the left before birth. Its floor is thin and can be probed from the left side.

Question 3565. A section of the heart is cut in a plane that divides it into an anterior part and a posterior part, passing through the right atrium and the right ventricle. Which plane is it, and would it open the left ventricle as well? The answer is a coronal plane, and it would not open the left ventricle, which lies behind and to the left.

Question 3566. Which structure lies between the right atrium and the left ventricle, so that a hole there lets blood pass from the left ventricle into the right atrium: the membranous interventricular septum, the atrioventricular part of the septum, the muscular septum, or the fossa ovalis? The answer is the atrioventricular part of the septum.

Question 3567. A described four-chamber section shows two thin-walled chambers above and two thick-walled chambers below, divided by a septum that is complete except for a thin membrane near its middle. Which septum holds that thin membrane? The answer is the interventricular septum, whose membranous part lies at that point.

Question 3568. Which structure lies immediately behind the membranous part of the interventricular septum, so that a defect there may involve it: the tricuspid valve, the aortic valve, the mitral valve, or the pulmonary valve? The answer is the aortic valve. The membranous septum lies just below the aortic root, and the septal cusp of the tricuspid valve is close beside it.

Question 3569. Which pair belongs together: the foramen ovale with the fossa ovalis, the ductus arteriosus with the ligamentum teres, the ductus venosus with the ligamentum arteriosum, or the umbilical vein with the fossa ovalis? The answer is the foramen ovale with the fossa ovalis. The ductus arteriosus becomes the ligamentum arteriosum, and the umbilical vein becomes the ligamentum teres of the liver.

Question 3570. Which artery supplies the greater part of the interventricular septum? The answer is the anterior interventricular branch of the left coronary artery. It runs down the front of the septum toward the apex. The posterior interventricular branch supplies the back of the septum, and the two branches meet near the apex.

Question 3571. A constructed illustration: a person has a hole in the muscular part of the interventricular septum, and the murmur is loud but the heart is not enlarged. Which part of the septum is affected? The answer is the muscular part. A hole surrounded by muscle is often small and tends to narrow as the muscle contracts.

Question 3572. The moderator band stretches from the interventricular septum to which structure: the anterior papillary muscle, the posterior papillary muscle, the septal cusp of the tricuspid valve, or the aortic valve? The answer is the anterior papillary muscle. The band therefore crosses the cavity of the right ventricle near its apex.

Question 3573. Name the two parts of the interventricular septum. The answer is the muscular part and the membranous part. The muscular part forms the greater part of the septum and is thick. The membranous part is a small thin area at the top, lying just below the aortic valve.

Question 3574. A described cross section of the heart shows the interventricular septum thick below and thin above, with muscle on both sides of the thick part and fibrous tissue around the thin part. Which part is which? The answer is that the thick lower part is muscular and the thin upper part is membranous.

Question 3575. Which is the largest artery in the body? The answer is the aorta. It begins at the aortic valve and ends by dividing into the two common iliac arteries in the abdomen. Its wall is thick and elastic, which lets it absorb the surge of each heartbeat and smooth the flow onward.

Question 3576. Which vessels arise from the aorta immediately above the aortic valve: the coronary arteries, the brachiocephalic trunk, the common carotid arteries, or the intercostal arteries? The answer is the coronary arteries. The right and left coronary arteries arise from the right and left aortic sinuses and supply the muscle of the heart itself.

Question 3577. A described chest radiograph shows a rounded bulge at the upper left border of the mediastinum, just below the point where the trachea divides. Which part of the aorta forms it? The answer is the aortic arch, and more exactly its knuckle. It lies to the left of the trachea and above the left pulmonary artery.

Question 3578. Which great vessel lies immediately in front of the aorta as the two leave the heart, and which lies behind? The answer is that the pulmonary trunk lies in front and to the left, while the aorta begins behind and to the right of it. The two vessels then spiral around one another as they rise.

Question 3579. A constructed illustration: a young person has a narrowed segment of the aorta just beyond the origin of the left subclavian artery, and the ribs show notches along their lower borders. Which segment is narrowed? The answer is the aortic isthmus, the short part of the arch between the left subclavian artery and the descending aorta.

Question 3580. Which statement about the pulmonary trunk is correct: it carries oxygenated blood away from the heart, it carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs, it carries blood from the lungs to the left atrium, or it is a branch of the aorta? The answer is that it carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs. It divides into the right and left pulmonary arteries.

Question 3581. What does the ligamentum arteriosum connect: the pulmonary trunk to the aortic arch, the aorta to the superior vena cava, the left pulmonary artery to the left atrium, or the aorta to the pulmonary veins? The answer is the pulmonary trunk to the aortic arch. It is the fibrous remnant of the ductus arteriosus.

Question 3582. Which two veins unite to form the superior vena cava? The answer is the right and left brachiocephalic veins. They join behind the lower end of the right first costal cartilage. The superior vena cava then descends behind the right border of the sternum and opens into the right atrium.

Question 3583. Within the pericardium, how do the aorta and the pulmonary trunk relate to one another along their first few centimeters? The answer is that the pulmonary trunk begins in front of the aorta and to its left, and the two then spiral, so that the aorta comes to lie behind and to the right. Their cusps face in different directions.

Question 3584. Which pair belongs together: the superior vena cava with the right atrium, the pulmonary veins with the right atrium, the coronary sinus with the left atrium, or the inferior vena cava with the left atrium? The answer is the superior vena cava with the right atrium. The pulmonary veins enter the left atrium and the coronary sinus enters the right atrium.

Question 3585. A described angiogram of the ascending aorta shows two small branches leaving the sinuses just above the valve and running down in the grooves between the atria and the ventricles. Which vessels are shown? The answer is the right and left coronary arteries. They are the first branches of the aorta.

Question 3586. A constructed illustration: after a severe chest injury a described radiograph shows a widened upper mediastinum and a small collection of fluid on the left. Which structure is most likely torn at the point where it is fixed? The answer is the descending thoracic aorta at the isthmus, just beyond the left subclavian artery.

Question 3587. Which statement about the coronary arteries is correct: the right coronary artery usually supplies the sinoatrial node, the left coronary artery arises from the pulmonary trunk, the right coronary artery arises from the left sinus, or the coronary arteries arise from the left ventricle? The answer is that the right coronary artery usually supplies the sinoatrial node in most hearts.

Question 3588. Where does the coronary sinus open? The answer is into the right atrium, just above the opening of the inferior vena cava and in front of the interatrial septum. It is the main collecting vein of the muscle of the heart, and a small fold guards its opening.

Question 3589. A described dissection follows the right coronary artery from its origin. In which groove does it run, and where does it end in most hearts? The answer is the coronary sulcus, and it ends by turning down the back of the heart as the posterior interventricular branch. Along the way it supplies the right atrium and the right ventricle.

Question 3590. Which vessel drains most of the blood from the muscle of the heart itself: the coronary sinus, the superior vena cava, the pulmonary veins, or the azygos vein? The answer is the coronary sinus. It opens into the right atrium. The superior vena cava drains the head and the upper limbs, and the pulmonary veins drain the lungs.

Question 3591. A constructed illustration: a person has a blockage in the artery that runs down the front of the interventricular groove toward the apex. Which part of the heart is at risk? The answer is the front and the apex of the left ventricle, together with the front of the interventricular septum.

Question 3592. A described oblique view of the aortic arch shows three branches arising from its upper border, the first of them dividing almost at once. How many branches are shown, and which is the first? The answer is three, and the first is the brachiocephalic trunk. It divides into the right common carotid and right subclavian arteries.

Question 3593. Which pair belongs together: the left common carotid artery with the left side of the head and neck, the brachiocephalic trunk with the right arm alone, the left subclavian artery with the right arm, or the right subclavian artery with the left side of the head? The answer is the left common carotid artery with the left side of the head and neck.

Question 3594. A described view of the back of the heart shows four veins entering the smooth posterior wall of one chamber, two on each side, and none of them has a valve. Which chamber is shown, and why is there no valve there? The answer is the left atrium, whose wall is smooth where the pulmonary veins enter.

Question 3595. A described dissection passes a finger behind the aorta and the pulmonary trunk and brings it out on the other side, all within the pericardium. Which space is the finger in: the oblique pericardial sinus, the transverse pericardial sinus, the coronary sulcus, or the aortic opening of the diaphragm? The answer is the transverse pericardial sinus.

Question 3596. Which small vein runs down the back of the left atrium and joins the coronary sinus? The answer is the oblique vein of the left atrium. It is a remnant of the left common cardinal vein of the embryo. It descends over the posterior wall of the left atrium on its way to the sinus.

Question 3597. A constructed illustration: a person has swelling of the face and the upper limbs with distended veins over the upper chest, while the legs are normal. Which large vein is obstructed? The answer is the superior vena cava. It drains the head, the neck, the upper limbs and the upper chest, so obstruction shows above the diaphragm.

Question 3598. Which structures lie immediately in front of the descending thoracic aorta as it passes down through the chest? The answer is the esophagus, which lies to its right and in front, and the root of the left lung. The aorta itself lies against the vertebral column, a little to the left of the midline.

Question 3599. In most people the aortic arch gives three branches. Which variation is common enough that a surgeon should expect it: a fourth branch, the left vertebral artery arising directly from the arch, an arch with only one branch, or a right-sided arch in most people? The answer is the left vertebral artery arising directly from the arch.

Question 3600. Name the three branches of the aortic arch, spoken in order from right to left. The answer is the brachiocephalic trunk, the left common carotid artery and the left subclavian artery. The brachiocephalic trunk divides into the right common carotid and the right subclavian arteries.

To summarize, the right atrium receives the two venae cavae and the coronary sinus, and it forms the right border of the heart, while the left atrium lies most posteriorly, in front of the esophagus. The right ventricle is the most anterior chamber and lies behind the sternum, but the left ventricle has the thickest wall and forms the apex, which is felt in the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line. Each atrioventricular valve is held shut by its cusps, chordae tendineae and papillary muscles, and the aortic and pulmonary valves are semilunar valves with three cusps each, the pulmonary valve being the most anterior of the four. The interventricular septum is muscular below and membranous above, the membranous part lying just below the aortic valve, while the fossa ovalis with its limbus marks the site of the foramen ovale in the interatrial septum. The aorta leaves the left ventricle and gives off the coronary arteries from its sinuses before arching to the left, and the pulmonary trunk passes in front of it and to the left.
