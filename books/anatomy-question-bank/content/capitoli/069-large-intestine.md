# Chapter 69 — Large Intestine

This chapter follows the last stretch of the small intestine and then the large intestine from its beginning to its end. It opens with the ileum, the terminal part of the small intestine, and then works through the cecum, the appendix, the colon, and the rectum. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 6801. Which part of the small intestine is the terminal one, joining the large intestine at the ileocecal junction in the right lower abdomen? The answer is the ileum. It follows the jejunum and is the longest part of the small intestine, and its terminal end opens into the cecum on the posteromedial wall.

Question 6802. Which structure guards the opening between the ileum and the large intestine and stops colonic contents from running back into the small intestine? The answer is the ileocecal valve. Two lips of mucous membrane project into the cecum at the ileocecal orifice and meet at the frenula.

Question 6803. A described specimen of the small intestine shows one segment whose wall carries large collections of lymphoid tissue along the border opposite its mesentery. Which segment is this? The answer is the ileum. Those collections are aggregated lymphoid nodules, known as Peyer patches, and they lie on the antimesenteric border, largest in the lower ileum.

Question 6804. Which of these features belongs to the ileum rather than the jejunum: a thick wall, long straight vessels, abundant fat in the mesentery, or tall circular folds throughout? The answer is abundant fat in the mesentery. The fat of the ileal mesentery encroaches on the wall of the bowel, while the jejunum has the thicker wall and the longer vessels.

Question 6805. As the terminal ileum crosses the right iliac fossa, which muscle and which vessels lie immediately behind it? The answer is the right psoas major muscle and the right external iliac vessels. The ileum is the most mobile part of the gut, so it comes to rest in front of them and may dip down into the pelvis.

Question 6806. Which artery supplies the terminal ileum and also gives off the artery of the appendix: the ileocolic artery, the right colic artery, the middle colic artery, or the inferior mesenteric artery? The answer is the ileocolic artery. It is the lowest branch of the superior mesenteric artery and divides into ileal, cecal, and appendicular branches.

Question 6807. True or false: the ileum receives its parasympathetic nerve supply from the vagus nerve. The answer is true. The vagus nerve reaches the midgut, and the ileum belongs to the midgut, through the superior mesenteric plexus, while sympathetic fibers come from the lower thoracic splanchnic nerves.

Question 6808. Which part of the small intestine absorbs vitamin B12 and the bile salts that the liver recycles? The answer is the terminal ileum. It is the only place where vitamin B12 is taken up, and it also carries the active transport that reclaims bile salts.

Question 6809. Name, as a list of three, the features of the ileum that distinguish it from the jejunum. The answer is aggregated lymphoid nodules, abundant fat in the mesentery, and a thinner wall with shorter straight vessels. The nodules lie on the antimesenteric border, and the fat creeps from the mesentery onto the wall.

Question 6810. Which of these pairs belongs together: the ileum with the midgut, the ileum with the hindgut, the ileum with the foregut, or the ileum with the proctodeum? The answer is the ileum with the midgut. The midgut runs from the opening of the bile duct to the junction of the right two thirds and the left third of the transverse colon, and its artery is the superior mesenteric artery.

Question 6811. The root of the mesentery runs from the duodenojejunal flexure down to the right iliac fossa. Which structures does it cross? The answer is the third part of the duodenum, the aorta, the inferior vena cava, and the right ureter. The right psoas muscle lies beneath it, which is why the mesentery slopes down to the right.

Question 6812. Which of these statements about the mesentery of the ileum is correct: it holds the ileum against the posterior abdominal wall, it allows the ileum to move freely, it contains no blood vessels, or it is attached to the colon? The answer is that it allows the ileum to move freely, because the small intestine hangs on a long mobile mesentery.

Question 6813. A described section across the abdomen at the level of the umbilicus shows coils of bowel wrapped in a fold that carries vessels to them. Which part of the gut is shown? The answer is the ileum, with its mesentery. The mesentery carries the branches of the superior mesenteric artery and the veins that accompany them.

Question 6814. A constructed illustration: at an operation for long-standing inflammatory bowel disease the surgeon finds patches of disease separated by healthy stretches, with the whole thickness of the wall involved. Which part of the gut is most often affected? The answer is the terminal ileum. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6815. Which of these statements about the ileocecal junction is correct: the ileum opens into the ascending colon, into the posteromedial wall of the cecum, into the transverse colon, or into the sigmoid colon? The answer is that it opens into the posteromedial wall of the cecum, and the appendicular orifice lies a little below and behind that opening.

Question 6816. Which part of the small intestine lies between the duodenojejunal flexure and the ileum, is thicker walled and more vascular, and carries the tallest circular folds? The answer is the jejunum, which occupies the upper left part of the abdominal cavity. The ileum follows it and ends at the ileocecal junction.

Question 6817. A described specimen shows a narrow finger-like pouch on the antimesenteric border of the lower ileum, about 60 centimeters from the ileocecal junction. What is the developmental explanation? The answer is a persistent vitellointestinal duct, the remains of the duct that once joined the midgut to the yolk sac. A pouch on the mesenteric border would not fit.

Question 6818. Which structure lies immediately behind the terminal ileum as it crosses the brim of the pelvis, and which structure crosses in front of that vessel? The answer is the right external iliac artery, with the right ureter crossing in front of it. The ileum often lies in front of the ureter as well.

Question 6819. A described radiograph taken after a barium meal shows the lower coils of the small intestine, whose lining carries fine feathery markings, ending in a rounded sac on the right. Which part of the gut is shown? The answer is the ileum, ending at the cecum. The feathery pattern comes from the lymphoid nodules and the mucosal folds.

Question 6820. Which of these structures is found in the mesentery of the ileum but not in the tissue behind the descending colon: tiers of arterial arcades, fat, lymph nodes, or a peritoneal covering? The answer is tiers of arterial arcades. The descending colon lies against the abdominal wall behind the peritoneum.

Question 6821. A constructed illustration: a person has lost a long stretch of the distal small intestine at an operation. Which two substances are now poorly absorbed? The answer is vitamin B12 and bile salts, with the terminal ileum removed. The loss of bile salt reabsorption causes watery diarrhea, and the vitamin deficiency follows later. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6822. Which of these statements about the blood supply of the ileum is correct: it comes from the inferior mesenteric artery, from the superior mesenteric artery, from the internal iliac artery, or from the celiac trunk? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery. The ileum belongs to the midgut, and its vessels run with the nerves of the superior mesenteric plexus.

Question 6823. A described dissection shows the terminal ileum lifted forward, uncovering a fold that runs down and to the right behind it. Which fold is shown? The answer is the root of the mesentery, carrying the superior mesenteric vessels and the lymph vessels of the small intestine. It crosses the duodenum, the aorta, and the inferior vena cava.

Question 6824. What is the name of the permanent circular folds of the small intestine that project into the lumen and increase its surface? The answer is the circular folds, also called the valvulae conniventes. They are tallest in the jejunum and small and widely spaced in the lower ileum.

Question 6825. Which of these is the longest part of the small intestine: the duodenum, the jejunum, the ileum, or the cecum? The answer is the ileum. The small intestine is about 6 meters long in an adult, and the ileum makes up rather more than half of that, while the duodenum is the shortest part.

Question 6826. Which part of the large intestine is the widest and lies below the ileocecal junction in the right iliac fossa? The answer is the cecum. It is a blind-ended pouch about 6 centimeters long, and it is the first part of the large intestine. The appendix hangs from its posteromedial wall.

Question 6827. Which of these features is found in the large intestine but not in the small intestine: circular folds, villi, sacculations called haustra, or a mesentery? The answer is haustra. The colon also carries three flat longitudinal bands, the teniae coli, and rows of fatty tags, the appendices epiploicae.

Question 6828. A described specimen shows a short blind pouch with a worm-like tube hanging from its lower part, and three flat bands converging on the base of that tube. Which structure is the pouch? The answer is the cecum, with the three teniae coli. The tube is the appendix, and the bands converging at its base are the surest guide to it.

Question 6829. Which structures lie immediately behind the cecum in the right iliac fossa? The answer is the iliacus and the psoas major muscles, with the femoral nerve and the lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh lying on them. The posterior surface of the cecum often lacks peritoneum.

Question 6830. A constructed illustration: a loop that includes the cecum and the lower ileum becomes trapped in a swelling of the lower abdominal wall just above the groin. Which named passage has it entered? The answer is the inguinal canal, though such contents may instead pass through the femoral canal below the inguinal ligament. Which it is belongs to the examining clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6831. True or false: the cecum lies in the right upper quadrant of the abdomen. The answer is false. The cecum lies in the right iliac fossa, below the ileocecal junction, at about the level of the anterior superior iliac spine in most people.

Question 6832. Which artery supplies the cecum: the ileocolic artery, the right colic artery, the middle colic artery, or the inferior mesenteric artery? The answer is the ileocolic artery. It gives an anterior cecal branch and a posterior cecal branch. The right colic artery supplies the ascending colon instead.

Question 6833. Which part of the colon is attached to the cecum above the ileocecal junction and runs upward toward the liver? The answer is the ascending colon. It is about 15 centimeters long and lies against the posterior abdominal wall. The peritoneum covers it in front and at the sides only.

Question 6834. Which of these statements about the peritoneum of the cecum is correct: it is completely behind the peritoneum, it is fully covered and often mobile, it has no peritoneal covering at all, or it lies within the lesser sac? The answer is that it is fully covered and often mobile. A cecum with a short mesentery may twist about its own axis.

Question 6835. The cecum lies in front of which nerve as that nerve crosses the iliacus muscle, and what follows from that relation? The answer is the femoral nerve. A distended cecum, or an inflamed appendix behind it, can press on the nerve and cause pain in front of the thigh.

Question 6836. A described section of the lower abdomen on the right shows a wide thin-walled pouch containing gas, with a small tube set in its wall and a flat band running along its surface. Which structure is the pouch? The answer is the cecum, with a tenia coli on its surface. The small tube in the wall is the appendix seen in section.

Question 6837. Which of these pairs belongs together: the cecum with the ileocolic artery, with the middle colic artery, with the inferior mesenteric artery, or with the left colic artery? The answer is the cecum with the ileocolic artery. The cecum belongs to the midgut, so its blood reaches it through the superior mesenteric artery.

Question 6838. A constructed illustration: a person with a mobile cecum arrives with a distended and tender right side of the abdomen, and a described radiograph shows a large gas-filled loop folded over on itself. What has happened? The answer is a twist of the cecum on its mesentery. The cecum is the widest segment of the colon and can rotate about the ileocolic axis. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6839. Which of these statements about the ileocecal junction is correct: the cecum lies above the junction, below it, to its left, or around it on all sides? The answer is that the cecum lies below the ileocecal junction. The ileum enters from above and to the left, and the appendix arises below and behind that opening.

Question 6840. What are the small fatty tags that hang from the outer surface of the colon, and from which part of the large intestine are they absent? The answer is the appendices epiploicae, which are absent from the rectum. They are little pouches of peritoneum filled with fat, and they lie in rows along the colon.

Question 6841. Where is the base of the appendix attached, and how is it recognized at operation? The answer is the posteromedial wall of the cecum, a little below the ileocecal junction, where the three teniae coli converge. Following the bands down to their meeting point leads to the base, which is the surest guide when the appendix is hidden.

Question 6842. Which of these is the most common position of the appendix: retrocecal, pelvic, preileal, or subcecal? The answer is retrocecal, behind the cecum in most people, with the pelvic position next. Because the positions vary so much, the pain of an inflamed appendix may point in different directions.

Question 6843. A described radiograph taken after a barium enema shows a thin blind-ending tube filled with contrast, running upward behind a gas-filled pouch on the right. Which structure is shown? The answer is the appendix in the retrocecal position. It lies behind the cecum and may reach as high as the right kidney.

Question 6844. Which vessel runs in the mesoappendix to reach the appendix, and from which artery does it arise? The answer is the appendicular artery, from the ileocolic artery, running in the free edge of the mesoappendix behind the terminal ileum. It is an end artery, so blockage of it starves the wall of the appendix.

Question 6845. A constructed illustration: an inflamed appendix lies behind the cecum, and the person feels pain when the right hip is straightened against resistance. Which muscle is being stretched? The answer is the psoas major, and the sign is named after it. The appendix rests on that muscle when it lies behind the cecum.

Question 6846. True or false: the appendix carries the same three longitudinal bands as the rest of the colon. The answer is false. The longitudinal muscle of the appendix forms a complete coat around the tube, which is one reason it has no haustra. Its wall also holds abundant lymphoid tissue in the young.

Question 6847. Which of these statements about the referred pain of an inflamed appendix is correct: it is first felt in the right iliac fossa, around the umbilicus, in the left groin, or never in the abdomen? The answer is that it is first felt around the umbilicus. The pain moves to the right iliac fossa only when the outer peritoneum is irritated.

Question 6848. What is the name of the point on the front of the abdomen that lies one third of the way from the right anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus? The answer is McBurney point, which marks the usual site of the base of the appendix. Tenderness there suggests inflammation of the appendix.

Question 6849. A described appendix is removed, and its tip is black while its base is sound. Which feature of its arterial supply explains this? The answer is that the appendicular artery is an end artery running in the mesoappendix. Blockage of the main trunk starves the tip first, while the base still receives branches from the cecum.

Question 6850. A described dissection shows a small tube whose mesentery contains a vessel and whose wall is thickened by many pale nodules in a young person. Which structure is shown? The answer is the appendix with its lymphoid follicles. The nodules are most numerous between the ages of ten and twenty and shrink with age.

Question 6851. A retrocecal appendix lies against which structures, and why may the pain then be felt in the back or the thigh? The answer is the right kidney, the right ureter, and the psoas major muscle. Irritation of the psoas makes the person hold the hip flexed and causes pain when it is straightened.

Question 6852. A constructed illustration: a person in late pregnancy has pain felt higher than usual on the right side of the abdomen. Where has the appendix moved? The answer is upward, because the enlarging uterus lifts the cecum and the appendix out of the pelvis. This is why the usual site of tenderness changes in pregnancy.

Question 6853. Which of these structures lies closest to the appendix when it lies behind the cecum: the right ureter, the spleen, the left kidney, or the pancreas? The answer is the right ureter. It runs down on the psoas muscle behind the cecum, so an inflamed appendix lying against it may cause pain on passing urine.

Question 6854. Name, as a list of three, the guides a surgeon uses to find the base of the appendix when it lies hidden behind the cecum. The answer is the three teniae coli, the ileocecal junction, and the terminal ileum. The bands are followed down to the point where all three meet.

Question 6855. Which of these pairs belongs together: a pelvic appendix with the rectum, a retrocecal appendix with the stomach, a preileal appendix with the spleen, or a subcecal appendix with the left kidney? The answer is a pelvic appendix with the rectum. It hangs over the brim of the pelvis and rests near the bladder and the rectum.

Question 6856. Which of these statements about the appendix is correct: it has no muscle in its wall, it holds abundant lymphoid tissue in the young, it always measures 2 centimeters, or it opens into the ascending colon? The answer is that it holds abundant lymphoid tissue in the young. The appendix opens into the cecum below the ileocecal orifice.

Question 6857. Which small tube hangs from the cecum and is the commonest site of sudden inflammation in the right lower abdomen? The answer is the vermiform appendix. It is a blind-ended tube that opens into the cecum and carries its own small mesentery, the mesoappendix.

Question 6858. A described frontal radiograph taken after a barium enema shows a pouch on the right filled with contrast, and no contrast entering the thin tube that hangs below it. Which structures are shown? The answer is the cecum and the appendix. The appendix fills only when the contrast runs back into its narrow lumen.

Question 6859. Which of these statements explains why an inflamed appendix in the pelvic position may cause diarrhea and a frequent need to pass urine: it lies against the rectum and the bladder, against the stomach, against the left kidney, or within the lesser sac? The answer is that it lies against the rectum and the bladder. Tenderness may then be found only on examination through the rectum.

Question 6860. Which layer of the wall of the appendix runs all the way around the tube, in contrast to the colon where it gathers into three bands? The answer is the longitudinal muscle coat. Because it is complete, the appendix is a smooth tube without haustra. Its circular muscle lies within it.

Question 6861. Which part of the colon runs upward from the cecum to the right colic flexure, lying against the posterior abdominal wall? The answer is the ascending colon. It is about 15 centimeters long and is covered by peritoneum in front and at the sides. The right kidney and the right ureter lie behind it.

Question 6862. Which of these parts of the colon lies between the right colic flexure and the left colic flexure: the ascending colon, the transverse colon, the descending colon, or the sigmoid colon? The answer is the transverse colon. It crosses the abdomen and hangs on its own mesentery, the transverse mesocolon, and it is the most mobile part of the colon.

Question 6863. A described barium enema shows a tube crossing the middle of the abdomen with a scalloped outline of rounded pouches, hanging lower in the middle than at either end. Which part of the colon is shown? The answer is the transverse colon, and the pouches are haustra. They appear because the three longitudinal bands are shorter than the bowel itself.

Question 6864. Which flexure of the colon lies higher and further back than the other, and is attached to the diaphragm by a fold of peritoneum? The answer is the left colic flexure, also called the splenic flexure. The phrenicocolic ligament holds it up beneath the spleen.

Question 6865. Which of these statements about the right colic flexure is correct: it lies higher than the left colic flexure, lower than it, in the pelvis, or behind the spleen? The answer is that it lies lower than the left colic flexure. It sits under the right lobe of the liver and in front of the right kidney.

Question 6866. Which part of the colon descends on the left side of the abdomen from the left colic flexure to the brim of the pelvis? The answer is the descending colon. It is about 25 centimeters long and lies behind the peritoneum, in front of the left kidney and the left psoas muscle.

Question 6867. True or false: the descending colon has a mesentery like the transverse colon. The answer is false. The descending colon lies behind the peritoneum against the posterior abdominal wall, and only the transverse and sigmoid parts of the colon carry a mesocolon. The ascending colon is fixed in the same way.

Question 6868. Which of these sites in the colon is a watershed where the territories of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries meet: the cecum, the right colic flexure, the left colic flexure, or the appendix? The answer is the left colic flexure. The middle colic artery meets the left colic artery there, and the channel between them is weak.

Question 6869. A described radiograph taken after a barium enema shows a colon of normal length with smooth haustra, and a narrow segment low in the pelvis where the column of contrast ends. Which junction is it? The answer is the rectosigmoid junction. It is a second watershed, between the inferior mesenteric artery and the vessels of the pelvis.

Question 6870. The transverse mesocolon is attached to the posterior abdominal wall along which structure, and what lies behind that attachment? The answer is the lower border of the pancreas, with the duodenum and the pancreas behind it. The middle colic artery runs between the two layers of the mesocolon.

Question 6871. Which part of the colon begins at the brim of the pelvis on the left, forms an S-shaped loop, and ends at the third sacral vertebra? The answer is the sigmoid colon. It hangs on the sigmoid mesocolon and is the narrowest part of the colon.

Question 6872. Which of these structures lies immediately behind the sigmoid colon: the left ureter, the right kidney, the spleen, or the gallbladder? The answer is the left ureter. It crosses the brim of the pelvis behind the sigmoid colon and its mesocolon, and the left gonadal vessels lie behind it as well.

Question 6873. A constructed illustration: an older person has long-standing pain in the left lower abdomen, and a described radiograph shows small out-pouchings along the wall of the sigmoid colon. Where do these pouches push through? The answer is at the points where the small arteries pierce the muscle wall. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6874. A described radiograph shows a large gas-filled loop that rises out of the pelvis and crosses to the right upper abdomen, with the haustra gathered around a central line. Which part of the colon is described? The answer is the sigmoid colon. It hangs on a long mesocolon, so it can rotate about its own axis.

Question 6875. Which of these pairs belongs together: the transverse colon with the greater omentum, the transverse colon with the spleen, the descending colon with the liver, or the sigmoid colon with the right kidney? The answer is the transverse colon with the greater omentum. The greater omentum hangs from the stomach and folds down over the transverse colon.

Question 6876. What are the three flat longitudinal bands on the surface of the colon called, and how many are there? The answer is the teniae coli, and there are three. They are shorter than the bowel, so the wall gathers into haustra between them. One lies where the mesocolon is attached, one where the omentum hangs, and one is free.

Question 6877. A described person has brisk painless bleeding from the large intestine. Which of these statements fits best: it comes from the right colon, from the rectum, from the appendix, or from the small intestine? The answer is that it comes from the right colon. The pouches there lie beside the small arteries, so the bleeding is often brisk and painless.

Question 6878. The left colic flexure is attached to which structure above it, and which organ lies immediately above and lateral to it? The answer is the diaphragm, by the phrenicocolic ligament, with the spleen above and lateral to it. The tail of the pancreas lies close behind the flexure.

Question 6879. Which of these statements explains why a growth in the right colon may cause anemia while one in the left causes obstruction: the right colon is wider and its contents are liquid, it is narrower, the left colon is wider, or the left colon lacks a blood supply? The answer is that the right colon is wider and its contents are liquid. The left colon is narrower and carries solid stool.

Question 6880. A constructed illustration: a young child has bouts of pain, a sausage-shaped mass in the right upper abdomen, and stool mixed with blood and mucus. Which part of the gut has telescoped into which? The answer is that the terminal ileum has slipped into the cecum. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 6881. Which artery runs along the inner border of the colon and joins the branches of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries? The answer is the marginal artery. It lies in the mesocolon close to the wall of the bowel and keeps the colon alive when a main branch narrows.

Question 6882. A described specimen of the colon shows a segment from the left side, with rows of fatty tags on its surface and peritoneum only at the front. Which part of the colon was removed? The answer is the descending colon, and the tags are appendices epiploicae. That part of the colon lies behind the peritoneum.

Question 6883. Which of these statements explains why the sigmoid colon is the commonest site of a twist in older people: it has a long mesentery and a narrow lumen, it is fixed to the posterior wall, it lies behind the peritoneum, or it has no blood supply? The answer is that it has a long mesentery and a narrow lumen, so a loaded loop can rotate on its axis.

Question 6884. In a described transverse section of the abdomen at the level of the third lumbar vertebra, which of these structures lies most anterior: the body of the vertebra, the inferior vena cava, the transverse colon, or the aorta? The answer is the transverse colon. It crosses the abdomen in front of the duodenum and the pancreas.

Question 6885. Which artery supplies the left colic flexure: the middle colic artery, the left colic artery, the right colic artery, or the ileocolic artery? The answer is the left colic artery, a branch of the inferior mesenteric artery. The middle colic artery approaches the same flexure from the superior mesenteric side, and the two meet there.

Question 6886. Which part of the large intestine runs from the third sacral vertebra to the anorectal junction, and has no haustra and no teniae coli? The answer is the rectum. It is about 12 centimeters long, and its lower dilated part is the rectal ampulla.

Question 6887. Which of these features is absent from the rectum: a muscular wall, a mucosal lining, haustra, or a blood supply? The answer is haustra. The rectum has no sacculations and no separate longitudinal bands, and its longitudinal muscle forms a complete coat around the tube.

Question 6888. A described section through the pelvis shows a tube lying in front of the sacrum, with a muscular sling passing behind its lower end. Which structure is the tube? The answer is the rectum, and the sling is the puborectalis part of the levator ani. It pulls the junction of the rectum and the anal canal forward and keeps the anorectal angle.

Question 6889. In the male, which structures lie immediately in front of the rectum, and which pouch lies between them and the rectum? The answer is the urinary bladder, the seminal vesicles, and the prostate, with the rectovesical pouch between. That pouch is the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity in the male.

Question 6890. A constructed illustration: a growth low in the rectum has spread to the lymph nodes of the groin. Which part of the rectum is involved? The answer is the lowest part of the rectum and the anal canal below the pectinate line. That region drains to the superficial inguinal nodes, unlike the upper rectum.

Question 6891. True or false: the peritoneum covers the whole of the rectum. The answer is false. The upper third is covered on its front and sides, the middle third only in front, and the lower third not at all, which is why the lower rectum lies outside the peritoneal cavity.

Question 6892. Which artery supplies the upper part of the rectum: the superior rectal artery, the middle rectal artery, the inferior rectal artery, or the internal pudendal artery? The answer is the superior rectal artery. It is the continuation of the inferior mesenteric artery below the brim of the pelvis.

Question 6893. Which line in the anal canal marks the meeting of the hindgut and the proctodeum? The answer is the pectinate line, also called the dentate line. Above it the lining is columnar and the veins drain to the portal system, and below it the veins drain to the systemic system.

Question 6894. Which of these statements explains why internal hemorrhoids are painless while external hemorrhoids are painful: the internal ones lie above the pectinate line, the internal ones lie below the line, the external ones lie above the line, or neither kind has a nerve supply? The answer is that the internal ones lie above the pectinate line, where the nerve supply is visceral. External hemorrhoids lie below the line, in skin.

Question 6895. A described radiograph taken after a barium enema shows a column of contrast that stops low in the pelvis, and the outline of a smooth tube curving forward behind the bladder. Which part of the gut is shown? The answer is the rectum. It has no haustra and no longitudinal bands, so its outline is smooth.

Question 6896. A constructed illustration: on examination through the back passage, a rounded firm structure is felt in front of the rectum in a man. Which organ is being felt? The answer is the prostate gland. In a woman the cervix is felt in the same position through the front wall of the rectum.

Question 6897. Which of these pairs belongs together: the upper rectum with the superior rectal artery, the upper rectum with the internal pudendal artery, the anal canal with the portal vein, or the lower rectum with the middle colic artery? The answer is the upper rectum with the superior rectal artery, the continuation of the inferior mesenteric artery.

Question 6898. Name, as a list of three, the arteries that supply the rectum and the anal canal. The answer is the superior rectal artery, the middle rectal arteries, and the inferior rectal arteries. The superior one comes from the inferior mesenteric artery, the middle ones from the internal iliac arteries, and the inferior ones from the internal pudendal arteries. They join one another, so the rectum has a rich supply.

Question 6899. Which of these statements about the veins of the rectum is correct: the superior rectal vein drains to the systemic system, all three drain to the portal system, all three drain to the systemic system, or the rectum has no veins? The answer is that the superior rectal vein drains to the portal system and the other two to the systemic system. The two systems meet in the wall of the rectum.

Question 6900. In a described sagittal section of the pelvis, the structures lie in the order pubic symphysis, bladder, prostate, rectum. Which of these statements is correct: the rectum lies in front of the bladder, the prostate lies in front of the bladder, the rectum lies behind all the others, or the pubic symphysis lies most posterior? The answer is that the rectum lies behind all the others, in front of the sacrum.

To summarize, the ileum is the longest and most distal part of the small intestine, it carries aggregated lymphoid nodules on its antimesenteric border, and its terminal segment absorbs vitamin B12 and bile salts before opening into the cecum through the ileocecal valve. The large intestine begins at the cecum, its widest part, in the right iliac fossa, and the appendix rises from the posteromedial wall of the cecum where the three teniae coli converge. The colon carries haustra, three longitudinal bands, and rows of fatty appendices epiploicae, and only its transverse and sigmoid parts have a mesentery. The midgut colon is supplied by the superior mesenteric artery and the hindgut colon and upper rectum by the inferior mesenteric artery, with watersheds at the left colic flexure and the rectosigmoid junction. The rectum has no haustra and no teniae, its peritoneal covering decreases from above downward, and the pectinate line divides the anal canal into a visceral upper part and a somatic lower part.
