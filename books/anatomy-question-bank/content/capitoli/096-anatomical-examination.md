# Chapter 96 — Anatomical Examination

This chapter turns to the living body as it is examined at the bedside, the anatomy that is used with the eyes, the hands and a stethoscope. It covers the surface landmarks that lead the examiner to deeper structures, the four classical methods of examination and the order in which they are applied, and the tests that reveal the position, the size and the movement of the organs beneath. It holds one hundred questions, and every one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right. The last part follows the nerves and the vessels that can be examined without cutting. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9501. Which of the four classical methods of examination uses the hands to feel the size, the texture and the tenderness of a structure? The answer is palpation. Inspection uses the eyes, percussion a light tap, and auscultation a stethoscope. Palpation brings the examining hand into contact with the tissues beneath the skin.

Question 9502. During an examination of the abdomen, which method is carried out before palpation: auscultation, percussion, deep palpation, or measurement of the girth? The answer is auscultation. Pressing on the abdomen can change the faint bowel sounds and make them harder to hear. Percussion and palpation therefore follow the stethoscope.

Question 9503. Which method of examination produces a note by tapping one finger against another finger laid flat on the body wall? The answer is percussion. The finger on the body acts as a plate and the striking finger as a hammer. A resonant note comes from air in the lung and a dull note from solid tissue such as the liver.

Question 9504. True or false: inspection is the first method applied in the examination of any region of the body. The answer is true. The examiner looks before touching, because the surface shows swelling, wasting, discoloration and asymmetry before the hands disturb anything. Palpation and percussion come afterwards.

Question 9505. As the examiner counts the ribs from above, which rib lies at the level of the sternal angle? The answer is the second rib. The sternal angle is the ridge where the manubrium meets the body of the sternum, and the second costal cartilage joins the sternum at that ridge.

Question 9506. Which statement about counting the ribs is correct: the first rib can be felt in front, the second rib lies at the sternal angle, the twelfth rib is the easiest to feel, or the ribs are counted from below? The answer is that the second rib lies at the sternal angle. The first rib hides behind the clavicle and the lower ribs are covered by muscle.

Question 9507. What is the name of the notch felt at the top of the sternum, between the two clavicles? The answer is the jugular notch, also called the suprasternal notch. It lies at the level of the second thoracic vertebra, with the trachea in the midline just above it.

Question 9508. A described chest radiograph shows a dark vertical band running down the middle of the upper chest and dividing into two branches behind the heart. Which structure is that band? The answer is the trachea. Air absorbs little of the beam, so the air-filled tube appears dark, and the two branches are the main bronchi.

Question 9509. A constructed illustration: a patient takes a deep breath, and the examiner sees that the right side of the chest moves less than the left. Which side is abnormal, and why does the difference matter? The answer is that the right side is abnormal. The two sides are compared at the same level, so a difference points to the side that moves less.

Question 9510. In a healthy adult, where is the apex beat usually felt: in the second intercostal space at the left sternal edge, in the fourth intercostal space, in the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line, or below the xiphoid process? The answer is the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line, over the apex of the heart.

Question 9511. Which surface line is drawn vertically downward from the midpoint of the clavicle, and why is it useful on the chest? The answer is the midclavicular line. It runs parallel to the midline and crosses the fifth intercostal space, where the apex beat is felt, and it marks the upper border of the liver below the diaphragm.

Question 9512. What is the name of the small pointed process at the lower end of the sternum? The answer is the xiphoid process. It lies at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra in the adult and is cartilaginous in youth, so it may be felt as a nodule in the midline.

Question 9513. Which of these pairs belongs together: the jugular notch with the top of the sternum, the xiphoid process with the upper end of the sternum, the sternal angle with the lower end of the sternum, or the costal margin with the clavicle? The answer is the jugular notch with the top of the sternum. The costal margin is the lower edge of the rib cage.

Question 9514. A described lateral radiograph of the chest shows a flat vertical bone in front, with the heart shadow behind it and the vertebral bodies at the back. Which bone is the flat one in front? The answer is the sternum. In a lateral view the beam travels from side to side, so the front and the back of the chest lie at opposite edges.

Question 9515. The lower edge of the rib cage, felt as a curved border on each side of the abdomen, is formed by which costal cartilages: the fifth to the seventh, the seventh to the tenth, the ninth to the twelfth, or the fourth to the sixth? The answer is the seventh to the tenth. They join one another in the midline to form the costal margin.

Question 9516. A constructed illustration: a needle is passed into the chest wall just below the lower border of a rib. Which structure of the costal groove is at greatest risk, and how do the three structures lie from above downward? The answer is the intercostal nerve. The vein lies highest in the groove, the artery in the middle and the nerve lowest.

Question 9517. At which vertebral level does the crest of the ilium lie, felt as the highest point of the hip bone? The answer is the fourth lumbar vertebra. The level is used to count the lumbar spines, because the crests are easy to feel on both sides.

Question 9518. The umbilicus lies at the level of which vertebra in a healthy adult? The answer is the third lumbar vertebra. The navel is a fixed landmark on the front of the abdomen, and the disc between the third and fourth lumbar vertebrae lies just above it.

Question 9519. Name two structures that lie in the midline of the anterior abdominal wall, spoken as a list. The answer is the linea alba and the umbilicus. The linea alba is the band of fibrous tissue between the two rectus sheaths, running from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis.

Question 9520. Which structure separates the right rectus abdominis muscle from the left one: the linea alba, the inguinal ligament, the arcuate line, or the pectineal line? The answer is the linea alba, the fibrous band in the midline of the abdominal wall. The arcuate line marks the lower end of the posterior wall of the rectus sheath.

Question 9521. A described photograph of an athlete's abdomen shows a vertical groove in the middle and three or four horizontal grooves crossing the muscle on each side. Which muscle is shown, and what are those bands? The answer is the rectus abdominis, with its tendinous intersections, which divide it into segments and are attached to the front of its sheath.

Question 9522. A constructed illustration: tenderness is greatest at a point one third of the way from the front of the right hip bone to the umbilicus. Which structure lies beneath that described point? The answer is the appendix, at the base of its attachment to the cecum. The point is named after McBurney. The appendix may lie elsewhere, so tenderness there is a guide rather than a proof.

Question 9523. In percussion of the abdomen, where does the dullness of the liver begin in the midclavicular line? The answer is at the fifth intercostal space. The upper border of the liver lies under the dome of the diaphragm, and the dullness extends down to the costal margin. Above it the note is resonant, because the lung lies beneath the tapping finger.

Question 9524. Percussion over the stomach gives a low-pitched, drum-like note, while percussion over the liver gives a dull note. Which statement explains the difference: the stomach contains air and the liver is solid, the stomach is larger, the liver lies behind the peritoneum, or the stomach lies against the spine? The answer is that the stomach contains air and the liver is solid. Air resonates, and a solid organ does not.

Question 9525. In a healthy adult, can the spleen be felt below the left costal margin, and if not, why not? The answer is that it cannot, because it lies deep to the ninth, tenth and eleventh ribs in the left midaxillary line. It must enlarge to about three times its normal size before its tip reaches the costal margin.

Question 9526. A described transverse section of the abdomen shows a thick-walled tube just in front of a vertebral body, slightly left of the midline, with a thinner vessel to its right. Which two vessels are described? The answer is the aorta on the left and the inferior vena cava on the right, both in front of the vertebra and behind the pancreas.

Question 9527. A constructed illustration: a patient cannot pass urine and a rounded swelling is felt in the lower abdomen, rising out of the pelvis. Which structure is distended, and why is it not felt when it is empty? The answer is the urinary bladder. When full it rises above the pubic symphysis, and when empty it lies behind that bone, out of reach of the hand.

Question 9528. Which of these pairs belongs together: the iliac crest with the fourth lumbar vertebra, the umbilicus with the first lumbar vertebra, the sternal angle with the third thoracic vertebra, or the jugular notch with the fifth thoracic vertebra? The answer is the iliac crest with the fourth lumbar vertebra, while the umbilicus lies at the third lumbar vertebra.

Question 9529. A described section across the abdomen shows the right kidney and the left kidney on the two sides of the vertebral column, with the inferior vena cava in front of the spine. In which plane was this section cut? The answer is a transverse plane. The section passes across the body, so both sides appear at once.

Question 9530. A described section of the trunk shows the bodies of the vertebrae cut through the middle and the aorta in front of them. Which plane produced that section, and why is only one kidney seen on its face? The answer is the median plane. A cut through the middle divides the body into right and left halves, so each face carries one kidney.

Question 9531. Which bone lies in front of the knee joint, within the tendon of the quadriceps muscle? The answer is the patella. It is the largest sesamoid bone in the body, and its posterior surface glides on the femur. It increases the power of the quadriceps by holding the tendon away from the joint.

Question 9532. A prominence is felt on the front of the upper end of the tibia, just below the knee, where the tendon of the quadriceps attaches. Which of these is it: the tibial tuberosity, the head of the fibula, the medial malleolus, or the adductor tubercle? The answer is the tibial tuberosity. The head of the fibula lies at the back and outer side of the knee.

Question 9533. Which structures bound the diamond-shaped hollow behind the knee, and what lies within it from the surface inward? The answer is the popliteal fossa, bounded above by the hamstring tendons and below by the two heads of the gastrocnemius. Within it the tibial nerve lies most superficial, with the popliteal vein and then the popliteal artery deeper.

Question 9534. A constructed illustration: a patient cannot straighten the knee against resistance after a fall, and the patella is no longer felt as a separate bone. Which structure has been torn? The answer is the tendon of the quadriceps above the patella or the patellar ligament below it. Either tear removes the pull of the quadriceps from the tibia.

Question 9535. A described drawing of the knee shows fluid bulging on both sides of the patella when the joint is full. Which space explains the bulging, and with which cavity does it communicate? The answer is the suprapatellar pouch, which communicates freely with the knee joint. Because it extends above the patella, fluid can be pressed from one side of the joint to the other.

Question 9536. Which muscle forms the rounded contour of the shoulder and is tested by asking the patient to lift the arm against resistance? The answer is the deltoid. It rises from the clavicle, the acromion and the spine of the scapula and ends on the humerus, and it abducts the arm from about fifteen degrees onward.

Question 9537. Which muscle initiates abduction of the shoulder in the first fifteen degrees: the deltoid, the supraspinatus, the teres major, or the pectoralis major? The answer is the supraspinatus. Its tendon passes under the acromion from the fossa above the spine of the scapula, and the deltoid then continues the movement.

Question 9538. Which vein runs in the groove between the deltoid and the pectoralis major, and what is that groove used for in examination? The answer is the cephalic vein, and the groove is the deltopectoral groove. The vein is a guide to that interval, where the examiner places a finger to find the coracoid process.

Question 9539. A constructed illustration: a patient reports pain in the shoulder when the arm is raised sideways between about sixty and one hundred and twenty degrees, while the movement is painless below and above that arc. Which structure is involved, and why does the pain occupy only part of the arc? The answer is the tendon of the supraspinatus, which is pressed against the acromion during that arc.

Question 9540. Which bony point is felt at the outer side of the wrist, just above the base of the thumb? The answer is the styloid process of the radius, which lies about one centimeter distal to the styloid process of the ulna. The radial artery passes close to it before entering the hand.

Question 9541. The hollow at the base of the thumb, seen when the thumb is extended, is bounded by two tendons. Which structures form its floor: the scaphoid and the trapezium, the lunate and the triquetrum, the hamate and the pisiform, or the capitate and the trapezoid? The answer is the scaphoid and the trapezium, crossed by the radial artery before it passes into the palm.

Question 9542. A described photograph of the palm shows a fleshy mound at the base of the thumb, raised above the level of the palm. Which group of muscles forms that mound, and which nerve supplies them? The answer is the thenar muscles, supplied by the median nerve. They move the thumb away from the palm and across it, and wasting of the mound is an early sign of injury.

Question 9543. Which bone forms the heel and can be felt as the point of the heel after the skin and the fat are passed? The answer is the calcaneus. It is the largest bone of the foot, the Achilles tendon attaches to its back, and its body rests on the ground behind the arch.

Question 9544. Which bony prominence is felt on the inner side of the ankle, and which bone does it belong to? The answer is the medial malleolus of the tibia. The lateral malleolus on the outer side belongs to the fibula and lies lower and further back.

Question 9545. A described examination of a standing patient shows a flattened arch of the foot and a heel tilted outward. Which of these ligaments supports the arch that has given way: the plantar calcaneonavicular ligament, the long plantar ligament, the deltoid ligament, or the anterior talofibular ligament? The answer is the plantar calcaneonavicular ligament, also called the spring ligament, which runs from the calcaneus to the navicular.

Question 9546. True or false: the inguinal ligament is the thickened lower border of the external oblique aponeurosis and stretches from the front of the hip bone to the pubic tubercle. The answer is true. It is felt as a firm band when the examiner presses along the groin, and the femoral vessels pass beneath it into the thigh.

Question 9547. Which of these sets of boundaries belongs to the femoral triangle: the inguinal ligament above with the sartorius laterally and the adductor longus medially, the inguinal ligament below with the sartorius medially, the gluteus maximus with the gracilis, or the inguinal ligament with the iliopsoas and the rectus femoris? The answer is the inguinal ligament above, with the sartorius laterally and the adductor longus medially.

Question 9548. A described photograph of the palm shows three creases crossing it, one near the wrist, one curving around the base of the thumb, and one running across the hand below the fingers. Which crease is the distal one, and what does it mark? The answer is the crease below the fingers, which marks the line of the metacarpophalangeal joints.

Question 9549. Which small mound of muscle lies on the inner side of the palm, opposite the mound at the base of the thumb? The answer is the hypothenar eminence, formed by the muscles that move the little finger and supplied by the ulnar nerve. The hollow between the two mounds is the hollow of the palm.

Question 9550. Within the femoral triangle, in what order do the femoral nerve, artery and vein lie from the outer side inward? Which of these is correct: nerve, artery and vein, vein, artery and nerve, artery, nerve and vein, or nerve, vein and artery? The answer is nerve, artery and vein from lateral to medial. The vein, the most medial, lies next to the opening into the abdomen.

Question 9551. A constructed illustration: after a fall, a patient lies with the right leg shorter than the left and turned outward, and the greater trochanter lies higher than on the other side. Which part of which bone is most likely broken, and why is the limb shortened? The answer is the neck of the femur. The muscles pull the shaft upward and rotate it outward.

Question 9552. In a standing person, the tip of the greater trochanter lies at the level of which structure: the pubic tubercle, the anterior superior iliac spine, the ischial tuberosity, or the fifth lumbar vertebra? The answer is the pubic tubercle. A line from the anterior superior iliac spine to the ischial tuberosity passes through the trochanter when the hip is normal.

Question 9553. A described radiograph of a normal hip shows a continuous curved line running from the lower border of the neck of the femur into the upper border of the opening in the pelvis. Which of these is correct: it is unbroken in a normal hip, it is broken in every adult, it crosses the joint in the middle, or it marks the greater trochanter? The answer is that it is unbroken.

Question 9554. Where is the femoral pulse best felt: above the inguinal ligament, at its midpoint with the artery just below it, at the pubic symphysis, or at the outer end of the ligament? The answer is at the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, with the artery just below it. There the vessel lies in the groove between the iliopsoas and the adductor longus.

Question 9555. Which cartilage forms the prominence in the front of the neck that moves when the voice changes pitch? The answer is the thyroid cartilage. Its upper part forms the laryngeal prominence, and the vocal folds lie within the larynx behind it. It lies at the level of the fourth and fifth cervical vertebrae.

Question 9556. The cricoid cartilage is the only complete ring of cartilage in the airway. At which vertebral level does it lie: the fourth cervical, the sixth cervical, the second thoracic, or the seventh cervical? The answer is the sixth cervical vertebra. It is the narrowest part of the adult airway, and the trachea begins below it.

Question 9557. The isthmus of the thyroid gland lies in front of which tracheal rings, and which nerve lies behind it? Which of these is correct: the second and third rings with the recurrent laryngeal nerve, the first ring with the facial nerve, the fifth and sixth rings with the phrenic nerve, or the fourth ring with the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the second and third rings, with the recurrent laryngeal nerve behind the gland.

Question 9558. A midline swelling in the neck rises when the patient swallows and again when the patient puts out the tongue. Which of these does that indicate: a thyroglossal cyst attached to the tongue, a swelling of the thyroid gland, an enlarged lymph node, or a branchial cyst? The answer is a thyroglossal cyst. It lies on the path the thyroid took down from the tongue, so the tongue pulls it.

Question 9559. A described photograph of the neck shows a long muscle running from behind the ear down to the upper chest, dividing each side into a smaller front part and a larger back part. Which muscle is shown, and what are the two parts called? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid, dividing the neck into the anterior triangle and the posterior triangle.

Question 9560. Name the two triangles into which each side of the neck is divided by the sternocleidomastoid muscle, spoken as a list. The answer is the anterior triangle and the posterior triangle. The anterior triangle contains the carotid sheath, and the posterior triangle contains the accessory nerve and the brachial plexus.

Question 9561. Which bony prominence can be felt just behind the ear, and which muscle attaches to it? The answer is the mastoid process of the temporal bone, to which the sternocleidomastoid attaches. It is filled with air cells that communicate with the middle ear, and the facial nerve leaves the skull just in front of it.

Question 9562. Which muscle pulls the head of the mandible and the articular disc forward when the mouth is opened? Which of these is correct: the lateral pterygoid muscle, the medial pterygoid muscle, the masseter, or the temporalis? The answer is the lateral pterygoid. The disc divides the joint into two compartments, so the upper one glides while the lower one hinges.

Question 9563. Which of these pairs belongs together: the zygomatic arch with the temporal bone and the zygomatic bone, the mastoid process with the maxilla, the mental protuberance with the occipital bone, or the angle of the mandible with the frontal bone? The answer is the zygomatic arch with the temporal bone and the zygomatic bone, felt as a bridge in front of the ear.

Question 9564. A described lateral radiograph of the skull shows a hollow in the middle of the base, shaped like a saddle, with a rounded shadow sitting inside it. Which structure is the hollow, and what normally lies in it? The answer is the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone, which holds the pituitary gland above the air-filled sphenoid sinus.

Question 9565. The duct of the parotid gland crosses the face to open inside the mouth. Which of these statements is correct: it opens opposite the upper second molar tooth, it opens under the tongue, it opens opposite the lower first molar, or it opens behind the last molar? The answer is that it opens opposite the upper second molar tooth. It crosses the masseter muscle and turns inward to pierce the cheek.

Question 9566. True or false: the duct of the parotid gland can be felt as a firm ridge running across the surface of the masseter muscle when the cheek is pressed against the teeth. The answer is true. The duct lies on the muscle and is crossed by the branches of the facial nerve.

Question 9567. Which arch of bone is felt in the face just in front of the ear and below the outer corner of the eye? The answer is the zygomatic arch, formed by the zygomatic process of the temporal bone and the temporal process of the zygomatic bone. The masseter muscle rises from its lower border.

Question 9568. When the spine is examined from behind, which of these is seen in a healthy person: a midline furrow with the spines in a straight line, a lateral curve toward the right, a prominent angle at the eighth thoracic vertebra, or a gap between the fourth and fifth lumbar spines? The answer is a midline furrow with the spines in a straight line.

Question 9569. A described photograph of the back shows two flat triangular bones whose inner edges lie parallel to the spine, with their lower angles at the level of the seventh rib. Which bones are shown, and how many ribs lie above each lower angle? The answer is the scapulae, with the seventh rib at the lower angle. The spine of each scapula lies at the level of the third thoracic vertebra.

Question 9570. The lower border of the pectoralis major forms which fold of the chest wall, and which fold behind it is formed by the latissimus dorsi? The answer is the anterior axillary fold in front and the posterior axillary fold behind. Between them lies the axilla, through which the vessels and nerves pass to the arm.

Question 9571. A described examination of an older patient shows a rounded swelling in the groin, below and lateral to the pubic tubercle and beside the femoral vein. Which of these is it: a femoral hernia, an inguinal hernia, an enlarged lymph node, or a saphenous varix? The answer is a femoral hernia, which passes below the inguinal ligament, while an inguinal hernia lies above and medial to the pubic tubercle.

Question 9572. Which angle is formed at the lower end of the sternum between the converging costal margins, and what is it called? The answer is the infrasternal angle, also called the costal angle. It is wide in a broad chest and narrow in a long one, giving the examiner an idea of the shape of the rib cage.

Question 9573. Tactile fremitus is tested with the flat of the hand while the patient speaks. Which of these is correct: it is increased over consolidated lung and reduced over fluid, it is increased over fluid, it is absent over normal lung, or it is felt only at the apex? The answer is that it is increased over consolidated lung and reduced over fluid. Sound travels better through solid tissue than through fluid.

Question 9574. A described photograph of a young person's chest shows a deep funnel-shaped hollow in the lower part of the sternum, drawing the ribs inward on both sides. Which deformity is shown, and in which direction is the sternum displaced? The answer is pectus excavatum, in which the sternum is pushed backward. The heart lies behind the sternum and may be displaced.

Question 9575. True or false: in a healthy adult the spleen can usually be felt as a soft tip below the left costal margin on deep inspiration. The answer is false. The spleen lies deep to the ribs and is not palpable unless it is enlarged, when it grows downward and forward and its notch may be felt.

Question 9576. Which artery is felt as a pulse on the outer side of the front of the wrist, above the base of the thumb? The answer is the radial artery. It lies just lateral to the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis, where it can be pressed against the radius before it curves into the palm.

Question 9577. Which artery is felt in the hollow at the base of the thumb when the wrist is extended: the ulnar artery, the radial artery, the median artery, or the anterior interosseous artery? The answer is the radial artery. It crosses the floor of that hollow, formed by the scaphoid and the trapezium.

Question 9578. Which artery is felt on the top of the foot, just lateral to the tendon that raises the big toe? The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery, which lies between the tendons of the extensor hallucis longus and the extensor digitorum longus. Its pulse may be absent in a healthy person when the artery is small.

Question 9579. Which nerve lies in the carpal tunnel, and which tendon forms a guide to the tunnel at the wrist? The answer is the median nerve, with the tendon of the palmaris longus as the guide. The nerve passes deep to that tendon and to the transverse carpal ligament, with the flexor tendons.

Question 9580. A described photograph of a hand shows a flattened mound at the base of the thumb, and the thumb cannot be drawn across the palm. Which nerve is affected? The answer is the median nerve. Its branch to the thenar muscles is lost, and sensation is lost over the outer three and a half fingers.

Question 9581. A pinprick is felt over the pad of the thumb. Which spinal segment supplies that area: the fifth cervical, the sixth cervical, the seventh cervical, or the eighth cervical? The answer is the sixth cervical segment. The fifth covers the outer side of the arm and the seventh the middle finger.

Question 9582. A constructed illustration: a cut across the front of the wrist leaves the little finger numb and the fingers unable to spread apart. Which nerve has been cut, and where does it lie at the wrist? The answer is the ulnar nerve, on the inner side of the wrist beside the ulnar artery. It supplies the muscles between the fingers and the muscles of the little finger.

Question 9583. A blow to the inner side of the elbow causes numbness of the little finger. Which of these explains the injury: the ulnar nerve lies behind the medial epicondyle, the median nerve crosses the front of the elbow, the radial nerve lies behind the lateral epicondyle, or the ulnar nerve passes through the cubital fossa? The answer is that the ulnar nerve lies behind the medial epicondyle, covered only by skin and fascia.

Question 9584. Which of these pairs belongs together: the facial nerve with the muscles of facial expression, the trigeminal nerve with the muscles of the tongue, the hypoglossal nerve with the muscles of chewing, or the accessory nerve with the muscles of the eye? The answer is the facial nerve with the muscles of facial expression, while the trigeminal nerve carries sensation from the face and supplies the muscles of chewing.

Question 9585. True or false: the knee jerk is tested by tapping the patellar ligament while the leg hangs relaxed, and it depends on the third and fourth lumbar segments. The answer is true. The tap stretches the quadriceps, and the muscle contracts in reply, so the reflex tests the segments that supply it.

Question 9586. Behind the medial malleolus, in what order do the structures pass from the inner side outward on their way to the sole? The answer is the tendon of tibialis posterior, the tendon of flexor digitorum longus, the posterior tibial artery, the tendon of flexor hallucis longus and the tibial nerve. The nerve lies most lateral of the five, under the flexor retinaculum.

Question 9587. Which tendon is tapped to test the biceps reflex, and which spinal segments does that reflex test? The answer is the tendon of the biceps brachii, and the reflex tests the fifth and sixth cervical segments. The examiner places a thumb on the tendon in the cubital fossa and strikes the thumb.

Question 9588. Tapping the Achilles tendon while the foot hangs relaxed makes the foot point downward. Which of these is correct: the reflex tests the first and second sacral segments, it tests the fifth lumbar segment, it tests the fourth lumbar segment, or it tests the first thoracic segment? The answer is the first and second sacral segments. The tibial nerve carries the reflex, and its loss points to those roots.

Question 9589. A described map of the skin shows bands running around the trunk, one at the level of the nipples, one at the level of the umbilicus and one at the level of the groin. Which spinal segments supply those three bands? The answer is the fourth thoracic at the nipples, the tenth thoracic at the umbilicus and the first lumbar at the groin.

Question 9590. A constructed illustration: a patient cannot lift the front of the foot and drags the toes, and sensation is lost over the front of the shin and the top of the foot. Which nerve is involved, and where is it most exposed to injury? The answer is the common fibular nerve, at the neck of the fibula, where it lies against bone under the skin.

Question 9591. A patient cannot extend the wrist and the fingers, and the back of the hand is numb between the thumb and the index finger. Which nerve is injured, and in which groove of the humerus does it lie? The answer is the radial nerve, in the spiral groove on the back of the humerus, where a fracture of the shaft may injure it.

Question 9592. Name two places where the ulnar nerve can be felt against bone, spoken as a list. The answer is behind the medial epicondyle of the humerus and at the inner side of the wrist. At the elbow it lies in a groove covered only by skin and fascia.

Question 9593. Which of these pairs belongs together: injury of the radial nerve with wrist drop, injury of the median nerve with clawing of the ring and little fingers, injury of the ulnar nerve with loss of the biceps reflex, or injury of the axillary nerve with loss of the ankle jerk? The answer is injury of the radial nerve with wrist drop, while the ulnar nerve produces clawing.

Question 9594. A described photograph of the side of the neck shows a thin nerve crossing the large posterior triangle from above downward, just under the skin, on its way to the shoulder. Which nerve is shown, and which muscles does it supply? The answer is the accessory nerve, which supplies the sternocleidomastoid and the trapezius and lies near the surface as it crosses.

Question 9595. Through which opening does the facial nerve leave the skull, and which movement is lost when it is injured? Which of these is correct: the stylomastoid foramen, with loss of the muscles of facial expression, the foramen ovale, with loss of chewing, the jugular foramen, with loss of the gag reflex, or the optic canal, with loss of sight? The answer is the stylomastoid foramen, with loss of the muscles of facial expression.

Question 9596. The corneal reflex is tested by touching the clear front of the eye with a wisp of cotton. Which of these describes it correctly: the eye closes, with the trigeminal nerve carrying the sensation and the facial nerve the movement, the pupil narrows, the jaw closes, or the eye waters? The answer is that the eye closes, with the trigeminal nerve carrying the sensation and the facial nerve the movement.

Question 9597. A constructed illustration: a patient opens the mouth, and the jaw deviates to the left, the side on which chewing is weak. Which muscle has lost its pull: the left lateral pterygoid, the right lateral pterygoid, the left masseter, or both pterygoids equally? The answer is the left lateral pterygoid, so the healthy muscle on the right pulls the jaw across and the chin turns toward the weak side.

Question 9598. Touching the back of the throat makes the soft palate rise and the throat tighten. Which two cranial nerves carry that reflex? The answer is the ninth and the tenth cranial nerves. The ninth carries the sensation from the back of the throat, and the tenth supplies the muscles that lift the palate.

Question 9599. Which of these statements about the arteries felt in the head is correct: the facial artery is felt where it crosses the lower border of the jaw, the temporal artery is felt behind the ear, the facial artery is felt behind the angle of the jaw, or the temporal artery is felt over the mastoid process? The answer is the facial artery, felt where it crosses the lower border of the jaw in front of the masseter.

Question 9600. A described angiogram of the hand shows two arches, one deep in the palm and one nearer the surface, joined by small vessels, with the radial artery entering the palm between the first two metacarpals. Which arch lies nearer the skin, and which artery completes it from the inner side? The answer is the superficial palmar arch, completed by the ulnar artery.

To summarize, the examination of the body rests on inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation, applied in that order except in the abdomen, where auscultation precedes palpation so that the bowel sounds are not disturbed. The surface landmarks carry the deeper anatomy: the sternal angle marks the second rib, the iliac crest the fourth lumbar vertebra, the umbilicus the third, and the midclavicular line the apex beat in the fifth intercostal space. Percussion separates the dull note of the liver and the spleen from the tympanitic note of the stomach. The neurovascular examination adds the pulses at the wrist, the foot and the groin, and the nerves that lie against bone, together with the reflex arcs and the dermatomes.
