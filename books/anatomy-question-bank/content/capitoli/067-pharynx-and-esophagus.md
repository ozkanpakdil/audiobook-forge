# Chapter 67 — Pharynx and Esophagus

This chapter follows swallowed food from the back of the nose and the mouth, through the throat, and down the tube that carries it to the stomach. It covers the three parts of the pharynx, the muscles of its wall, the gaps between them, its nerve supply and its tonsils, and then the course, the relations and the blood supply of the esophagus. It closes with the stomach, its parts, its curvatures, the peritoneum that suspends it, and the vessels and nerves that reach it. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer at once, followed by the reason, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 6601. Which part of the pharynx lies directly behind the nasal cavity and above the level of the soft palate? The answer is the nasopharynx. It reaches from the base of the skull down to the soft palate, and it carries air only. Behind the mouth lies the oropharynx, and behind the larynx lies the laryngopharynx.

Question 6602. Which list gives the three parts of the pharynx from above downward: nasopharynx, oropharynx, laryngopharynx; oropharynx, nasopharynx, laryngopharynx; laryngopharynx, oropharynx, nasopharynx; or nasopharynx, laryngopharynx, oropharynx? The answer is nasopharynx, then oropharynx, then laryngopharynx. Each part is named for the space it faces, and the three are continuous from the skull to the esophagus.

Question 6603. A described dissection of the back of the pharynx shows three broad muscles stacked one above another, all of them ending in a fibrous band that runs down the midline. What is that band called? The answer is the pharyngeal raphe. The three constrictors meet in it from both sides, and it hangs from the base of the skull. It gives the back of the pharynx its smooth median seam.

Question 6604. Which cavity opens into the nasopharynx through the two posterior nasal apertures? The answer is the nasal cavity. Air passes from the nose through those openings into the pharynx on its way to the larynx. The soft palate forms the floor of the nasopharynx and shuts it off from the mouth during swallowing.

Question 6605. A constructed illustration: after a palatine tonsil is removed, brisk bleeding comes from the lower part of the tonsillar fossa. Which vessel is the likely source? The answer is the tonsillar branch of the facial artery. It enters the lower pole of the tonsil from the region of the angle of the mandible. Branches of the ascending palatine and the dorsal lingual arteries reach its upper part as well.

Question 6606. True or false: the pharynx is a passage used by both air and food. The answer is true. The nasopharynx carries air only, but the oropharynx and the laryngopharynx carry food and drink as well as air. The larynx guards the entrance to the airway while a person swallows.

Question 6607. A long slender muscle arises from the styloid process, slips down between the superior and the middle constrictor, and joins the thyroid cartilage. Which muscle is it? The answer is the stylopharyngeus. It raises the pharynx and the larynx during swallowing. It is the only muscle of the pharynx supplied by the glossopharyngeal nerve.

Question 6608. Which nerve carries the motor fibers to almost every muscle of the pharynx: the glossopharyngeal, the vagus, the hypoglossal, or the accessory? The answer is the vagus. Its fibers reach the constrictors through the pharyngeal plexus. The one exception is the stylopharyngeus, which the glossopharyngeal nerve supplies.

Question 6609. A described barium swallow shows a pouch bulging backward from the lower part of the pharynx, just above the cricopharyngeus. Through which weak point has it pushed? The answer is the gap between the thyropharyngeus and the cricopharyngeus, often called Killian dehiscence. The two parts of the inferior constrictor pull in different directions, and the wall between them is thin.

Question 6610. The glossopharyngeal nerve curves forward to reach the tongue. Between which two muscles of the pharyngeal wall does it pass? The answer is the superior constrictor and the middle constrictor. It slips through the gap where the stylopharyngeus also enters. That position explains why a growth in the tonsillar region can involve the nerve early.

Question 6611. Which tonsil sits in the fossa bounded in front by the palatoglossal arch and behind by the palatopharyngeal arch? The answer is the palatine tonsil. It lies on the side wall of the oropharynx. Its bed is formed by the superior constrictor, and the glossopharyngeal nerve runs deep to it.

Question 6612. Which structures pass through the gap between the middle constrictor and the inferior constrictor: the internal laryngeal nerve and the superior laryngeal artery, the glossopharyngeal nerve and the stylopharyngeus, the auditory tube and the levator veli palatini, or the hypoglossal nerve and the lingual artery? The answer is the internal laryngeal nerve with the superior laryngeal artery. That gap is the lowest of the three openings in the pharyngeal wall.

Question 6613. A constructed illustration: a child has a large mass of lymphoid tissue on the roof of the nasopharynx, and fluid gathers in the middle ear. Which structure has been obstructed? The answer is the auditory tube. It opens on the lateral wall of the nasopharynx, and the swollen tissue blocks that opening. Air cannot reach the middle ear, and fluid collects there.

Question 6614. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superior constrictor and the pterygomandibular raphe, the middle constrictor and the thyroid cartilage, the inferior constrictor and the stylohyoid ligament, or the stylopharyngeus and the hyoid bone? The answer is the superior constrictor with the pterygomandibular raphe. The middle constrictor arises from the hyoid, and the inferior constrictor from the thyroid and cricoid cartilages.

Question 6615. Which of these structures lies in the oropharynx: the palatine tonsil, the pharyngeal tonsil, the auditory tube, or the piriform fossa? The answer is the palatine tonsil. The pharyngeal tonsil and the auditory tube belong to the nasopharynx, and the piriform fossa belongs to the laryngopharynx.

Question 6616. A described endoscopic view shows a small sharp bone lodged in a groove that runs down beside the opening of the larynx. Where has it stuck, and why is that place a trap? The answer is the piriform fossa. The groove is wide above and narrow below, and its walls hide the object from view. It is one of the commonest sites for a lodged fish bone.

Question 6617. Which sheet of fascia lies between the muscles of the pharynx and its mucous membrane? The answer is the pharyngobasilar fascia. It is attached to the base of the skull and fills the gap that the constrictors leave there. The muscles lie outside it and the mucous membrane lines it from within.

Question 6618. Sensation from the lining of the nasopharynx is carried by which nerve: the glossopharyngeal, the maxillary division of the trigeminal, the facial, or the hypoglossal? The answer is the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve. The glossopharyngeal nerve takes the oropharynx and the vagus takes the laryngopharynx. Students often hand the whole pharynx to the glossopharyngeal nerve.

Question 6619. Name the two muscles that lift the soft palate. The answer is the levator veli palatini and the tensor veli palatini. The levator forms the bulk of the soft palate and raises it to shut off the nasopharynx during swallowing. The tensor tightens the palate and also opens the auditory tube.

Question 6620. During the pharyngeal stage of swallowing, which of these events takes place: the larynx is raised and the epiglottis tips back, the soft palate is pulled down, the cricopharyngeus stays closed, or the tongue is pulled backward? The answer is that the larynx is raised and the epiglottis tips back. The upward pull widens the pharynx and helps the bolus past the opening of the airway.

Question 6621. Which fibrous band does the superior constrictor share with the buccinator muscle? The answer is the pterygomandibular raphe. It runs from the hook of the medial pterygoid plate to the back of the mylohyoid line of the mandible. The buccinator pulls from in front and the superior constrictor from behind, so the two work as one sheet.

Question 6622. Which two muscles form the arches that bound the tonsillar fossa: the palatoglossus and the palatopharyngeus, the levator and the tensor veli palatini, the styloglossus and the stylopharyngeus, or the superior and the middle constrictors? The answer is the palatoglossus and the palatopharyngeus. They form the front and the back pillars of the fauces, with the tonsil between them.

Question 6623. A described section through the oropharynx shows a rounded mass on each side wall, held between two mucosal folds, with a thin muscle deep to it. What is the mass, and what muscle lies deep to it? The answer is the palatine tonsil, with the superior constrictor deep to its bed. A thin capsule separates the tonsil from the muscle.

Question 6624. Which nerve runs immediately deep to the capsule of the palatine tonsil and is at risk during its removal? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve. It passes forward between the superior and the middle constrictor to reach the back of the tongue. Injury to it causes loss of taste and of sensation in the back of the tongue.

Question 6625. A constructed illustration: an infection behind the pharynx spreads downward until it reaches the posterior mediastinum. Which space allowed that spread? The answer is the danger space, between the alar fascia and the prevertebral fascia. The retropharyngeal space lies in front of the alar fascia and ends in the upper chest. Only the danger space is open all the way to the diaphragm.

Question 6626. True or false: the pharyngeal tonsil lies on the roof and the posterior wall of the nasopharynx. The answer is true. It sits in the midline where the roof meets the back wall, and it is largest in childhood. When it swells it can block the auditory tube and the airway behind the nose.

Question 6627. Which muscle forms the upper esophageal sphincter and keeps air out of the esophagus between swallows? The answer is the cricopharyngeus. It is the lowest part of the inferior constrictor and it arises from the cricoid cartilage. It relaxes at the moment of swallowing to let the bolus pass.

Question 6628. Which nerve carries sensation from the laryngopharynx above the vocal folds: the internal laryngeal, the recurrent laryngeal, the hypoglossal, or the glossopharyngeal? The answer is the internal laryngeal nerve, a branch of the vagus. Below the vocal folds the same lining is served by the recurrent laryngeal nerve. That split matters when a growth is felt on one side only.

Question 6629. A described lateral radiograph of the neck shows a column of air running down in front of the vertebral column. Which part of the pharynx occupies the level just below the soft palate? The answer is the oropharynx. It lies behind the mouth, between the soft palate above and the epiglottis below. The nasopharynx above it is hidden behind the shadow of the facial bones.

Question 6630. The valleculae are two small pockets in the oropharynx. Between which two structures do they lie? The answer is between the base of the tongue in front and the epiglottis behind. Two folds, the glossoepiglottic folds, separate them in the midline. A swallowed object can lodge in a vallecula, out of sight.

Question 6631. Which small muscle arises from the cartilage of the auditory tube and forms a vertical fold on the side wall of the pharynx? The answer is the salpingopharyngeus. It raises the pharyngeal fold during swallowing. Unlike the constrictors, it lies inside the mucous membrane.

Question 6632. Which muscle forms the posterior pillar of the fauces and runs from the soft palate down to the thyroid cartilage: the palatoglossus, the palatopharyngeus, the salpingopharyngeus, or the styloglossus? The answer is the palatopharyngeus. It lies behind the tonsil, and during swallowing it helps to narrow the pharynx behind the bolus.

Question 6633. A constructed illustration: a growth in the tonsillar region of the pharynx causes loss of taste at the back of the tongue. Which nerve is involved? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve. It carries taste from the posterior third of the tongue as well as sensation from the oropharynx. The growth also involves the tissue deep to the tonsil, where the nerve passes.

Question 6634. Which of these pairs belongs together: the stylopharyngeus and the glossopharyngeal nerve, the superior constrictor and the hypoglossal nerve, the cricopharyngeus and the facial nerve, or the palatopharyngeus and the trigeminal nerve? The answer is the stylopharyngeus with the glossopharyngeal nerve. The constrictors are supplied through the pharyngeal plexus, which the vagus forms with branches of the glossopharyngeal and sympathetic nerves.

Question 6635. Which bones form the roof of the pharynx: the sphenoid and the basilar part of the occipital, the maxillae and the palatines, the temporal and the parietal, or the mandible and the hyoid? The answer is the sphenoid bone with the basilar part of the occipital bone. The pharyngobasilar fascia is attached to that roof, and the mucous membrane covers it from below.

Question 6636. A described coronal section through the neck shows a wedge of space whose medial wall is the pharynx and whose lateral wall is the deep surface of the parotid gland. Which space is it? The answer is the parapharyngeal space. It is filled with fat and lies between the pharynx and the muscles of the styloid process. A growth in the deep lobe of the parotid gland can push the pharynx inward.

Question 6637. Which structure forms the anterior wall of the laryngopharynx? The answer is the larynx. The laryngopharynx lies behind the opening of the larynx and behind the posterior surface of the larynx itself. Below, it becomes continuous with the esophagus at the level of the cricoid cartilage.

Question 6638. In which order do the pharyngeal constrictors squeeze the bolus: from above downward in sequence, from below upward in sequence, all at the same instant, or in no fixed order? The answer is from above downward, the superior first and the inferior last. The three muscles overlap one another, and the wave passes from the nasopharynx to the esophagus in about one second.

Question 6639. In the neck, how does the pharynx lie in relation to the vertebral column, and where does it end below? The answer is that it lies in front of the vertebral column and ends at the lower border of the cricoid cartilage, at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra, where the esophagus begins. Behind it lie the prevertebral fascia and the bodies of the cervical vertebrae.

Question 6640. Which statement about the retropharyngeal space is correct: it lies between the buccopharyngeal fascia and the prevertebral fascia, it lies in front of the pharyngeal mucosa, it ends at the diaphragm, or it is the same space as the danger space? The answer is that it lies between the buccopharyngeal fascia in front and the prevertebral fascia behind. It runs from the skull down into the upper chest and stops there.

Question 6641. At which level does the esophagus begin? The answer is at the lower border of the cricoid cartilage, at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra. It continues upward as the pharynx without a break. Its first centimeters lie in the neck, behind the trachea and in front of the vertebral column.

Question 6642. Which part of the esophagus is the shortest: the cervical, the thoracic, the abdominal, or the cardiac? The answer is the abdominal part. It is about 1 to 2 centimeters long and passes through the diaphragm at the tenth thoracic vertebra to reach the cardia. The thoracic part is the longest.

Question 6643. A described barium swallow shows three narrowings along the esophagus. Where is the lowest one, and what causes it? The answer is at the esophageal hiatus of the diaphragm, at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra. The hiatus grips the tube as it passes through. The upper two narrowings lie at the cricopharyngeus and where the aortic arch crosses the tube.

Question 6644. Which structure lies immediately in front of the cervical part of the esophagus? The answer is the trachea. The esophagus sits between the trachea in front and the vertebral column behind. The recurrent laryngeal nerves run upward in the groove on each side, between the two.

Question 6645. A constructed illustration: a person reports that food sticks at the lower end of the esophagus, and a described barium swallow shows the tube dilated above a smooth narrowing at the cardia. What does this illustrate? The answer is failure of the lower esophageal sphincter to relax, the condition called achalasia. The ganglion cells of the myenteric plexus there are lost. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6646. True or false: in the chest the esophagus is covered by a serous coat in the same way as the stomach. The answer is false. In the thorax it has only a coat of loose fibrous tissue, the adventitia. That is why infection from a perforation spreads freely through the mediastinum instead of being walled off.

Question 6647. Which artery supplies the cervical part of the esophagus? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery. It gives small esophageal branches in the neck. The thoracic part is fed by branches of the descending thoracic aorta, and the abdominal part by the left gastric artery.

Question 6648. The muscular wall of the esophagus differs along its length. Which statement is correct: the upper third is striated, the upper third is smooth, the whole length is striated, or the whole length is smooth? The answer is that the upper third is striated muscle. The middle third is a mixture, and the lower third is smooth. The change follows the shift in control from the pharynx to the gut.

Question 6649. A described computed tomography section of the lower chest shows a flattened tube lying against the back of the heart, with the aorta behind it and to its left. Which chamber of the heart lies directly in front of the tube? The answer is the left atrium. The esophagus and the left atrium are separated there only by the pericardium and a little connective tissue.

Question 6650. For most of its course, on which side of the esophagus does the thoracic duct ascend? The answer is behind the esophagus and on its right side. It crosses to the left at about the level of the fourth thoracic vertebra. From there it climbs behind the aortic arch to reach the root of the neck.

Question 6651. Which membrane anchors the lower end of the esophagus to the diaphragm? The answer is the phrenoesophageal ligament. It is a fold of fascia under the peritoneum that binds the tube to the margins of the hiatus. It allows the esophagus to move a little as a person breathes and swallows.

Question 6652. How long is the esophagus in an adult: about 15 centimeters, about 25 centimeters, about 35 centimeters, or about 45 centimeters? The answer is about 25 centimeters. It runs from the lower border of the cricoid cartilage to the cardiac orifice of the stomach.

Question 6653. A constructed illustration: a person vomits forcefully after a large meal and is seized by sudden pain in the lower chest. A tear is found in the esophagus. Where is such a tear usually placed? The answer is the left posterolateral wall of the lower thoracic esophagus, just above the diaphragm. The wall there is thin and the pressure rises fastest. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6654. Which of these pairs belongs together: the cervical esophagus and the inferior thyroid artery, the cervical esophagus and the left gastric artery, the thoracic esophagus and the inferior thyroid artery, or the abdominal esophagus and the bronchial arteries? The answer is the cervical esophagus with the inferior thyroid artery. The thoracic part is fed by branches of the descending thoracic aorta, and the abdominal part by the left gastric artery.

Question 6655. The abdominal part of the esophagus is covered by peritoneum on which surfaces: the front and the right, the front and the left, the back and the left, or all surfaces? The answer is the front and the right. Its left and posterior surfaces lie against the left crus of the diaphragm and are bare. The peritoneum that covers it is continuous with that of the stomach.

Question 6656. A described endoscopic view of the lower esophagus shows the pale lining of the tube changing to the redder lining of the stomach along a wavy line. What is that line called? The answer is the squamocolumnar junction, often called the Z line. There the stratified squamous epithelium of the esophagus meets the columnar epithelium of the stomach. The junction normally lies just above the cardia.

Question 6657. Above the diaphragm the fibers of the two vagus nerves break up into a mesh that wraps around the esophagus. What is that mesh called? The answer is the esophageal plexus. The two nerves exchange fibers in it as they descend. Below the diaphragm the mesh gathers again into one trunk in front of the tube and one behind it.

Question 6658. Which structure forms the muscular margin of the esophageal hiatus of the diaphragm: the right crus, the left crus, the central tendon, or the median arcuate ligament? The answer is the right crus. Its fibers split around the esophagus and act as a pinch valve. The left crus lies to the left and does not encircle the opening.

Question 6659. Name the three normal narrowings of the esophagus, in order from above downward. The answer is the narrowing at the cricopharyngeus, the narrowing where the aortic arch and the left main bronchus cross the tube, and the narrowing at the esophageal hiatus of the diaphragm. Each is a place where a swallowed object may stick and where an instrument may injure the wall.

Question 6660. Which veins drain the thoracic part of the esophagus: the azygos and hemiazygos, the left gastric, the portal, or the inferior thyroid? The answer is the azygos and hemiazygos veins. They carry the blood to the superior vena cava, while the abdominal part drains through the left gastric vein to the portal vein. That difference explains where enlarged veins appear.

Question 6661. The esophagus passes from the chest into the abdomen through an opening of the diaphragm. At which vertebral level does that opening lie? The answer is the tenth thoracic vertebra. The opening is the esophageal hiatus, and the right crus of the diaphragm surrounds it. The abdominal part of the esophagus below it is short.

Question 6662. A swallowed foreign body most often lodges at which level: the cricopharyngeus, the aortic arch, the diaphragm, or the cardia? The answer is at the cricopharyngeus. That is the narrowest point of the whole tube, and it is the first narrowing the object meets. The other two narrowings lie at the aortic arch and at the diaphragm.

Question 6663. A described radiograph of a newborn shows a coiled tube in the upper chest and gas in the stomach on a later film. Which developmental fault does this illustrate? The answer is esophageal atresia with a fistula from the lower segment to the trachea. The trachea and the esophagus begin as one tube, and an incomplete division leaves the upper segment blind while the lower segment opens into the airway.

Question 6664. Which structures lie immediately to the right of the thoracic esophagus? The answer is the azygos vein and the right mediastinal pleura. The azygos vein climbs in front of the vertebral bodies and arches over the root of the right lung. On the left of the esophagus lie the descending thoracic aorta and the left mediastinal pleura.

Question 6665. A constructed illustration: a person has a long history of stomach contents flowing back into the lower esophagus, and a sample of the lining there shows columnar cells instead of flat squamous cells. What change is described? The answer is a change of the lining to the columnar type, called Barrett esophagus. It follows long-standing reflux of acid into the lower tube. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6666. True or false: the recurrent laryngeal nerve runs upward in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus, so it lies in the field of an operation on the cervical esophagus. The answer is true. The left nerve hooks under the aortic arch and the right under the right subclavian artery before both climb to the larynx in that groove. Injury on one side weakens the voice.

Question 6667. Which nerve plexus lies in the submucosa of the digestive tube, between the mucous membrane and the muscle coat? The answer is the submucosal plexus, also called the plexus of Meissner. It controls the local secretion and the flow of blood in the wall. The myenteric plexus lies further out, between the circular and the longitudinal muscle layers.

Question 6668. Lymph from the middle third of the esophagus drains mainly to which group of nodes: the posterior mediastinal, the deep cervical, the left gastric, or the superficial inguinal? The answer is the posterior mediastinal nodes. The upper third drains to the deep cervical nodes and the lower third to the left gastric and celiac nodes. The three territories follow the three parts of the tube.

Question 6669. A described computed tomography section at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra shows a collapsed tube in the middle of the posterior mediastinum, with a second tubular structure behind it and to its left. Which structure is the collapsed tube? The answer is the esophagus. The tube behind it and to its left is the descending thoracic aorta. The esophagus lies in front of the aorta at that level.

Question 6670. In the upper chest the esophagus lies to the right of the midline, and lower down it returns to the left. Which structure pushes it to the right? The answer is the aortic arch, which crosses the esophagus on its left side at the level of the fourth thoracic vertebra. Lower down the descending aorta lies behind the tube and to its left.

Question 6671. Which two layers make up the muscular coat of the esophagus, and in which order do they lie? The answer is an outer longitudinal layer and an inner circular layer. The longitudinal fibers shorten the tube, and the circular fibers narrow it behind the bolus. The lower end of the circular layer is thicker and helps to close the junction with the stomach.

Question 6672. Which structure lies immediately in front of the abdominal part of the esophagus: the left lobe of the liver, the spleen, the pancreas, or the left kidney? The answer is the left lobe of the liver. The esophagus enters the abdomen through the diaphragm and ends at the cardia. The pancreas and the left kidney lie behind the stomach, not in front of the esophagus.

Question 6673. A constructed illustration: a sharp object perforates the esophagus in the middle of the chest, and the person loses blood rapidly. Which great vessel lies immediately to the left of the tube at that level and is the likely source? The answer is the descending thoracic aorta. It runs down behind and to the left of the esophagus through the posterior mediastinum. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6674. Which pair belongs together: the beginning of the esophagus and the sixth cervical vertebra, the beginning of the esophagus and the first thoracic vertebra, the esophageal hiatus and the twelfth thoracic vertebra, or the aortic hiatus and the tenth thoracic vertebra? The answer is the esophagus beginning at the sixth cervical vertebra. The aortic arch crosses it at the fourth thoracic vertebra, and the aortic hiatus lies at the twelfth.

Question 6675. In the neck, the esophagus lies in which position relative to the midline: slightly to the left, slightly to the right, exactly in the midline, or far to the right? The answer is slightly to the left. It keeps that position down to the upper chest, where the aortic arch presses it to the right. Lower down it returns toward the midline and ends left of it at the cardia.

Question 6676. A described barium meal shows the esophagus joining the stomach at a sharp angle, with the narrowest part of the junction just below the diaphragm. Which part of the stomach lies at that junction? The answer is the cardia. It surrounds the cardiac orifice, where the esophagus empties into the stomach. The fundus rises above the level of that orifice.

Question 6677. Which notch on the lesser curvature of the stomach marks the boundary between the body and the pyloric part? The answer is the angular incisure. It is a small angle in the curve where the lesser curvature turns toward the pylorus. The pyloric antrum lies to its right.

Question 6678. Which of these structures forms part of the bed on which the stomach rests: the left kidney, the right kidney, the gallbladder, or the third part of the duodenum? The answer is the left kidney. The bed also includes the diaphragm, the left adrenal gland, the pancreas, the splenic flexure of the colon, and the transverse mesocolon. The stomach is separated from them by the lesser sac.

Question 6679. A described transverse section is taken through the upper abdomen at the level of the first lumbar vertebra. Which of these structures lies furthest back: the stomach, the pancreas, the spleen, or the left kidney? The answer is the left kidney. It lies against the posterior abdominal wall behind the peritoneum. The pancreas lies in front of it, and the stomach is the most forward of the four.

Question 6680. Which artery runs along the left half of the greater curvature of the stomach: the left gastro-omental, the right gastro-omental, the left gastric, or the short gastric? The answer is the left gastro-omental artery, a branch of the splenic artery. The right gastro-omental artery comes from the gastroduodenal artery and runs along the right half. The two meet and join along the curve.

Question 6681. Which part of the stomach lies between the fundus above and the pyloric antrum below? The answer is the body of the stomach. It is the largest part, and its lining holds the gastric glands. The fundus is the dome that rises above the level of the cardiac orifice.

Question 6682. In which regions of the abdomen does the stomach lie: the epigastric and left hypochondriac, the right hypochondriac and epigastric, the umbilical and left lumbar, or the right iliac and pubic? The answer is the epigastric and left hypochondriac regions. The fundus reaches up under the left dome of the diaphragm, and the pylorus turns to the right across the midline. The spleen lies behind it on the left.

Question 6683. A described barium meal shows a narrow channel at the lower end of the stomach, opening into the first part of the duodenum, with a muscle around it that is thicker than the rest of the wall. Which muscle is it? The answer is the pyloric sphincter, a thickening of the circular layer. It holds the pyloric canal closed except when food is being let through.

Question 6684. Which structure lies immediately behind the pylorus and the first part of the duodenum? The answer is the head of the pancreas. The gastroduodenal artery also passes behind the first part of the duodenum on its way down. An ulcer that erodes the back wall there can reach the pancreas.

Question 6685. A constructed illustration: an ulcer on the back wall of the body of the stomach erodes into a large vessel that runs along the upper border of the pancreas. Which vessel is it? The answer is the splenic artery. It runs a tortuous course along the upper border of the pancreas to the spleen. An ulcer in that position can open the artery. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6686. True or false: the peritoneum covers every surface of the stomach. The answer is false. The peritoneum covers the front and the back of the stomach but leaves a bare strip along each curvature where the omenta are attached. Those bare strips are where the vessels run and where the ligaments of the stomach begin.

Question 6687. Which fold of peritoneum runs from the greater curvature of the stomach to the spleen? The answer is the gastrosplenic ligament. It carries the short gastric arteries and the left gastro-omental artery to the stomach. The gastrophrenic ligament runs upward instead, and the gastrocolic ligament runs downward.

Question 6688. Which structure forms the front wall of the epiploic foramen: the free edge of the lesser omentum, the inferior vena cava, the caudate lobe of the liver, or the first part of the duodenum? The answer is the free edge of the lesser omentum, which contains the portal vein, the hepatic artery and the bile duct. The inferior vena cava lies behind the foramen and the caudate lobe above it.

Question 6689. A described plain radiograph of a standing person shows a rounded shadow of gas under the left dome of the diaphragm, in front of the spleen. Which part of the stomach holds that gas? The answer is the fundus. In a standing person the gas collects in the highest part of the stomach. The shadow disappears when the person lies down.

Question 6690. Which space lies immediately behind the body of the stomach and separates it from the pancreas? The answer is the lesser sac of peritoneum, also called the omental bursa. It is a closed space behind the stomach and the lesser omentum. It communicates with the greater sac of peritoneum through the epiploic foramen.

Question 6691. Which small vein crosses the front of the pylorus and marks the boundary between the stomach and the duodenum? The answer is the prepyloric vein. It runs upward across the pylorus just in front of the sphincter. A surgeon uses it as a guide to the exact line of the junction.

Question 6692. Which statement about the nerves of the stomach is correct: the anterior vagal trunk comes from the left vagus, the anterior trunk comes from the right vagus, both come from the right vagus, or the stomach has no vagal supply? The answer is the left vagus nerve, which forms the anterior trunk. The stomach rotates during development, carrying the left vagus to the front and the right vagus to the back.

Question 6693. A constructed illustration: some weeks after birth an infant vomits forcefully after feeding, and at operation the pylorus is a firm, olive-shaped mass. Which layer is thickened, and why does the stomach empty poorly? The answer is the circular muscle of the pyloric sphincter, which is enlarged. The canal through it becomes narrow and long, so milk leaves the stomach slowly. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 6694. Which of these pairs belongs together: the short gastric arteries and the fundus, the short gastric arteries and the pylorus, the right gastric artery and the fundus, or the left gastric artery and the greater curvature? The answer is the short gastric arteries with the fundus. They arise from the splenic artery. The left gastric artery runs along the lesser curvature instead.

Question 6695. At which level does the cardiac orifice of the stomach lie: the ninth thoracic vertebra, the eleventh thoracic vertebra, the first lumbar vertebra, or the third lumbar vertebra? The answer is the eleventh thoracic vertebra. It lies about 2 to 3 centimeters to the left of the midline. The esophagus reaches it after passing through the diaphragm at the tenth thoracic vertebra.

Question 6696. A described specimen of the opened stomach shows folds of the lining running along the organ, most prominent on its lower and left border and almost absent near the inlet. What are the folds called, and where are they largest? The answer is the gastric rugae, and they are largest along the greater curvature. They allow the wall to stretch after a meal.

Question 6697. Which layer of muscle is found in the wall of the stomach but not in that of the rest of the digestive tube? The answer is the innermost oblique layer. Its fibers run across the cardia and the body of the stomach. The outer longitudinal and the middle circular layers are found throughout the digestive tube.

Question 6698. Into which group of lymph nodes does most of the lymph from the stomach finally drain: the celiac nodes, the lumbar nodes, the deep cervical nodes, or the inguinal nodes? The answer is the celiac nodes. The nodes along the curvatures, the pyloric nodes and the splenic nodes all pass their lymph to that group, which lies around the celiac trunk.

Question 6699. Name the two arteries that run along the greater curvature of the stomach. The answer is the right gastro-omental artery and the left gastro-omental artery. The right one arises from the gastroduodenal artery, and the left one from the splenic artery. They meet and join along the curve.

Question 6700. Which of these structures helps to close the lower end of the esophagus against the flow of stomach contents: the right crus, the left crus, the pyloric sphincter, or the median arcuate ligament? The answer is the right crus of the diaphragm, which encircles the hiatus like a sling. The sharp angle of entry and the oblique fibers of the cardia help too. No single ring of muscle can be found there.

To summarize, the pharynx is one tube in three parts, the nasopharynx, the oropharynx and the laryngopharynx, and its wall is built of three overlapping constrictors that meet in the pharyngeal raphe. The gaps between them carry the auditory tube, the glossopharyngeal nerve and the internal laryngeal nerve, and the vagus supplies every pharyngeal muscle except the stylopharyngeus. The esophagus begins at the lower border of the cricoid cartilage at the sixth cervical vertebra, is crossed by the aortic arch at the fourth thoracic vertebra, and pierces the diaphragm at the tenth. Its upper third is striated muscle and its lower third is smooth, its thoracic part has no serous coat, and its veins drain into the azygos system above and the left gastric vein below. The stomach receives food at the cardia, stores it in the fundus and the body, and empties it through the pylorus, while its arteries run along the two curvatures.
