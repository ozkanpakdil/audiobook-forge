# Chapter 105 — Organ Development

This chapter follows the embryo from a flat disc of three layers to a body whose organs lie in their adult places. It covers the germ layers and the folding that shapes the body, the pharyngeal arches and the face, and then the development of the airway, the heart, the great vessels, the gut, the kidneys and the reproductive organs. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The facts gathered here are the reason an adult structure lies where it does, and many of them explain the anomalies that a clinician meets. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 10401. Which of the three germ layers of the early embryo gives rise to the lining of the gut and to the organs that bud from it? The answer is the endoderm. The endoderm forms the inner lining of the primitive gut tube. The liver, the pancreas and the lining of the airway all grow out of it. The ectoderm lies outside it and the mesoderm lies between them.

Question 10402. Which germ layer gives rise to the muscles, the bones and the blood: the ectoderm, the mesoderm, the endoderm, or the neural crest? The answer is the mesoderm. It lies between the other two layers and forms the skeleton, the voluntary muscles and the blood. The ectoderm forms the skin and the nervous system, and the endoderm lines the gut.

Question 10403. A described section of an embryo at about three weeks shows a flat disc with one layer above, one layer below, and a third layer lying between them. Which layer is the middle one, and what does it become? The answer is the mesoderm. It gives rise to the skeleton, the muscles and the blood, and it splits open to enclose the cavities of the body.

Question 10404. What is the name of the fold that carries the head end of the embryo downward and forward, bringing the developing heart and the future mouth to the front of the body? The answer is the head fold, also called the cephalic fold. It turns the flat disc into a three-dimensional body and pulls the heart into the chest.

Question 10405. The space that appears within the mesoderm and later lines the chest, the abdomen and the pelvis is called what: the amniotic cavity, the intraembryonic coelom, the yolk sac, or the chorionic cavity? The answer is the intraembryonic coelom. It becomes the pericardial cavity, the two pleural cavities and the peritoneal cavity.

Question 10406. True or false: the neural crest gives rise only to nerve tissue. The answer is false. Neural crest cells also form the melanocytes of the skin, the medulla of the adrenal gland, the bones of the face and the Schwann cells that wrap the peripheral nerves. They migrate widely before they settle.

Question 10407. Which structure lies immediately deep to the neural plate and induces it to become the nervous system? The answer is the notochord. It runs in the midline beneath the neural plate and above the gut, and it sends out the signal that starts the nervous system. It persists in the adult as the nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral discs.

Question 10408. In the early embryo, which layer lies outermost, nearest the surface: the ectoderm, the mesoderm, the endoderm, or the notochord? The answer is the ectoderm. It covers the whole embryo and gives rise to the skin and the nervous system. The endoderm lies innermost, around the yolk sac, and the mesoderm lies between them.

Question 10409. What is the name of the stiff rod of cells that forms the axis of the early embryo, lying beneath the neural tube and above the gut? The answer is the notochord. It defines the midline and induces the nervous system above it. Its remnant becomes the nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral discs.

Question 10410. A described series of sections through the back of an embryo shows a flat plate of ectoderm sinking into the midline and closing into a tube that separates from the surface. What is being formed? The answer is the neural tube, formed by the process called neurulation. The tube becomes the brain and the spinal cord.

Question 10411. The separate fingers of the hand are carved out of a solid paddle of tissue. Which process removes the tissue between them: cell division, programmed cell death, cell migration, or cell enlargement? The answer is programmed cell death, also called apoptosis. The cells between the digits are instructed to die, and the digits are left separate.

Question 10412. Name two structures of the adult body that develop from the neural crest, spoken as a list. The answer is the melanocytes of the skin and the medulla of the adrenal gland. The Schwann cells of the peripheral nerves and the bones of the face are neural crest derivatives as well.

Question 10413. Which cavity, lined by a thin serous membrane, surrounds the developing heart and later holds the adult heart? The answer is the pericardial cavity. It is formed from the intraembryonic coelom, which also gives rise to the two pleural cavities and to the peritoneal cavity. It is one single space at first and is divided later by the diaphragm and the pleuroperitoneal membranes.

Question 10414. The gut tube of the embryo is formed from the endoderm of which structure: the amnion, the yolk sac, the allantois, or the chorion? The answer is the yolk sac. Its endoderm is folded into the body of the embryo to become the lining of the gut. The allantois contributes only to the bladder.

Question 10415. A constructed illustration: a newborn is described with a loop of intestine protruding at the umbilicus inside a thin transparent sac. Which developmental event has failed? The answer is the return of the midgut from the umbilical cord. The gut normally returns to the abdomen and the body wall closes behind it. The care of the infant belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 10416. Which of these pairs belongs together: the notochord with the induction of the nervous system, the amnion with the lining of the gut, the allantois with the heart, or the yolk sac with the lungs? The answer is the notochord with the induction of the nervous system. The notochord lies beneath the neural plate and starts its development.

Question 10417. A described experiment leaves the tissue between two developing digits alive, so that it is never removed. Which appearance follows: the digits stay joined, the digits are lost, the hand is doubled, or the arm fails to form? The answer is that the digits stay joined, a condition called syndactyly. Programmed cell death normally separates them.

Question 10418. A described series of sections shows both lateral walls of the embryo curling toward the midline while the yolk sac narrows into a tube inside the body. Which process is shown, and which cavity does it close? The answer is lateral folding, which closes the ventral body wall and forms the gut tube from the roof of the yolk sac.

Question 10419. The brain and the spinal cord develop from which germ layer? The answer is the ectoderm. It forms the neural plate, which rolls up into the neural tube. The tube thickens at its head end to form the brain and narrows behind to form the spinal cord. Its cavity becomes the central canal of the spinal cord and the ventricles of the brain.

Question 10420. Which of these structures develops from the neural crest: the adrenal cortex, the adrenal medulla, the notochord, or the lining of the thyroid follicles? The answer is the adrenal medulla. Its cells migrate from the neural crest into the center of the gland. The adrenal cortex develops from the mesoderm.

Question 10421. A described embryo shows the opening at the head end of the neural tube already closed, while the opening at the tail end is still open. Which opening closes last, and at which end of the embryo? The answer is the caudal opening, at the tail end, which closes a few days after the cranial one.

Question 10422. Which cells lie immediately lateral to the neural tube and later form the sensory ganglia of the spinal nerves? The answer is the neural crest. Its cells lie along both sides of the closing neural tube and migrate away from it. They form the sensory ganglia, the pigment cells and much of the face.

Question 10423. Failure of the opening at the head end of the neural tube to close produces which malformation: spina bifida, anencephaly, a meningocele, or a dermal sinus? The answer is anencephaly. The vault of the skull fails to form over an open brain, and the defect is not compatible with life.

Question 10424. A constructed illustration: a newborn is described with a small dimple and a tuft of hair in the lower back, and the skin over the spot is unbroken. Which defect is illustrated? The answer is spina bifida occulta, in which the bony arch is incomplete but the spinal cord is covered by skin.

Question 10425. Which pharyngeal arch gives rise to the muscles of mastication? The answer is the first pharyngeal arch, also called the mandibular arch. It appears first and is the largest of the arches. It also forms the malleus and the incus, and its nerve is the trigeminal nerve. Each arch also carries its own artery and its own cartilage, and the first arch artery largely disappears.

Question 10426. The muscles of facial expression belong to which pharyngeal arch: the first, the second, the third, or the fourth? The answer is the second arch, also called the hyoid arch. Its nerve is the facial nerve, which winds around the cartilage of the second arch as it runs to the face.

Question 10427. Which of these structures develops from the cartilage of the second pharyngeal arch: the malleus, the stapes, the greater horn of the hyoid bone, or the thyroid cartilage? The answer is the stapes. The second arch cartilage also forms the styloid process and the lesser horn of the hyoid bone.

Question 10428. A described frontal view of the face of an early embryo shows one prominence above the mouth and two pairs of prominences around it. What are these five prominences called? The answer is the facial prominences: the frontonasal prominence above, the maxillary prominences at the sides, and the mandibular prominences below.

Question 10429. Which pharyngeal arch gives rise to the greater horn of the hyoid bone and to the common carotid artery? The answer is the third pharyngeal arch. Its nerve is the glossopharyngeal nerve, and the third arch also forms the lower part of the body of the hyoid bone.

Question 10430. Which pharyngeal pouch gives rise to the palatine tonsil: the first, the second, the third, or the fourth? The answer is the second pouch. Its lining grows into the surrounding tissue to form the tonsil. The third pouch forms the thymus and the inferior parathyroid gland, and the fourth forms the superior parathyroid gland.

Question 10431. Which of these pairs belongs together: the first pharyngeal pouch with the middle ear and the pharyngotympanic tube, the second pouch with the thymus, the third pouch with the palatine tonsil, or the fourth pouch with the thyroid gland? The answer is the first pouch with the middle ear and the pharyngotympanic tube.

Question 10432. A constructed illustration: a child is described with a soft midline swelling in the neck that moves upward when the tongue is put out. From which structure does it arise? The answer is a persistent thyroglossal duct, the track along which the thyroid gland descended from the tongue to the neck.

Question 10433. The thyroid gland begins on the floor of the pharynx and descends to the front of the neck. Which structures does it pass in front of on the way? The answer is the hyoid bone and the laryngeal cartilages. A remnant of its track may persist anywhere along that path.

Question 10434. A cleft lip results from failure of fusion between which two prominences: the two mandibular prominences, the medial nasal prominence and the maxillary prominence, the two lateral nasal prominences, or the frontonasal prominence and the mandibular prominence? The answer is the medial nasal prominence and the maxillary prominence.

Question 10435. Which group of muscles develops from the mesoderm of the second pharyngeal arch? The answer is the muscles of facial expression. They are supplied by the facial nerve, which is the nerve of the second arch. The stapedius muscle and the stylohyoid muscle belong to the same group.

Question 10436. A described coronal section through the roof of the mouth of a fetus shows a gap between two shelves that should meet in the midline, with the nasal septum above them. Which defect does it show? The answer is a cleft palate, from failure of the palatal shelves to meet and fuse.

Question 10437. The fourth pharyngeal pouch gives rise to which pair of structures: the inferior parathyroid gland and the thymus, the superior parathyroid gland and the ultimobranchial body, the palatine tonsil and the thymus, or the thyroid gland and the parathyroid glands? The answer is the superior parathyroid gland and the ultimobranchial body, whose cells enter the thyroid gland.

Question 10438. On the left side of the body the nerve of the sixth pharyngeal arch hooks beneath the ligament that remains after the ductus arteriosus closes. Which nerve is it, and where does it lie in relation to that artery? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, which passes beneath the aortic arch behind the ligamentum arteriosum.

Question 10439. From which wall of the foregut does the respiratory diverticulum arise? The answer is the ventral wall, in the midline of the primitive pharynx. It grows forward and downward to form the lining of the larynx, the trachea and the lungs. The cartilage and muscle around it come from the mesoderm.

Question 10440. Which structure separates the developing trachea from the developing esophagus: the tracheoesophageal septum, the pleuroperitoneal membrane, the septum transversum, or the urogenital septum? The answer is the tracheoesophageal septum. It divides the single foregut tube into an airway in front and a food channel behind. When it forms incompletely, the two tubes stay joined and a passage persists between them.

Question 10441. A described section of a fetal lung from the middle months of pregnancy shows branching tubes with thick walls and no air spaces at all. Which stage of lung development is shown, and can gas exchange take place? The answer is the pseudoglandular stage, and gas exchange cannot take place because no air sacs have formed.

Question 10442. A constructed illustration: a newborn is described with frothy secretions, coughing at the first feed, and a coiled loop of bowel shown on a described chest radiograph. Which structure failed to separate? The answer is the trachea and the esophagus, which stayed joined because the tracheoesophageal septum formed incompletely.

Question 10443. The lining of the airway and the cells that produce surfactant develop from which germ layer: the ectoderm, the endoderm, the mesoderm, or the neural crest? The answer is the endoderm. The cartilage, the muscle and the connective tissue of the airway come from the mesoderm that surrounds the endodermal tubes.

Question 10444. True or false: the cartilage and the smooth muscle of the bronchial tree are derived from the endoderm. The answer is false. They develop from the splanchnic mesoderm that wraps around the endodermal tubes of the lung. The lining of the tubes alone is endodermal. The same mesoderm gives the lung its blood vessels and its elastic tissue, and it follows every branch of the endodermal tree.

Question 10445. The heart begins as a tube formed by which germ layer? The answer is the mesoderm, from the splanchnic mesoderm of the cardiogenic area. The tube is lined by endocardium and wrapped by myocardium. It begins to beat early in the fourth week, before the chambers are formed.

Question 10446. Which chamber of the adult heart develops from the bulbus cordis: the left atrium, the right ventricle, the left ventricle, or the right atrium? The answer is the right ventricle. The primitive ventricle becomes the left ventricle, and the bulbus cordis also forms the outflow tract of the right side.

Question 10447. A described section of a heart shows an opening between the right atrium and the left atrium with no flap of tissue guarding it. Which defect does it show? The answer is an atrial septal defect, from failure of the two atrial septa to overlap and close the opening between the atria.

Question 10448. The ductus arteriosus connects which two vessels: the aorta and the pulmonary trunk, the aorta and the superior vena cava, the pulmonary veins and the left atrium, or the umbilical vein and the inferior vena cava? The answer is the aorta and the pulmonary trunk. It runs from the left pulmonary artery to the aortic arch.

Question 10449. In the fetal heart the foramen ovale lies immediately above which structure, and in which direction does blood pass through it? The answer is above the valve of the inferior vena cava, and blood passes from the right atrium into the left atrium. The valve directs the stream from the placenta toward the opening.

Question 10450. Which part of the interventricular septum closes the last opening between the two ventricles, and from which structure does it arise? The answer is the membranous part, which arises from the endocardial cushions of the outflow tract together with the muscular septum below it. The muscular part grows upward from the two ventricles and leaves a gap at the top until the cushions close it.

Question 10451. When the heart tube loops, in which direction do the two ends move: the ventricle to the right and the atrium upward and backward, the ventricle to the left and the atrium downward, both ends to the left, or both ends to the right? The answer is that the ventricle moves to the right while the atrium moves upward and backward.

Question 10452. A constructed illustration: a newborn is described as blue, and a described study shows the aorta arising from the right ventricle and lying in front of the pulmonary trunk. Which developmental error is illustrated? The answer is transposition of the great arteries, from failure of the outflow septum to spiral as it divides.

Question 10453. The smooth-walled part of the right atrium, into which the superior vena cava opens, develops from which structure: the primitive atrium, the right horn of the sinus venosus, the bulbus cordis, or the truncus arteriosus? The answer is the right horn of the sinus venosus. The rough-walled part comes from the primitive atrium.

Question 10454. A described specimen of a fetal heart shows a short wide tube joining the left pulmonary artery to the aortic arch. What is the tube, and what does it become after birth? The answer is the ductus arteriosus, which constricts after birth and becomes the ligamentum arteriosum.

Question 10455. A constructed illustration: a patient with a greatly enlarged left atrium is described as hoarse. Which nerve is most likely compressed: the right phrenic nerve beside the heart, the left recurrent laryngeal nerve beneath the aortic arch, the left vagus nerve, or the right recurrent laryngeal nerve? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve.

Question 10456. In a section through the fetal heart, the foramen ovale lies in the wall between the two atria, and blood crosses it in one direction only. Describe that direction and where the stream is aimed. The answer is from the right atrium into the left atrium, aimed at the opening above the valve of the inferior vena cava.

Question 10457. Which vessel carries oxygen-rich blood from the placenta to the fetus? The answer is the umbilical vein. It runs inside the umbilical cord, enters the body at the umbilicus and passes to the liver, where most of its blood bypasses the hepatic sinusoids through the ductus venosus.

Question 10458. After birth, the umbilical vein becomes which structure: the ligamentum teres of the liver, the ligamentum venosum, the medial umbilical ligament, or the ligamentum arteriosum? The answer is the ligamentum teres of the liver, which runs in the free edge of the falciform ligament. The ductus venosus, which carried blood past the liver in the fetus, becomes the ligamentum venosum beside it.

Question 10459. A described dissection of the inside of the anterior abdominal wall of a newborn shows two arteries running upward from the pelvis to the umbilicus, one on each side. Which vessels are shown, and what do they become? The answer is the umbilical arteries, which become the medial umbilical ligaments.

Question 10460. Which arch artery and which part of the dorsal aorta form the right subclavian artery: the third arch artery with the right dorsal aorta, the fourth arch artery with the right dorsal aorta, the sixth arch artery with the left dorsal aorta, or the second arch artery with the right dorsal aorta? The answer is the fourth right arch artery with the right dorsal aorta.

Question 10461. The umbilical vein runs upward from the umbilicus to the liver inside the free edge of which membrane? The answer is the falciform ligament, which connects the liver to the anterior abdominal wall. The vein closes after birth, and its fibrous remnant stays in that edge. The vein carried oxygen-rich blood before birth, and the falciform ligament marks its old path in the adult.

Question 10462. A constructed illustration: a child is described with a continuous murmur below the left clavicle, and a described study shows blood still passing between two great vessels. Which structure has stayed open? The answer is the ductus arteriosus, and the decision about closing it belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 10463. The superior vena cava develops mainly from which veins: the left common cardinal vein and the left anterior cardinal vein, the right common cardinal vein and the right anterior cardinal vein, the right umbilical vein, or the left posterior cardinal vein? The answer is the right common cardinal vein and the right anterior cardinal vein.

Question 10464. True or false: the umbilical arteries carry oxygen-rich blood away from the fetus to the placenta. The answer is false. They carry blood that is low in oxygen from the fetus to the placenta, where it is oxygenated. The umbilical vein brings that oxygen-rich blood back.

Question 10465. During its return to the abdomen, the loop of midgut rotates about which artery? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery. The loop turns counterclockwise around it as it comes back from the umbilical cord, and that turn carries the colon across the abdomen. The same rotation carries the cecum down to the right lower abdomen and leaves the transverse colon in front of the duodenum.

Question 10466. The liver develops as an outgrowth from which part of the gut tube: the foregut, the midgut, the hindgut, or the cloaca? The answer is the foregut. Its endodermal cells grow into the septum transversum to form the liver cords, and the bile ducts follow them. The liver keeps the blood supply of the foregut, though most of its blood reaches it through the portal vein.

Question 10467. Which diverticulum of the foregut gives rise to the liver, the gallbladder and the bile ducts? The answer is the hepatic diverticulum, which appears on the ventral wall of the foregut. It grows into the surrounding mesoderm, where the liver cords and the ducts take shape. The gallbladder and the ducts develop from the same outgrowth and keep their connection with the duodenum.

Question 10468. The spleen develops from which germ layer: the endoderm, the ectoderm, the mesoderm, or the neural crest? The answer is the mesoderm. It forms within the dorsal mesogastrium to the left of the stomach. Its framework and its blood cells both come from mesenchyme. A spleen may lie in an unusual place when this mesoderm fails to follow the stomach as it turns.

Question 10469. A described specimen of an embryo shows a loop of gut pushed out into the umbilical cord and turned one quarter of a turn about the artery at its base. What is this event called? The answer is the physiological herniation of the midgut, which makes room for the growing liver.

Question 10470. The uncinate process of the pancreas passes behind which two vessels? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery and the superior mesenteric vein. The neck of the pancreas lies in front of them, and the uncinate process hooks behind the artery. The head of the gland lies within the curve of the duodenum, and the tail reaches the spleen.

Question 10471. The cecum and the appendix develop from which part of the gut: the foregut, the midgut, the hindgut, or the cloaca? The answer is the midgut. The cecal diverticulum appears on the returning loop, and the appendix grows from the lower end of the cecum. This part of the gut keeps the blood supply of the midgut, which is the superior mesenteric artery.

Question 10472. Name two organs that develop from the foregut, spoken as a list. The answer is the liver and the pancreas. The esophagus, the stomach and the first part of the duodenum also develop from the foregut, and so does the lining of the airway. Each of these parts keeps the blood supply of the foregut, which is the celiac trunk.

Question 10473. The main pancreatic duct of the adult is formed by the union of which two ducts? The answer is the duct of the ventral pancreatic bud and the distal part of the duct of the dorsal bud. The proximal part of the dorsal duct usually persists as the accessory duct.

Question 10474. The movement that brings the transverse colon in front of the duodenum is which one: a clockwise rotation of the midgut, a counterclockwise rotation of the midgut, an upward movement of the hindgut, or a folding of the cloaca? The answer is a counterclockwise rotation of the midgut about the superior mesenteric artery.

Question 10475. A constructed illustration: a newborn is described with bile-stained vomiting, and a described radiograph shows gas in the stomach and in the first part of the duodenum only, with nothing beyond. Which developmental failure is illustrated? The answer is failure of the duodenum to recanalize, so that its lumen stays closed.

Question 10476. Which of these pairs belongs together: the vitelline duct with Meckel diverticulum, the allantois with the appendix, the vitelline duct with the urinary bladder, or the urachus with the cecum? The answer is the vitelline duct with Meckel diverticulum, the pouch that persists when the duct fails to disappear.

Question 10477. Which artery supplies the derivatives of the hindgut: the celiac trunk, the superior mesenteric artery, the inferior mesenteric artery, or the internal iliac artery? The answer is the inferior mesenteric artery. The hindgut gives rise to the descending colon, the sigmoid colon and the upper part of the rectum.

Question 10478. A described specimen of the small intestine shows a finger-like pouch on the border opposite the mesentery, about sixty centimeters from the junction with the large intestine, joined to the umbilicus by a fibrous cord. What is it? The answer is a Meckel diverticulum, a remnant of the vitelline duct.

Question 10479. In the adult, the tail of the pancreas lies immediately in front of which structures? The answer is the left kidney and the splenic vessels, with the spleen at its tip. The tail is the only part of the pancreas that reaches the left side of the body.

Question 10480. The upper part of the anal canal, above the pectinate line, develops from which structure: the hindgut, the proctodeum, the urogenital sinus, or the allantois? The answer is the hindgut. Below the pectinate line the canal develops from the proctodeum, which is a pouch of ectoderm. The two parts of the canal are supplied by different nerves, which is why they feel pain in different places.

Question 10481. Three sets of kidneys form in the embryo one after another. Which is the third and definitive set? The answer is the metanephros, which begins to form in the fifth week and becomes the adult kidney. The pronephros and the mesonephros disappear or leave only small remnants.

Question 10482. True or false: the collecting ducts of the kidney develop from the metanephric mesenchyme. The answer is false. They develop from the ureteric bud, which grows out of the mesonephric duct, while the mesenchyme forms the nephrons themselves. The bud and the mesenchyme together form one organ, and neither part develops properly without the other.

Question 10483. The ureteric bud grows out of which duct, and which tissue does it induce as it pushes into it? The answer is the mesonephric duct, and it induces the metanephric mesenchyme to form the nephrons. The two tissues then induce each other in turn. A failure of this induction leaves the kidney without nephrons, however normal the collecting system may look.

Question 10484. A described section of a developing kidney shows a branching duct pushing into a cap of loose mesenchyme. Which two structures are shown? The answer is the ureteric bud and the metanephric mesenchyme. The bud will form the collecting system and the mesenchyme the nephrons. The branching of the bud follows the pattern that becomes the calyces and the renal pelvis.

Question 10485. The Bowman capsule and the proximal convoluted tubule arise from which tissue? The answer is the metanephric mesenchyme. The vessels that form the glomerulus grow into the capsule from the surrounding mesoderm, so the tuft itself is not mesenchyme. The nephrons and the collecting ducts join later, so that the urine made in one reaches the other.

Question 10486. A constructed illustration: a child is described with repeated urinary infections, and a described study shows two separate collecting systems in one kidney with a ureter that opens below the bladder. Which error of development is illustrated? The answer is a divided ureteric bud, giving a duplex kidney and an ectopic ureter.

Question 10487. True or false: the lining of the urinary bladder develops mainly from the endoderm of the urogenital sinus. The answer is true. The trigone is the exception, for it is formed from the mesoderm of the mesonephric ducts that are absorbed into the bladder wall. The muscle of the bladder wall comes from the mesoderm that wraps the sinus, and it forms the detrusor muscle.

Question 10488. In the developing bladder the ureteric buds open above and lateral to the mesonephric ducts. What does this arrangement explain about the adult openings? The answer is that the ureters open above and lateral to the ductus deferens, because the ducts are drawn downward as the bladder grows.

Question 10489. Which of these pairs belongs together: the metanephros with the definitive kidney, the mesonephros with the placenta, the pronephros with the ureter, or the ureteric bud with the nephron? The answer is the metanephros with the definitive kidney. The mesonephros serves for a time and then mostly regresses.

Question 10490. Which structure, formed from the allantois, runs from the top of the bladder to the umbilicus, and what does it become? The answer is the urachus, which becomes the median umbilical ligament. If it stays open, urine may leak from the umbilicus of the newborn. A cyst may form along that track when only part of it closes.

Question 10491. The gonads develop from which paired structure on the posterior body wall? The answer is the gonadal ridge, which forms from the mesoderm and receives the primordial germ cells that migrate into it from the yolk sac. The ridge later becomes the testis or the ovary. The germ cells enter the ridge early, and without them the gonad becomes a fibrous streak.

Question 10492. In the male, the mesonephric duct gives rise to which structures: the uterine tube and the uterus, the epididymis and the ductus deferens, the prostate gland and the bulbourethral gland, or the scrotum and the testis? The answer is the epididymis and the ductus deferens, with the seminal vesicle.

Question 10493. A described specimen of a testis shows a small stalked remnant attached at the upper pole beside the head of the epididymis. What is it, and from which duct does it arise? The answer is the appendix testis, a remnant of the paramesonephric duct, which regresses in the male.

Question 10494. A constructed illustration: a newborn girl is described with virilized external genitalia, and the adrenal cortex is the source of the excess hormone. Which shared developmental origin links the adrenal cortex and the gonad? The answer is the mesoderm of the urogenital ridge, from which both arise.

Question 10495. Which of these pairs belongs together: the paramesonephric duct with the uterus and the uterine tube, the mesonephric duct with the uterus, the urogenital sinus with the ovary, or the gonadal ridge with the prostate? The answer is the paramesonephric duct with the uterus and the uterine tube.

Question 10496. The ovary descends from the posterior abdominal wall into the pelvis along the gubernaculum. Which two ligaments does the gubernaculum become in the female? The answer is the ovarian ligament and the round ligament of the uterus, which lie in line with each other on either side of the uterine tube.

Question 10497. Which pair of swellings on either side of the urogenital opening gives rise to the scrotum in the male and to the labia majora in the female? The answer is the labioscrotal swellings, also called the genital swellings. In the male they fuse in the midline, and the seam between them remains as the raphe of the scrotum.

Question 10498. A described dissection of the scrotum of an infant shows a narrow channel running from the abdomen into the scrotum beside the spermatic cord, with a little fluid inside it. What is the channel? The answer is a patent processus vaginalis, a pocket of peritoneum that normally closes before birth.

Question 10499. Name two tissues that develop from the somites, spoken as a list. The answer is the vertebrae with the ribs, from the sclerotome, and the skeletal muscles, from the myotome. The dermis of the back also comes from the somite. The somites lie in pairs along the back, and they give the body its segmented plan.

Question 10500. A constructed illustration: a newborn is described with loops of bowel lying outside the body to the right of the umbilicus, with no sac covering them. Which defect is illustrated, and how does it differ from a defect in which the bowel lies inside a sac? The answer is gastroschisis, in which the body wall itself failed to close, so no sac formed; when the bowel returns inside a sac the defect is an omphalocele. The care of the infant belongs to the treating clinician.

To summarize, the three germ layers divide the work of building the body, with the ectoderm forming the skin and the nervous system, the endoderm lining the gut and the airway, and the mesoderm building the skeleton, the muscles, the blood and the kidneys. The notochord induces the neural plate, the neural crest migrates widely to form the sensory ganglia, the melanocytes, the adrenal medulla and much of the face, and programmed cell death carves out the digits and the spaces around the organs. Each pharyngeal arch carries its own nerve, artery and cartilage, so the muscles of mastication, of facial expression and of the larynx can be traced to the arches they came from. The heart is the first organ to work, the gut rotates counterclockwise about the superior mesenteric artery while the liver and the pancreas bud from the foregut, and the kidney passes through three sets of organs before the metanephros takes over. The urinary and genital tracts share a common origin, which explains the course of the ureter, the trigone of the bladder and the descent of the gonad.
