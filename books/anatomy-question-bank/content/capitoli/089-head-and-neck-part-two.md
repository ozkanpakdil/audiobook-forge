# Chapter 89 — Head and Neck, Part Two

The head and the neck carry the face, the jaws, the salivary glands, the tongue and the spaces between them, and above the diaphragm the thorax holds the lungs, the heart and the great vessels. This chapter works through the muscles of the face and of chewing, the temporomandibular joint, the tongue and the pharynx, the root of the neck with its vessels and its nerves, and then the chest wall, the pleura, the lungs, the mediastinum and the heart. It holds one hundred questions, and every one gives its answer at once and then explains why, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 8801. Which muscle forms a complete ring around the mouth and closes the lips? The answer is the orbicularis oris. Its fibers circle the opening of the mouth and blend with the muscles that radiate toward it. It is supplied by the facial nerve.

Question 8802. Which muscle of the face runs from the cheekbone to the angle of the mouth and lifts that angle when a person smiles? The answer is the zygomaticus major. It arises from the zygomatic bone and passes downward and forward to the corner of the mouth. A weak muscle on one side flattens that smile.

Question 8803. Which nerve supplies the muscles of facial expression: the trigeminal nerve, the facial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, or the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the facial nerve. It leaves the skull through the stylomastoid foramen and fans out within the parotid gland. The trigeminal nerve supplies the muscles of chewing instead.

Question 8804. A described dissection of the cheek shows a sheet of muscle running from the back of the mouth to the angle of the mouth, with a duct piercing it to open opposite the upper second molar tooth. Which structures are described? The answer is the buccinator muscle and the parotid duct. The buccinator keeps food between the teeth, and the duct carries saliva from the gland.

Question 8805. Which structure lies inside the parotid gland and divides it into a superficial and a deep part? The answer is the facial nerve with its branches. The nerve enters the gland behind the jaw and spreads through it in a fan. The external carotid artery and the retromandibular vein also lie within the gland, but the nerve separates the two parts.

Question 8806. Which muscle of chewing lies in the space behind the upper jaw and pulls the lower jaw forward: the temporalis, the masseter, the medial pterygoid, or the lateral pterygoid? The answer is the lateral pterygoid. It runs from the base of the skull to the neck of the mandible. It is the only muscle of chewing that protrudes the jaw, and it also helps to open the mouth.

Question 8807. Which fan-shaped muscle fills the hollow above the ear and raises the lower jaw, with its tendon passing deep to the cheekbone? The answer is the temporalis. Its tendon passes behind the zygomatic arch and inserts on the coronoid process. It also retracts a jaw that has been pushed forward.

Question 8808. True or false: the muscles of chewing are supplied by the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. The answer is true. That division leaves the skull through the foramen ovale and supplies the masseter, the temporalis and both pterygoid muscles. The same division also carries sensation from the lower teeth and the chin.

Question 8809. A constructed illustration: after a deep cut in front of the left ear, the left side of the face does not move, the left eye will not close, and only the right side of the forehead creases. Which nerve has been divided? The answer is the left facial nerve, before it divides inside the gland. Its branches supply every muscle of facial expression there.

Question 8810. Through which opening does the space behind the upper jaw communicate with the orbit: the superior orbital fissure, the inferior orbital fissure, the foramen rotundum, or the foramen ovale? The answer is the inferior orbital fissure. It lies between the floor and the lateral wall of the orbit. The maxillary nerve and the inferior ophthalmic veins pass through it.

Question 8811. Which of these pairs belongs together: the chorda tympani with the submandibular ganglion, the greater petrosal nerve with the submandibular ganglion, the lesser petrosal nerve with the ciliary ganglion, or the nasociliary nerve with the pterygopalatine ganglion? The answer is the chorda tympani with the submandibular ganglion. It carries taste from the front of the tongue and joins the lingual nerve.

Question 8812. Which gland lies in the floor of the mouth, wraps around the back of the mylohyoid muscle, and opens by a duct beside the frenulum of the tongue? The answer is the submandibular gland. Its duct runs forward under the lining of the mouth. The sublingual gland lies beside it and opens by several small ducts.

Question 8813. Which cranial nerve supplies the muscles of the tongue: the trigeminal nerve, the facial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, or the vagus nerve? The answer is the hypoglossal nerve. It leaves the skull through the hypoglossal canal and runs to the tongue. It supplies every muscle of the tongue except the palatoglossus, which the vagus nerve reaches.

Question 8814. A described cross section of the neck shows a tube of cartilage in the midline, a flat gland wrapped around its front and sides, and two small rounded bodies set in the back of that gland. Which structures are described? The answer is the larynx and the trachea, the thyroid gland, and the parathyroid glands. The parathyroids usually lie on the back of the thyroid gland.

Question 8815. Which structure lies immediately in front of the ear, deep to the skin, and can be felt pulsating over the root of the zygomatic arch? The answer is the superficial temporal artery. It is one of the two terminal branches of the external carotid artery. It ascends across the temple and divides into a frontal and a parietal branch.

Question 8816. A constructed illustration: a stab wound in the posterior triangle of the neck is followed by a drooping shoulder and weakness in shrugging that shoulder. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the accessory nerve. It crosses the posterior triangle on its way to the trapezius muscle. That muscle therefore loses its main nerve supply.

Question 8817. Name three structures that lie within the carotid sheath in the neck. The answer is the common carotid artery and its continuation, the internal jugular vein, and the vagus nerve. The sheath runs from the base of the skull to the root of the neck. The vein lies lateral to the artery, and the nerve lies behind and between them.

Question 8818. Which statement about the thoracic duct is correct: it ends at the left venous angle, it ends at the right venous angle, it ends at the arch of the aorta, or it ends at the azygos vein? The answer is that it ends at the left venous angle. That angle is where the left internal jugular and left subclavian veins meet. The right lymphatic duct ends at the right venous angle.

Question 8819. Which trunk arises from the first part of the subclavian artery and gives off the inferior thyroid, the suprascapular and the transverse cervical arteries? The answer is the thyrocervical trunk. It arises just medial to the scalenus anterior muscle. The inferior thyroid artery supplies the lower part of the thyroid gland.

Question 8820. A described radiograph of the side of the head shows the lower jaw with two processes at its upper end, separated by a notch, and the rounded head of the jaw in a hollow in front of the ear. Which structures are described? The answer is the coronoid process, the condylar process and the temporomandibular joint. The notch between them is the mandibular notch.

Question 8821. Which statement about the temporomandibular joint is correct: it is divided into two compartments by a disc, it is a simple hinge with one cavity, it has no fibrous capsule, or its two sides move independently? The answer is that it is divided into two compartments by a fibrocartilaginous disc. The lower compartment allows the hinge movement, and the upper allows the jaw to glide.

Question 8822. The hyoid bone, the thyroid cartilage and the cricoid cartilage lie one below another in the neck. In which plane do all three lie, and in which plane would all three be seen from the side? The answer is the median plane, and a sagittal plane would show them from the side. All three lie in the midline.

Question 8823. Which cartilage of the larynx forms a complete ring and is felt as a firm rounded prominence below the thyroid cartilage? The answer is the cricoid cartilage. It is the only complete ring in the airway and lies below the thyroid cartilage. The first ring of the trachea begins beneath it.

Question 8824. Which nerve ascends in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus to reach the larynx? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve. On the right it hooks around the right subclavian artery, and on the left around the arch of the aorta. It supplies all the intrinsic muscles of the larynx except the cricothyroid.

Question 8825. The recurrent laryngeal nerve supplies all the intrinsic muscles of the larynx except which one: the cricothyroid, the thyroarytenoid, the posterior cricoarytenoid, or the lateral cricoarytenoid? The answer is the cricothyroid. It is supplied by the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. The posterior cricoarytenoid is the only muscle that opens the vocal folds.

Question 8826. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the lower neck, a person has a hoarse voice and the left vocal fold lies near the midline. Which nerve was injured, and why is the left one more exposed? The answer is the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, which hooks around the arch of the aorta and has a much longer course than the right. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8827. A described specimen shows the tongue drawn forward, with a fold of lining running from the side of the tongue to the wall of the pharynx, and a small mass of lymphoid tissue behind that fold. Which structures are described? The answer is the palatoglossal fold and the palatine tonsil. The fold forms the anterior pillar of the fauces, in front of the tonsil.

Question 8828. Which pair belongs together: the temporalis with the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve, the orbicularis oculi with the mandibular division, the lateral pterygoid with the facial nerve, or the front belly of the digastric with the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the temporalis with the mandibular division. That division supplies every muscle of chewing and also the front belly of the digastric.

Question 8829. Which of these arteries is a branch of the external carotid artery: the maxillary artery, the ophthalmic artery, the vertebral artery, or the thyrocervical trunk? The answer is the maxillary artery. It is one of the two terminal branches of the external carotid artery. The ophthalmic artery arises from the internal carotid artery.

Question 8830. Which bone forms the lower jaw, carries the lower teeth, and joins the skull at a joint in front of each ear? The answer is the mandible. It has a body that carries the teeth and two ascending rami. Each ramus ends above in a coronoid process and a condyle, which forms the joint.

Question 8831. True or false: the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve leaves the skull through the foramen ovale. The answer is false. It leaves through the foramen rotundum. The foramen ovale carries the mandibular division. The maxillary division carries sensation from the upper teeth, the cheek and the upper lip.

Question 8832. Which muscle forms the floor of the mouth and is supplied by a branch of the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve: the mylohyoid, the geniohyoid, the stylohyoid, or the thyrohyoid? The answer is the mylohyoid. It runs from the mandible to the hyoid bone and supports the tongue. The geniohyoid and the thyrohyoid are supplied by the first cervical nerve.

Question 8833. A described dissection of the root of the neck shows three trunks formed between the anterior and the middle scalene muscles. Which structure is described? The answer is the brachial plexus. The trunks are the upper, the middle and the lower. The subclavian artery also passes between those muscles, in front of the trunks.

Question 8834. Which vein lies immediately in front of the subclavian artery at the root of the neck, separated from it by the scalenus anterior muscle? The answer is the subclavian vein. It lies between the clavicle and the first rib, in front of that muscle. The phrenic nerve crosses the front of the muscle and passes behind the vein.

Question 8835. Which sympathetic ganglion in the neck is the largest, lies at the level of the second and third cervical vertebrae, and gives off the superior cardiac nerve? The answer is the superior cervical ganglion. It lies behind the carotid sheath and in front of the prevertebral fascia. The middle ganglion is the smallest, at the level of the cricoid cartilage.

Question 8836. A constructed illustration: a person has a drooping upper eyelid, a small pupil and a dry flushed face after a deep injury in the neck. Which part of the nervous system has been damaged? The answer is the sympathetic chain in the neck. Its fibers reach the eye and the sweat glands of the face. Interruption of that pathway produces these signs.

Question 8837. Which fascial space of the neck lies behind the pharynx and the esophagus, in front of the prevertebral fascia, and continues downward into the chest? The answer is the retropharyngeal space. It runs from the base of the skull to the upper part of the posterior mediastinum. Because it is continuous with the chest, infection can travel along it.

Question 8838. Which pair belongs together: the sternocleidomastoid with the accessory nerve, the trapezius with the thoracodorsal nerve, the rhomboid muscles with the axillary nerve, or the levator scapulae with the suprascapular nerve? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid with the accessory nerve, which also supplies the trapezius. The thoracodorsal nerve supplies the latissimus dorsi, and the dorsal scapular nerve supplies the rhomboids.

Question 8839. Which muscle of the neck has one head on the manubrium of the sternum and one on the medial part of the clavicle, and divides the neck into an anterior and a posterior triangle? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid. It runs upward and backward to the mastoid process. Turning the head to the opposite side makes it stand out.

Question 8840. Which of these structures lies in the triangle bounded by the two bellies of the digastric muscle and the lower border of the mandible: the submandibular gland, the accessory nerve, the phrenic nerve, or the brachial plexus? The answer is the submandibular gland. It fills that triangle and wraps around the mylohyoid muscle. The other three lie elsewhere.

Question 8841. A described dissection of the cheek shows a pad of fat lying between the buccinator and the masseter, with a fan of nerve branches spreading over it. Which structures are described? The answer is the buccal fat pad and the branches of the facial nerve. The pad separates the muscles and is prominent in a newborn.

Question 8842. Which statement about the muscles of the soft palate is correct: the tensor veli palatini is supplied by the mandibular division, the levator veli palatini by the facial nerve, the palatoglossus by the hypoglossal nerve, or the uvular muscle by the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is that the tensor veli palatini is supplied by the mandibular division. The remaining muscles of the palate are supplied through the pharyngeal plexus.

Question 8843. Which nerve descends on the front of the scalenus anterior muscle, behind the prevertebral fascia, and continues through the chest to the diaphragm? The answer is the phrenic nerve. It is formed mainly from the fourth cervical nerve, with a contribution from the third and the fifth. In the neck it lies lateral to the vagus nerve and in front of the subclavian artery.

Question 8844. Which muscle has a front belly from the chin to the hyoid bone and a back belly from the mastoid process to the hyoid, with a tendon between them? The answer is the digastric muscle. The front belly is supplied by the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve, and the back belly by the facial nerve. Together they raise the hyoid bone.

Question 8845. A constructed illustration: bleeding begins from the lower part of the tonsillar bed during an operation in the throat, and the artery there must be named. Which artery is it? The answer is the tonsillar branch of the facial artery. The facial artery winds over the jaw and gives that branch near the back of the tongue. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8846. Which artery crosses the lower border of the mandible in front of the masseter muscle and continues upward toward the angle of the mouth? The answer is the facial artery. It is a branch of the external carotid artery and grooves the submandibular gland as it turns around the jaw. Its pulse can be felt where it crosses the bone.

Question 8847. A described specimen of the larynx is opened from behind and shows two folds running from front to back on each side, with a space between them. Which structures are described? The answer is the vestibular fold above and the vocal fold below, with the ventricle of the larynx between them. The vocal fold contains the vocal ligament.

Question 8848. Which statement about the nerves of the tongue is correct: taste from the front of the tongue travels through the facial nerve and general sensation through the trigeminal nerve, taste from the front travels with the hypoglossal nerve, sensation from the back travels with the facial nerve, or all sensation travels with the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the first statement. Taste from the back of the tongue travels with the glossopharyngeal nerve.

Question 8849. A described dissection of the floor of the mouth shows a thin sheet of muscle stretched between the two halves of the jaw, with a duct and a nerve running above it. Which structures lie above that sheet? The answer is the submandibular duct and the lingual nerve. The duct opens beside the frenulum, and the lingual nerve carries sensation and taste.

Question 8850. Through which opening does the facial nerve leave the skull: the stylomastoid foramen, the jugular foramen, the foramen ovale, or the internal acoustic meatus? The answer is the stylomastoid foramen. The nerve enters the skull at the internal acoustic meatus and leaves at the stylomastoid foramen, just behind the styloid process. It then enters the parotid gland.

Question 8851. Which dome-shaped muscle separates the chest from the abdomen and is the chief muscle of breathing? The answer is the diaphragm. It arises from the lower ribs, the back of the sternum and the upper lumbar vertebrae, and its fibers converge on a central tendon. When it contracts, it flattens and draws air into the lungs.

Question 8852. Which of these structures passes through the aortic opening of the diaphragm: the aorta, the inferior vena cava, the esophagus, or the right phrenic nerve? The answer is the aorta. The thoracic duct and the azygos vein pass through the same opening beside it. The opening lies at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra.

Question 8853. Which opening in the diaphragm lies in the central tendon, at the level of the eighth thoracic vertebra, and carries the inferior vena cava? The answer is the caval opening. It lies a little to the right of the midline, and the right phrenic nerve pierces the muscle beside it. The opening admits the widest of the three main channels.

Question 8854. A described frontal radiograph of the chest shows a dark area on each side crossed by faint curved lines, and a dense shadow in the middle. Which structures are shown? The answer is the two lungs with the ribs crossing them, and the heart with the mediastinum between. The diaphragm forms the curved lower border.

Question 8855. Which nerves supply the diaphragm: the intercostal nerves alone, the phrenic nerves with the lower intercostal nerves, the vagus nerves, or the sympathetic trunk? The answer is the phrenic nerves with the lower intercostal nerves. The phrenic nerves carry the motor supply, and the lower intercostal nerves carry sensation from the outer rim. Irritation can therefore refer pain to the shoulder.

Question 8856. Which structure lies immediately behind the sternum in the chest of a child, and what becomes of it in adult life? The answer is the thymus, which shrinks and is replaced largely by fat. It lies in the anterior mediastinum in front of the heart and the great vessels. It is largest in early childhood.

Question 8857. Which serous membrane lines the inside of the chest wall and covers the surface of each lung: the pleura, the pericardium, the peritoneum, or the endothoracic fascia? The answer is the pleura. The parietal layer lines the wall, and the visceral layer is stuck to the lung. The film of fluid between them lets the layers slide as the lung moves.

Question 8858. True or false: the lowest part of the pleural sac on each side is not filled by lung, even in full inspiration. The answer is true. That part is the costodiaphragmatic recess, between the lower border of the lung and the diaphragm. It fills with fluid when the pleural cavity is inflamed.

Question 8859. Which of these structures lies in the posterior mediastinum: the thoracic duct, the phrenic nerve, the thymus, or the arch of the aorta? The answer is the thoracic duct. It ascends in front of the vertebral column, behind the esophagus, and to the left of the midline. The phrenic nerve and the thymus lie further forward.

Question 8860. A constructed illustration: after a broken rib, air enters the space around a lung and the lung shrinks away from the chest wall. Which space has the air entered, and what are its two layers? The answer is the pleural cavity, between the parietal pleura and the visceral pleura. It normally holds only a thin film of fluid.

Question 8861. Which fissure separates the upper lobe of the right lung from the middle lobe: the horizontal fissure, the oblique fissure, the azygos fissure, or the superior accessory fissure? The answer is the horizontal fissure. It runs forward from the oblique fissure at the level of the fourth costal cartilage. The left lung has no horizontal fissure, because it has only two lobes.

Question 8862. Which of these statements about the left lung is correct: it has two lobes with a cardiac notch and a lingula, it has three lobes with a horizontal fissure, it has two lobes with a horizontal fissure, or it has four lobes? The answer is that it has two lobes, with a cardiac notch and a lingula. The notch is where the heart presses on the lung.

Question 8863. A described cross section of the chest at the level of the sternal angle shows a large vessel crossing in front of the windpipe and passing backward to its left. Which vessel is described? The answer is the arch of the aorta. It begins behind the sternal angle and curves over the left main bronchus. The trachea divides beneath it.

Question 8864. Which structure lies immediately behind the left atrium of the heart and in front of the vertebral column? The answer is the esophagus, with the descending aorta to its left. The esophagus passes down through the posterior mediastinum and pierces the diaphragm. The left atrium therefore lies in front of it.

Question 8865. Which chamber of the heart forms the apex, pointing downward, forward and to the left? The answer is the left ventricle. Its wall is the thickest of the four chambers because it pumps blood around the whole body. The right ventricle forms most of the front of the heart instead.

Question 8866. Which valve guards the opening between the right atrium and the right ventricle, and how many cusps does it have: the mitral valve, the tricuspid valve, the aortic valve, or the pulmonary valve? The answer is the tricuspid valve, which has three cusps. The mitral valve lies on the left side of the heart and has two cusps.

Question 8867. Name the two coronary arteries and the branch of the left one that runs down the front of the heart in the groove between the two ventricles. The answer is the right and the left coronary arteries, with the anterior interventricular branch of the left. The right coronary artery usually supplies the back of the heart.

Question 8868. A constructed illustration: a person feels a tearing pain between the shoulder blades, and imaging shows a split in the wall of the aorta beyond the origin of the left subclavian artery. Which part of the aorta is involved? The answer is the descending thoracic aorta. It continues downward in the posterior mediastinum to the diaphragm. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8869. Which statement about the pericardium is correct: the transverse pericardial sinus lies behind the aorta and the pulmonary trunk, the oblique sinus lies in front of the aorta, the fibrous pericardium is the serous layer, or the visceral layer lines the inside of the fibrous pericardium? The answer is that the transverse sinus lies behind those two vessels. The oblique sinus lies behind the left atrium.

Question 8870. Which vein drains the right posterior intercostal spaces and the right side of the back, and empties into the superior vena cava? The answer is the azygos vein. It begins in the abdomen and ascends on the right of the vertebral column. It arches over the root of the right lung to join the superior vena cava.

Question 8871. A described chest radiograph shows the windpipe dividing into two branches, the left one running more horizontally than the right. Why is the left main bronchus more horizontal? The answer is that the arch of the aorta passes over it. The right main bronchus is wider and more vertical, so an inhaled object lodges there more often.

Question 8872. Which of these pairs belongs together: the right ventricle with the pulmonary trunk, the left ventricle with the pulmonary trunk, the right atrium with the aorta, or the left atrium with the superior vena cava? The answer is the right ventricle with the pulmonary trunk. The right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs, and the left ventricle pumps it into the aorta.

Question 8873. Which chamber of the heart lies immediately behind the sternum and forms most of the front of the heart: the right atrium, the right ventricle, the left ventricle, or the left atrium? The answer is the right ventricle. It lies behind the lower sternum and the costal cartilages. The right atrium forms the right border of the heart.

Question 8874. Which vein lies immediately to the right of the thoracic duct in the posterior mediastinum and receives the hemiazygos vein? The answer is the azygos vein. The hemiazygos vein ascends on the left and crosses the vertebral column to join it. The thoracic duct ascends between the aorta and the azygos vein.

Question 8875. Which nerve descends behind the root of the lung, forms a plexus on the back of the esophagus and continues to the abdomen? The answer is the vagus nerve. Its branches intertwine around the esophagus to form the esophageal plexus. They pass through the diaphragm beside the esophagus.

Question 8876. True or false: the apex of each lung rises above the level of the medial third of the clavicle. The answer is true. The apex reaches about 2 to 3 centimeters above that point. It is covered by the cervical pleura, which is reinforced above by a membrane.

Question 8877. Which of these structures is found in the right atrium: the fossa ovalis, the moderator band, the papillary muscles, or the aortic vestibule? The answer is the fossa ovalis. It is the remnant of the opening between the two atria in the fetus. The moderator band and the papillary muscles belong to the ventricles.

Question 8878. A constructed illustration: blood collects rapidly in the sac around the heart, and the chambers cannot fill. Which space holds the blood, and which two layers bound it? The answer is the pericardial cavity, between the fibrous pericardium and the serous layer on the heart. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8879. Which large artery leaves the left ventricle, arches backward to the left, and then descends through the chest to the abdomen? The answer is the aorta. Its first part is the ascending aorta, hidden behind the pulmonary trunk. The arch gives off the arteries to the head, the neck and the upper limbs.

Question 8880. A described specimen of the heart is opened and shows a chamber with ridges on its inner wall and a band of muscle crossing from the wall between the ventricles to the front wall. Which chamber and which band are described? The answer is the right ventricle and the moderator band. The band carries part of the conducting system.

Question 8881. Which statement about the coronary arteries is correct: the right coronary artery supplies the right atrium and the right ventricle, the circumflex artery arises from the right coronary artery, the anterior interventricular artery from the right coronary artery, or the posterior interventricular artery from the left coronary artery? The answer is the first statement. In most people the posterior interventricular artery comes from the right coronary artery.

Question 8882. The heart lies in the middle of the chest with its long axis running downward, forward and left. In which plane does that axis lie, and on which side of the midline is the apex? The answer is an oblique plane, and the apex lies to the left of the midline. A cut in the plane of the long axis shows all four chambers.

Question 8883. Which plane divides the mediastinum into a superior and an inferior part, and between which two landmarks is it drawn? The answer is the transverse thoracic plane, drawn from the sternal angle back to the lower border of the fourth thoracic vertebra. The superior mediastinum lies above it, and the inferior mediastinum below.

Question 8884. Which statement about an intercostal space is correct: the vein, the artery and the nerve lie in the groove under the rib, in that order from above down, the nerve lies above the artery, the artery lies above the vein, or all three lie deep to the lining of the chest? The answer is the first statement. The bundle is protected by the lower border of the rib above it.

Question 8885. Which structure lies in the costal groove under a rib, deepest of the three that run there, and can be injured by a needle passed just above a rib? The answer is the intercostal nerve. The vein lies above it and the artery between them. A needle is therefore passed just above the lower rib of a space.

Question 8886. A constructed illustration: after a fall, a person struggles to breathe, the right side of the chest does not move, and tapping gives a hollow note. Which space has filled with air, and what has happened to the right lung? The answer is the right pleural cavity, with the lung collapsed. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8887. A described cross section of the chest shows the heart in the middle, a lung on each side, and a nerve running in front of the root of each lung. Which nerve is described, and what does it supply below? The answer is the phrenic nerve, which supplies the diaphragm. It also carries sensation from the pericardium and the mediastinal pleura.

Question 8888. Which of these pairs belongs together: the aortic opening of the diaphragm with the aorta, the caval opening with the esophagus, the esophageal opening with the inferior vena cava, or the aortic opening with the inferior vena cava? The answer is the aortic opening with the aorta. The caval opening carries the inferior vena cava, and the esophageal opening carries the esophagus and the vagus nerves.

Question 8889. Which structures pass through the esophageal opening of the diaphragm with the esophagus: the vagus nerves, the phrenic nerves, the thoracic duct, or the azygos vein? The answer is the vagus nerves, with the esophageal branches of the left gastric vessels. The opening lies at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra, in the muscular part of the diaphragm.

Question 8890. Which muscle of the chest wall lies deepest of the three layers of the intercostal muscles and separates the space from the pleura? The answer is the innermost intercostal muscle. It is separated from the internal intercostal muscle by the vessels and nerve. Its fibers run in the same direction as the internal intercostal muscle.

Question 8891. Name two structures that lie in the middle mediastinum, spoken as a list. The answer is the heart with its pericardium, and the ascending aorta with the pulmonary trunk. The main bronchi and the phrenic nerves also lie in the middle mediastinum. That space lies at the level of the heart.

Question 8892. Which statement about the bronchopulmonary segments of the lung is correct: each has its own segmental bronchus and artery, the pulmonary veins run inside the segments, each lung has eight segments, or all the segments share one bronchus? The answer is that each segment has its own segmental bronchus and artery. The veins run between segments, which is why a segment can be removed.

Question 8893. A described chest radiograph shows a rounded shadow at the right cardiophrenic angle, and a side view shows it behind the lower sternum and in front of the heart. Which part of the mediastinum does it occupy? The answer is the anterior mediastinum. That space lies between the sternum and the pericardium, and it narrows toward the diaphragm.

Question 8894. Which structure ascends through the posterior mediastinum on the left of the vertebral column and ends at the junction of the left internal jugular and left subclavian veins? The answer is the thoracic duct. It begins in the abdomen as the cisterna chyli and passes through the aortic opening. It carries lymph from the whole body except the right upper quarter.

Question 8895. Which chamber of the heart receives oxygenated blood from the pulmonary veins: the right atrium, the left atrium, the right ventricle, or the left ventricle? The answer is the left atrium. The four pulmonary veins open into its back wall. Blood then passes through the mitral valve into the left ventricle.

Question 8896. True or false: the visceral pleura covering the lung is insensitive to pain, while the parietal pleura lining the chest wall is sensitive. The answer is true. The parietal pleura is supplied by the intercostal and phrenic nerves, which carry pain. The visceral pleura has only autonomic nerves, so the lung feels no pain.

Question 8897. Which of these arteries is a branch of the right coronary artery in most people: the right marginal artery, the anterior interventricular artery, the circumflex artery, or the left marginal artery? The answer is the right marginal artery. The anterior interventricular and circumflex arteries are branches of the left coronary artery. The left marginal artery also comes from the left coronary artery.

Question 8898. A constructed illustration: a person is short of breath, the left side of the chest is dull when tapped, breath sounds are absent at the left base, and the windpipe stays in the midline. Which space is filled, and where does the fluid collect first? The answer is the left pleural cavity, with fluid first gathering in the costodiaphragmatic recess. The management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8899. A described specimen of the heart shows smooth inner surfaces in the two upper chambers and ridged surfaces in the two lower ones, and a probe passes through an opening between the upper chambers. Which opening is described? The answer is the fossa ovalis, the remnant of the foramen ovale. It lies between the right and the left atrium.

Question 8900. Which statement about the development of the diaphragm is correct: it is formed from the septum transversum, the pleuroperitoneal membranes, the dorsal mesentery of the esophagus and the body wall, it comes only from the septum transversum, it comes from the neural tube, or it comes from the neural crest? The answer is the first statement. The phrenic nerve reaches the diaphragm because it arises in the neck.

To summarize, the face is moved by the facial nerve and the jaws by the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve, with the lateral pterygoid the only muscle of chewing that pulls the jaw forward, and the temporomandibular joint divided by a disc into a hinge and a gliding part. The parotid gland wraps around the ramus of the mandible with the facial nerve inside it, the submandibular gland lies in the floor of the mouth, and the tongue is moved by the hypoglossal nerve while its taste and its general sensation travel with other nerves. The neck is divided by the sternocleidomastoid muscle into two triangles, holds the carotid sheath with the carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve, and carries the phrenic nerve on the front of the scalenus anterior. In the chest, the diaphragm separates the two cavities, and its three openings carry the inferior vena cava, the esophagus with the vagus nerves, and the aorta with the thoracic duct, while the right lung has three lobes and the left two.
