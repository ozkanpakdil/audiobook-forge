# Chapter 65 — Oral Cavity

The mouth is the entrance to the digestive tract, and this chapter maps it from the lips to the isthmus of the fauces and from the teeth to the uvula. It covers the two spaces of the mouth, the palate, the tongue, the salivary glands and their ducts, the floor of the mouth, the nerves and vessels that serve these parts, and the teeth with their tissues and roots. It holds one hundred questions, and each one states its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in the mind until the end. Everything here describes the ordinary structure of the mouth and its common variations. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 6401. Which space of the mouth lies between the lips and the teeth, and which space lies behind the teeth and the gums? The answer is the oral vestibule in front and the oral cavity proper behind. The vestibule is the outer slit bounded by the lips and the cheeks. The cavity proper lies within the alveolar arches, and the two communicate behind the last molar teeth.

Question 6402. Which structures separate the oral vestibule from the oral cavity proper: the lips, the alveolar arches with their teeth and gums, the soft palate, or the palatoglossal arches? The answer is the alveolar arches with their teeth and gums. They form the wall that runs around the mouth between the two spaces. The lips and the cheeks bound the vestibule on its outer side, and the soft palate lies far behind.

Question 6403. What is the name of the fold of mucous membrane that ties the middle of the upper lip to the gum above the central incisors? The answer is the upper labial frenulum. A matching fold connects the lower lip to the gum below it. Folds of the same kind tie the tongue to the floor of the mouth, and a short one there limits its movement.

Question 6404. A described section through the roof of the mouth shows a bony plate covered by a thick layer of mucous membrane that is bound firmly to the bone beneath it. Which region is shown, and why does a swelling here feel fixed? The answer is the hard palate, whose mucosa is bound firmly to the bone. That firm attachment is why a swelling here is fixed rather than mobile.

Question 6405. A described specimen shows the roof of the mouth from below, with a pale midline band running from behind the incisor teeth back to a small projection that hangs at the end. Name the band and the projection. The answer is the palatine raphe and the uvula. The raphe marks the line along which the two halves of the palate fused during development.

Question 6406. Which muscle lies immediately anterior to the superior constrictor of the pharynx and is joined to it by a fibrous band? The answer is the buccinator. The band between them is the pterygomandibular raphe, which runs from the hamulus of the medial pterygoid plate down to the back of the mandible. The two muscles therefore act as one sheet.

Question 6407. Which small muscle lies within the soft palate and shortens the uvula? The answer is the musculus uvulae. It lies on either side of the midline inside the palatine aponeurosis. When it shortens, the uvula thickens and the soft palate seals the nasopharynx more completely during swallowing. It is one of the muscles supplied through the pharyngeal plexus.

Question 6408. Which muscle of the soft palate opens the auditory tube during swallowing: the levator veli palatini, the tensor veli palatini, the palatopharyngeus, or the musculus uvulae? The answer is the tensor veli palatini. It also tenses the soft palate and helps it resist the pressure of swallowing. It is the one muscle of the palate supplied by the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve.

Question 6409. True or false: every muscle of the soft palate is supplied by the pharyngeal plexus of nerves except one. The answer is true. The tensor veli palatini is the exception, and it takes its nerve from the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. The other muscles of the palate are supplied through the pharyngeal plexus, which carries fibers of the vagus nerve.

Question 6410. A constructed illustration: after a described injury, the soft palate droops on one side and the uvula points away from that side. Which structures have most likely lost their nerve supply? The answer is the muscles of the soft palate on the affected side, which are supplied through the pharyngeal plexus. The palate cannot lift, so the healthy muscles pull the uvula toward their own side.

Question 6411. Which muscle of the tongue forms the anterior pillar of the fauces and pulls the root of the tongue upward and backward? The answer is the palatoglossus. It lies within the palatoglossal arch, which runs from the soft palate down to the side of the tongue. When it contracts, it narrows the isthmus of the fauces.

Question 6412. Which structures form the isthmus of the fauces: the two palatoglossal arches with the soft palate above and the tongue below, the two palatopharyngeal arches alone, the uvula and the epiglottis, or the buccinator with the pterygomandibular raphe? The answer is the two palatoglossal arches with the soft palate above and the tongue below. That opening leads from the mouth into the oropharynx.

Question 6413. Which of these pairs belongs together: the palatoglossal arch with the palatoglossus muscle, the palatopharyngeal arch with the palatoglossus, the palatoglossal arch with the salpingopharyngeus, or the palatopharyngeal arch with the tensor veli palatini? The answer is the palatoglossal arch with the palatoglossus muscle. The arch behind it contains the palatopharyngeus, and the salpingopharyngeus lies higher still.

Question 6414. A described dissection of the side wall of the oropharynx shows a mass of lymphoid tissue sitting in a bed of loose tissue between two arched folds of mucous membrane. Which structure is described, and what lies between it and the muscle of the wall? The answer is the palatine tonsil, separated from the superior constrictor by loose connective tissue. That loose bed allows the tonsil to be shelled out.

Question 6415. Which papillae of the tongue sit in front of a V-shaped groove and carry taste buds in their walls? The answer is the circumvallate papillae. The groove is the sulcus terminalis, which separates the body of the tongue from its root. These papillae are larger than the others and lie in a row just in front of that line.

Question 6416. Roughly how many circumvallate papillae are found on a normal tongue: two, four to six, eight to twelve, or twenty? The answer is eight to twelve. They are arranged in a V just in front of the sulcus terminalis, with the point of the V facing backward. Their number varies between people, and that variation is normal.

Question 6417. Name the four kinds of papillae found on the surface of the tongue. The answer is the filiform, the fungiform, the foliate, and the circumvallate papillae. The filiform papillae are the smallest and the most numerous, and they carry no taste buds. The circumvallate are the largest and lie at the back of the tongue.

Question 6418. Which papillae of the tongue are the most numerous but carry no taste buds at all? The answer is the filiform papillae. They cover the front and the sides of the tongue and give its surface its rough feel. Taste buds lie in the walls of the fungiform, foliate, and circumvallate papillae instead.

Question 6419. As it enters the floor of the mouth, the lingual nerve lies in which relation to the duct of the submandibular gland? The answer is that the nerve lies lateral at first and then loops below the duct. The duct crosses the nerve and lies medial to it further forward. The submandibular ganglion hangs from the nerve close to that crossing.

Question 6420. Which nerve carries taste from the anterior two thirds of the tongue: the lingual nerve alone, the chorda tympani, the glossopharyngeal nerve, or the hypoglossal nerve? The answer is the chorda tympani. It joins the lingual nerve in the infratemporal region and travels with it to the tongue. The lingual nerve itself carries only general sensation from that region.

Question 6421. Which nerve supplies both general sensation and taste from the posterior third of the tongue? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve, the ninth cranial nerve. It serves the region behind the sulcus terminalis. It also carries sensation from the palatine tonsil and from the mucous membrane of the oropharynx.

Question 6422. Which nerve provides general sensation from the anterior two thirds of the tongue: the hypoglossal nerve, the lingual nerve, the chorda tympani, or the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the lingual nerve. It is a branch of the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. The chorda tympani joins it and adds the fibers of taste.

Question 6423. Which statement about the lingual nerve is correct: it is a branch of the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve, it carries motor fibers to the muscles of the tongue, it carries taste fibers that it borrows from the chorda tympani, or it supplies the skin over the chin? The answer is that it carries taste fibers borrowed from the chorda tympani. The nerve belongs to the mandibular division and carries general sensation.

Question 6424. A constructed illustration: after a meal, a person feels a hard swelling under the jaw that grows larger as the mouth waters, and the swelling lies along the floor of the mouth beside the tongue. Which structure is most likely blocked? The answer is the duct of the submandibular gland, most often by a small stone. The gland lies below the jaw, and its duct opens beside the frenulum.

Question 6425. Which of these pairs belongs together: the parotid duct with the floor of the mouth, the submandibular duct with the cheek opposite the upper second molar, the sublingual ducts with the floor of the mouth, or the parotid duct with the lower incisors? The answer is the sublingual ducts with the floor of the mouth. The parotid duct opens in the cheek, and the submandibular duct opens beside the frenulum.

Question 6426. Which duct opens on a small papilla on the inside of the cheek, opposite the crown of the upper second molar? The answer is the parotid duct. It leaves the front of the parotid gland, crosses the masseter muscle, and turns inward through the buccinator. Its opening is a small raised papilla that can be felt with the tip of the tongue.

Question 6427. Where does the duct of the submandibular gland open: on the sublingual papilla beside the frenulum of the tongue, on the uvula, on the soft palate, or opposite the upper first molar? The answer is on the sublingual papilla beside the frenulum of the tongue. It runs forward under the mucous membrane of the floor of the mouth. The ducts of the sublingual gland open along the same fold.

Question 6428. Which muscle does the submandibular gland hook around as it passes from the neck into the floor of the mouth? The answer is the mylohyoid. The gland has a larger part below the muscle and a smaller part above it. The deep part lies in the floor of the mouth beside the tongue, close to the duct of the gland.

Question 6429. Which salivary gland lies in the floor of the mouth above the mylohyoid muscle and raises a fold of mucous membrane on either side of the frenulum? The answer is the sublingual gland. It is the smallest of the three pairs of major salivary glands. Its many small ducts open along the ridge of mucosa that it raises.

Question 6430. A described dissection of the floor of the mouth shows a raised ridge of mucous membrane running backward and outward from the frenulum of the tongue, with several tiny openings along its crest. Which structure is described? The answer is the sublingual fold, raised by the sublingual gland beneath it, with the small ducts of that gland opening along the ridge.

Question 6431. True or false: the parotid gland produces a watery secretion rich in enzymes and containing very little mucus. The answer is true. The parotid is a serous gland, and its secretion is thin and rich in enzymes. The sublingual gland, by contrast, is a mucous gland, and the submandibular gland produces a mixture of the two.

Question 6432. Which statement about the major salivary glands is correct: the parotid gland is a mucous gland, the sublingual gland is mainly serous, the submandibular gland is mixed but mostly serous, or all three glands are purely serous? The answer is that the submandibular gland is mixed but mostly serous. The parotid gland is serous, and the sublingual gland is mainly mucous.

Question 6433. A constructed illustration: a person has a tender swelling in front of the ear that lifts the earlobe and fills the hollow behind the jaw, while the mouth stays moist. Which gland is involved? The answer is the parotid gland. The swelling fills the space between the ramus of the mandible and the mastoid process. The opening of its duct in the cheek stays clear.

Question 6434. Which muscle forms the bulk of the tongue and fans out from the front of the mandible into the substance of the tongue? The answer is the genioglossus. The two muscles together protrude the tongue. If one of them acts alone, it pushes the tip of the tongue toward the opposite side.

Question 6435. Which muscle of the tongue is not supplied by the hypoglossal nerve: the genioglossus, the styloglossus, the hyoglossus, or the palatoglossus? The answer is the palatoglossus. It is supplied through the pharyngeal plexus and belongs to the palate as much as to the tongue. The hypoglossal nerve supplies all the other muscles of the tongue.

Question 6436. The lingual artery passes deep to which muscle on its way to the tongue, and which structures lie superficial to that muscle? The answer is that the artery passes deep to the hyoglossus, while the lingual nerve and the duct of the submandibular gland lie superficial to it. The artery therefore runs on the medial side of the muscle, hidden from the floor of the mouth.

Question 6437. Which statement about the blood supply of the tongue is correct: the dorsal lingual branches supply the tip, the deep lingual branch supplies the back, the dorsal lingual branches supply the back while the deep lingual branch reaches the tip, or the tongue is supplied only by the facial artery? The answer is that the dorsal lingual branches supply the back while the deep lingual branch reaches the tip.

Question 6438. In the mouth, does the tip of the tongue lie anterior or posterior to the sulcus terminalis, and which papillae lie on each side of that line? The answer is that the tip lies anterior to the line, along with the filiform and fungiform papillae. The circumvallate papillae lie in a row just behind the line, on the root of the tongue.

Question 6439. Which vessels drain the under surface of the tongue and lie just beneath its mucous membrane, where they may bleed briskly if they are cut: the ranine veins, the facial vein, the retromandibular vein, or the external jugular vein? The answer is the ranine veins, which accompany the deep lingual arteries. They drain into the lingual veins. Their superficial position is why a cut under the tongue bleeds so freely.

Question 6440. Which muscle of the tongue pulls its root upward and backward, retracting the tongue after it has been protruded? The answer is the styloglossus. It runs from the styloid process forward and downward into the side of the tongue. It works with the hyoglossus, which pulls the side of the tongue downward and backward.

Question 6441. A constructed illustration: a child cannot lift the tip of the tongue to the roof of the mouth, and the fold of tissue beneath the tongue is short and tight. Which structure is described? The answer is a short lingual frenulum, the condition often called tongue-tie. The frenulum ties the under surface of the tongue to the floor of the mouth.

Question 6442. True or false: taste from the back of the tongue is carried by the same nerve that supplies the muscles of the tongue. The answer is false. Taste from the back of the tongue travels with the glossopharyngeal nerve, while the muscles of the tongue are supplied by the hypoglossal nerve. The two nerves are entirely separate.

Question 6443. Which of these pairs belongs together: the anterior two thirds of the tongue with taste through the chorda tympani, the posterior third with taste through the chorda tympani, the anterior two thirds with taste through the hypoglossal nerve, or the posterior third with taste through the lingual nerve? The answer is the anterior two thirds with taste through the chorda tympani. The posterior third takes its taste from the glossopharyngeal nerve.

Question 6444. Which small pit on the upper surface of the tongue marks the point at which the thyroid gland began its descent in the embryo? The answer is the foramen cecum. It lies at the apex of the sulcus terminalis, in the midline. The duct that ran down from it usually disappears, but a remnant may persist along its path in the neck.

Question 6445. A described midline swelling of the neck moves upward when the person swallows and lies just below the hyoid bone. Which embryonic structure does it follow, and where does that structure begin? The answer is a persistent thyroglossal duct, which begins at the foramen cecum of the tongue. Because the duct is tied to the tongue, the swelling rises when the tongue moves.

Question 6446. Which muscle forms the muscular floor of the mouth, and which structures lie above it in that floor? The answer is the mylohyoid, with the sublingual gland, the duct of the submandibular gland, the lingual nerve, and the tongue above it. The muscle also helps raise the hyoid bone during the first stage of swallowing.

Question 6447. From which nerve does the submandibular ganglion hang, and which glands does it supply: the lingual nerve, supplying the submandibular and sublingual glands; the hypoglossal nerve, supplying the tongue; the glossopharyngeal nerve, supplying the parotid gland; or the buccal nerve, supplying the cheek? The answer is the lingual nerve, supplying the submandibular and sublingual glands. Its fibers come from the chorda tympani.

Question 6448. Which nerves carry the fibers that make the salivary glands secrete: fibers of the trigeminal nerve itself, fibers traveling with the facial and glossopharyngeal nerves, fibers of the hypoglossal nerve, or fibers of the vagus nerve? The answer is fibers traveling with the facial and glossopharyngeal nerves. The facial fibers reach the submandibular and sublingual glands, and the glossopharyngeal fibers reach the parotid gland.

Question 6449. A described cross section of the tongue shows bundles of muscle running in three directions, some lengthwise, some from above downward, and some across the organ. Which group of muscles is shown, and what do these muscles do? The answer is the intrinsic muscles of the tongue, which change its shape rather than its position.

Question 6450. A constructed illustration: an infection of the floor of the mouth spreads as a firm swelling on both sides under the jaw and lifts the tongue. Which space is involved, and which structures lie in it? The answer is the submandibular space, which contains the submandibular gland, its duct, and the lingual and hypoglossal nerves.

Question 6451. Which muscle forms the wall of the cheek and presses food between the teeth? The answer is the buccinator. Its fibers run forward from the pterygomandibular raphe to the angle of the mouth. It is pierced by the parotid duct and is supplied by the buccal branch of the facial nerve.

Question 6452. Which muscle surrounds the mouth and closes the lips: the buccinator, the orbicularis oris, the mentalis, or the risorius? The answer is the orbicularis oris. Its fibers circle the opening of the mouth and act as a sphincter. The buccinator lies further back in the cheek and presses the cheek against the teeth.

Question 6453. The buccal nerve, which carries general sensation from the inside of the cheek, is a branch of which division of the trigeminal nerve, and between which two muscles does it pass? The answer is the mandibular division, and it passes between the two heads of the lateral pterygoid muscle on its way down to the cheek.

Question 6454. Which nerve supplies general sensation from the skin of the lower lip and the chin, and through which opening does it reach the face: the mental nerve through the mental foramen, the infraorbital nerve through the infraorbital foramen, the buccal nerve, or the auriculotemporal nerve? The answer is the mental nerve through the mental foramen. It is the terminal branch of the inferior alveolar nerve.

Question 6455. A described dissection shows the lower lip turned outward, with a midline fold of mucous membrane running from the lip to the gum and a shallow gutter on either side of it. Which region is shown? The answer is the oral vestibule, with the labial frenulum in the midline and the reflection of the mucosa at the sides.

Question 6456. The body of the tongue develops from which structure, and the root from which other structure: the tuberculum impar and the hypobranchial eminence, the hypobranchial eminence and the tuberculum impar, both from the first arch, or both from the second arch? The answer is the body from the tuberculum impar and the root from the hypobranchial eminence. That is why the two parts have different nerve supplies.

Question 6457. What name is given to the raised transverse ridges on the front of the hard palate, behind the incisive papilla? The answer is the palatine rugae. They help hold food in place against the tongue. They are covered by mucous membrane that is bound firmly to the bone beneath, so they do not move.

Question 6458. A described view of the roof of the mouth shows a small rounded elevation just behind the upper central incisor teeth, sitting in the midline at the front. Which structure is shown? The answer is the incisive papilla, which covers the lower opening of the incisive canal, where the nasopalatine nerves pass forward.

Question 6459. The greater palatine nerve and vessels pass through which opening to reach the hard palate, and in which direction do they run across it? The answer is the greater palatine foramen, at the back of the hard palate between the maxilla and the palatine bone, and they run forward and medially toward the incisor teeth.

Question 6460. A described specimen shows the hard palate from below, with a large opening at the back on each side and a smaller opening just behind it. Which structures pass through these two openings? The answer is the greater palatine nerve and vessels through the larger opening and the lesser palatine nerves through the smaller one behind it.

Question 6461. A constructed illustration: a needle placed into the back of the hard palate beside the last molar strikes a nerve that runs forward across the palate and causes sharp pain. Which nerve is struck? The answer is the greater palatine nerve. It has already left its foramen and lies on the bone just under the mucous membrane.

Question 6462. Which nerve supplies the mucous membrane of the hard palate just behind the upper incisor teeth: the greater palatine nerve, the nasopalatine nerve, the lesser palatine nerve, or the lingual nerve? The answer is the nasopalatine nerve. It reaches the palate through the incisive canal. The greater palatine nerve supplies the rest of the hard palate behind that small area.

Question 6463. A constructed illustration: after a described injury to the soft palate, liquid escapes through the nose when the person swallows. Which muscles have failed, and what is their normal action together? The answer is the levator veli palatini and the tensor veli palatini. They lift and tense the soft palate so that it seals the nasopharynx during swallowing.

Question 6464. In the gag reflex, which nerve carries sensation from the back of the throat, and which nerve makes the pharynx contract: the glossopharyngeal nerve and the vagus nerve, the vagus nerve alone, the hypoglossal nerve and the trigeminal nerve, or the facial nerve and the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the glossopharyngeal nerve for sensation and the vagus nerve for the movement. Both must be intact for the reflex to work.

Question 6465. Which of these pairs belongs together: the levator veli palatini with raising the soft palate, the tensor veli palatini with shortening the uvula, the musculus uvulae with opening the auditory tube, or the palatopharyngeus with protruding the tongue? The answer is the levator veli palatini with raising the soft palate. The tensor opens the auditory tube, and the musculus uvulae shortens the uvula.

Question 6466. Which slender muscle of the soft palate runs from the auditory tube down into the wall of the pharynx, and what does it do? The answer is the salpingopharyngeus, which raises the pharynx during swallowing and helps open the auditory tube. It lies in the fold of mucous membrane below the opening of the tube.

Question 6467. A described view of the under surface of the tongue shows two deep veins running forward on either side of the midline, with a fringed fold of mucous membrane lying lateral to each of them. Which structures are shown? The answer is the ranine veins, running under the tongue beside the fringed folds, with the deep lingual arteries close to them.

Question 6468. Lymph from the tip of the tongue drains first to which group of nodes, and lymph from the posterior third to which other group? The answer is the submental nodes for the tip and the deep cervical nodes for the posterior third. The middle of the tongue drains to the submandibular nodes and the jugulo-omohyoid nodes.

Question 6469. Which statement about lymphatic drainage of the tongue is correct: the tip drains to the deep cervical nodes, the middle part drains only to the submental nodes, the tip drains to the submental nodes and the posterior third to the deep cervical nodes, or each half drains to the opposite side? The answer is that the tip drains to the submental nodes and the posterior third to the deep cervical nodes.

Question 6470. A constructed illustration: a growth on the side of the tongue near its middle has spread, and firm nodes are felt under the jaw and deep in the neck. Which groups of nodes drain that part of the tongue? The answer is the submandibular nodes and the jugulo-omohyoid nodes of the deep cervical chain.

Question 6471. A described specimen shows folds of mucous membrane on the side of the tongue, just in front of the palatoglossal arch, with taste buds buried in their walls. Which structures are shown, and which nerves carry their taste? The answer is the foliate papillae, whose taste fibers travel with the chorda tympani and the glossopharyngeal nerve.

Question 6472. True or false: the oral cavity proper is bounded above by the hard palate and below by the tongue and the floor of the mouth. The answer is true. In front and at the sides it is bounded by the alveolar arches and their teeth. Behind, it opens into the oropharynx at the isthmus of the fauces.

Question 6473. Name the two bones that form the hard palate. The answer is the maxilla and the palatine bone. The palatine process of the maxilla forms the front and larger part of it, and the horizontal plate of the palatine bone forms the back part. The two meet at a suture that runs across the palate.

Question 6474. Which statement about the sublingual papilla is correct: the duct of the submandibular gland opens on it, the parotid duct opens on it, it lies on the hard palate, or it carries the chorda tympani? The answer is that the duct of the submandibular gland opens on it. The papilla lies beside the frenulum of the tongue in the floor of the mouth.

Question 6475. Which structures lie in the pterygomandibular space: the buccinator and the parotid duct, the inferior alveolar and lingual nerves with their vessels, the sublingual gland and the ranine veins, or the palatine tonsil and its bed? The answer is the inferior alveolar and lingual nerves with their vessels. That space lies between the medial pterygoid muscle and the ramus of the mandible.

Question 6476. Which hard white substance forms the outer covering of the crown of a tooth and is the hardest tissue in the body? The answer is enamel. It covers the crown only, and it contains no living cells. Once it is worn away or broken, it cannot grow back.

Question 6477. Which of these is the hardest substance in the human body: bone, dentin, enamel, or cementum? The answer is enamel. It is almost entirely mineral and covers the crown of the tooth. Dentin lies beneath it and is softer, and cementum covers the root instead.

Question 6478. A described dental radiograph shows the crowns of the upper back teeth in a row, with their roots outlined against bone and two large air-filled spaces lying above them. Which region is shown, and which structure lies above the roots? The answer is the upper posterior teeth, with the maxillary sinus above the roots of the molars and premolars.

Question 6479. Which tissue forms the bulk of a tooth, lying beneath the enamel in the crown and beneath the cementum in the root? The answer is dentin. It is softer than enamel but harder than bone. It surrounds the pulp cavity and is crossed by fine tubules that run outward from the pulp.

Question 6480. What lies within the cavity at the center of a tooth, and through which opening does it communicate with the tissues around the root? The answer is the pulp, which contains vessels and nerves, and it communicates through the apical foramen at the tip of the root. The pulp keeps the tooth alive.

Question 6481. Which tissue covers the surface of the root of a tooth and meets the enamel at the cementoenamel junction: dentin, cementum, enamel, or the periodontal ligament? The answer is cementum. It is a thin, bone-like layer that anchors the fibers of the periodontal ligament. Enamel stops at the neck of the tooth.

Question 6482. A constructed illustration: a small cavity in a molar has reached the tissue beneath the enamel, and the person feels pain with sweet food but not at rest. Which tissue has been lost, and which tissue is now exposed? The answer is that enamel has been lost and dentin is exposed. Dentin conducts the stimulus toward the pulp.

Question 6483. Which structure is the fibrous membrane that surrounds the root of a tooth and joins it to the wall of its socket? The answer is the periodontal ligament. It is a layer of collagen fibers between the cementum and the alveolar bone. It cushions the tooth under the force of chewing.

Question 6484. Which set lists the kinds of teeth in order from the front of the mouth to the back: incisors, canines, premolars, molars; incisors, premolars, canines, molars; molars, premolars, canines, incisors; or canines, incisors, molars, premolars? The answer is incisors, canines, premolars, molars. Each half of each jaw carries two incisors and one canine.

Question 6485. True or false: the deciduous teeth number twenty in all, five in each half of each jaw. The answer is true. Each half holds two incisors, one canine, and two molars. No premolars appear in the deciduous set, because the premolars belong to the permanent dentition.

Question 6486. How many teeth are there in a complete permanent set: twenty-eight in four quadrants of seven, thirty-two in four quadrants of eight, thirty in two arches of fifteen, or twenty-four in four quadrants of six? The answer is thirty-two in four quadrants of eight. Each quadrant holds two incisors, one canine, two premolars, and three molars.

Question 6487. Which teeth have flat, chisel-shaped crowns and a single root, and are used for cutting food? The answer is the incisors. Each jaw carries four of them, two on either side of the midline. Their crowns meet edge to edge when the teeth are brought together.

Question 6488. Which tooth has a pointed crown and a single long root, and is used for tearing food? The answer is the canine, which is also called the cuspid. There is one in each half of each jaw. It stands at the angle of the dental arch, between the incisors and the premolars.

Question 6489. Which permanent teeth replace the deciduous molars: the premolars, the permanent molars, the canines, or the incisors? The answer is the premolars. Each deciduous molar is replaced by a premolar beneath it. The permanent molars have no deciduous predecessors and erupt behind the deciduous teeth.

Question 6490. A described radiograph of the whole mouth shows a mixed set of teeth: some are small with short roots, and others are larger and newly erupted. Which stage is shown, and what are the small teeth? The answer is the mixed dentition of a child, and the small teeth are the deciduous set.

Question 6491. Which structure lies immediately above the roots of the upper molars and premolars, and why does that relation matter? The answer is the maxillary sinus, whose floor lies close to those roots. An infection around a molar root can therefore spread to the sinus, because the root may lie just beneath its lining.

Question 6492. How many roots does an upper first molar usually have: one, two, three, or four? The answer is three. Two of them lie on the buccal side and one on the palatal side. The lower molars usually have two roots instead, and variation in root number is common and normal.

Question 6493. A constructed illustration: a young adult has pain and swelling at the back of the lower jaw, and a described radiograph shows a molar lying at an angle against the tooth in front of it. Which tooth is described? The answer is the third molar, the wisdom tooth, which has failed to erupt in line.

Question 6494. Which nerve supplies the lower teeth and the skin over the chin: the lingual nerve, the inferior alveolar nerve, the buccal nerve, or the maxillary nerve? The answer is the inferior alveolar nerve. It runs in the mandibular canal and ends as the mental nerve. It belongs to the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve.

Question 6495. Which nerves supply the upper teeth: the anterior, middle, and posterior superior alveolar nerves; the inferior alveolar nerve; the buccal nerve alone; or the lingual nerve? The answer is the anterior, middle, and posterior superior alveolar nerves. They arise from the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve. The posterior nerve supplies the molars.

Question 6496. A described specimen shows a tooth cut in half lengthwise, with a white cap over a yellowish body, a narrow channel running down the root, and a thin layer over the root itself. Name the tissues and the channel. The answer is enamel over the crown, dentin forming the body, cementum over the root, and the pulp canal within.

Question 6497. Which of these pairs belongs together: the upper first molar with three roots, the lower first molar with three roots, the upper first premolar with a single root always, or the canine with two roots? The answer is the upper first molar with three roots. The lower molars usually have two roots, and the upper first premolar often has two.

Question 6498. Name the tissues that make up a tooth. The answer is enamel, dentin, cementum, and the pulp. Enamel covers the crown, cementum covers the root, and dentin lies beneath both of them. The pulp lies in the center of the tooth and keeps it alive.

Question 6499. In the permanent dentition, is the upper first molar anterior or posterior to the upper second molar, and which tooth lies immediately in front of the first molar? The answer is that the first molar lies anterior to the second molar. The second premolar lies immediately in front of it, and the third molar lies behind the second.

Question 6500. A described section of the jaw of a child shows a row of small teeth already in the mouth and, beneath them, larger crowns developing inside bony crypts, with one tooth lying behind the last small tooth. Which tooth is that, and how does it erupt? The answer is the first permanent molar, which erupts behind the deciduous teeth without replacing any of them.

To summarize, the oral cavity is divided into the vestibule in front and the cavity proper behind, separated by the alveolar arches, and it is roofed by the hard and soft palate and floored by the tongue and the mylohyoid. The soft palate is lifted and tensed by the levator veli palatini and the tensor veli palatini, and every muscle of the palate is supplied through the pharyngeal plexus except the tensor, which takes the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. Taste from the anterior two thirds of the tongue travels with the chorda tympani, taste and sensation from the posterior third travel with the glossopharyngeal nerve, and the muscles of the tongue take the hypoglossal nerve. The three pairs of salivary glands empty through the parotid duct opposite the upper second molar, the submandibular duct beside the frenulum of the tongue, and the sublingual ducts along the sublingual fold. Each tooth has enamel over the crown, dentin beneath it, cementum over the root, and pulp within, and the permanent teeth number thirty-two while the deciduous teeth number twenty.
