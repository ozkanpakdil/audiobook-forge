# Chapter 78 — Female Reproductive Anatomy

This chapter follows the internal organs of the female reproductive tract, from the ovaries on the lateral wall of the pelvis, along the uterine tubes, to the uterus and the lining that is shed each month. It covers the ovaries and their follicles, the four parts of the uterine tube, the parts, supports and relations of the uterus, and the two layers of the endometrium with the cycle that renews them. It holds one hundred questions, and every one gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right. Each item is written to be heard rather than seen. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7701. Which paired organ lies in the ovarian fossa against the lateral wall of the pelvis, holds the follicles, and releases an oocyte at ovulation? The answer is the ovary. The two ovaries flank the uterus below the pelvic brim. Each is held by a fold of peritoneum and by two ligaments.

Question 7702. What is the name of the fold of peritoneum that suspends the ovary from the broad ligament and carries vessels and nerves to its hilum? The answer is the mesovarium. It is the part of the broad ligament that reaches the ovary. The ovarian vessels and nerves travel along it.

Question 7703. Which of these ligaments carries the ovarian artery and vein from the pelvic wall to the ovary: the ovarian ligament, the round ligament of the uterus, the suspensory ligament of the ovary, or the uterosacral ligament? The answer is the suspensory ligament of the ovary. It runs from the ovary to the pelvic wall and carries the ovarian vessels. The ovarian ligament joins the ovary to the uterus and carries no large vessel.

Question 7704. A described preparation of the surface of an ovary shows a single layer of cuboidal cells on a dense white fibrous coat. Which two layers are described? The answer is the germinal epithelium over the tunica albuginea. The epithelium covers the free surface, and the dense coat lies beneath it. The follicles lie deeper, in the cortex.

Question 7705. Which artery reaches the ovary directly from the abdominal aorta rather than from a branch within the pelvis? The answer is the ovarian artery. It arises below the renal arteries and descends across the pelvic brim. In the broad ligament it meets the ovarian branch of the uterine artery.

Question 7706. A described longitudinal section of an ovary shows an outer zone crowded with small rounded structures at every stage of growth and an inner zone of loose tissue with many vessels. Which zones are shown? The answer is the cortex outside and the medulla within. The follicles grow in the cortex, near the surface, and the vessels enter at the hilum.

Question 7707. A described bisected ovary shows a thin rim of tiny follicles and one large thin-walled cavity bulging from the surface. What is that bulging structure? The answer is a mature vesicular follicle, also called a Graafian follicle. It has grown a large fluid-filled antrum and is about to rupture. The small follicles are at earlier stages.

Question 7708. True or false: the ovary is covered by peritoneum on every surface. The answer is false. The peritoneum reaches the ovary only where the mesovarium attaches, and the free surface is covered by germinal epithelium. The ovary is described as intraperitoneal, yet it is not wrapped by peritoneum.

Question 7709. The ovary rests in a shallow depression on the lateral wall of the pelvis. Which structure forms its posterior boundary? The answer is the ureter, with the internal iliac artery beside it. The front of the fossa is bounded by the obliterated umbilical artery, and its floor lies over the obturator internus muscle.

Question 7710. Name, as a list of two, the ligament that holds the ovary to the pelvic wall above and the ligament that ties it to the uterus below. The answer is the suspensory ligament of the ovary and the ovarian ligament. The first carries the ovarian vessels to the organ. The second is a short fibrous cord running to the side of the uterus.

Question 7711. Into which vein does the left ovarian vein usually drain? The answer is the left renal vein. It ascends and joins the left renal vein rather than the inferior vena cava. On the right the ovarian vein joins the inferior vena cava directly.

Question 7712. The right ovarian vein drains into which vessel: the right renal vein, the inferior vena cava, the internal iliac vein, or the portal vein? The answer is the inferior vena cava. The right vein ascends straight to it. Only the vein on the left crosses to the left renal vein.

Question 7713. The ovarian artery arises from the abdominal aorta at about which level, spoken as a vertebra: the tenth thoracic, the first lumbar, the second lumbar, or the fourth lumbar? The answer is the second lumbar vertebra. It leaves the aorta below the renal arteries and descends to the pelvis. The fourth lumbar level lies nearer the aortic bifurcation.

Question 7714. A described section of an ovary shows a row of follicles of increasing size, from a small cell wrapped by a single layer to a large cavity with a rim of cells. What does this sequence show? The answer is the growth of the ovarian follicle through its stages. A group of follicles begins to grow in each cycle, and usually only one reaches maturity.

Question 7715. Which cord of fibrous tissue joins the medial pole of the ovary to the lateral angle of the uterus within the broad ligament? The answer is the ovarian ligament. It runs between the two organs and is the upper part of the gubernaculum. The round ligament lies in front of it and travels toward the groin.

Question 7716. Which of these pairs belongs together: the suspensory ligament with the ovarian vessels, the ovarian ligament with the labium majus, the round ligament with the pelvic wall, or the mesovarium with the uterine cavity? The answer is the suspensory ligament with the ovarian vessels. The ovarian ligament joins the ovary to the uterus, and the round ligament ends in the labium majus.

Question 7717. Which remnant of the mesonephric duct may persist in the mesovarium beside the ovary and the uterine tube as a short blind tubule? The answer is the epoophoron. It lies between the ovary and the tube. A smaller remnant of the mesonephric tubules, the paroophoron, lies near it.

Question 7718. Lymph from the ovary travels with the ovarian vessels to which nodes: the inguinal nodes, the internal iliac nodes, the para-aortic nodes, or the sacral nodes? The answer is the para-aortic nodes, beside the aorta where the ovarian artery arises. The nodes follow the vessels that drain the organ. The inguinal nodes receive lymph from the round ligament and the labium majus.

Question 7719. Which vessel runs along the anterior boundary of the ovarian fossa and is known in the adult as the medial umbilical ligament? The answer is the obliterated umbilical artery. It runs forward along the pelvic wall to the front of the bladder. The ovary lies behind it, within the fossa.

Question 7720. A constructed illustration: a woman notices a dull ache deep around the navel at the middle of each cycle. Which organ is the likely source, and why is the pain felt so far from the pelvis? The answer is the ovary, whose nerves reach the tenth thoracic segment of the spinal cord. Pain from that segment is felt around the umbilicus, though the ovary lies far below.

Question 7721. A described specimen of an ovary contains a cyst filled with thick, dark, old blood, and its surface is bound by adhesions. What is that cyst? The answer is an endometrioma, a cyst of endometrium growing within the ovary. Its dark contents have given it the name chocolate cyst. Such implants outside the uterus are called endometriosis.

Question 7722. Which two arteries meet in the broad ligament so that either can carry blood to both the ovary and the uterus: the ovarian and the internal pudendal, the ovarian and the uterine, the uterine and the obturator, or the ovarian and the inferior epigastric? The answer is the ovarian and the uterine arteries, which anastomose along the broad ligament. Neither organ loses all its supply when one vessel is closed.

Question 7723. Which of these pairs belongs together: the gubernaculum with the ovarian and round ligaments, the mesonephric duct with the ovarian follicle, the paramesonephric duct with the ovarian artery, or the coelomic epithelium with the uterine cavity? The answer is the gubernaculum with the ovarian and round ligaments. Its upper part becomes the ovarian ligament and its lower part the round ligament.

Question 7724. Which structure forms in the ovary after the follicle ruptures, secretes progesterone for a time, and later shrinks to a pale scar? The answer is the corpus luteum, whose scar is the corpus albicans. It grows from the wall of the ruptured follicle and lasts about two weeks if no pregnancy begins.

Question 7725. A constructed illustration: a young woman has sudden severe pain in the lower abdomen, and examination suggests that the ovary has twisted on its supporting fold. Which fold carries the vessels that are compressed? The answer is the suspensory ligament of the ovary, with the mesovarium. The ovarian vessels run in that fold, and the twist stops the blood supply.

Question 7726. Where does the ovary lie in relation to the uterine tube: above the tube, below and behind it, in front of it, or medial to it? The answer is below and behind the tube. The tube arches over the upper pole of the ovary, and its fimbriae reach toward it. The ovary rests against the lateral wall of the pelvis.

Question 7727. The two ovarian veins drain differently, the left into the left renal vein and the right into the inferior vena cava. Which explanation fits best: the left renal vein keeps its connection with the developing ovarian vein, the left ovary lies higher, the right ovarian vein is longer, or the left vein arises elsewhere? The answer is that the left renal vein keeps its connection. The right vein drains into the inferior vena cava.

Question 7728. A constructed illustration: a growth in an ovary spreads to nodes beside the aorta rather than to nodes in the groin. Why is that? The answer is that the lymphatics of the ovary follow the ovarian vessels upward to the para-aortic nodes. Only the round ligament and the labium majus drain to the inguinal nodes. The nodes follow the path of the vessels.

Question 7729. A described operation frees the ovary from the pelvic wall. Which fold is divided to take the ovarian vessels: the ovarian ligament, the mesovarium, the suspensory ligament of the ovary, or the round ligament? The answer is the suspensory ligament of the ovary, which carries the ovarian artery, vein and lymphatics. The ovarian ligament is a fibrous cord without large vessels.

Question 7730. The germ cells that will become oocytes reach the developing ovary from which site: the coelomic epithelium of the gonadal ridge, the wall of the yolk sac, the mesonephric duct, or the paramesonephric duct? The answer is the wall of the yolk sac. They migrate along the dorsal mesentery of the hindgut to the gonadal ridge, where the ovary forms. The supporting cells come from the ridge itself.

Question 7731. Which paired duct, about 10 centimeters long, lies in the free upper margin of the broad ligament and carries the oocyte toward the uterus? The answer is the uterine tube, also called the fallopian tube. It opens into the peritoneal cavity at its lateral end and into the uterine cavity at its medial end.

Question 7732. What name is given to the finger-like processes at the lateral end of the uterine tube, one of which is attached to the ovary? The answer is the fimbriae. They fringe the funnel-shaped opening and sweep the oocyte into it. The longest, the ovarian fimbria, lies against the ovary itself.

Question 7733. Which part of the uterine tube is the widest and the longest, and is the usual site of fertilization: the infundibulum, the ampulla, the isthmus, or the part within the uterine wall? The answer is the ampulla. It forms about the lateral half of the tube and has a thin wall.

Question 7734. A described specimen shows the lateral end of a uterine tube as a funnel opening into the abdominal cavity, fringed by many slender processes. Which part is shown? The answer is the infundibulum with its fimbriae. It lies next to the ovary but is not fused to it. The abdominal ostium lies at the center of the funnel.

Question 7735. Which part of the uterine tube is buried within the muscular wall of the uterus and opens into the uterine cavity? The answer is the intramural part, also called the interstitial part. It is the shortest and narrowest segment of the tube, and it opens at the upper angle of the cavity.

Question 7736. Which part of the tube is the narrow, thick-walled segment about one third of its length, joining the ampulla to the wall of the uterus: the infundibulum, the ampulla, the isthmus, or the fimbriae? The answer is the isthmus. Its muscle is well developed and its lumen is narrow. The junction with the ampulla holds the oocyte for a time.

Question 7737. The uterine tube lies in the free upper edge of the broad ligament. What is the name of the peritoneal fold that connects the tube to the ovary below it? The answer is the mesosalpinx, the part of the broad ligament that suspends the tube. The mesovarium below it reaches the ovary.

Question 7738. In which part of the uterine tube does fertilization normally take place: the intramural part, the isthmus, the ampulla, or the infundibulum? The answer is the ampulla. The oocyte is usually met there, close to the ovary but already inside the tube. An egg that fails to reach the uterus may implant in the tube.

Question 7739. Which of these pairs belongs together: the infundibulum with the fimbriae, the ampulla with the muscular wall of the uterus, the isthmus with the funnel opening, or the intramural part with the widest lumen? The answer is the infundibulum with the fimbriae. The ampulla is the widest part, the isthmus narrow and thick walled, and the intramural part lies inside the uterus.

Question 7740. A described section of the wall of a uterine tube shows a mucosa thrown into branching folds and lined by tall ciliated cells mixed with cells that bear no cilia. What does that lining do? The answer is that the cilia beat toward the uterus and carry the oocyte inward. The folds slow the current. The muscle of the wall adds waves of contraction.

Question 7741. What is the name of the opening by which the uterine tube communicates with the peritoneal cavity? The answer is the abdominal ostium. It lies at the center of the infundibulum, surrounded by the fimbriae. Through it the peritoneal cavity and the exterior communicate.

Question 7742. Which fimbria of the uterine tube is the longest and lies in contact with the ovary? The answer is the ovarian fimbria. It runs along the surface of the ovary near its lateral pole. The other fimbriae fringe the rest of the funnel.

Question 7743. True or false: in the female the peritoneal cavity is not completely closed, because it opens to the exterior through the uterine tube, the uterus and the vagina. The answer is true. The abdominal ostium of the tube opens into the peritoneal cavity. That pathway is one reason an infection of the vagina can spread upward to the peritoneum.

Question 7744. In which direction do the cilia of the uterine tube beat, and where is the oocyte delayed: toward the uterus, with a delay at the ampullary-isthmus junction, toward the ovary, with a delay at the fimbriae, toward the uterus, with a delay at the infundibulum, or in both directions equally? The answer is toward the uterus, with a delay at the ampullary-isthmus junction. The delay lets the oocyte mature and the lining prepare.

Question 7745. A constructed illustration: an infection spreads along the lining of a uterine tube, and the fimbriae become stuck to the ovary and the pelvic wall. Which structure has been damaged? The answer is the uterine tube, in a condition described as salpingitis. Adhesions around the abdominal ostium keep the oocyte from entering. The tube may then be closed to the egg.

Question 7746. A described specimen shows a small stalked cystic nodule hanging from one of the fimbriae at the lateral end of a uterine tube. Which structure is it? The answer is the hydatid of Morgagni, a remnant of the paramesonephric duct. It is a normal finding and usually has no effect. The epoophoron, a remnant of the mesonephric duct, lies in the mesovarium instead.

Question 7747. Which two vessels supply the uterine tube and anastomose along its length: the uterine artery and the middle rectal, the ovarian artery and the obturator, the uterine artery and the ovarian artery, or the internal pudendal and the inferior epigastric? The answer is the uterine artery and the ovarian artery. A tubal branch of each reaches the tube from either end. The same pair supplies the ovary and the uterus.

Question 7748. A constructed illustration: a woman misses a period and then has pain and bleeding, and the tube on one side is swollen and leaking. Why does a fertilized egg so often stop in that part of the tube? The answer is that the ampulla is the usual site of fertilization, while the isthmus beyond it is narrow and muscular. The egg may fail to pass that junction and implant where it lies.

Question 7749. Trace the oocyte from the point at which it leaves the ovary to the cavity of the uterus. Which parts of the uterine tube does it pass, in order? The answer is the fimbriae and the infundibulum, then the ampulla, then the isthmus, and finally the intramural part. It enters at the abdominal ostium and leaves at the uterine opening.

Question 7750. The uterine tube develops from which duct: the mesonephric duct, the paramesonephric duct, the vitelline duct, or the urogenital sinus? The answer is the paramesonephric duct. Its upper, unfused part becomes the tube, and its cranial end opens into the peritoneal cavity. The lower parts of the two ducts fuse to form the uterus.

Question 7751. Which part of the uterus lies above the openings of the two uterine tubes and forms the rounded upper end? The answer is the fundus. It is the dome of the uterus, covered by peritoneum. The body lies below it, between the tubes and the cervix.

Question 7752. What is the name of the lower, narrow part of the uterus that projects into the vagina and contains the cervical canal? The answer is the cervix, the least mobile part of the uterus. Its lower end opens into the vagina at the external os.

Question 7753. In a woman who has not borne a child, the uterus measures about how much: 3 centimeters long, 5 centimeters wide and 2 centimeters thick, 7.5 centimeters long, 5 centimeters wide and 2.5 centimeters thick, 12 centimeters long, 8 centimeters wide and 4 centimeters thick, or 15 centimeters long, 10 centimeters wide and 5 centimeters thick? The answer is 7.5 centimeters long, 5 centimeters wide and 2.5 centimeters thick. It is smaller before puberty and larger after childbirth.

Question 7754. A described specimen shows a hollow organ shaped like a flattened pear, with a rounded upper end, a wider middle, and a narrow neck below. Which organ is it, and which parts are shown? The answer is the uterus, with its fundus, body and cervix. The uterine tubes open at the upper angles of the body.

Question 7755. The wall of the uterus has three coats. Which list gives them from outside inward: endometrium, myometrium and perimetrium, perimetrium, myometrium and endometrium, myometrium, perimetrium and endometrium, or perimetrium, endometrium and myometrium? The answer is perimetrium, myometrium and endometrium. The perimetrium is the peritoneal covering, the myometrium the muscle, and the endometrium lines the cavity.

Question 7756. Which coat of the uterus is made of interlacing bundles of smooth muscle and forms the greater part of its wall? The answer is the myometrium. Its fibers run in several directions and lie in layers. This muscle is the source of the contractions of labor.

Question 7757. The cavity of the uterus opens into the vagina through which opening: the internal os, the external os, the abdominal ostium, or the isthmus? The answer is the external os. The internal os is the upper opening of the cervical canal, where it meets the cavity of the body. The abdominal ostium belongs to the uterine tube.

Question 7758. True or false: in most women the uterus is anteverted and anteflexed, so that the body leans forward over the bladder. The answer is true. Anteversion is the forward tilt of the cervix on the vagina, and anteflexion the forward bend between body and cervix. A uterus that tilts backward is a common normal variant.

Question 7759. What is the name of the cord that runs from the lateral angle of the uterus, through the inguinal canal, to the labium majus? The answer is the round ligament of the uterus. Like the ovarian ligament, it is a remnant of the gubernaculum, and it helps keep the fundus tipped forward.

Question 7760. Which organ lies immediately in front of the body of the uterus, separated from it by a pouch of peritoneum? The answer is the urinary bladder. The pouch between them is the vesicouterine pouch, and it is shallow. The peritoneum is reflected from the bladder onto the uterus at about the level of the internal os.

Question 7761. A described sagittal section of the female pelvis shows a deep pouch of peritoneum dipping between the uterus and the rectum, behind the cervix. Which pouch is shown? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, also called the pouch of Douglas. It is the lowest point of the peritoneal cavity when a woman lies on her back. The posterior fornix of the vagina lies just below it.

Question 7762. Which vein drains the uterus into the internal iliac vein, running beside the uterine artery: the uterine vein, the ovarian vein, the internal pudendal vein, or the obturator vein? The answer is the uterine vein. It leaves the side of the uterus and joins the internal iliac vein, while the ovarian vein drains the ovary and the upper part of the uterus.

Question 7763. A constructed illustration: after several pregnancies a woman's cervix descends toward the opening of the vagina. Which ligaments, the chief support of the uterus, have become weak? The answer is the transverse cervical ligaments, also called the cardinal ligaments. They run from the cervix to the lateral walls of the pelvis. The uterosacral ligaments and the pelvic floor assist them.

Question 7764. Which of these pairs belongs together: the cardinal ligament with the cervix, the round ligament with the rectum, the uterosacral ligament with the bladder, or the broad ligament with the vagina? The answer is the cardinal ligament with the cervix. The uterosacral ligaments run from the cervix backward to the sacrum, and the round ligaments forward to the labium majus.

Question 7765. What is the name of the thick band of fibrous tissue that runs from the cervix to the lateral wall of the pelvis and is the main support of the uterus? The answer is the transverse cervical ligament, also called the cardinal ligament. The uterine artery and the ureter pass through its base, and its fibers blend with the pelvic fascia.

Question 7766. The uterine artery arises from which vessel: the internal iliac artery, the external iliac artery, the abdominal aorta, or the inferior epigastric artery? The answer is the internal iliac artery, from its anterior division. It runs medially in the base of the broad ligament toward the cervix, then turns upward along the side of the uterus.

Question 7767. Within the broad ligament, where does the round ligament lie in relation to the uterine tube? The answer is in front of the tube and below it. The tube occupies the free upper margin of the broad ligament, while the round ligament runs in the anterior part of the fold.

Question 7768. Lymph from the fundus of the uterus drains chiefly to which nodes: the superficial inguinal nodes, the para-aortic nodes, the internal iliac nodes, or the sacral nodes? The answer is the para-aortic nodes, following the ovarian vessels. Lymph from the body of the uterus goes to the external iliac nodes, and the cervix drains to the iliac, obturator and sacral nodes.

Question 7769. A described coronal section of the uterus shows the cavity as a narrow triangle with its base above and its apex below, opening at each upper corner into a tube. Which parts lie at the base and the apex? The answer is the fundus at the base and the internal os at the apex. The cavity is a slit in the coronal plane, flattened from front to back.

Question 7770. Which ligament runs from the cervix backward on each side of the rectum to the front of the sacrum? The answer is the uterosacral ligament. It helps hold the cervix backward and upward and forms the ridge that bounds the rectouterine pouch on each side.

Question 7771. Name, as a list of two, the ligaments that support the cervix and hold it backward toward the sacrum. The answer is the transverse cervical ligament and the uterosacral ligament. The first holds the cervix to the side wall of the pelvis, the second holds it backward. The levator ani muscle supports the pelvic floor beneath them.

Question 7772. The ureter passes beneath the uterine artery and then runs forward to reach the bladder. Which structure does it lie in front of on the way? The answer is the lateral vaginal fornix, against which it runs before entering the bladder. That is why the ureter is at risk in operations on the cervix and the upper vagina.

Question 7773. Which of the recesses around the cervix is the deepest, lying behind the cervix with the rectouterine pouch immediately above it? The answer is the posterior fornix of the vagina. The anterior and lateral fornices are shallower. The posterior fornix is the route by which the pouch above it can be reached.

Question 7774. A described specimen of a uterus near the end of pregnancy shows the upper part thick and muscular and a thin band above the cervix that has stretched. Which part is that thin band? The answer is the isthmus, now called the lower uterine segment. In the nonpregnant uterus it is the narrow part between the body and the cervix.

Question 7775. A constructed illustration: a growth of the cervix spreads into the tissue beside it, and the kidney on that side stops draining. Which structure has been narrowed where it crosses the pelvis? The answer is the ureter, which passes beneath the uterine artery close to the cervix. It crosses so near that it is easily involved. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7776. Lymph from the cervix drains to which set of nodes: only the superficial inguinal nodes, only the para-aortic nodes, the internal iliac, external iliac, obturator and sacral nodes, or the axillary nodes? The answer is the internal iliac, external iliac, obturator and sacral nodes. The cervix lies low in the pelvis, so its lymph follows the iliac vessels rather than the ovarian vessels.

Question 7777. At which level does the uterine artery reach the side of the uterus, and which structure does it cross: at the fundus, crossing the uterine tube, at the isthmus and cervix, crossing the ureter, at the cervix, crossing the obturator nerve, or at the body, crossing the round ligament? The answer is at the isthmus and cervix, crossing the ureter about 2 centimeters lateral to the cervix.

Question 7778. Which part of the uterus receives parasympathetic fibers from the second to the fourth sacral segments: the fundus, the body, the cervix, or the whole organ equally? The answer is the cervix. Those fibers arrive through the pelvic splanchnic nerves. The body is supplied instead by sympathetic fibers from the tenth thoracic to the first lumbar segments.

Question 7779. A described specimen of a uterus opened from the front shows a spindle-shaped canal in the lower part, with a narrow opening above into the cavity of the body and a small round opening below. Which part has been opened? The answer is the cervix, with the internal os above and the external os below. Its canal is lined by a columnar epithelium that secretes mucus.

Question 7780. True or false: both the uterine tube and the round ligament lie within the broad ligament. The answer is true. The tube lies in its free upper margin, and the round ligament runs in the anterior part of the fold. The ovarian ligament also lies within the broad ligament, behind the tube.

Question 7781. A constructed illustration: a routine examination finds a uterus tilted backward in a woman with no symptoms. What is the explanation? The answer is a retroverted uterus, a normal variant found in about one woman in five. The cervix tilts backward on the vagina and the body lies toward the rectouterine pouch. It is not a disease and usually causes no trouble.

Question 7782. A described uterus has a septum dividing its cavity into two parts, while the cervix and the vagina remain single. Which developmental event failed: the fusion of the two paramesonephric ducts, the formation of the gonadal ridge, the descent of the ovary, or the canalization of the vagina? The answer is the failure to absorb the midline septum after the ducts had fused. The two ducts joined, but the wall between them persisted.

Question 7783. Which pouch of the peritoneum is the lowest point of the peritoneal cavity when a woman lies on her back: the vesicouterine pouch, the rectouterine pouch, the pouch of the broad ligament, or the paravesical fossa? The answer is the rectouterine pouch, between the uterus and the rectum, just above the posterior fornix of the vagina. Fluid there can be reached through that fornix.

Question 7784. A constructed illustration: the pelvic floor of a woman who has borne several children has lost its tone, and the pelvic organs descend. Which structures form the floor that has given way? The answer is the levator ani muscles and the perineal body, with the fascia that covers them. The cardinal and uterosacral ligaments support the cervix from above.

Question 7785. Which structure lies immediately in front of the supravaginal part of the cervix, separated from it only by loose connective tissue: the rectum, the bladder, the sacrum, or the obturator internus muscle? The answer is the bladder. Because the peritoneum is reflected above it, this part of the cervix lies outside the peritoneal cavity. The rectum lies behind.

Question 7786. The peritoneum covers only one surface of the cervix. Which one is it? The answer is the posterior surface. The peritoneum sweeps down behind the cervix and up over the rectum, covering that surface on the way. In front the cervix is separated from the bladder by connective tissue and has no peritoneal covering.

Question 7787. Which layer lines the cavity of the uterus and is shed at each menstruation? The answer is the endometrium. It rests on the myometrium and faces the uterine cavity. Its thickness and its glands change with each phase of the cycle.

Question 7788. The endometrium has two layers. Which list names them: the perimetrium and the myometrium, the stratum functionalis and the stratum basalis, the cortex and the medulla, or the tunica albuginea and the germinal epithelium? The answer is the stratum functionalis and the stratum basalis. The functional layer lies next to the cavity and is shed. The basal layer rests on the muscle and remains.

Question 7789. Which layer of the endometrium is not shed at menstruation and serves to rebuild the lining in the next cycle? The answer is the stratum basalis. It lies against the myometrium and keeps its own glands and vessels. From it the functional layer regenerates after each period.

Question 7790. Within the wall of the uterus, where does the functional layer of the endometrium lie in relation to the basal layer and to the cavity? The answer is that the functional layer lies between the basal layer and the cavity. The basal layer rests on the myometrium. Only the layer nearest the cavity is shed.

Question 7791. During the proliferative phase of the cycle, which change is seen in the endometrium: the glands become coiled and full of secretion, the glands are straight and the stroma grows, the functional layer is shed, or the spiral arteries close? The answer is that the glands are straight and the stroma grows. Estrogen from the growing follicles drives the repair and the thickening. Secretion belongs to the phase after ovulation.

Question 7792. A described section of the endometrium shows glands that are wide and twisted and filled with secretion, in a stroma swollen with fluid. Which phase of the cycle is shown? The answer is the secretory phase, under the influence of progesterone from the corpus luteum. The glands are coiled and the arteries are more coiled still. The lining is now ready to receive an embryo.

Question 7793. Which vessels of the endometrium coil through the cycle and respond to the withdrawal of progesterone: the straight arteries of the basal layer, the spiral arterioles of the functional layer, the arcuate arteries of the myometrium, or the radial arteries of the outer muscle? The answer is the spiral arterioles of the functional layer. They are sensitive to the hormone, and when its level falls they narrow and starve the tissue above them.

Question 7794. A described section of the endometrium shows a thin layer of tissue against the muscle, with straight vessels running up into it and no glands above them. Which phase is shown? The answer is the menstrual phase, after the functional layer has been shed. Only the basal layer remains, and its straight arteries are not sensitive to the fall in hormone.

Question 7795. Why does the basal layer of the endometrium survive menstruation while the functional layer is shed: the basal layer has no blood supply, it is supplied by straight arteries that do not constrict when the hormone falls, it lies inside the myometrium, or it is protected by the peritoneum? The answer is that its vessels are straight arteries that do not constrict. The spiral arterioles of the functional layer do constrict.

Question 7796. Which of these pairs belongs together: the proliferative phase with straight glands and a growing stroma, the secretory phase with shedding of the functional layer, the menstrual phase with coiled glands full of secretion, or the ischemic phase with rapid regrowth of the lining? The answer is the proliferative phase with straight glands and a growing stroma. Coiled secreting glands belong to the secretory phase, and shedding to menstruation.

Question 7797. A constructed illustration: a uterus removed for heavy bleeding is found to be enlarged, and its wall is studded with small islands of glandular tissue surrounded by muscle. Where has that endometrium grown? The answer is within the myometrium, a condition described as adenomyosis. Endometrium outside the uterus altogether is described as endometriosis.

Question 7798. True or false: the functional layer of the endometrium is supplied by straight arteries that do not respond to hormones. The answer is false. The functional layer is supplied by spiral arterioles, which are sensitive to hormones and constrict when progesterone falls. The straight arteries supply the basal layer, which is not shed.

Question 7799. Name the two layers of the endometrium and say which of them is shed at menstruation. The answer is the stratum functionalis and the stratum basalis, and it is the functional layer that is shed. The basal layer stays against the myometrium. It supplies the cells from which the lining is rebuilt in the next cycle.

Question 7800. A described early pregnancy shows a small embryo embedded in the lining of the uterus. Which layer receives it, and in which phase: the basal layer, in the menstrual phase, the functional layer, in the secretory phase, the myometrium, in the proliferative phase, or the perimetrium, at any time? The answer is the functional layer, in the secretory phase. The lining is then thick and full of secretion, and the embryo sinks into it.

To summarize, the ovaries lie in the ovarian fossa on the lateral pelvic wall, held by the mesovarium, by the suspensory ligament that carries the ovarian vessels, and by the ovarian ligament that ties each ovary to the uterus. The ovarian artery arises from the abdominal aorta at the second lumbar vertebra, the left ovarian vein joins the left renal vein while the right joins the inferior vena cava, and the lymphatics of the ovary follow the vessels to the para-aortic nodes. Each uterine tube lies in the free margin of the broad ligament and has four parts: the infundibulum with its fimbriae, the ampulla where fertilization usually occurs, the narrow isthmus, and the intramural part within the uterine wall. The uterus is pear shaped and normally anteverted, with a fundus, body, isthmus and cervix, supported by the cardinal and uterosacral ligaments and related in front to the bladder and behind to the rectum. The endometrium has a functional layer that is shed at menstruation and a basal layer that rebuilds it, and the spiral arterioles of the functional layer, not the straight arteries of the basal layer, respond to the hormones of the cycle.
