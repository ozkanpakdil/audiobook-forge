# Chapter 83 — Adrenal Glands

This chapter takes up four small endocrine structures that lie deep in the body and speak to it in chemical signals: the paired adrenal glands that cap the kidneys, the endocrine tissue in the pancreas, the pineal gland in the roof of the third ventricle, and the hypothalamus at the base of the brain. It covers the position, the relations, the blood supply and the nerve supply of each, and the hormones they make. It holds one hundred questions, and every question states its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 8201. Which paired glands sit on the upper poles of the kidneys, one on each side, inside the same fatty envelope as the kidney? The answer is the adrenal glands, also called the suprarenal glands. They lie behind the peritoneum and are enclosed with the kidney by the renal fascia. The right gland is shaped like a pyramid and the left like a crescent.

Question 8202. Which statement about the arterial supply of the adrenal gland is correct: branches of three different arteries reach it, only the renal artery supplies it, only the aorta supplies it, or one artery divides inside it? The answer is that three different arteries reach it. The superior suprarenal arteries come from the inferior phrenic artery, the middle suprarenal artery from the aorta, and the inferior suprarenal artery from the renal artery.

Question 8203. Which zone of the adrenal cortex lies immediately beneath the fibrous capsule, and which group of hormones does it make? The answer is the zona glomerulosa, which makes the mineralocorticoid aldosterone. Its cells lie in rounded clusters just under the capsule, and the enzyme needed to make aldosterone is confined to this zone.

Question 8204. Which zone of the adrenal cortex is the widest and lies between the zona glomerulosa and the zona reticularis: the zona fasciculata, the zona reticularis, the medulla, or the capsule? The answer is the zona fasciculata. Its cells are arranged in long straight cords that run inward from the capsule, and it makes the glucocorticoid cortisol.

Question 8205. Which large vein lies immediately medial to the right adrenal gland, so that the two are separated by only a thin layer of tissue? The answer is the inferior vena cava. The right adrenal gland lies behind the liver and against the right side of that vessel, and its short vein drains straight into it.

Question 8206. A described transverse section of the upper abdomen, taken through the upper pole of the right kidney, shows a triangular gland lying behind the liver and against the side of a large vein. Which gland is described, and which vein lies against it? The answer is the right adrenal gland, with the inferior vena cava against it. It lies behind the peritoneum, inside the renal fascia.

Question 8207. Which part of the adrenal gland is described as a modified sympathetic ganglion, and what does it secrete? The answer is the adrenal medulla, which secretes epinephrine and norepinephrine. Its cells are called chromaffin cells and lie in the center of the gland, where nerve fibers end directly on them.

Question 8208. Which fibers end on the chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla: preganglionic sympathetic fibers, postganglionic sympathetic fibers, preganglionic parasympathetic fibers, or postganglionic parasympathetic fibers? The answer is preganglionic sympathetic fibers. They arrive through the greater splanchnic nerve from the lower thoracic segments and the first lumbar segment. A postganglionic fiber would mean a relay outside the gland.

Question 8209. A constructed illustration: a person has attacks of pounding headache, sweating and a racing heart, and imaging shows a small mass in the center of one adrenal gland. Which cells are giving rise to the attacks? The answer is the chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla, which release epinephrine and norepinephrine in bursts. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 8210. Which organ lies immediately in front of the left adrenal gland, so that the two are separated only by peritoneum? The answer is the pancreas. Its body and tail cross in front of the left adrenal gland, and the spleen and the stomach lie in front of it as well.

Question 8211. A described dissection of the posterior abdominal wall shows a crescent-shaped gland lying above the left kidney, with the spleen against its outer border and the tail of the pancreas crossing its front. Which gland is described, and on which side? The answer is the left adrenal gland, on the left side. Its crescent shape and its relations distinguish it from the triangular right gland.

Question 8212. Which of these pairs belongs together: the zona glomerulosa with aldosterone, the zona fasciculata with androgens, the zona reticularis with cortisol, or the medulla with insulin? The answer is the zona glomerulosa with aldosterone. That outer zone is the only place where aldosterone is made. The zona fasciculata makes cortisol, the zona reticularis makes weak androgens, and insulin comes from the pancreas.

Question 8213. Into which vein does the right suprarenal vein drain: the right renal vein, the inferior vena cava, the right hepatic vein, or the portal vein? The answer is the inferior vena cava. The right suprarenal vein is very short and passes straight into that vessel, because the gland lies against it.

Question 8214. Into which vein does the left suprarenal vein drain: the inferior vena cava, the left renal vein, the splenic vein, or the left hepatic vein? The answer is the left renal vein. The left vein is longer than the right one and joins the left renal vein before that vessel crosses the aorta.

Question 8215. True or false: the cortex of the adrenal gland is formed from neural crest cells that migrate into the gland. The answer is false. The cortex is formed from the intermediate mesoderm of the urogenital ridge, the same tissue that gives rise to the kidney. The neural crest cells form the medulla instead.

Question 8216. Which embryonic tissue gives rise to the medulla of the adrenal gland, and what does that origin explain? The answer is the neural crest. Those cells migrate into the cortical primordium and settle in its center, where they become chromaffin cells. Their origin explains why the medulla behaves like a sympathetic ganglion and releases catecholamines.

Question 8217. Which large artery lies immediately behind the left renal vein, so that the vein crosses in front of it on its way to the inferior vena cava? The answer is the abdominal aorta. The left renal vein is longer than the right for that reason, and it receives the left suprarenal and left gonadal veins.

Question 8218. A described computed tomography scan of the upper abdomen shows two organs lying behind the peritoneum on either side of the spine, each with a smaller gland sitting on its upper pole. What are the two organs, and what caps them? The answer is the kidneys, each capped by an adrenal gland. The smaller gland is easily mistaken for part of the kidney.

Question 8219. A constructed illustration: a person with a long illness has low blood pressure, tiredness and darkening of the skin, and both adrenal cortices are destroyed. Which hormones are missing? The answer is the glucocorticoids and the mineralocorticoids, made by the zona fasciculata and the zona glomerulosa. The darkening follows the rise in the pituitary hormone that drives the cortex.

Question 8220. Which hormone is made by the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal cortex: cortisol, aldosterone, epinephrine, or testosterone? The answer is aldosterone. It acts on the kidney to hold sodium and to lose potassium, and it is the main mineralocorticoid of the body. Cortisol comes from the zona fasciculata and epinephrine from the medulla.

Question 8221. Which zone of the adrenal cortex lies deepest, right against the medulla? The answer is the zona reticularis. Its cells form an irregular network rather than straight cords, and they make weak androgens such as dehydroepiandrosterone. The medulla lies immediately inside it, and the zona fasciculata lies outside it.

Question 8222. The inferior suprarenal artery arises from which vessel: the renal artery, the aorta, the inferior phrenic artery, or the superior mesenteric artery? The answer is the renal artery. It reaches the gland from below and also sends small branches to the upper part of the ureter. The middle suprarenal artery comes from the aorta.

Question 8223. Which statement about the nerve supply of the adrenal medulla is correct: the fibers end directly on the secretory cells, the fibers relay in a ganglion beside the gland, the fibers are parasympathetic, or the gland has no nerve supply? The answer is that the fibers end directly on the secretory cells. They are preganglionic sympathetic fibers arriving along the splanchnic nerves, and with no relay the medulla can act very quickly.

Question 8224. A described dissection of the posterior abdominal wall shows a gland lifted away from the upper pole of the kidney, with a thick layer of fat beneath it and a thin fibrous envelope around both organs. Which coverings are described? The answer is the perirenal fat and the renal fascia. The adrenal gland lies within the same envelope as the kidney.

Question 8225. What name is given to the small extra masses of adrenal cortical tissue that are sometimes found in the fat around the gland or lower down along the path of the gonad? The answer is accessory adrenal cortical tissue, sometimes called adrenal rests. They are a common variation rather than a disease, and they contain cortical tissue only.

Question 8226. Which part of the pancreas lies within the curve of the duodenum, so that the gut wraps around its right border? The answer is the head of the pancreas. The descending and horizontal parts of the duodenum curve around it. The uncinate process hangs down from the head behind the superior mesenteric vessels.

Question 8227. Which part of the pancreas reaches the spleen, lying inside a fold of peritoneum: the head, the neck, the body, or the tail? The answer is the tail. It is the only part of the pancreas that is not behind the peritoneum, because it runs in the splenorenal ligament with the splenic vessels. The head lies against the duodenum instead.

Question 8228. Which vein lies immediately behind the neck of the pancreas, formed there by the union of two other veins? The answer is the portal vein. The splenic vein joins the superior mesenteric vein behind the neck, and the new vessel then runs upward to the liver. The neck of the pancreas therefore lies directly in front of it.

Question 8229. What name is given to the round clusters of endocrine cells scattered through the pancreas, and which cells lie at the center of each cluster? The answer is the islets of Langerhans, with the beta cells at the center. The beta cells make insulin and are surrounded by the other cell types. The islets form only a small part of the organ.

Question 8230. Which cells of the pancreatic islet make insulin: the beta cells, the alpha cells, the delta cells, or the cells that make pancreatic polypeptide? The answer is the beta cells. They lie in the center of the islet and are the most numerous of its cells. The alpha cells at the edge make glucagon.

Question 8231. A described transverse section of the upper abdomen shows a long gland lying behind the stomach, with a large vein running in a groove along its back and the spleen at its left end. Which organ is described, and which vein lies in the groove? The answer is the pancreas, with the splenic vein in the groove, behind the stomach.

Question 8232. A constructed illustration: a person slowly turns yellow, and imaging shows a mass in the head of the pancreas. Which duct is being compressed as it passes behind that part of the gland? The answer is the common bile duct. It runs down behind the head of the pancreas and joins the pancreatic duct before opening into the duodenum. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8233. Which of these pairs belongs together: the beta cells with insulin, the alpha cells with somatostatin, the delta cells with glucagon, or the islet cells with pancreatic juice? The answer is the beta cells with insulin. The alpha cells make glucagon and the delta cells make somatostatin, while the digestive enzymes of pancreatic juice come from the acinar cells.

Question 8234. The head of the pancreas is supplied by two pancreaticoduodenal arteries. Where do they arise: one from the gastroduodenal artery and one from the superior mesenteric artery, both from the splenic artery, both from the aorta, or one from each gastric artery? The answer is that one arises from the gastroduodenal artery and one from the superior mesenteric artery. Their branches meet between the duodenum and the head.

Question 8235. Which part of the pancreas hooks backward and downward behind the superior mesenteric vessels, and where does it lie relative to them? The answer is the uncinate process. It springs from the lower part of the head and passes behind the superior mesenteric artery and vein. Its position explains why a mass there can press on those vessels.

Question 8236. A described computed tomography scan shows a ring of pancreatic tissue completely encircling the descending part of the duodenum. What is this arrangement called, and from which developmental event does it arise? The answer is an annular pancreas, which arises when the ventral pancreatic bud divides and one part encircles the gut. The ring may narrow the duodenum. It is a variation present from birth.

Question 8237. Which vessels lie immediately to the left of the head of the pancreas, so that the head is said to lie to their right? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery and the superior mesenteric vein. The head and the uncinate process wrap around them. The portal vein is formed behind the neck just above them.

Question 8238. A constructed illustration: a blow to the upper abdomen drives a handlebar into the belly, and the injury lies in the midline just below the sternum. Which organ lies against the vertebral column there and is easily crushed against it? The answer is the pancreas. Its body crosses in front of the aorta and the vertebral column at the level of the first lumbar vertebra.

Question 8239. Into which part of the gut does the main pancreatic duct open: the stomach, the first part of the duodenum, the descending part of the duodenum, or the jejunum? The answer is the descending part of the duodenum. There it joins the common bile duct at the hepatopancreatic ampulla and opens on the major duodenal papilla. The accessory pancreatic duct opens above it.

Question 8240. A described specimen of the duodenum shows a small raised opening on its inner surface, with a duct entering it from behind. Which opening is described, and which two ducts meet before it? The answer is the major duodenal papilla, where the main pancreatic duct and the common bile duct meet. They join in the wall of the duodenum to form the hepatopancreatic ampulla.

Question 8241. Which part of the pancreas lies inside a fold of peritoneum and is therefore the only part that is not retroperitoneal? The answer is the tail. It runs in the splenorenal ligament together with the splenic vessels. The head, the neck and the body all lie behind the peritoneum.

Question 8242. True or false: the islets of Langerhans are most numerous in the tail of the pancreas. The answer is true. The tail holds a greater density of islets than the rest of the gland, even though it is the smallest part. That is one reason why a mass of the islet cells is often found there.

Question 8243. Which cells of the pancreatic islet make somatostatin: the delta cells, the beta cells, the alpha cells, or the acinar cells? The answer is the delta cells. They lie among the other cells of the islet, and their hormone damps the release of its neighbors. The acinar cells are not endocrine and pour enzymes into the ducts.

Question 8244. Which artery runs along the upper border of the pancreas, giving branches to the body and the tail, and is itself a branch of the celiac trunk? The answer is the splenic artery. It is remarkable for its tortuous course, and it lies in a groove along the upper border of the gland. The gastroduodenal artery supplies the head instead.

Question 8245. Name the two ducts of the pancreas, spoken as a list. The answer is the main pancreatic duct and the accessory pancreatic duct. The main duct runs the length of the gland and opens together with the common bile duct. The accessory duct, when it is present, opens a little above it at the minor papilla.

Question 8246. The pancreas develops from two buds of the embryonic gut. Which parts does the ventral bud form: the body and the tail, the lower head and the uncinate process, the whole gland, or only the tail? The answer is the lower part of the head and the uncinate process. The ventral bud rotates backward beneath the dorsal bud and fuses with it.

Question 8247. Within a pancreatic islet the cells are supplied in a particular order. Which statement is correct: the beta cells receive blood first, the alpha cells receive blood first, all the cells are supplied equally, or the islet has no blood supply? The answer is that the beta cells receive blood first, so insulin washes over the other cells before it leaves. That lets insulin damp the release of glucagon.

Question 8248. A constructed illustration: a person has repeated spells of shakiness and confusion that settle after food, and a small mass is found in the tail of the pancreas. Which cells are overactive? The answer is the beta cells of the islets, which are making too much insulin. The tail holds the greatest density of islets. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8249. A described section of the pancreas, viewed through a lens, shows many small round clusters of pale cells with capillaries running between them, set among larger darker lobules. What are the round clusters, and what do they form? The answer is the islets of Langerhans, the endocrine part of the gland. The darker lobules around them are the acini, which make the digestive enzymes.

Question 8250. Which cells lie at the edge of a pancreatic islet and make glucagon? The answer is the alpha cells. They form a rim around the beta cells in the center of the islet. Glucagon raises the level of sugar in the blood and so opposes insulin.

Question 8251. Which small cone-shaped gland lies in the roof of the third ventricle, behind the thalamus and above the midbrain? The answer is the pineal gland, also called the pineal body or the epiphysis. Its name comes from its shape, which resembles a pine cone. It is attached to the roof of the third ventricle by a short stalk.

Question 8252. Which structure lies immediately above and behind the pineal gland, forming the back of the corpus callosum? The answer is the splenium of the corpus callosum. The pineal gland sits in the small space between the splenium above and the superior colliculi below. The great cerebral vein curves over the gland in the same region.

Question 8253. Which arteries supply the pineal gland: the posterior choroidal arteries, the anterior cerebral arteries, the middle meningeal arteries, or the ophthalmic arteries? The answer is the posterior choroidal arteries, which are branches of the posterior cerebral artery. They also supply the choroid plexus of the third ventricle. The gland has a rich blood supply for its small size.

Question 8254. A described radiograph of the skull, taken from the front, shows a small dense fleck lying in the midline above and behind the sella. What is the fleck, and why is it useful on such a film? The answer is calcification in the pineal gland. Because the gland lies in the midline, the fleck shows whether the brain has been pushed to one side.

Question 8255. Which cells of the pineal gland make melatonin, and from which part of the nervous system do they come? The answer is the pinealocytes, which are modified nerve cells derived from the roof of the diencephalon. They convert serotonin into melatonin and release it into the blood and the cerebrospinal fluid. Supporting cells lie between them.

Question 8256. True or false: the pineal gland lies outside the blood-brain barrier, so substances in the blood can reach its cells directly. The answer is true. Its capillaries are fenestrated rather than tight, which places it among the small group of organs around the third and fourth ventricles that lack the barrier. That arrangement lets the gland sample the blood.

Question 8257. Which of these pairs belongs together: the pineal gland with melatonin, the adrenal cortex with epinephrine, the pancreatic islets with bile, or the posterior pituitary with insulin? The answer is the pineal gland with melatonin. Epinephrine comes from the adrenal medulla and not the cortex, insulin comes from the pancreatic islets, and bile comes from the liver.

Question 8258. A described section of the brain passes from front to back through the pineal gland and the third ventricle, dividing the brain into a right and a left part. In which plane does that section lie? The answer is a sagittal plane, and it shows the gland in the midline of the roof of the third ventricle. A section placed to one side would miss the gland.

Question 8259. Which nerve fibers reach the pineal gland: postganglionic sympathetic fibers from the superior cervical ganglion, preganglionic sympathetic fibers from the spinal cord, fibers from the vagus nerve, or fibers from the optic nerve? The answer is postganglionic sympathetic fibers from the superior cervical ganglion. They travel along the blood vessels to the gland, and the optic nerve does not reach it, though light influences it by a longer route.

Question 8260. A constructed illustration: a growing mass in the pineal region blocks the narrow channel between the third and the fourth ventricle, and the ventricles above it swell. Which channel is blocked? The answer is the cerebral aqueduct of the midbrain. The pineal gland lies just above and behind the tectal plate, so a mass there presses on the aqueduct. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 8261. What name is given to the gritty concretions of calcium salts that are commonly found in the pineal gland of an adult? The answer is corpora arenacea, also called brain sand. They lie among the cells of the gland and grow more numerous with age. They are why the gland often appears as a dense fleck on a plain radiograph.

Question 8262. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the brain in the midline shows a small cyst in the pineal region, with a vein curving over its upper surface. Which vein is described? The answer is the great cerebral vein, which receives the two internal cerebral veins behind the gland. It runs above the pineal gland and below the splenium of the corpus callosum.

Question 8263. A constructed illustration: a person cannot look upward, and the pupils do not narrow when a light is shone into the eye, though the eyes still focus on a near object. A mass lies in the pineal region. Which structure is pressed on? The answer is the tectal plate, especially the superior colliculi and the pretectal area above them, which lie below the gland.

Question 8264. Name the two small bands of fibers that form the upper and lower laminae of the pineal stalk, spoken as a list. The answer is the habenular commissure and the posterior commissure. They lie one above the other in the roof of the third ventricle, and the pineal stalk is attached between them. The upper one belongs to the habenular nuclei.

Question 8265. How does light reaching the eye change the output of the pineal gland: it increases the release of melatonin, it reduces the release of melatonin, it has no effect, or it makes the gland secrete insulin? The answer is that light reduces the release of melatonin. The signal travels from the retina to the suprachiasmatic nucleus and then by a long route to the gland.

Question 8266. True or false: the pineal gland lies in front of the thalamus. The answer is false. It lies behind the thalamus, in the roof of the third ventricle, and it belongs to the epithalamus. The structures in front of the thalamus include the interventricular foramen and the front part of the third ventricle.

Question 8267. In the pathway that carries the light signal to the pineal gland, which structure lies immediately after the suprachiasmatic nucleus: the superior cervical ganglion, the paraventricular nucleus, the pineal gland itself, or the retina? The answer is the paraventricular nucleus. From there the signal descends the spinal cord and leaves in the upper thoracic segments, and the fibers of the superior cervical ganglion then reach the gland.

Question 8268. Which structure lies immediately below the pineal gland and forms the roof of the midbrain? The answer is the tectal plate, formed by the superior and the inferior colliculi. The pineal gland rests just above the superior colliculi. A mass in the gland therefore presses on them before it presses on anything else.

Question 8269. A described specimen shows a small lobulated body attached by a short stalk to the roof of the third ventricle, lying behind the two thalami. Which organ is shown? The answer is the pineal gland. Its stalk carries the two commissures, and the gland hangs down behind the third ventricle. It is one of the smallest organs of the body.

Question 8270. From the roof of which part of the brain does the pineal gland develop as a small outpouching: the diencephalon, the telencephalon, the midbrain, or the cerebellum? The answer is the diencephalon. The gland grows out from the roof of the third ventricle, which is part of the diencephalon, and that origin explains why its cells are nerve cells.

Question 8271. Which cells form the endocrine part of the pineal gland, and from which germ layer are they derived? The answer is the pinealocytes, which come from the neuroectoderm of the diencephalon. They are modified nerve cells that make melatonin. They are not derived from the neural crest, which separates them from the chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla.

Question 8272. Into which veins does the pineal gland drain: the internal cerebral veins, the superior sagittal sinus, the transverse sinus, or the cavernous sinus? The answer is the internal cerebral veins. They pass backward above the gland and unite to form the great cerebral vein. From there the blood reaches the straight sinus.

Question 8273. At which synapse do the nerve fibers that control the pineal gland relay: the superior cervical ganglion, the ciliary ganglion, the pterygopalatine ganglion, or the otic ganglion? The answer is the superior cervical ganglion. Its postganglionic fibers follow the blood vessels into the gland. The other three ganglia supply the eye, the nasal lining and the salivary glands.

Question 8274. A described computed tomography scan of the head shows a small dense dot in the midline between the two halves of the brain. Which structure has calcified, and why is its position helpful? The answer is the pineal gland. Because it lies in the midline, a shift of the dot to one side suggests that the brain has been pushed across.

Question 8275. A described section is cut from side to side through the head, dividing the brain into a front and a back part, and it passes through the pineal gland. In which plane does it lie? The answer is a coronal plane, also called a frontal plane. It shows the gland in the midline between the two thalami.

Question 8276. Which part of the brain forms the floor and the lower walls of the third ventricle, lying below the hypothalamic sulcus? The answer is the hypothalamus. It lies at the base of the brain, below the thalamus, and it stretches from the region in front of the optic chiasm to the mammillary bodies behind.

Question 8277. Which structure lies immediately in front of the hypothalamus and carries fibers from the retina toward the brain? The answer is the optic chiasm. It lies at the front of the floor of the third ventricle, just above the pituitary gland. Behind it lies the infundibulum, and behind that the mammillary bodies.

Question 8278. Which of these structures marks the front of the hypothalamus: the lamina terminalis, the mammillary bodies, the splenium of the corpus callosum, or the pineal gland? The answer is the lamina terminalis, a thin sheet that closes the front of the third ventricle. The mammillary bodies mark the back of the hypothalamus.

Question 8279. Which two nuclei of the hypothalamus make antidiuretic hormone and oxytocin, and where do their axons end? The answer is the supraoptic nucleus and the paraventricular nucleus, whose axons run down the stalk to the posterior pituitary. The hormones are stored in the nerve endings there and released into the blood. The supraoptic nucleus lies above the optic tract.

Question 8280. Which small nucleus lies just above the optic chiasm and acts as the clock that sets the daily rhythm of the body: the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the arcuate nucleus, the ventromedial nucleus, or the mammillary body? The answer is the suprachiasmatic nucleus. It receives fibers straight from the retina and keeps the rhythms of the body in step with daylight.

Question 8281. A described coronal section through the front of the brain shows a small paired nucleus sitting just above the optic chiasm on each side of the third ventricle, with dark fibers entering it from the optic pathway. Which nucleus is shown? The answer is the suprachiasmatic nucleus. Fibers of the retinohypothalamic tract reach it directly from the retina.

Question 8282. A constructed illustration: after an operation in the region above the pituitary gland, a person passes large volumes of dilute urine day and night. Which tract has been damaged? The answer is the supraopticohypophyseal tract, which carries axons from the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei to the posterior pituitary. Cutting the stalk above the gland leaves those nerve endings unable to release their hormone.

Question 8283. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supraoptic nucleus with antidiuretic hormone, the arcuate nucleus with oxytocin, the suprachiasmatic nucleus with growth hormone, or the mammillary body with insulin? The answer is the supraoptic nucleus with antidiuretic hormone. The paraventricular nucleus makes oxytocin as well as antidiuretic hormone, and the arcuate nucleus makes the releasing hormones.

Question 8284. Which pathway carries the releasing hormones from the hypothalamus to the front of the pituitary gland: the supraopticohypophyseal tract, the tuberoinfundibular tract with the portal veins, the mammillothalamic tract, or the fornix? The answer is the tuberoinfundibular tract together with the hypophyseal portal veins. Its fibers come from the arcuate nucleus and end on the capillaries of the median eminence.

Question 8285. Which structure lies immediately below the hypothalamus and is joined to it by a hollow stalk? The answer is the pituitary gland, also called the hypophysis. The stalk, called the infundibulum, carries the axons of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei down to the back of the gland. The front of the gland is controlled through the portal veins.

Question 8286. Which two rounded bodies lie at the back of the floor of the hypothalamus, one on each side of the midline? The answer is the mammillary bodies. They receive fibers from the hippocampus through the fornix and send fibers on to the anterior nucleus of the thalamus. They belong to the circuit that is concerned with memory.

Question 8287. True or false: the median eminence lies outside the blood-brain barrier, so substances in the blood can reach its cells. The answer is true. Its capillaries are fenestrated, and the releasing hormones pass from the nerve endings there into the hypophyseal portal veins. This is what allows the hypothalamus to read the chemical state of the blood.

Question 8288. A described sagittal section of the brain shows, from front to back in the midline, a flat crossing of fibers, then a hollow stalk, then a rounded swelling, and finally two small rounded bodies. Which region is displayed by that row of structures? The answer is the floor of the hypothalamus, with the optic chiasm, the infundibulum and the mammillary bodies in order.

Question 8289. A constructed illustration: a person never feels thirsty, drinks very little, and passes large amounts of dilute urine, and a small lesion is found in the front of the hypothalamus. Which nucleus is most likely involved? The answer is the supraoptic nucleus, whose cells sense the concentration of the blood and release antidiuretic hormone. Damage to it removes both the thirst and the hormone signal.

Question 8290. Name the two nuclei whose axons form the supraopticohypophyseal tract, spoken as a list. The answer is the supraoptic nucleus and the paraventricular nucleus. Their axons run through the infundibulum into the posterior pituitary. The hormones they carry are released into the blood from the nerve endings there.

Question 8291. Which part of the hypothalamus drives the responses that lose heat, such as flushing and sweating, and which part drives the responses that save heat? The answer is the anterior part for losing heat and the posterior part for saving it. The anterior region lies above the optic chiasm and the posterior region near the mammillary bodies.

Question 8292. Which structure lies immediately lateral to the hypothalamus and separates it from the lentiform nucleus? The answer is the internal capsule. Its fibers run between the thalamus and the lentiform nucleus, and the hypothalamus lies medial to them. The optic tract also lies along the lateral surface of the hypothalamus.

Question 8293. Which region of the hypothalamus is called the feeding center, and which nucleus is called the satiety center? The answer is the lateral hypothalamic area for feeding and the ventromedial nucleus for satiety. Damage to the ventromedial nucleus removes the brake on eating, and damage to the lateral area removes the drive to eat.

Question 8294. Which nucleus of the hypothalamus makes the releasing hormones that control the front of the pituitary gland, and what is its other name? The answer is the arcuate nucleus, also called the infundibular nucleus. It lies in the middle part of the hypothalamus, close to the median eminence, and its cells are the source of the tuberoinfundibular tract.

Question 8295. Which statement about the hypophyseal portal system is correct: it carries releasing hormones from the median eminence to the front of the pituitary gland, it carries antidiuretic hormone to the kidney, it drains the posterior pituitary into the neck, or it carries oxytocin to the breast? The answer is that it carries releasing hormones from the median eminence to the front of the pituitary gland.

Question 8296. Which structure forms the back of the floor of the hypothalamus, at the point where the hypothalamus meets the midbrain? The answer is the mammillary bodies, behind which lies the tegmentum of the midbrain. Together with the posterior nucleus they make up the posterior part of the hypothalamus.

Question 8297. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan in the sagittal plane shows a rounded mass in the space above the pituitary gland, pushing the optic chiasm upward and flattening the floor of the third ventricle. Which two structures are being compressed? The answer is the optic chiasm and the hypothalamus, with the pituitary stalk stretched over the mass. Such a mass can disturb both vision and hormone control.

Question 8298. A constructed illustration: a person who has recovered from a blow to the base of the brain no longer feels hungry and has lost much weight, and the damage lies in one part of the hypothalamus. Which part is it? The answer is the lateral hypothalamic area, the feeding center. Damage there removes the drive to eat, while damage to the ventromedial nucleus has the opposite effect.

Question 8299. Which of these pairs belongs together: the ventromedial nucleus with the sensation of fullness after a meal, the lateral hypothalamic area with the clock of the body, the suprachiasmatic nucleus with the drive to eat, or the paraventricular nucleus with vision? The answer is the ventromedial nucleus with the sensation of fullness after a meal. The lateral area drives eating, and the suprachiasmatic nucleus keeps daily time.

Question 8300. Which bundle carries fibers from the hippocampus to the mammillary body: the fornix, the mammillothalamic tract, the stria terminalis, or the medial forebrain bundle? The answer is the fornix, which arches forward beneath the corpus callosum and ends in the mammillary body. From there the mammillothalamic tract carries the signal to the anterior nucleus of the thalamus.

To summarize, the adrenal glands cap the upper poles of the kidneys inside the renal fascia, with a cortex of three zones making aldosterone, cortisol and weak androgens, and a medulla of chromaffin cells that behaves as a modified sympathetic ganglion under the control of preganglionic fibers. The cortex comes from intermediate mesoderm and the medulla from neural crest, three arteries supply each gland, and the right vein drains into the inferior vena cava while the left drains into the left renal vein. The endocrine pancreas lies in the islets of Langerhans, most numerous in the tail, where beta cells make insulin, alpha cells make glucagon and delta cells make somatostatin. The pineal gland sits in the midline behind the thalamus and above the superior colliculi, is supplied by the posterior choroidal arteries, and is controlled by sympathetic fibers from the superior cervical ganglion. The hypothalamus forms the floor of the third ventricle, sends axons to the posterior pituitary and releasing hormones to the portal veins, and holds the nuclei for temperature, feeding, thirst and the daily rhythm.
