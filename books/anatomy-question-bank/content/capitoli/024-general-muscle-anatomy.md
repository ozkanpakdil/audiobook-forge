# Chapter 24 — General Muscle Anatomy

Every movement of the body comes from muscle, and this chapter sets out the general anatomy of that tissue rather than the details of particular named muscles. It follows the muscle fiber from its membrane and its filaments to the sheaths that bind the fibers into a belly, then the tendons and the broad aponeuroses by which a muscle takes hold of bone, then the origin and the insertion that fix the two ends of that pull, and finally the agonists, the antagonists and the helpers that carry a movement out. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 2301. Which contractile cell is the basic unit of a skeletal muscle, and does it hold one nucleus or many? The answer is the muscle fiber, which holds many nuclei. A fiber forms in development by the fusion of many precursor cells, so its nuclei lie just beneath the surface membrane. A single fiber may run the whole length of a short muscle.

Question 2302. Which of these is the thin filament of a muscle fiber: myosin, actin, titin, or collagen? The answer is actin. Thin filaments are anchored at the Z line and are drawn toward the middle of the sarcomere by the thicker myosin filaments. Titin is an elastic protein that keeps the thick filaments centered, and collagen belongs to the connective tissue around the fiber.

Question 2303. A described microscopic section of a muscle fiber shows a dark band crossed at its middle by a thin darker line, and beside it a light band divided by another dark line. Which structure is the line in the light band? The answer is the Z line, which marks the boundary between one sarcomere and the next. The line in the middle of the dark band is the M line.

Question 2304. What name is given to the segment of a myofibril that lies between one Z line and the next? The answer is the sarcomere. It is the smallest unit that shortens when a muscle contracts. Within it, thin actin filaments slide past thick myosin filaments toward the middle of the segment.

Question 2305. During the contraction of a muscle fiber, which of these changes its length: the A band, the thick myosin filament, the sarcomere, or the thin actin filament? The answer is the sarcomere. The filaments themselves keep their length and slide past one another, so the A band stays the same while the sarcomere shortens.

Question 2306. Which structure lies within the fiber, wraps around each myofibril as a sleeve of flattened sacs, and stores the calcium that starts contraction? The answer is the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Its expanded ends, the terminal cisternae, lie close to a T tubule, and the three together form a triad.

Question 2307. What is the name of the narrow channel that is an inward fold of the sarcolemma and carries the signal for contraction deep into a muscle fiber? The answer is the T tubule. Because it is a fold of the surface membrane, its interior is continuous with the space outside the fiber. Two terminal cisternae of the sarcoplasmic reticulum lie beside it.

Question 2308. Which ion must reach the cytoplasm of a muscle fiber before the myofilaments can slide: sodium, calcium, potassium, or chloride? The answer is calcium. It is released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum and binds to troponin, which moves tropomyosin off the actin and lets the myosin heads attach. Sodium carries the signal along the membrane but does not switch the filaments on.

Question 2309. Name the two kinds of myofilament that make up a sarcomere, spoken as a list. The answer is actin and myosin. Actin forms the thin filament, which is anchored at the Z line, and myosin forms the thick filament, whose heads pull the thin filaments inward. The regular overlap of the two gives skeletal muscle its striated appearance.

Question 2310. Which pair belongs together: the Z line with the boundary of the sarcomere, the M line with the boundary of the sarcomere, the A band with thin filaments only, or the I band with thick filaments only? The answer is the Z line with the boundary of the sarcomere. The M line lies in the middle of the A band, which holds the thick filaments, while the I band holds only thin filaments.

Question 2311. True or false: the A band of a sarcomere is the dark band and it contains the thick myosin filaments. The answer is true. The dark appearance comes from the thick filaments together with the thin filaments that overlap them at each end of the band. The light I band beside it contains thin filaments alone.

Question 2312. A described cross section of a muscle shows a bundle of fibers wrapped in a sheath of connective tissue, and the whole muscle wrapped in a second sheath. What are those two sheaths called? The answer is the perimysium around the bundle and the epimysium around the whole muscle. The finest of the three sheaths lies inside the bundle.

Question 2313. What name is given to a bundle of muscle fibers wrapped together by perimysium? The answer is a fascicle. The fibers in a fascicle run in the same direction, so the bundle acts as one strand of the muscle. The pattern in which the fascicles are arranged gives a muscle its shape and its power.

Question 2314. A muscle whose fascicles run the whole length of the belly and pull on a tendon at each end is described by which of these words: pennate, parallel, circular, or convergent? The answer is parallel. A parallel muscle shortens over a long distance and can move a joint through a wide range. A pennate muscle packs in more fibers and pulls harder, but it shortens less.

Question 2315. A described specimen shows a muscle whose fibers run at a slant into a tendon that lies along one side of the belly only. How is that arrangement named? The answer is unipennate. In a bipennate muscle the tendon lies in the middle and fibers slant into it from both sides. A multipennate muscle has several such tendons and looks like a feather.

Question 2316. Which arrangement allows a muscle to produce the greatest force: a long parallel belly, many short fibers set at a slant, fibers forming a ring around an opening, or fibers converging on one tendon? The answer is many short fibers set at a slant, which is the pennate pattern. More fibers fit into the same volume, and each adds its pull. The price is a shorter range of movement.

Question 2317. A constructed illustration: after weeks in a cast, a limb is weak and the muscle feels smaller than the other side. Which part of the muscle has been lost? The answer is the myofilaments inside the fibers, which shrink when they are not used. The connective tissue sheaths remain, and the fibers can enlarge again when the muscle works.

Question 2318. In the thigh, which layer of connective tissue lies between the skin and the epimysium of the quadriceps, and sends a septum inward to the femur? The answer is the deep fascia, called the fascia lata in the thigh. It wraps the whole limb like a stocking and gives attachment to muscles. The epimysium lies deep to it.

Question 2319. Which protein lies along the actin filament and blocks the sites where the myosin heads would attach while the fiber is at rest? The answer is tropomyosin. It is held in that blocking position by troponin. When calcium binds troponin, tropomyosin shifts aside and the myosin heads can attach.

Question 2320. A described high magnification section shows a thick filament with fine elastic strands running from its middle to the Z line. Which protein forms those strands, and what is their task? The answer is titin. It springs back after the fiber has been stretched, and it keeps the thick filament centered in the sarcomere.

Question 2321. A constructed illustration: after the nerve to a limb muscle is cut, the muscle becomes weak and flat over several months. What has happened inside the fibers, and which sheaths remain? The answer is that the myofilaments are lost and the fibers shrink, while the endomysium, the perimysium and the epimysium stay. The muscle cannot recover unless the nerve grows back to it.

Question 2322. Which statement about a motor unit is correct: it is one muscle fiber with one nerve fiber, it is one motor neuron with all the fibers it supplies, it is all the fibers of one fascicle, or it is one muscle with its tendon? The answer is one motor neuron with all the fibers it supplies. Muscles that need fine control, such as those moving the eye, have small motor units.

Question 2323. In the arm, which nerve lies in the plane between the biceps brachii and the brachialis, after piercing the coracobrachialis? The answer is the musculocutaneous nerve. It supplies both of those muscles and then continues as a sensory nerve to the outer side of the forearm.

Question 2324. Which statement about the two kinds of skeletal muscle fiber is correct: type one fibers are fast and white, type one fibers are slow, red and resistant to fatigue, type two fibers resist fatigue best, or both kinds hold equal myoglobin? The answer is that type one fibers are slow, red and resistant to fatigue. Type two fibers contract faster and tire sooner. Postural muscles such as the soleus are rich in them.

Question 2325. During a maximal contraction, which statement about the bands of the sarcomere is correct: both the A band and the I band shorten, the A band shortens and the I band is unchanged, the A band is unchanged and the I band shortens, or neither band changes? The answer is that the A band is unchanged and the I band shortens. The thick filaments keep their length as the thin filaments slide inward.

Question 2326. What is the name of the tough cord of fibrous tissue that joins a muscle to a bone? The answer is a tendon. It is built of parallel bundles of collagen that resist being pulled apart. At its two ends the tendon blends with the muscle at one end and with the bone at the other.

Question 2327. Which of these tissues makes up the bulk of a tendon: loose fatty tissue, dense regular collagen, elastic cartilage, or smooth muscle? The answer is dense regular collagen. The bundles lie parallel to the line of pull, which is why a tendon is so strong in one direction and weak across it. A ligament is made of the same tissue.

Question 2328. Which tendon, the strongest in the body, is formed by the gastrocnemius and the soleus and ends on the heel bone? The answer is the calcaneal tendon, also called the Achilles tendon. The two calf muscles join above the heel, and the plantaris tendon lies beside them. The tendon reaches the calcaneus, where it can be felt as a taut cord.

Question 2329. A described section through the ankle shows a thick dark band passing behind the joint to reach the heel. Which structure is it, and what does it carry? The answer is the calcaneal tendon, which carries the pull of the calf muscles to the calcaneus. It lies just beneath the skin, and it stands out when the foot is pointed.

Question 2330. Which group of tendons forms a cuff around the shoulder joint and holds the head of the humerus against its socket: the tendons of the rotator cuff, the tendons of the forearm flexors, the tendons of the hamstrings, or the tendons of the calf? The answer is the tendons of the rotator cuff. Four short muscles blend with the capsule of the joint before they insert on the humerus.

Question 2331. Which tendon runs through the intertubercular groove of the humerus, held there by the transverse humeral ligament? The answer is the tendon of the long head of the biceps brachii. It arises from the supraglenoid tubercle above the shoulder joint and passes down the groove to join the muscle in the arm.

Question 2332. What is the name of the fibrous band that joins one bone to another, as opposed to the cord that joins a muscle to a bone? The answer is a ligament. It crosses a joint and holds the bones together while still allowing them to move. The band that joins muscle to bone is a tendon.

Question 2333. Which statement about a tendon and a ligament is correct: a tendon joins muscle to bone and a ligament joins bone to bone, both join muscle to bone, a tendon joins bone to bone, or neither contains collagen? The answer is that a tendon joins muscle to bone and a ligament joins bone to bone. Both are dense regular collagen, so only their two ends tell them apart.

Question 2334. A described ultrasound picture of the back of the ankle shows a band that is no longer continuous, with a dark gap between the two ends. Which structure has been torn, and what fills the gap? The answer is the calcaneal tendon, and the gap fills with blood and fluid. A normal tendon would appear as unbroken bright parallel lines.

Question 2335. True or false: a tendon has a poor blood supply, especially in its middle, and therefore heals slowly. The answer is true. Blood reaches a tendon mainly where it joins the muscle and where it joins the bone. The middle of a long tendon receives little, which is one reason a rupture there is slow to repair.

Question 2336. Which pair belongs together: the gastrocnemius with the calcaneus, the biceps brachii with the olecranon, the deltoid with the radial tuberosity, or the triceps brachii with the lesser tubercle? The answer is the gastrocnemius with the calcaneus. The triceps brachii ends on the olecranon, the biceps brachii on the radial tuberosity, and the deltoid on the deltoid tuberosity.

Question 2337. What name is given to the seam of fibrous tissue where the fibers of two muscles interlace, as in the floor of the mouth or the middle of the back of the neck? The answer is a raphe. It is not a tendon, because no bone lies at its ends. It is the meeting line of two muscles that share a task.

Question 2338. Which structure is a small fluid filled sac that lies between a tendon and a bone and lessens friction where the tendon changes direction? The answer is a bursa. It is lined by a synovial membrane and holds a thin film of fluid. A bursa differs from a tendon sheath, which wraps the tendon along its length.

Question 2339. A described microscopic section shows dense wavy bundles of collagen running in one direction, with flattened cells squeezed in rows between the bundles, and no blood vessels in the field. Which tissue is it? The answer is dense regular connective tissue, the tissue of a tendon. The flattened cells are the tendon cells that make and maintain the collagen.

Question 2340. A constructed illustration: while pushing off to run, a person feels a sudden snap at the back of the heel and can no longer point the foot. Which structure has given way? The answer is the calcaneal tendon. The illustration describes the usual story of a tendon that was already worn; what is done about it belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2341. Which structure wraps a tendon where it passes under a fibrous band at the wrist, and allows it to glide to and fro? The answer is a synovial tendon sheath. It has an outer fibrous layer and an inner layer that secretes fluid. Where a tendon rubs over a bone without such a sheath, a bursa takes its place.

Question 2342. Which receptor warns the muscle when the tension in its tendon becomes dangerous: the muscle spindle, the Golgi tendon organ, a free nerve ending, or a Pacinian corpuscle? The answer is the Golgi tendon organ. It lies in the tendon and fires as tension rises, which causes the muscle to relax. The muscle spindle measures length rather than tension.

Question 2343. A constructed illustration: repeated gripping and lifting brings pain to the outer side of the elbow, over the point where the forearm muscles take hold. Which tendon is involved? The answer is the common extensor tendon at the lateral epicondyle. The muscles that straighten the wrist and the fingers share that tendon, and its care belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 2344. Which band of fibrous tissue holds the tendons of the wrist against the bones and forms the roof of the carpal tunnel? The answer is the flexor retinaculum. It stretches between the two rows of wrist bones and stops the tendons from bowing forward when the fingers bend. The median nerve passes beneath it with the tendons.

Question 2345. Which statement about the blood supply of a tendon is correct: it is rich throughout and heals quickly, it is richest in the middle and poorest at the ends, it is poor in the middle and comes mainly from the muscle end and the bone end, or a tendon holds no vessels at all? The answer is that it is poor in the middle and comes mainly from the ends.

Question 2346. A described section through the attachment of a tendon to bone shows fibers running obliquely into the bone and blending with its outer layer. Which fibers are they, and what do they achieve? The answer is Sharpey fibers. They fix the tendon into the bone and spread the pull of the muscle over a wide area instead of a single point.

Question 2347. Which statement about a small bone that develops inside a tendon is correct: it changes the direction of the muscle pull and reduces friction, it is made of elastic cartilage, it replaces the tendon, or it is found only in the hand? The answer is that it changes the direction of the pull and reduces friction. The patella is the largest example, lying in the tendon of the quadriceps.

Question 2348. What is the name of a broad flat sheet of fibrous tissue that takes the place of a tendon where a muscle is wide? The answer is an aponeurosis. It is built like a tendon, of parallel collagen bundles, but spread out as a sheet rather than gathered into a cord. It gives the muscle a wide grip on what it pulls.

Question 2349. Which of these is an aponeurosis: the calcaneal tendon, the fibrous sheet in the palm of the hand, the round cord behind the heel, or the anterior cruciate ligament of the knee? The answer is the fibrous sheet in the palm, which is the palmar aponeurosis. The others are a round tendon and a ligament.

Question 2350. Which aponeurosis turns its lower border inward to form the inguinal ligament along the groin? The answer is the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle. Its lower edge thickens and rolls back on itself to make the ligament. It also leaves a gap, the superficial ring, above the pubic bone.

Question 2351. A described dissection of the palm shows a thick triangular sheet of fibrous tissue running from the wrist to the bases of the fingers, with long tendons lying deep to it. Which structure is it? The answer is the palmar aponeurosis. It protects the tendons, vessels and nerves beneath it and anchors the skin so that the grip does not slip.

Question 2352. Which broad aponeurosis covers the top of the skull and is tugged from front and back by the muscles of the scalp: the epicranial aponeurosis, the thoracolumbar fascia, the linea alba, or the plantar aponeurosis? The answer is the epicranial aponeurosis, also called the galea. The frontalis pulls it forward and the occipitalis pulls it back.

Question 2353. Which deep sheet of fibrous tissue in the lower back gives attachment to the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis, and lies behind the muscles that straighten the spine? The answer is the thoracolumbar fascia. It stretches between the lower ribs, the spine and the pelvis, and it also wraps the deep muscles of the back.

Question 2354. True or false: an aponeurosis is a flattened tendon. The answer is true. It is made of the same parallel collagen bundles as a tendon but spread as a broad sheet. The aponeurosis of the external oblique and the plantar aponeurosis of the foot are two familiar examples.

Question 2355. Name two aponeuroses of the body, spoken as a list, one in the abdominal wall and one in the sole of the foot. The answer is the aponeurosis of the external oblique and the plantar aponeurosis. The first helps to hold the abdominal contents in place. The second runs from the heel to the toes and supports the arch.

Question 2356. A constructed illustration: a bulge appears in the groin above the pubic bone when a person coughs, and it passes through a gap in a flat fibrous sheet. Which sheet is it, and what is the gap called? The answer is the aponeurosis of the external oblique, and the gap is the superficial inguinal ring. The illustration is described rather than real.

Question 2357. Which midline band of fibrous tissue in the abdomen is formed by the interlocking aponeuroses of the two sides, and why is it a convenient line of approach? The answer is the linea alba. It runs from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis and carries few vessels, so it can be opened with little bleeding. It is the seam of the abdominal wall.

Question 2358. A described section through the sole of the foot shows a thick band of fibrous tissue running from the heel to the heads of the metatarsal bones, with short muscles deep to it. Which structure is it, and what does it do? The answer is the plantar aponeurosis, which supports the arch and holds the parts of the foot together.

Question 2359. Which structures lie deep to the bicipital aponeurosis where it passes from the tendon of the biceps to the deep fascia of the forearm? The answer is the brachial artery and the median nerve. The sheet therefore protects them, and the pulse of the artery can be felt just medial to the tendon.

Question 2360. A constructed illustration: a person has pain beneath the heel that is worst on the first steps of the morning and eases with walking. Which structure is the seat of the pain? The answer is the plantar aponeurosis where it takes hold of the heel bone. The illustration is constructed rather than drawn from a real patient, and its care belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 2361. Which pair belongs together: the aponeurosis of the external oblique with the inguinal ligament, the palmar aponeurosis with the sole, the epicranial aponeurosis with the abdominal wall, or the plantar aponeurosis with the palm? The answer is the aponeurosis of the external oblique with the inguinal ligament, which is formed by its rolled lower border.

Question 2362. Above the umbilicus, which aponeuroses form the front of the sheath around the rectus abdominis? The answer is the aponeurosis of the external oblique together with the front layer of the internal oblique. The back of the sheath at that level is formed by the other layer of the internal oblique and by the aponeurosis of the transversus abdominis.

Question 2363. What name is given to the attachment of a muscle that is relatively fixed and usually lies nearer the trunk? The answer is the origin. When the muscle contracts it pulls the movable end toward this fixed end. If the far end is held still instead, the two ends exchange their parts.

Question 2364. From which two points does the biceps brachii arise: the olecranon and the radial tuberosity, the coracoid process and the supraglenoid tubercle, the acromion and the spine of the scapula, or the lateral epicondyle and the ulna? The answer is the coracoid process, for the short head, and the supraglenoid tubercle, for the long head.

Question 2365. From which bone does the gastrocnemius arise, above the knee joint? The answer is the femur, from the back of its two condyles. Because it starts above the knee, the gastrocnemius crosses that joint and can help to bend it. The soleus arises below the knee from the tibia and the fibula.

Question 2366. A described dissection of the back shows a large flat muscle arising from the skull, the ligament of the neck and the spines of the thoracic vertebrae, and ending on the shoulder blade and the outer third of the clavicle. Which muscle is it? The answer is the trapezius. Its two sides together make a diamond across the upper back.

Question 2367. Which broad muscle arises from the spines of the lower thoracic vertebrae, the lumbar vertebrae and the iliac crest, and then turns forward to end on the humerus? The answer is the latissimus dorsi. Its fibers converge into a flat tendon that twists to reach the front of the upper arm. It forms the back wall of the armpit.

Question 2368. Which of these muscles arises from the sternum, the upper six costal cartilages and the medial half of the clavicle: the pectoralis major, the pectoralis minor, the serratus anterior, or the trapezius? The answer is the pectoralis major. Its fibers converge on the humerus and form the front wall of the armpit.

Question 2369. Which muscle arises from the xiphoid process, the lower six ribs and the upper lumbar vertebrae, and forms the floor of the thoracic cavity? The answer is the diaphragm. Its muscular fibers arch up from those origins and end in a central tendon. The two crura, its lowest parts, arise from the lumbar vertebrae.

Question 2370. Which strap-like muscle arises from the pubic crest and the pubic symphysis and runs straight up the front of the abdomen? The answer is the rectus abdominis. It lies within a sheath formed by the aponeuroses of the flat muscles at each side, and its fibers are crossed by tendinous bands.

Question 2371. A constructed illustration: a person hangs from a bar and pulls the body upward until the chin reaches the hands. Which end of the elbow flexors is fixed in that action? The answer is the insertion, on the radius and the ulna, while the origin moves upward with the shoulders. The same muscle therefore reverses its two ends.

Question 2372. A muscle arises from the surface of the shoulder blade that lies against the ribs and passes around the joint to insert on the front of the upper end of the humerus. In which direction does it turn the arm? The answer is inward, toward the midline. Its line of pull lies in front of the joint, and the muscle described is the subscapularis.

Question 2373. Which pair belongs together: the trapezius with the spine of the shoulder blade, the latissimus dorsi with the coracoid process, the deltoid with the lesser trochanter, or the gastrocnemius with the radius? The answer is the trapezius with the spine of the shoulder blade. The other three are paired with bones they never touch.

Question 2374. Which statement about the reversal of origin and insertion is correct: it never happens, it happens only in the limbs, the same muscle can move either bone depending on which end is fixed, or it means the muscle has changed its nerve supply? The answer is that the same muscle can move either bone depending on which end is fixed. A chin-up lifts the body toward the bar by fixing the insertion.

Question 2375. Which of these decides whether an attachment is called the origin or the insertion: the size of the muscle, which end moves during the action, how deep the muscle lies, or the number of heads it has? The answer is which end moves during the action. The fixed end is the origin and the moving end is the insertion.

Question 2376. What name is given to the attachment of a muscle that moves when the muscle contracts and usually lies farther from the trunk? The answer is the insertion. It is pulled toward the fixed origin in the usual action. The two names describe a particular movement rather than a permanent label.

Question 2377. On which bony point does the biceps brachii insert: the radial tuberosity, the olecranon, the coronoid process of the ulna, or the styloid process of the radius? The answer is the radial tuberosity. Because it pulls on the radius, the muscle bends the elbow and also turns the palm upward.

Question 2378. Which muscle inserts on the deltoid tuberosity of the humerus, roughly halfway down its shaft? The answer is the deltoid. It arises from the outer third of the clavicle, the acromion and the spine of the scapula, and it lifts the arm sideways.

Question 2379. A described dissection of the front of the thigh shows a broad flat tendon that wraps the kneecap and continues below it to the shin. Which muscle group ends in that tendon? The answer is the quadriceps femoris. Its four parts share one tendon, which holds the kneecap and then continues as the patellar ligament to the tibial tuberosity.

Question 2380. A described dissection of the inner side of the knee shows three tendons inserting in a row on the upper part of the tibia, fanned out like the foot of a goose. Which muscles form that group? The answer is the sartorius, the gracilis and the semitendinosus. The group is named the pes anserinus, which means the goose foot.

Question 2381. True or false: when a muscle contracts in the usual way, its insertion is pulled toward its origin. The answer is true. The origin is the fixed point at that moment and the insertion moves toward it. When the far end is held instead, the pull turns the other way around.

Question 2382. Which muscle inserts on the lateral lip of the intertubercular groove of the humerus, after arising from the sternum, the upper costal cartilages and the clavicle? The answer is the pectoralis major. Its fibers twist as they approach the bone, so the lower fibers insert above the upper ones.

Question 2383. Which of these muscles inserts on the lesser trochanter of the femur: the iliopsoas, the gluteus medius, the adductor magnus, or the piriformis? The answer is the iliopsoas. It arises from the lumbar vertebrae and the inner surface of the ilium and bends the hip as it pulls on that small knob.

Question 2384. Name two muscles that insert on the coracoid process of the scapula, spoken as a list. The answer is the short head of the biceps brachii and the coracobrachialis. The pectoralis minor also ends there. All three pull on the same small bony hook.

Question 2385. Which ridge on the back of the femur receives the insertion of the gluteus maximus? The answer is the gluteal tuberosity. It lies below the greater trochanter and blends with the upper part of the linea aspera. The gluteus maximus is the largest muscle of the body and straightens the hip.

Question 2386. A constructed illustration: a person stumbles and cannot straighten the knee, and the band that should run from the kneecap to the shin is no longer felt. Which structure has torn? The answer is the patellar ligament, the continuation of the quadriceps tendon. It carries the pull of the thigh muscles from the kneecap to the tibial tuberosity.

Question 2387. Where does the trapezius insert: on the lateral border of the scapula, on the coracoid process, on the spine of the scapula with the acromion and the outer third of the clavicle, or on the lesser tubercle of the humerus? The answer is the spine of the scapula, the acromion and the outer third of the clavicle.

Question 2388. Which statement about the lower attachments of the diaphragm is correct: it is a purely tendinous sheet, it reaches the lumbar vertebrae through two muscular crura, it attaches only to the sternum, or it has no bony attachment at all? The answer is that it reaches the lumbar vertebrae through two crura. Its other origins are the xiphoid process and the lower six ribs.

Question 2389. What name is given to the muscle that is chiefly responsible for a movement, as opposed to the muscle that opposes it? The answer is the agonist, also called the prime mover. The muscle that opposes the movement is the antagonist, and it usually yields by lengthening under control.

Question 2390. With the palm facing up, which muscle is the prime mover in bending the elbow: the triceps brachii, the biceps brachii, the anconeus, or the extensor digitorum? The answer is the biceps brachii. It is at its strongest in that position because it also turns the palm upward. The brachialis beneath it bends the elbow in any position.

Question 2391. True or false: the antagonist of a movement stays completely relaxed while the agonist works. The answer is false. The antagonist usually lengthens under control and steadies the movement, and it may also shorten to brake it. A muscle that is paralyzed is different from one that is simply not the prime mover.

Question 2392. What name is given to a muscle that helps an agonist by preventing an unwanted movement, such as a muscle that stops the wrist from bending while the fingers close? The answer is a synergist. A fixator is different: it steadies the origin of the prime mover, as the shoulder blade is steadied when the arm is raised.

Question 2393. A described cross section through the middle of the arm shows two muscles in front and one large muscle behind, separated by a single bone. Which muscles are they? The answer is the biceps brachii with the brachialis in front and the triceps brachii behind. The humerus lies between them and gives the section its shape.

Question 2394. A constructed illustration: a person lifts a heavy box with a bent elbow and feels a tearing pain at the front of the arm. Which muscle is the prime mover that has been strained? The answer is the biceps brachii, with the brachialis lying beneath it. The illustration is described rather than real, and its care belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 2395. Which pair belongs together: the supraspinatus with the first part of raising the arm, the teres major with raising the arm above the head, the subscapularis with turning the arm outward, or the infraspinatus with turning the arm inward? The answer is the supraspinatus with the first part of raising the arm.

Question 2396. Which muscles act as fixators of the shoulder blade while the arm is raised above the head? The answer is the trapezius and the serratus anterior, helped by the rhomboids. They turn the shoulder blade upward so that its socket faces the arm, which lets the arm pass the vertical.

Question 2397. A constructed illustration: a person cannot raise the arm sideways beyond the horizontal, and there is pain over the top of the shoulder. Which tendons are chiefly involved? The answer is the tendons of the rotator cuff, above all the supraspinatus as it passes under the acromion. The illustration is described, and its care belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 2398. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the shoulder shows a gap in the dark band that passes beneath the bony arch of the acromion. Which tendon has torn? The answer is the tendon of the supraspinatus, one of the four muscles of the rotator cuff. That tendon is the one most often injured as it passes through the narrow space.

Question 2399. A muscle arises from the front of the pelvis just above the hip socket and runs down in front of the hip joint to end on the shin. About which axis does its pull act, and which movement results? The answer is the transverse axis, producing flexion of the hip, because the line of pull lies in front of the joint.

Question 2400. Which statement about the levers of the body is correct: most joints act as third class levers, most act as first class levers, most act as second class levers, or the body uses no levers? The answer is that most joints act as third class levers. The muscle pull lies between the joint and the load, which favors speed and range of movement over force.

To summarize, a skeletal muscle fiber is a long multinucleated cell whose surface membrane carries the signal inward along the T tubules to a sarcoplasmic reticulum that releases the calcium for contraction. Within each sarcomere the thin actin filaments slide past the thick myosin filaments, so the light band and the central H zone narrow while the A band keeps its length, and the three sheaths named the endomysium, the perimysium and the epimysium bind the fibers into fascicles and the fascicles into a belly. A tendon is dense regular collagen that joins muscle to bone, and an aponeurosis is the same tissue spread as a flat sheet. The origin is the fixed attachment and the insertion the moving one, though the two exchange roles when the far end is held, and an agonist carries out a movement while its antagonist, its synergists and its fixators govern it.
