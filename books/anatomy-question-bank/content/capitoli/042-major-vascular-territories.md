# Chapter 42 — Major Vascular Territories

This chapter maps the arterial territories of the body, the region each great vessel is responsible for and the border zones where two supplies meet. It follows the coronary arteries across the heart, the branches of the aortic arch into the head and the neck, the arteries of the brain and the spinal cord, and the vessels of the chest wall, the abdomen, the pelvis and both limbs. It holds one hundred questions, numbered from question four thousand one hundred and one, and every one gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind. A territory is described here by what it feeds, by the named branches it gives off, and by the anastomoses that keep a tissue alive when one supply fails. Described computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging studies appear among the questions, and after the first full mention the two are called CT and MRI. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 4101. Which artery normally supplies the anterior two thirds of the interventricular septum? The answer is the anterior interventricular artery. It runs down the front of the heart in the groove between the two ventricles and sends septal branches into the muscle. The posterior third of the septum is fed by the posterior interventricular artery.

Question 4102. In most hearts the artery to the sinoatrial node arises from which vessel: the right coronary artery, the circumflex artery, the anterior interventricular artery, or the posterior interventricular artery? The answer is the right coronary artery. Its nodal branch climbs near the top of the right atrium in about 60 per cent of people, and in the rest it comes from the circumflex artery.

Question 4103. Which artery gives off the branch that supplies the atrioventricular node in most hearts? The answer is the right coronary artery. The branch leaves it where the vessel turns at the crux of the heart, behind the meeting point of the four chambers. The node lies in the lower part of the atrial septum, just above that turn.

Question 4104. Which artery supplies most of the lateral wall of the left ventricle: the right coronary artery, the anterior interventricular artery, the circumflex artery, or the posterior interventricular artery? The answer is the circumflex artery. It runs around the left border in the groove between the left atrium and the left ventricle and gives off marginal branches to that wall.

Question 4105. A described coronary angiogram fills a vessel that runs in the groove between the right atrium and the right ventricle and then turns at the lower border of the heart. Which artery is filled? The answer is the right coronary artery. That groove and that turn at the crux identify it, and the vessel it meets there is the circumflex artery.

Question 4106. In the posterior interventricular groove, which vein runs beside the artery of the same name? The answer is the middle cardiac vein. It climbs with the posterior interventricular artery and ends by turning to the left into the coronary sinus, which opens into the right atrium. The great cardiac vein keeps the anterior groove company instead.

Question 4107. A described tracing shows damage to the inferior wall and the posterior third of the interventricular septum. Which artery is most likely blocked: the anterior interventricular artery, the circumflex artery, the right coronary artery, or the left coronary artery? The answer is the right coronary artery, which in most hearts gives the posterior interventricular artery. That branch supplies the inferior wall and the posterior septum.

Question 4108. Which pair belongs together: the anterior interventricular artery with the front of the left ventricle, the circumflex artery with the right ventricle, the right coronary artery with the side of the left ventricle, or the posterior interventricular artery with the front of the septum? The answer is the anterior interventricular artery with the front of the left ventricle. It runs down the front of the heart and feeds that wall.

Question 4109. True or false: in a right dominant circulation the posterior interventricular artery arises from the right coronary artery. The answer is true. The right coronary artery reaches the crux and turns down the back of the heart. The heart is right dominant in most people, and the arrangement is a normal variant.

Question 4110. A described MRI study of the heart shows late enhancement of the inferior wall and the posterior part of the septum. Which territory is that? The answer is the territory of the right coronary artery. Late enhancement marks scar, and the inferior wall with the posterior septum belongs to the posterior interventricular branch. The lateral wall would belong to the circumflex artery.

Question 4111. Which artery runs in the groove between the left atrium and the left ventricle: the right coronary artery, the anterior interventricular artery, the circumflex artery, or the posterior interventricular artery? The answer is the circumflex artery. That groove is the left atrioventricular groove, and the vessel in it is the continuation of the left coronary artery.

Question 4112. A constructed illustration: during an operation on the mitral valve the surgeon stays close to the left atrioventricular groove. Which artery is at risk in that described position, and what does it supply? The answer is the circumflex artery. It runs in that groove, and its branches supply the lateral wall of the left ventricle.

Question 4113. Which two vessels arise from the brachiocephalic trunk? The answer is the right common carotid artery and the right subclavian artery. The trunk is the first branch of the aortic arch and divides behind the right sternoclavicular joint. On the left the two vessels arise separately from the arch.

Question 4114. Which vessel is the third and last branch of the aortic arch: the brachiocephalic trunk, the left common carotid artery, the left subclavian artery, or the right subclavian artery? The answer is the left subclavian artery. It passes under the clavicle and becomes the axillary artery at the outer border of the first rib.

Question 4115. Which arteries arise from the subclavian arteries and unite to form the basilar artery? The answer is the two vertebral arteries. Each climbs through the openings in the transverse processes of the cervical vertebrae and enters the skull through the foramen magnum. They join at the lower border of the pons.

Question 4116. A described CT angiogram of the upper chest shows one trunk dividing behind the right sternoclavicular joint and two separate vessels arising farther along the arch. Which arrangement of the arch branches is shown? The answer is a brachiocephalic trunk, a left common carotid artery and a left subclavian artery. That is the usual pattern, and the trunk divides into the right common carotid and the right subclavian.

Question 4117. A described angiogram shows narrowing of the left subclavian artery before the origin of the vertebral artery. Which vessel fills backward: the basilar artery, the left vertebral artery, the left common carotid artery, or the left internal thoracic artery? The answer is the left vertebral artery. Blood reaches it across the basilar artery from the other side, so the arm draws blood from the head.

Question 4118. Which group of arteries arises from the thyrocervical trunk: the inferior thyroid, suprascapular and transverse cervical arteries; the vertebral and internal thoracic arteries; the costocervical trunk and the deep cervical artery; or the superior thyroid and facial arteries? The answer is the inferior thyroid, suprascapular and transverse cervical arteries.

Question 4119. Within the carotid sheath, which structure lies immediately lateral to the common carotid artery? The answer is the internal jugular vein. The sheath also contains the vagus nerve, which lies behind and between the two vessels. The vein therefore lies lateral to the artery.

Question 4120. True or false: the left common carotid artery arises directly from the aortic arch. The answer is true. The arch gives the brachiocephalic trunk first, then the left common carotid artery, then the left subclavian artery. The right common carotid takes its origin from the trunk instead.

Question 4121. Which artery supplies most of the lateral surface of a cerebral hemisphere: the anterior cerebral artery, the middle cerebral artery, the posterior cerebral artery, or the basilar artery? The answer is the middle cerebral artery. It runs in the lateral sulcus and covers the insula and the operculum. The anterior cerebral artery supplies the medial surface instead.

Question 4122. Which cerebral artery supplies the medial surface of the hemisphere, including the part of the motor cortex for the leg? The answer is the anterior cerebral artery. It runs in the interhemispheric fissure above the corpus callosum. Its territory includes the paracentral lobule, so a blockage there weakens the opposite leg.

Question 4123. Which artery supplies the occipital lobe and the lower part of the temporal lobe? The answer is the posterior cerebral artery. It arises from the basilar artery at the top of the pons, and it also sends branches to the thalamus and the midbrain. Its blockage removes part of the visual field of both eyes.

Question 4124. A described CT scan of the head shows a new area of low density over the lateral frontal and parietal operculum, with the medial surface spared. Which artery is blocked? The answer is the middle cerebral artery. Its cortical branches cover the lateral surface, and the medial surface belongs to the anterior cerebral artery.

Question 4125. A described MRI study shows a small infarct in the posterior limb of the internal capsule, while the cortex of the hemisphere looks normal. Which group of vessels is blocked? The answer is the lenticulostriate arteries. They arise from the middle cerebral artery and pass deep to supply the basal ganglia and the internal capsule.

Question 4126. A constructed illustration: a person reports that the right half of the visual field of each eye has been lost. Which arterial territory is involved? The answer is the territory of the left posterior cerebral artery. Damage to the left occipital cortex removes the right half of the visual field of both eyes. The middle cerebral artery serves the lateral surface instead.

Question 4127. Which vessel supplies the medial part of the medulla oblongata: the posterior inferior cerebellar artery, the anterior spinal artery, the superior cerebellar artery, or the anterior inferior cerebellar artery? The answer is the anterior spinal artery. It is formed by branches of the two vertebral arteries and runs down the front of the medulla and the cord. The posterior inferior cerebellar artery supplies the lateral medulla.

Question 4128. Which artery arises from the vertebral artery and supplies the lateral part of the medulla oblongata and the inferior surface of the cerebellum? The answer is the posterior inferior cerebellar artery. Its blockage causes the lateral medullary syndrome, with loss of pain and temperature sense on the opposite side and unsteadiness. The anterior inferior cerebellar artery comes from the basilar artery instead.

Question 4129. Which vessel supplies the middle cerebellar peduncle and the anteroinferior surface of the cerebellum: the posterior inferior cerebellar artery, the anterior inferior cerebellar artery, the superior cerebellar artery, or the anterior spinal artery? The answer is the anterior inferior cerebellar artery. It arises from the basilar artery and runs to the border between the pons and the cerebellum.

Question 4130. A described MRI study shows restricted diffusion in the paramedian part of the pons on both sides. Which group of vessels is blocked? The answer is the paramedian branches of the basilar artery. They pass straight into the pons to supply the corticospinal tracts and the medial lemniscus. The circumferential branches of the same vessel would give a lateral pattern instead.

Question 4131. Which small artery arises from the anterior cerebral artery and supplies the anterior part of the internal capsule and the head of the caudate nucleus? The answer is the recurrent artery of Heubner. It turns back toward the base of the brain after leaving the anterior cerebral artery. The lenticulostriate arteries of the middle cerebral artery supply the rest of that region.

Question 4132. True or false: the circle of Willis is complete in every healthy adult. The answer is false. The circle is complete in only a minority of people, and a missing or duplicated segment is a common and harmless variant. The communicating arteries in particular vary in size.

Question 4133. A constructed illustration: after a long period of very low blood pressure, a scan shows damage in a band along the upper lateral surface of both hemispheres, sparing the deep structures. Which zones have suffered? The answer is the watershed zones between the territories of the anterior and middle cerebral arteries. Those border regions lie farthest from both supplies, so they fail first when the pressure falls.

Question 4134. Which artery joins the two anterior cerebral arteries across the midline: the anterior communicating artery, the posterior communicating artery, the basilar artery, or the ophthalmic artery? The answer is the anterior communicating artery. It is a short vessel between the two anterior cerebral arteries at the front of the circle of Willis.

Question 4135. Which vessel connects the internal carotid artery with the posterior cerebral artery: the ophthalmic artery, the anterior choroidal artery, the posterior communicating artery, or the middle cerebral artery? The answer is the posterior communicating artery. It runs backward from the internal carotid artery to join the posterior cerebral artery and completes the side of the circle.

Question 4136. A described angiogram of the back of the brain shows a single midline vessel that gives off paired branches to the pons and ends by dividing into two vessels that curve around the midbrain. Which vessel is shown and what are its two endings? The answer is the basilar artery, which ends as the two posterior cerebral arteries. Its paired branches to the pons are the paramedian and circumferential arteries.

Question 4137. Which group of arteries supplies the thalamus and the midbrain: the lenticulostriate arteries, the thalamoperforating branches of the posterior cerebral artery, the recurrent artery of Heubner, or the anterior choroidal artery? The answer is the thalamoperforating branches of the posterior cerebral artery. They pass upward through the interpeduncular fossa. The lenticulostriate arteries supply the basal ganglia and the internal capsule instead.

Question 4138. On a described coronal section of the brain, a vessel lies in the deep midline cleft between the two hemispheres, above the corpus callosum. Which vessel is it, and which surface of the hemisphere does it supply? The answer is the anterior cerebral artery, and it supplies the medial surface. In that plane it lies in the interhemispheric fissure.

Question 4139. Name the two terminal branches of the internal carotid artery, spoken as a list. The answer is the anterior cerebral artery and the middle cerebral artery. Both arise at the top of the internal carotid artery after it leaves the cavernous sinus.

Question 4140. Which branches of the basilar artery supply the medial part of the pons: the paramedian branches, the posterior cerebral arteries, the superior cerebellar arteries, or the posterior inferior cerebellar arteries? The answer is the paramedian branches. They enter the front of the pons in the midline and supply the corticospinal tracts and the medial lemniscus.

Question 4141. Which single artery supplies the anterior two thirds of the spinal cord? The answer is the anterior spinal artery. It is formed by the union of a branch from each vertebral artery and runs down the front of the cord in the anterior median fissure. The posterior columns are supplied by a different pair of vessels.

Question 4142. Which vessel is the great radicular artery that reinforces the anterior spinal artery in the lower part of the cord: the artery of Adamkiewicz, the posterior spinal artery, the artery of the ligamentum teres, or the median sacral artery? The answer is the artery of Adamkiewicz. It usually arises from a lower intercostal or upper lumbar artery on the left and reaches the cord with a spinal nerve root.

Question 4143. A constructed illustration: a described cord injury spares the posterior columns and the dorsal horns but removes pain and temperature sense below the level and weakens both legs. Which vessel is blocked? The answer is the anterior spinal artery. Its territory is the anterior two thirds of the cord, which carries the motor tracts and the fibers for pain and temperature.

Question 4144. Name the paired arteries that descend along the back of the spinal cord and the part of the cord they supply. The answer is the two posterior spinal arteries, which supply the posterior columns and the dorsal horns. They are small and run one on each side of the posterior median sulcus.

Question 4145. Which artery descends behind the costal cartilages just lateral to the sternum? The answer is the internal thoracic artery. It arises from the first part of the subclavian artery and ends by dividing into the musculophrenic artery and the superior epigastric artery. Its branches include the anterior intercostal arteries.

Question 4146. From which vessel do the posterior intercostal arteries of the third to the eleventh spaces arise: the subclavian artery, the descending thoracic aorta, the internal thoracic artery, or the costocervical trunk? The answer is the descending thoracic aorta. The first two spaces take theirs from the supreme intercostal artery instead.

Question 4147. A described CT scan of the chest wall shows an artery running downward behind the costal cartilages a little lateral to the sternum, with branches passing between the ribs. Which artery is shown? The answer is the internal thoracic artery. Its anterior intercostal branches run in the spaces, and it ends as the superior epigastric artery below.

Question 4148. A constructed illustration: a segment of the internal thoracic artery is removed for use elsewhere, and the front of the chest wall must still be supplied. Which vessels take over that described territory? The answer is the posterior intercostal arteries with the lateral thoracic and thoracoacromial branches. They anastomose with the anterior intercostal arteries that come from the internal thoracic artery.

Question 4149. Which statement about the blood supply of the lungs is correct: the bronchial arteries carry deoxygenated blood for gas exchange, the pulmonary arteries nourish the bronchi, the bronchial arteries supply the bronchi and the lung tissue, or the pulmonary arteries arise from the descending aorta? The answer is that the bronchial arteries supply the bronchi and the tissue of the lung. The pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood to the alveoli for gas exchange.

Question 4150. Which artery supplies the first and second posterior intercostal spaces, and from which trunk does it arise? The answer is the supreme intercostal artery, from the costocervical trunk of the subclavian artery. The remaining posterior intercostal spaces are supplied by the descending thoracic aorta.

Question 4151. Which three arteries arise from the celiac trunk? The answer is the left gastric artery, the splenic artery and the common hepatic artery. The trunk leaves the front of the abdominal aorta just below the diaphragm and supplies the foregut. The superior mesenteric artery supplies the midgut below it.

Question 4152. Which part of the gut lies in the territory of the superior mesenteric artery: the stomach, the midgut from the distal duodenum to the proximal two thirds of the transverse colon, the descending colon, or the upper rectum? The answer is the midgut from the distal duodenum to the proximal two thirds of the transverse colon. Its branches are the jejunal, ileal, ileocolic, right colic and middle colic arteries.

Question 4153. Which three named arteries arise from the inferior mesenteric artery? The answer is the left colic artery, the sigmoid arteries and the superior rectal artery. The trunk leaves the aorta at the third lumbar vertebra and supplies the hindgut from the distal transverse colon downward.

Question 4154. Which two arteries arise from the gastroduodenal artery? The answer is the right gastroepiploic artery and the superior pancreaticoduodenal artery. The trunk itself comes from the common hepatic artery. The right gastric artery arises from the proper hepatic artery instead, so the two gastric vessels have different parents.

Question 4155. A constructed illustration: after a period of low blood flow, a described scan shows damage at the splenic flexure of the colon while the rest of the bowel looks normal. Which border zone has failed? The answer is the watershed between the superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric territories. The middle colic artery and the left colic artery meet there, and that meeting point is the farthest from both supplies.

Question 4156. A described CT angiogram of the upper abdomen shows one short trunk dividing into three branches, one of which runs to the left along the upper border of the pancreas. Which trunk and which branch are shown? The answer is the celiac trunk with its splenic branch. The other two branches are the left gastric and the common hepatic arteries.

Question 4157. Which statement about the arteries of the stomach is correct: the left gastric artery arises from the common hepatic artery, the right gastric artery arises from the proper hepatic artery, the short gastric arteries arise from the left gastric artery, or the right gastroepiploic artery arises from the splenic artery? The answer is that the right gastric artery arises from the proper hepatic artery. The left gastric artery comes from the celiac trunk, and the short gastric arteries come from the splenic artery.

Question 4158. At which level of the spine do the renal arteries arise from the abdominal aorta, and which kidney does the higher artery supply? The answer is the level of the second lumbar vertebra, and the right artery is the longer and usually the lower. The right kidney lies lower because the liver occupies the space above it.

Question 4159. The gonadal arteries arise from the abdominal aorta at which level relative to the renal arteries? The answer is below them, at the level of the second lumbar vertebra. They descend a long way to reach the ovary or the testis. The left gonadal vein drains into the left renal vein, while the right drains into the inferior vena cava.

Question 4160. A constructed illustration: a described angiogram shows narrowing of the celiac trunk where the crura of the diaphragm cross above it, and the narrowing changes with breathing. Which structure is compressing the artery? The answer is the median arcuate ligament. That fibrous band joins the two crura of the diaphragm over the front of the aorta.

Question 4161. The portal vein is formed by the union of which two veins: the superior and inferior mesenteric veins, the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein, the hepatic veins and the inferior vena cava, or the left and right gastric veins? The answer is the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein. They join behind the neck of the pancreas, and the portal vein then runs to the liver.

Question 4162. Name two organs of the upper abdomen that are supplied through the celiac trunk, spoken as a list. The answer is the stomach and the spleen. The left gastric and splenic arteries carry blood to them, and the common hepatic artery supplies the liver and the upper part of the duodenum.

Question 4163. Into which vessel do the hepatic veins drain: the portal vein, the inferior vena cava, the superior mesenteric vein, or the splenic vein? The answer is the inferior vena cava. The hepatic veins leave the back of the liver and open into the inferior vena cava just below the diaphragm, so the liver has one venous outlet.

Question 4164. A described CT scan of the lower chest shows swollen veins in the wall of the lower esophagus and a large left gastric vein. Which condition do those collateral channels indicate, and where is the obstruction? The answer is portal hypertension with obstruction in the liver. Blood finds its way from the portal vein through the left gastric vein into the esophageal veins and then to the azygos system.

Question 4165. Which veins drain the rectum into the portal system, and which drain it into the systemic system? The answer is the superior rectal vein into the portal system and the middle and inferior rectal veins into the systemic system. That junction between the two systems is one place where collateral channels open when the liver is obstructed.

Question 4166. Which pair belongs together: the splenic flexure with the meeting of the superior and inferior mesenteric territories, the rectosigmoid junction with the meeting of the celiac and superior mesenteric territories, the ileocecal junction with the meeting of the inferior mesenteric and systemic territories, or the duodenum with the meeting of the two iliac territories? The answer is the splenic flexure with the meeting of the superior and inferior mesenteric territories.

Question 4167. Which named artery runs along the inner border of the colon and links the branches of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries? The answer is the marginal artery of Drummond. It lies in the mesenteric border of the colon and gives it a continuous supply. Small branches from it, the straight arteries, enter the wall of the bowel.

Question 4168. From which vessel does the inferior epigastric artery arise: the internal iliac artery, the external iliac artery, the femoral artery, or the obturator artery? The answer is the external iliac artery. It arises just above the inguinal ligament and climbs behind the rectus abdominis muscle to meet the superior epigastric artery.

Question 4169. True or false: the marginal artery of Drummond runs along the colon and connects the territories of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries. The answer is true. It lies in the mesenteric border of the colon from the cecum to the rectum. The connection is the reason a gradual narrowing of one of the two trunks may pass unnoticed.

Question 4170. A described CT angiogram shows a branch arising from the superior mesenteric artery and climbing to the right side of the porta hepatis, while the common hepatic artery gives no right hepatic branch. Which variation is shown? The answer is a replaced right hepatic artery from the superior mesenteric artery. It is a common variant, so the surgeon expects it before operating in that region.

Question 4171. Which small artery arises from the back of the abdominal aorta, just above its bifurcation, and runs down the front of the sacrum? The answer is the median sacral artery. It is the continuation of the aorta in the midline and is the counterpart of the median sacral vein. The common iliac arteries arise at the same level.

Question 4172. Which pair belongs together: the celiac trunk with the spleen, the superior mesenteric artery with the rectum, the inferior mesenteric artery with the stomach, or the inferior epigastric artery with the testis? The answer is the celiac trunk with the spleen, through the splenic artery. The superior mesenteric artery serves the midgut and the inferior mesenteric artery serves the hindgut.

Question 4173. Which three arteries form the posterior division of the internal iliac artery? The answer is the iliolumbar artery, the lateral sacral arteries and the superior gluteal artery. They supply the back wall of the pelvis, the sacrum and the buttock. The anterior division supplies the viscera and the perineum.

Question 4174. Through which opening does the superior gluteal artery leave the pelvis: the greater sciatic foramen above the piriformis muscle, the greater sciatic foramen below the piriformis muscle, the lesser sciatic foramen, or the obturator canal? The answer is the greater sciatic foramen above the piriformis muscle. The inferior gluteal artery leaves below that muscle.

Question 4175. A described CT scan of the buttock shows a vessel emerging below the piriformis muscle and dividing into branches that pass into the gluteus maximus. Which artery is shown? The answer is the inferior gluteal artery. The superior gluteal artery emerges above the same muscle and supplies the gluteus medius and minimus instead.

Question 4176. A described CT scan of the pelvis follows a vessel through the obturator canal into the medial part of the thigh, where it lies between the adductor muscles. Which artery is shown? The answer is the obturator artery. It arises from the anterior division of the internal iliac artery and supplies the medial compartment of the thigh.

Question 4177. A constructed illustration: bleeding follows a described injury to the perineum near the ischial spine. Which artery is most likely torn, and where does it run? The answer is the internal pudendal artery, which hooks around the ischial spine and then travels in the pudendal canal. It supplies the perineum and the erectile tissues.

Question 4178. Which artery is the persistent proximal part of the umbilical artery: the superior vesical artery, the inferior vesical artery, the middle rectal artery, or the obturator artery? The answer is the superior vesical artery. The part of the umbilical artery beyond it closes after birth and becomes the medial umbilical ligament. It supplies the upper part of the bladder.

Question 4179. Which two named branches arise from the second part of the axillary artery? The answer is the thoracoacromial artery and the lateral thoracic artery. The first part gives the superior thoracic artery, and the third part gives the subscapular artery and the two circumflex humeral arteries.

Question 4180. Which artery winds around the surgical neck of the humerus together with the axillary nerve: the anterior circumflex humeral artery, the posterior circumflex humeral artery, the profunda brachii artery, or the suprascapular artery? The answer is the posterior circumflex humeral artery. It passes through the quadrangular space with the nerve, and together they can be injured by a fracture there.

Question 4181. Which artery runs with the radial nerve in the spiral groove of the humerus? The answer is the profunda brachii artery. It arises from the brachial artery and passes backward and downward with the nerve. A fracture of the shaft of the humerus can injure both.

Question 4182. A described arteriogram of the hand shows an arch across the palm that is fed mainly by the artery on the little finger side. Which arch is shown, and which artery completes it? The answer is the superficial palmar arch, completed by the superficial palmar branch of the radial artery. The deep arch is fed mainly by the radial artery.

Question 4183. A constructed illustration: before a procedure at the wrist, the clinician compresses one artery and then the other while the hand is opened and closed. What is being tested? The answer is the collateral circulation of the hand between the radial and ulnar arteries. The two palmar arches join them, and the test shows whether the hand keeps its supply when either vessel is compressed.

Question 4184. Which artery supplies most of the deep palmar arch: the ulnar artery, the radial artery, the median artery, or the anterior interosseous artery? The answer is the radial artery. Its deep palmar branch forms most of the deep arch, and the deep branch of the ulnar artery completes it. The superficial arch is formed mainly by the ulnar artery.

Question 4185. Which artery arises from the radial artery and supplies the thumb? The answer is the princeps pollicis artery. It arises as the radial artery turns into the palm and divides into branches along the thumb. The thumb therefore receives its main supply from the radial side of the hand.

Question 4186. Which artery supplies the supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles, and from which trunk does it arise? The answer is the suprascapular artery, from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery. It passes over the transverse scapular ligament into the back of the shoulder. Its territory anastomoses with the circumflex scapular and dorsal scapular arteries around the scapula.

Question 4187. If the axillary artery is tied just below the clavicle, by which route does blood reach the arm: through the scapular anastomosis between the suprascapular, dorsal scapular and circumflex scapular arteries, through the internal thoracic artery alone, through the intercostal arteries, or through the cephalic vein? The answer is through the scapular anastomosis. That network bypasses the blockage.

Question 4188. In the cubital fossa, where does the brachial artery lie relative to the tendon of the biceps and to the median nerve? The answer is that the artery lies medial to the tendon and lateral to the median nerve. The nerve is the most medial of the three. The artery divides into the radial and ulnar arteries at the lower border of the fossa.

Question 4189. Which three groups of branches arise from the profunda femoris artery? The answer is the lateral circumflex femoral artery, the medial circumflex femoral artery and the perforating arteries. The trunk arises from the femoral artery in the femoral triangle and is the main supply to the muscles of the thigh.

Question 4190. Into which two vessels does the popliteal artery divide at the lower border of the popliteus muscle: the femoral and profunda femoris arteries, the anterior tibial artery and the tibioperoneal trunk, the dorsalis pedis and the plantar arteries, or the fibular and the great saphenous arteries? The answer is the anterior tibial artery and the tibioperoneal trunk, which divides into the posterior tibial and fibular arteries.

Question 4191. Which artery continues in front of the ankle as the dorsalis pedis artery, and where is its pulse felt? The answer is the anterior tibial artery. Its pulse is felt on the dorsum of the foot between the tendons of the extensor hallucis longus and the extensor digitorum longus. The posterior tibial artery passes behind the medial malleolus instead.

Question 4192. A described arteriogram of the leg shows three vessels descending between the bones, one in front and two behind. Which three arteries are shown? The answer is the anterior tibial artery in front and the posterior tibial and fibular arteries behind. All three come from the popliteal artery, directly or through the tibioperoneal trunk.

Question 4193. A constructed illustration: a described fracture of the neck of the femur is followed by loss of blood supply to the femoral head. Which artery is most likely disrupted? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral artery. Its retinacular branches climb along the neck of the femur to the head, and they carry most of its supply. A branch of the obturator artery supplies only a small part.

Question 4194. Which four arteries form the cruciate anastomosis around the hip: the medial circumflex femoral, the lateral circumflex femoral, the inferior gluteal and the first perforating arteries; the obturator, the superior gluteal and the two circumflex iliac arteries; the femoral, the popliteal and the two tibial arteries; or the internal pudendal and the two rectal arteries? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral, the lateral circumflex femoral, the inferior gluteal and the first perforating arteries.

Question 4195. Which artery supplies the lateral compartment of the leg and gives a branch that joins the anterior tibial artery? The answer is the fibular artery. It descends close to the fibula and gives the perforating branch that passes through the interosseous membrane. Its territory is the muscles that evert the foot.

Question 4196. True or false: the dorsalis pedis artery is the continuation of the anterior tibial artery. The answer is true. The anterior tibial artery crosses the front of the ankle and changes its name there. The dorsalis pedis artery then runs to the first space between the toes and helps to form the plantar arch.

Question 4197. Which pair belongs together: the anterior tibial artery with the front of the leg, the posterior tibial artery with the lateral compartment, the fibular artery with the sole of the foot, or the dorsalis pedis artery with the calf? The answer is the anterior tibial artery with the front of the leg. The posterior tibial artery supplies the sole through the plantar arteries, and the fibular artery supplies the lateral compartment.

Question 4198. A surgeon plans a flap of the rectus abdominis muscle for a described defect elsewhere. On which artery is the flap based: the superior epigastric artery, the deep inferior epigastric artery, the superficial epigastric artery, or the internal thoracic artery? The answer is the deep inferior epigastric artery, which enters the lower part of the muscle from the external iliac artery. Its territory reaches the upper part of the muscle as well.

Question 4199. Which artery supplies the latissimus dorsi muscle and forms the pedicle of a flap taken from it? The answer is the thoracodorsal artery. It arises from the subscapular artery and runs down the posterior wall of the axilla into the muscle. The muscle also receives small branches from the posterior intercostal arteries.

Question 4200. Which pair belongs together: the deltopectoral flap with perforators of the internal thoracic artery, the latissimus dorsi flap with the deep inferior epigastric artery, the rectus abdominis flap with the thoracodorsal artery, or the groin flap with the superior gluteal artery? The answer is the deltopectoral flap with perforators of the internal thoracic artery. The latissimus dorsi flap belongs to the thoracodorsal artery and the rectus abdominis flap to the deep inferior epigastric artery.

To summarize, each great artery is known by the territory it feeds. The right coronary artery gives the posterior interventricular branch in most hearts and supplies the inferior wall and the posterior septum, the anterior interventricular artery supplies the front of the left ventricle and the anterior two thirds of the septum, and the circumflex artery runs in the left atrioventricular groove. The aortic arch gives the brachiocephalic trunk, the left common carotid artery and the left subclavian artery, the two vertebral arteries join to form the basilar artery, and the internal carotid artery ends as the anterior and middle cerebral arteries. The middle cerebral artery covers the lateral surface, the anterior cerebral artery the medial surface with the leg area, and the posterior cerebral artery the occipital lobe, the thalamus and the midbrain, while the anterior spinal artery supplies the anterior two thirds of the cord. Below the diaphragm the celiac trunk serves the foregut, the superior mesenteric artery the midgut as far as the proximal two thirds of the transverse colon, and the inferior mesenteric artery the hindgut, with watersheds at the splenic flexure and the rectosigmoid junction and with portosystemic junctions in the rectum and the esophagus. In the limbs the profunda femoris and the medial circumflex femoral arteries carry the thigh and the femoral head, the popliteal artery ends in the anterior tibial artery and the tibioperoneal trunk, and the radial and ulnar arteries meet in the two palmar arches.
