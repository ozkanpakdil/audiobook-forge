# Chapter 27 — Neck

This chapter maps the neck, the narrow region between the base of the skull above and the thoracic inlet below that carries the airway, the food passage, the great vessels and the nerves between the head and the trunk. It works through the triangles of the neck and their boundaries, the muscles, the fascia and the spaces between the fascial layers, the arteries, the veins, the lymphatic channels and the cervical nerves, and it ends with the thyroid gland, the larynx and the root of the neck. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 2601. Which muscle divides each side of the neck into an anterior triangle and a posterior triangle? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid muscle. It runs from the upper part of the sternum and the medial part of the clavicle to the mastoid process behind the ear. Its diagonal course gives the neck its two great triangles.

Question 2602. Which broad muscle covers the back and the sides of the neck and takes its name from its shape? The answer is the trapezius. It arises from the base of the skull, the ligamentum nuchae and the spines of the thoracic vertebrae, and it inserts on the shoulder girdle. Its upper fibers elevate the shoulder.

Question 2603. Which of these structures forms the anterior boundary of the posterior triangle of the neck: the clavicle, the trapezius, the sternocleidomastoid, or the mandible? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid. The trapezius forms the posterior boundary and the clavicle the inferior boundary. The mandible lies far above, in the anterior triangle.

Question 2604. A described dissection shows the skin of the neck turned back, with a thin sheet of muscle lying immediately beneath it whose fibers run upward and medially across the whole front of the neck. Which muscle is described, and what does it do? The answer is the platysma. It tenses the skin of the neck and draws the lower lip and the corner of the mouth downward.

Question 2605. Which of these muscles lies below the hyoid bone and belongs to the infrahyoid group: the digastric, the stylohyoid, the mylohyoid, or the omohyoid? The answer is the omohyoid. Its inferior belly crosses the lower part of the posterior triangle and its superior belly lies in the anterior triangle. The other three lie above the hyoid bone.

Question 2606. Which muscle of the neck has two bellies joined by an intermediate tendon that is held to the hyoid bone by a loop of fascia? The answer is the digastric muscle. Its anterior belly lies in the submental triangle and its posterior belly in the submandibular triangle. The tendon pierces the stylohyoid muscle.

Question 2607. Which flat muscle forms the floor of the mouth and separates the sublingual space from the submandibular space? The answer is the mylohyoid. It runs from the mylohyoid line of the mandible to the body of the hyoid bone and meets its partner in a midline raphe. The anterior belly of the digastric lies below it.

Question 2608. Which nerve supplies the anterior belly of the digastric muscle: the facial nerve, the hypoglossal nerve, the nerve to the mylohyoid, or a branch of the cervical plexus? The answer is the nerve to the mylohyoid, a branch of the inferior alveolar nerve from the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve. The posterior belly of the same muscle is supplied by the facial nerve.

Question 2609. True or false: the posterior belly of the digastric muscle is supplied by the facial nerve. The answer is true. The facial nerve gives off its digastric branch as it leaves the stylomastoid foramen. The anterior belly has a different nerve supply, the nerve to the mylohyoid.

Question 2610. Which of these pairs belongs together: the mylohyoid muscle with the facial nerve, the mylohyoid muscle with the nerve to the mylohyoid, the stylohyoid muscle with the hypoglossal nerve, or the geniohyoid muscle with the facial nerve? The answer is the mylohyoid muscle with the nerve to the mylohyoid. The stylohyoid is supplied by the facial nerve.

Question 2611. A described cross section of the neck at the level of the thyroid gland shows a thick layer of deep fascia wrapping two long strap muscles on each side of the midline. Which fascia and which muscles are described? The answer is the investing layer of the deep cervical fascia around the sternohyoid and the sternothyroid. The same layer encloses the sternocleidomastoid and the trapezius.

Question 2612. Which structure crosses the lower part of the posterior triangle of the neck and divides it into an occipital triangle above and a supraclavicular triangle below? The answer is the inferior belly of the omohyoid muscle. It runs from the superior border of the scapula toward the intermediate tendon near the clavicle. The transverse cervical vessels cross the same zone.

Question 2613. A sagittal section is cut exactly through the midline of the neck. Which of these structures does the section pass through: the internal jugular vein, the sternocleidomastoid muscle, the thyroid cartilage, or the brachial plexus? The answer is the thyroid cartilage. The midline plane carries the hyoid bone, the larynx, the trachea and the thyroid isthmus.

Question 2614. Which nerve supplies the sternohyoid, the sternothyroid and the omohyoid muscles: the ansa cervicalis, the hypoglossal nerve, the spinal accessory nerve, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the ansa cervicalis. Its superior root carries fibers that travel briefly with the hypoglossal nerve, and its inferior root comes from the cervical plexus. The thyrohyoid is supplied separately.

Question 2615. A constructed illustration: during the removal of a lymph node in the posterior triangle of the neck, the shoulder droops afterward. Which nerve has been injured, and why was it at risk? The answer is the spinal accessory nerve, which runs superficially through the posterior triangle on its way to the trapezius. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2616. What is the name of the fascial tube that carries the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve through the neck? The answer is the carotid sheath. It begins at the base of the skull and continues into the thorax. The three structures keep the same relation to one another along it.

Question 2617. At which level does the common carotid artery divide into the internal and the external carotid arteries? The answer is the upper border of the thyroid cartilage, at the level of the fourth cervical vertebra. The cricoid cartilage below it marks the level of the sixth cervical vertebra, where the trachea begins.

Question 2618. True or false: the carotid sinus is a chemoreceptor that monitors the oxygen content of the arterial blood. The answer is false. The carotid sinus is a baroreceptor, a slight dilation at the origin of the internal carotid artery that responds to stretch and helps regulate blood pressure. The chemoreceptor at the bifurcation is the carotid body.

Question 2619. Which small chemoreceptor lies in the angle between the internal and the external carotid arteries and monitors the oxygen content of arterial blood? The answer is the carotid body. It is supplied by branches of the glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves. The nearby structure that senses stretch and helps control blood pressure is the carotid sinus.

Question 2620. Which of these is a branch of the external carotid artery: the vertebral artery, the facial artery, the inferior thyroid artery, or the suprascapular artery? The answer is the facial artery. The vertebral artery and the inferior thyroid and suprascapular arteries belong to the subclavian system. The external carotid also gives the superior thyroid, the lingual and the occipital.

Question 2621. Within the carotid sheath, where does the internal jugular vein lie in relation to the common carotid artery? The answer is lateral to it. The vein runs down the outer side of the artery, and the vagus nerve lies behind and between the two. The sheath is attached behind to the prevertebral fascia.

Question 2622. Which of these is the first branch given off by the external carotid artery above its origin: the lingual artery, the superior thyroid artery, the facial artery, or the occipital artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery. It arises close to the bifurcation and runs down to the upper pole of the thyroid gland. The ascending pharyngeal artery may arise at the same level.

Question 2623. True or false: the internal carotid artery gives off no branches while it lies in the neck. The answer is true. It ascends from the bifurcation to the base of the skull without branching and enters the skull through the carotid canal. Its branches begin inside the cranial cavity.

Question 2624. A described dissection of the carotid triangle shows the common carotid artery dividing into two vessels, with a small body in the angle between them and a nerve running down behind the sheath. Which nerve is described? The answer is the vagus nerve. It lies in the carotid sheath behind and between the internal jugular vein and the common carotid artery. The small body is the carotid body.

Question 2625. What is the name of the nerve that lies within the carotid sheath behind and between the internal jugular vein and the common carotid artery? The answer is the vagus nerve. The vein lies lateral and the artery medial. The nerve keeps this relation from the base of the skull to the root of the neck.

Question 2626. Which artery of the neck ascends through the openings in the transverse processes of the cervical vertebrae and enters the skull through the foramen magnum? The answer is the vertebral artery. It is usually the first branch of the first part of the subclavian artery. The two vertebral arteries join to form the basilar artery.

Question 2627. Which of these arteries arises from the first part of the subclavian artery and supplies the brain and the spinal cord: the costocervical trunk, the dorsal scapular artery, the vertebral artery, or the suprascapular artery? The answer is the vertebral artery. The costocervical trunk comes from the second part and the dorsal scapular artery usually from the third part.

Question 2628. At the root of the neck, which structure lies immediately anterior to the anterior scalene muscle: the subclavian artery, the subclavian vein, the brachial plexus, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the subclavian vein. The subclavian artery and the brachial plexus pass behind the muscle, and the phrenic nerve lies on its anterior surface.

Question 2629. Which of these arteries arises from the second part of the subclavian artery behind the anterior scalene muscle: the vertebral artery, the costocervical trunk, the thyrocervical trunk, or the internal thoracic artery? The answer is the costocervical trunk, which gives the supreme intercostal and deep cervical arteries. The other three arise from the first part of the subclavian artery.

Question 2630. Which of these is a branch of the thyrocervical trunk: the deep cervical artery, the inferior thyroid artery, the internal thoracic artery, or the supreme intercostal artery? The answer is the inferior thyroid artery. The thyrocervical trunk also gives the suprascapular and transverse cervical arteries. The deep cervical and supreme intercostal arteries come from the costocervical trunk.

Question 2631. A constructed illustration: a deep cut in the lower part of the neck divides a superficial vein that crosses the sternocleidomastoid muscle, and the bleeding is brisk. Which vein is involved, and which vein does it drain into? The answer is the external jugular vein, which drains into the subclavian vein. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2632. A described dissection of the front of the neck shows two small veins running close to the midline on either side of the infrahyoid muscles and joining across the midline above the sternum. Which veins are shown, and what is the arch they form called? The answer is the anterior jugular veins, which form the jugular venous arch.

Question 2633. Which of these pairs belongs together: the external jugular vein with the subclavian vein, the internal jugular vein with the subclavian artery, the anterior jugular vein with the internal carotid artery, or the retromandibular vein with the vertebral vein? The answer is the external jugular vein with the subclavian vein. The internal jugular vein joins the subclavian vein to form the brachiocephalic vein.

Question 2634. Which two veins unite to form the retromandibular vein: the superficial temporal and the maxillary veins, the facial and the posterior auricular veins, the internal jugular and the subclavian veins, or the superior and middle thyroid veins? The answer is the superficial temporal and the maxillary veins. The retromandibular vein lies in the parotid gland and divides into an anterior and a posterior division.

Question 2635. Which structure ends at the junction of the left internal jugular and left subclavian veins, and where does it lie in relation to the carotid sheath? The answer is the thoracic duct, which ends at that junction and lies behind the carotid sheath on the left. On the right the right lymphatic duct ends at the same junction.

Question 2636. Which of these statements about the thoracic duct is correct: it drains only the right side of the head, it ends at the junction of the left internal jugular and left subclavian veins, it lies in front of the carotid sheath, or it begins in the neck? The answer is that it ends at that junction on the left. It begins in the abdomen and drains most of the body.

Question 2637. Name the four cutaneous branches of the cervical plexus, spoken as a list. The answer is the lesser occipital nerve, the greater auricular nerve, the transverse cervical nerve and the supraclavicular nerves. All four emerge from behind the middle of the posterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle and supply the skin of the neck and the ear.

Question 2638. The phrenic nerve is formed by fibers from which spinal nerves: the first and second cervical, the third, fourth and fifth cervical, the fifth and sixth cervical, or the first and second thoracic? The answer is the third, fourth and fifth cervical nerves. The nerve descends on the anterior scalene muscle into the chest. It supplies the diaphragm.

Question 2639. On which surface of the anterior scalene muscle does the phrenic nerve descend through the neck? The answer is the anterior surface. The nerve runs down the muscle from lateral to medial. At the root of the neck it passes in front of the subclavian artery and behind the subclavian vein.

Question 2640. A constructed illustration: a stab wound at the root of the neck is followed by a raised hemidiaphragm on the same side. Which nerve has been injured, and where do its fibers come from? The answer is the phrenic nerve, from the third, fourth and fifth cervical nerves. The illustration is anatomical only, and management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2641. Which branch of the superior laryngeal nerve supplies the cricothyroid muscle: the internal branch, the external branch, the recurrent branch, or the pharyngeal branch? The answer is the external branch. The internal branch pierces the thyrohyoid membrane and carries sensation from the larynx above the vocal folds. The recurrent laryngeal nerve supplies all the other intrinsic muscles.

Question 2642. Which of these structures does the hypoglossal nerve cross in the upper part of the neck: the trachea, the internal and external carotid arteries, the phrenic nerve, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the internal and external carotid arteries. The nerve curves forward above the hyoid bone, deep to the posterior belly of the digastric. It supplies the muscles of the tongue.

Question 2643. A constructed illustration: a wound just below the angle of the jaw is followed by wasting of one side of the tongue and a tongue that deviates toward the injured side when put out. Which nerve has been injured? The answer is the hypoglossal nerve, which supplies the muscles of the tongue. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2644. A constructed illustration: a tumor at the apex of the lung presses on the sympathetic trunk in the neck. Which signs appear on the same side of the face, and why? The answer is a drooping upper eyelid, a constricted pupil, a sunken appearance of the eye and loss of sweating. The sympathetic fibers concerned travel with the internal carotid artery.

Question 2645. What is the name of the chain of ganglia that lies behind the carotid sheath on the prevertebral muscles and sends branches to the cervical spinal nerves? The answer is the sympathetic trunk. Its uppermost swelling is the superior cervical ganglion. Its fibers travel with the internal carotid artery into the head.

Question 2646. The brachial plexus is formed by the anterior rami of which spinal nerves: the first to the fourth cervical, the fifth cervical to the first thoracic, the first to the fourth thoracic, or the fifth to the eighth cervical only? The answer is the fifth cervical to the first thoracic nerves. They form the roots and then the trunks of the plexus in the lower part of the posterior triangle.

Question 2647. A described dissection of the lower posterior triangle shows three muscles descending to the first two ribs, with a thick bundle of nerves emerging between two of them. Which muscles and which bundle are described? The answer is the three scalene muscles, with the brachial plexus emerging between the anterior and the middle scalene. The subclavian artery emerges with the plexus.

Question 2648. A constructed illustration: a person has an extra bone that springs from the seventh cervical vertebra and curves toward the first rib. Which structure is it, and which structures may it compress? The answer is a cervical rib, which may press on the lower trunk of the brachial plexus and on the subclavian vessels. Such a rib is a developmental variant.

Question 2649. A constructed illustration: a person reports aching in the arm and a heavy feeling in the hand whenever the arm is held overhead, because the lower trunk of the brachial plexus passes over a band at the thoracic outlet. Which space is narrowed? The answer is the space between the clavicle and the first rib. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2650. A described dissection of the neck shows a nerve curving around the middle of the posterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle and running upward over the parotid gland toward the ear. Which nerve is described, and from which nerves does it come? The answer is the greater auricular nerve, from the second and third cervical nerves.

Question 2651. Which of these pairs belongs together: the greater auricular nerve with the skin over the parotid gland and the ear, the lesser occipital nerve with the skin of the front of the neck, the supraclavicular nerves with the skin of the back of the head, or the transverse cervical nerve with the skin over the shoulder? The answer is the greater auricular nerve with the skin over the parotid gland and the ear. The lesser occipital nerve supplies the scalp behind the ear.

Question 2652. Which of these pairs belongs together: the submandibular gland with the carotid triangle, the thyroid gland with the muscular triangle, the brachial plexus with the submental triangle, or the parotid gland with the carotid triangle? The answer is the thyroid gland with the muscular triangle. The submandibular gland lies in the submandibular triangle and the parotid gland lies around the angle of the jaw.

Question 2653. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery, the inferior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery, the suprascapular artery with the internal carotid artery, or the facial artery with the thyrocervical trunk? The answer is the superior thyroid artery with the external carotid artery. The inferior thyroid artery comes from the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery.

Question 2654. Name the three structures that lie within the carotid sheath, spoken as a list. The answer is the common carotid artery with its internal and external continuations, the internal jugular vein and the vagus nerve. The ansa cervicalis lies in the front of the sheath for part of its course, and the sheath is attached behind to the prevertebral fascia.

Question 2655. A described radiograph of the lower part of the neck shows an extra bony bar extending from the seventh cervical vertebra on one side and meeting the first rib. Which structure is shown, and which nervous structure lies immediately above it? The answer is a cervical rib, with the lower trunk of the brachial plexus lying above it. The subclavian artery also passes over the rib.

Question 2656. Where does the thyroid gland lie in relation to the larynx and the trachea, and in which layer of fascia is it wrapped? The answer is that it wraps around the front and the sides of the upper trachea below the larynx, inside the pretracheal layer of the deep cervical fascia. That wrapping is why it moves when the person swallows.

Question 2657. The isthmus of the thyroid gland lies in front of which structures: the first and second tracheal rings, the second to the fourth tracheal rings, the cricoid cartilage and the hyoid bone, or the bodies of the cervical vertebrae? The answer is the second to the fourth tracheal rings. The gland moves with the larynx when the patient swallows. Its lobes reach up to the thyroid cartilage.

Question 2658. Which structure lies immediately posterior to the isthmus of the thyroid gland? The answer is the trachea, with its second, third and fourth rings behind the gland. The gland is wrapped in the pretracheal fascia, which binds it to the airway. The esophagus lies behind the trachea.

Question 2659. Which artery supplies the upper pole of the thyroid gland: the inferior thyroid artery, the superior thyroid artery, the thyroidea ima, or the transverse cervical artery? The answer is the superior thyroid artery, the first branch of the external carotid artery. It descends to the upper pole in company with the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve.

Question 2660. The inferior thyroid artery arises from which parent vessel: the external carotid artery, the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian artery, the costocervical trunk, or the internal thoracic artery? The answer is the thyrocervical trunk. It ascends to the lower pole of the gland after looping upward. Its relation to the recurrent laryngeal nerve is variable.

Question 2661. Where does the recurrent laryngeal nerve usually lie in relation to the branches of the inferior thyroid artery, and why does the relation matter? The answer is that the nerve passes between the branches of the artery, or deep to them, near the lower pole of the thyroid gland. The arrangement is variable and the nerve is at risk in operations on the gland.

Question 2662. Which small artery, present in only a minority of people, ascends in the midline in front of the trachea to supply the thyroid gland? The answer is the thyroidea ima, which arises from the brachiocephalic trunk or from the arch of the aorta. It runs upward in front of the trachea and must be remembered in operations on the midline of the neck.

Question 2663. Which statement about the parathyroid glands is correct: there are usually two of them, they lie on the anterior surface of the thyroid gland, they lie on the posterior surface of the thyroid gland, or they lie inside the trachea? The answer is that they lie on the posterior surface of the thyroid gland, usually four in number, two on each side. The inferior glands vary in position more than the superior ones.

Question 2664. A constructed illustration: after an operation on the thyroid gland, a person's voice becomes hoarse and weak. Which nerve has most likely been injured, and what is its course on the right side? The answer is the recurrent laryngeal nerve, which hooks around the right subclavian artery and ascends in the groove between the trachea and the esophagus. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2665. A described specimen shows the thyroid gland in place, with a small separate nodule of the same tissue rising from the isthmus toward the hyoid bone. What is that upward extension called, and what does its presence explain? The answer is the pyramidal lobe, a remnant of the thyroglossal duct along which the gland descended during development. The trachea lies immediately deep to the isthmus.

Question 2666. Which cartilage of the larynx forms a complete ring shaped like a signet, narrow in front and broad behind? The answer is the cricoid cartilage. It lies at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra and is the only complete ring in the airway. The trachea begins below it.

Question 2667. Which of these cartilages of the larynx is unpaired: the arytenoid cartilage, the corniculate cartilage, the cricoid cartilage, or the cuneiform cartilage? The answer is the cricoid cartilage. The thyroid cartilage and the epiglottis are also unpaired. The arytenoid, corniculate and cuneiform cartilages are paired.

Question 2668. Which bone of the neck lies between the mandible and the larynx, has greater and lesser horns, and does not articulate with any other bone? The answer is the hyoid bone. It is suspended by muscles and ligaments from the skull and the larynx. The thyroid cartilage lies below it.

Question 2669. A described radiograph of the front of the neck shows a small bone shaped like a horseshoe lying above a larger shield-shaped cartilage, with a leaf-like flap behind it. Which three structures are shown? The answer is the hyoid bone, the thyroid cartilage below it and the epiglottis behind. All three lie in the midline.

Question 2670. What is the name of the fold of mucous membrane in the larynx that contains the vocal ligament and vibrates to produce the voice? The answer is the vocal fold, also called the true vocal cord. The space between the two vocal folds is the rima glottidis. The vestibular fold lies above it and does not produce sound.

Question 2671. Which nerve supplies the cricothyroid muscle: the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve, the recurrent laryngeal nerve, or the glossopharyngeal nerve? The answer is the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve. The cricothyroid is the only intrinsic muscle of the larynx not supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve.

Question 2672. Which movement takes place at the cricothyroid joint when the cricothyroid muscle contracts, and what does that do to the vocal folds? The answer is that the thyroid cartilage tilts forward and downward, which lengthens and tenses the vocal folds. The joint allows rotation and a little gliding. The result is a rise in the pitch of the voice.

Question 2673. Which structure forms the lid over the entrance of the larynx during swallowing: the epiglottis, the arytenoid cartilage, the cricoid cartilage, or the hyoid bone? The answer is the epiglottis. It is a leaf-shaped plate of elastic cartilage attached to the thyroid cartilage and the hyoid bone. It folds back over the opening of the larynx as the larynx rises during swallowing.

Question 2674. True or false: the thyroid gland lies deep to the pretracheal layer of the deep cervical fascia. The answer is true. That layer encloses the gland and binds it to the larynx and the trachea. This is why the gland moves up and down when the patient swallows.

Question 2675. A described section through the lower part of the neck in front of the trachea shows a layer of fascia that passes behind the strap muscles and encloses both the thyroid gland and the airway. Which layer is described? The answer is the pretracheal layer of the deep cervical fascia. It also encloses the esophagus behind the trachea and blends with the carotid sheath at each side.

Question 2676. Which tube of the neck begins below the cricoid cartilage, is held open by rings of hyaline cartilage, and lies in the midline in front of the esophagus? The answer is the trachea. Its rings are incomplete behind, where the esophagus lies against it. It continues into the chest and divides into the two main bronchi.

Question 2677. Which statement about the cartilaginous rings of the trachea is correct: they are complete rings, they are incomplete behind where the esophagus lies against the trachea, they are absent in the neck, or they lie only in the chest? The answer is that they are incomplete behind, where a sheet of smooth muscle closes the gap.

Question 2678. Which structure lies immediately posterior to the trachea in the neck, beginning at the level of the cricoid cartilage? The answer is the esophagus, which carries food from the pharynx to the stomach. It descends slightly to the left of the midline. Its upper end is closed by the cricopharyngeus muscle.

Question 2679. A described specimen of the larynx shows a membrane stretched between the arch of the cricoid cartilage below and the lower border of the thyroid cartilage above, with a small muscle lying over it. Which membrane and which muscle are shown? The answer is the cricothyroid membrane, with the cricothyroid muscle over it. The membrane lies in the midline just below the thyroid prominence.

Question 2680. Name two unpaired cartilages of the larynx, spoken as a list. The answer is the thyroid cartilage and the cricoid cartilage. The epiglottis is a third unpaired cartilage. The arytenoid, corniculate and cuneiform cartilages are paired. The thyroid cartilage forms the prominence in front of the neck.

Question 2681. Which long muscle of the prevertebral group lies against the front of the cervical vertebral bodies and flexes the neck? The answer is the longus colli. It runs from the bodies of the upper thoracic and lower cervical vertebrae to the atlas. The longus capitis lies above it and reaches the base of the skull.

Question 2682. The anterior scalene muscle arises from which structures and inserts on which rib: the transverse processes of the third to the sixth cervical vertebrae and the first rib, the bodies of the cervical vertebrae and the second rib, the mastoid process and the clavicle, or the occipital bone and the first rib? The answer is the transverse processes of the third to the sixth cervical vertebrae and the first rib, at the scalene tubercle.

Question 2683. Which muscle of the neck separates the subclavian vein in front from the subclavian artery and the brachial plexus behind? The answer is the anterior scalene muscle. It runs from the cervical transverse processes down to the first rib. The phrenic nerve lies on its anterior surface.

Question 2684. Which two cervical vertebrae are unlike the others in shape: the first and the second, the third and the fourth, the fifth and the sixth, or the sixth and the seventh? The answer is the first and the second. The first has no body and no spine, and the second carries the dens. Together they allow most of the rotation of the head.

Question 2685. True or false: the first cervical vertebra has no body and no bony spine. The answer is true. It is a ring with an anterior arch, a posterior arch and two lateral masses. It articulates above with the occipital condyles and below with the second cervical vertebra.

Question 2686. What is the name of the upward peg of bone on the second cervical vertebra about which the first vertebra and the head rotate? The answer is the dens, also called the odontoid process. It lies inside the ring of the first cervical vertebra. It is held in place by the transverse ligament of the atlas.

Question 2687. A described radiograph taken through the open mouth shows the upper cervical spine, with a ring of bone around a central peg and two lateral masses on either side. Which two vertebrae are shown, and what holds the peg in place? The answer is the first and the second cervical vertebrae, and the transverse ligament of the atlas holds the dens.

Question 2688. The uncovertebral joints of the cervical spine lie between which structures: the bodies of adjacent vertebrae, the uncinate processes of one vertebra and the body of the vertebra above, the articular processes of adjacent vertebrae, or the spinous processes? The answer is the uncinate processes and the body of the vertebra above. They lie from the third to the seventh cervical vertebrae, with the vertebral artery close by laterally.

Question 2689. Which layer of the deep cervical fascia encloses the sternocleidomastoid and the trapezius muscles and forms the roof of the posterior triangle: the investing layer, the pretracheal layer, the prevertebral layer, or the carotid sheath? The answer is the investing layer. It also encloses the parotid gland and the submandibular gland. It is the outermost of the deep layers.

Question 2690. What is the name of the fascial layer that encloses the thyroid gland, the trachea and the esophagus and continues downward into the chest? The answer is the pretracheal layer of the deep cervical fascia. It lies behind the infrahyoid muscles. Behind the pharynx it is continued as the buccopharyngeal fascia.

Question 2691. The prevertebral layer of the deep cervical fascia extends laterally to form which covering: the sheath of the thyroid gland, the axillary sheath around the brachial plexus and the axillary vessels, the covering of the parotid gland, or the sheath of the carotid vessels? The answer is the axillary sheath. It follows the brachial plexus and the axillary vessels into the armpit.

Question 2692. Which layers of the deep cervical fascia contribute to the carotid sheath? The answer is the investing layer, the pretracheal layer and the prevertebral layer. The investing layer forms its anterior wall, the pretracheal layer its medial wall and the prevertebral layer its posterior wall. The sheath is attached to the prevertebral fascia behind.

Question 2693. A described cross section shows a narrow space between the back of the pharynx and the front of the prevertebral muscles, filled with loose tissue. Which space is described, and where does it extend? The answer is the retropharyngeal space, which reaches from the base of the skull down to the upper part of the posterior mediastinum. The buccopharyngeal fascia forms its front wall.

Question 2694. The retropharyngeal space is bounded in front by which structure: the prevertebral fascia, the buccopharyngeal fascia, the investing layer of the deep cervical fascia, or the carotid sheath? The answer is the buccopharyngeal fascia, which lies behind the pharynx. Infection in this space can reach the mediastinum.

Question 2695. A described sagittal section through the back of the pharynx shows a narrow space between two fascial layers that continues downward into the chest as far as the diaphragm. Which space is described, and what is its clinical importance? The answer is the danger space, which lies between the alar fascia in front and the prevertebral fascia behind. Infection can travel along it from the neck to the posterior mediastinum.

Question 2696. A constructed illustration: a spreading infection from the back of the throat passes down behind the esophagus into the chest. Along which space has it traveled, and what are the front and back boundaries of that space? The answer is the retropharyngeal space, bounded in front by the buccopharyngeal fascia and behind by the prevertebral fascia. The illustration is not a real patient.

Question 2697. A constructed illustration: an infection spreading from an infected lower molar tooth raises the floor of the mouth on both sides and pushes the tongue upward. Which spaces are involved, and which muscle forms the floor of the mouth? The answer is the submandibular and sublingual spaces on both sides, with the mylohyoid forming the floor. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2698. Which of these structures forms the upper boundary of the submandibular triangle: the hyoid bone, the lower border of the mandible, the posterior belly of the digastric, or the sternocleidomastoid muscle? The answer is the lower border of the mandible. The two bellies of the digastric form the other boundaries and the hyoid bone lies at their meeting point.

Question 2699. A described dissection of the submandibular region shows a large gland wrapping around the posterior border of the mylohyoid muscle, with an artery grooving its surface. Which gland is described, and where does its duct open? The answer is the submandibular gland, whose duct opens beside the frenulum of the tongue. The artery is the facial artery.

Question 2700. In a described transverse section of the neck at the level of the thyroid gland, where does the carotid sheath lie in relation to the trachea and the prevertebral muscles? The answer is lateral to the trachea and in front of the prevertebral muscles. The internal jugular vein forms its lateral part and the common carotid artery its medial part.

To summarize, the sternocleidomastoid muscle divides each side of the neck into an anterior triangle and a posterior triangle, and the investing, pretracheal and prevertebral layers of the deep cervical fascia define the carotid sheath, the retropharyngeal space and the danger space behind them. The common carotid artery divides at the upper border of the thyroid cartilage, the internal carotid artery gives no branches in the neck, and the internal jugular vein, the vagus nerve and the sympathetic trunk keep fixed relations to the artery within and behind the sheath. The cervical plexus gives the cutaneous nerves of the neck and the phrenic nerve, while the spinal accessory nerve runs superficially through the posterior triangle and is at risk there. The thyroid gland wraps the upper trachea inside the pretracheal fascia and receives the superior thyroid artery from the external carotid and the inferior thyroid artery from the thyrocervical trunk, with the recurrent laryngeal nerve passing near the latter. The larynx is built from the thyroid, cricoid and epiglottic cartilages, and the cricothyroid muscle is the only intrinsic laryngeal muscle supplied by the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve.
