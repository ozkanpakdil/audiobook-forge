# Chapter 21 — Elbow and Forearm

This chapter follows the upper limb from the shoulder to the fingertips, beginning with the ligaments that steady the shoulder. It works through the bony points of the elbow, its capsule and ligaments, the two bones of the forearm, the muscles that move it, and the nerves that cross the joint. It then turns to the wrist and the hand, whose small bones and long tendons do the fine work of the limb. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer straight after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 2001. Which ligament passes from the coracoid process to the acromion and forms a bridge above the shoulder joint? The answer is the coracoacromial ligament. It arches over the head of the humerus. The supraspinatus tendon passes beneath it, and the arch keeps the head of the humerus from riding upward.

Question 2002. Which bone of the arm carries a spool-shaped surface that grips the ulna and a rounded head that meets the radius? The answer is the humerus. Its trochlea fits the trochlear notch of the ulna, and the capitulum takes the head of the radius. The olecranon fossa behind them takes the tip of the olecranon in extension.

Question 2003. Which of these bony points is the large bump felt on the inner side of the elbow: the capitulum, the medial epicondyle, the radial head, or the olecranon? The answer is the medial epicondyle. The common flexor tendon is attached to it, and the ulnar nerve lies in a groove behind it.

Question 2004. Which structure lies immediately posterior to the medial epicondyle and passes into the forearm beneath the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the ulnar nerve. A knock on it sends a tingling shock down the little finger. It lies close to the bone, covered only by skin and fascia.

Question 2005. A described radiograph of a young child's elbow shows a small bony center beside the lower humerus. Which center does it represent? The answer is the center for the capitulum, the first center of the lower humerus to appear. It fuses with the shaft years later, so the gap between them is normal and easily mistaken for a fracture.

Question 2006. Which bone lies on the little finger side of the forearm and ends above in the point of the elbow? The answer is the ulna. Its upper end carries the olecranon behind and the coronoid process in front, with the trochlear notch between them. The radius lies beside it on the thumb side.

Question 2007. Which of these structures forms the ring in which the head of the radius turns when the palm is turned up and down: the annular ligament, the olecranon, the coronoid process, or the radial tuberosity? The answer is the annular ligament. It encircles the head of the radius and holds it against the radial notch of the ulna.

Question 2008. True or false: the elbow is a simple hinge joint that permits movement in one plane only. The answer is false, though the statement is nearly right. The humerus and the ulna form a hinge, but the head of the radius turns in the annular ligament at the same level. That turn produces pronation and supination of the hand.

Question 2009. A constructed illustration: a small child is lifted by one hand and afterwards keeps the arm still and slightly bent. Which structure has slipped? The answer is the annular ligament, which has slipped over the head of the radius. The ligament is lax and the radial head is small in a young child. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2010. Name the two bones that meet the humerus at the elbow, spoken as a list. The answer is the radius and the ulna. The ulna lies on the little finger side and its trochlear notch grips the trochlea of the humerus. The radius lies on the thumb side and its head meets the rounded capitulum.

Question 2011. Which of these movements is produced by the muscles that cross the front of the elbow: supination, flexion, abduction, or rotation? The answer is flexion. They bend the forearm toward the arm, and the muscles behind the arm straighten it again. Supination belongs to the forearm and abduction to the shoulder.

Question 2012. Which disc-shaped structure sits at the top of the radius and turns against the capitulum above and the ulna beside it? The answer is the head of the radius. Its upper surface is hollowed to meet the rounded capitulum, and its rim is held in the ring formed by the annular ligament and the radial notch.

Question 2013. A described radiograph from the side shows the bent elbow, with a broad bony point in a hollow at the back of the lower humerus. Which structure is that point, and in which hollow does it lie? The answer is the olecranon, lying in the olecranon fossa. When the elbow straightens the point leaves the hollow, so the back of the elbow is flat.

Question 2014. Which nerve winds around the neck of the radius inside the supinator, where a fracture can injure it? The answer is the deep branch of the radial nerve, which becomes the posterior interosseous nerve. It supplies the extensor muscles of the fingers and the thumb, so their weakness is the sign of injury.

Question 2015. Which of these muscles is the strongest bender of the elbow, lying deep to the biceps: the brachialis, the brachioradialis, the triceps, or the anconeus? The answer is the brachialis. It reaches the ulna, so it bends the elbow in any position of the forearm. The brachioradialis bends the elbow best when the forearm is half turned.

Question 2016. Which muscle forms the rounded mass on the front of the arm and ends in a tendon that reaches the radial tuberosity? The answer is the biceps brachii. It arises by two heads, one from the scapula above the socket and one from the coracoid process. Its tendon also sends the bicipital aponeurosis into the deep fascia of the forearm.

Question 2017. A constructed illustration: a person who grips and twists with the forearm straight develops pain over the outer side of the elbow. Which tendon is strained? The answer is the common extensor tendon, attached to the lateral epicondyle. The illustration shows a pattern of overuse, not a real patient. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2018. Which of these statements about the carrying angle is correct: it is measured with the elbow straight, it is the same in men and women, it vanishes when the elbow bends, or it is produced by the triceps? The answer is that it is measured with the elbow straight, as the angle between the arm and the forearm. A slight angle is therefore normal.

Question 2019. A described cross section of the upper forearm shows two bones joined by a thin fibrous sheet. Which bone lies lateral, and which way do the fibers of the sheet run? The answer is that the radius lies lateral, and the fibers run downward and medially to the ulna. Force that travels up the radius from the hand crosses the sheet to the ulna.

Question 2020. Which of these pairs belongs together: the olecranon with the ulna, the capitulum with the ulna, the radial head with the humerus, or the coronoid process with the radius? The answer is the olecranon with the ulna, the point of the elbow that the triceps pulls on. The coronoid process is part of the ulna, the capitulum of the humerus, and the radial head of the radius.

Question 2021. Which projection of the ulna slides into the coronoid fossa of the humerus when the elbow bends? The answer is the coronoid process. It lies in front of the ulna, just below the trochlear notch. When the elbow bends fully, the process fills the small hollow above the trochlea.

Question 2022. Which of these structures forms the medial boundary of the cubital fossa: the brachioradialis, the pronator teres, the biceps tendon, or the supinator? The answer is the pronator teres. The brachioradialis forms the lateral boundary and the line between the two epicondyles the upper boundary. The floor is the brachialis and the supinator.

Question 2023. Which structure lies immediately medial to the brachial artery in the middle of the cubital fossa? The answer is the median nerve. The biceps tendon lies lateral to the artery, so the three form a row from the thumb side outward. The bicipital aponeurosis covers the artery and the nerve as it crosses them.

Question 2024. A described section across the front of the elbow shows the biceps tendon, the brachial artery and the median nerve in a row. In which plane was the section cut, and in what order do the three lie? The answer is a transverse plane, with the tendon first, the artery second and the nerve third. A section along the arm would show the joint in profile.

Question 2025. A described radiograph of a child's elbow shows a small bony fleck in front of the lower humerus. Which normal center does it represent, and why must it not be read as a fracture? The answer is the center for the capitulum, which appears in the first year. The gap between it and the shaft is normal until the two fuse.

Question 2026. Which muscle forms the mass at the back of the arm, arises by three heads and inserts into the olecranon? The answer is the triceps brachii. Its long head arises from the scapula below the socket and its other two heads from the back of the humerus. It is the chief extensor of the elbow and its tendon also steadies the joint.

Question 2027. Which of these muscles pulls the radius across the ulna so that the palm turns downward: the supinator, the pronator teres, the brachioradialis, or the anconeus? The answer is the pronator teres. It runs from the medial epicondyle and the coronoid process to the outer radius. The supinator turns the palm upward and the anconeus helps to extend the elbow.

Question 2028. A constructed illustration: a person who leans on the point of the elbow develops a soft swelling over the bone. Which structure is inflamed? The answer is the olecranon bursa, which lies between the skin and the olecranon. The illustration shows a common pattern, not a real patient. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2029. True or false: the biceps brachii is the strongest bender of the elbow. The answer is false. The biceps is a powerful bender and the strongest muscle that turns the palm upward, but the brachialis beneath it is the chief bender, and it works whatever the position of the forearm.

Question 2030. Name the three structures that lie in a row in the cubital fossa, spoken as a list from the thumb side to the little finger side. The answer is the biceps tendon, the brachial artery and the median nerve. The tendon lies nearest the thumb and the nerve nearest the little finger, and the bicipital aponeurosis crosses over both.

Question 2031. Which of these structures passes between the two heads of the pronator teres: the ulnar nerve, the median nerve, the radial nerve, or the basilic vein? The answer is the median nerve. The ulnar nerve enters the forearm behind the medial epicondyle instead. The radial nerve divides above the elbow and its deep branch passes through the supinator.

Question 2032. A described dissection of the front of the elbow shows a flat fibrous band spreading from the biceps tendon into the deep fascia. Which structure is shown, and what does it cover? The answer is the bicipital aponeurosis, which forms part of the roof of the cubital fossa. It covers the brachial artery and the median nerve beneath it.

Question 2033. Which artery descends on the thumb side of the forearm and can be felt beating at the wrist? The answer is the radial artery. It begins at the elbow as the smaller of the two terminal branches of the brachial artery. It lies under the brachioradialis in the upper forearm and becomes superficial above the wrist.

Question 2034. Which structure lies between the brachialis on its medial side and the brachioradialis on its lateral side, and divides there into two branches? The answer is the radial nerve. Its superficial branch carries sensation from the back of the hand, and its deep branch supplies the extensor muscles through the supinator.

Question 2035. Which statement about the interosseous membrane is correct: its fibers run upward from the radius, it prevents movement between the bones, it carries force from the radius to the ulna, or it forms part of the elbow capsule? The answer is that it carries force from the radius to the ulna. Its fibers run downward and medially, so force on the hand crosses to the ulna.

Question 2036. A constructed illustration: after a fall, a child cannot bend the last joint of the thumb or index finger. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the anterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the median nerve. It supplies the long flexor of the thumb and the deep flexor fibers of the index and middle fingers. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2037. A described radiograph shows a break across the upper ulna with the head of the radius in front of the capitulum. Which pattern is described here? The answer is a break of the ulna with dislocation of the head of the radius. The annular ligament tears as the shaft bends and the head slips out. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2038. Which border of the ulna lies directly beneath the skin along the back of the forearm? The answer is the posterior border. It can be traced with a finger from the olecranon down to the wrist. The other borders lie hidden among the flexor and extensor muscles.

Question 2039. Which of these statements about the extensor muscles of the forearm is correct: all of them are supplied by the radial nerve, all by the median nerve, all by the musculocutaneous nerve, or all by the ulnar nerve? The answer is that all are supplied by the radial nerve. Most of them receive the posterior interosseous nerve, which passes through the supinator.

Question 2040. Which of these pairs belongs together: the brachialis with bending of the elbow, the triceps with turning the palm upward, the brachioradialis with straightening of the elbow, or the anconeus with turning the palm downward? The answer is the brachialis with bending of the elbow. The triceps and the anconeus straighten the elbow, and the brachioradialis bends it with the forearm half turned.

Question 2041. Which muscle runs along the outer side of the upper forearm and stands out when the elbow is bent against resistance? The answer is the brachioradialis. It arises from the lower part of the lateral ridge above the lateral epicondyle. It bends the elbow most strongly when the forearm is halfway between turning the palm up and down.

Question 2042. Which branch of the ulnar artery turns upward in front of the medial epicondyle to join the network of vessels around the elbow? The answer is the anterior ulnar recurrent artery. It climbs in front of the medial epicondyle to meet branches from above. That network keeps the lower arm supplied when the elbow is bent.

Question 2043. Which muscle arises from the common tendon on the medial epicondyle: the extensor carpi radialis brevis, the flexor carpi ulnaris, the supinator, or the brachioradialis? The answer is the flexor carpi ulnaris. The other three lie on the outer side of the elbow or the back of the forearm. The common flexor tendon gives rise to the muscles that bend the wrist and the fingers.

Question 2044. A described drawing of the back of the forearm shows muscles whose tendons pass under a fibrous band at the wrist. From which common attachment do they arise, and what do they do? The answer is the common extensor tendon on the lateral epicondyle. They straighten the wrist and the fingers and pull the hand backward under the extensor retinaculum.

Question 2045. Which muscle wraps around the upper part of the radius and turns the palm upward? The answer is the supinator. It arises from the lateral epicondyle and from the ulna and wraps around the upper third of the radius. Its fibers run in the direction that unwinds the radius and so turns the palm to face forward.

Question 2046. Which of these structures forms the floor of the cubital fossa: the brachialis, the pronator teres, the biceps tendon, or the brachioradialis? The answer is the brachialis, helped below by the supinator. The brachioradialis and the pronator teres form the two sides of the triangle. The biceps tendon lies within the fossa and not in its floor.

Question 2047. Which of these descriptions of the radial nerve is correct: it runs in front of the humerus with the brachial artery, it runs against the back of the humerus, it passes behind the medial epicondyle, or it supplies the forearm flexors? The answer is that it runs against the back of the humerus, in the spiral groove, where a break can injure it.

Question 2048. True or false: the median nerve supplies every muscle on the front of the forearm except two. The answer is true, with one qualification. The flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial half of the deep flexor of the fingers are supplied by the ulnar nerve. Every other muscle there is supplied by the median nerve.

Question 2049. Which slender muscle of the front of the forearm has a long tendon that is absent in about one person in seven? The answer is the palmaris longus. Its tendon crosses the wrist in front of the flexor retinaculum and spreads into the palmar aponeurosis. Because it can be spared, surgeons use its tendon for grafts.

Question 2050. A described specimen shows a muscle arising from the medial epicondyle whose tendon splits into two slips before it reaches each finger. Which muscle is shown, and where does it attach? The answer is the flexor digitorum superficialis. Its slips straddle the deeper tendon and attach to the middle phalanx, so the deeper muscle can bend the last joint.

Question 2051. Which of these muscles arises from the common extensor tendon and helps to straighten and steady the wrist: the flexor carpi ulnaris, the extensor carpi radialis brevis, the pronator teres, or the flexor digitorum superficialis? The answer is the extensor carpi radialis brevis. The other three lie on the front of the forearm or arise from the medial epicondyle.

Question 2052. During turning of the palm, around which bone does the radius move, and through which two points does the axis pass? The answer is that the radius turns around the ulna, and the axis passes through the head of the radius above and the triangular disc below. So the upper end of the radius spins in its ring while the lower end swings around.

Question 2053. Which muscle arises from the lateral epicondyle and the back of the ulna and reaches the base of the fifth metacarpal bone? The answer is the extensor carpi ulnaris. It straightens the wrist and pulls the hand toward the little finger side, and with the flexor carpi ulnaris it is a chief mover in that direction.

Question 2054. A constructed illustration: after a deep cut on the inner elbow, a person cannot grip with the little and ring fingers or pinch the thumb and index finger together. Which nerve is divided? The answer is the ulnar nerve, which supplies the small muscles between the metacarpals and those of the little finger side. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2055. A described movement straightens the elbow against resistance and the tendon at the point of the elbow becomes taut. Which of these muscles is acting: the triceps brachii, the brachialis, the anconeus, or the biceps brachii? The answer is the triceps brachii, supplied by the radial nerve. It reaches the olecranon in the back of the arm and is the chief extensor of the elbow.

Question 2056. A described dissection of the inner side of the elbow shows a fan-shaped tendon attached to a bony point. Which bony point is shown, and which muscles arise there? The answer is the medial epicondyle and the flexor group that bends the wrist and fingers. The flexor carpi radialis, the palmaris longus, the flexor carpi ulnaris and the superficial flexor arise from it.

Question 2057. Which of these pairs belongs together: the supinator with the upper radius, the pronator quadratus with the elbow, the biceps brachii with the little finger, or the triceps with the radial tuberosity? The answer is the supinator with the upper radius, around which it wraps. The pronator quadratus lies at the lower ends of the bones, the biceps at the radial tuberosity, the triceps at the olecranon.

Question 2058. Which muscle forms the fleshy mass deep in the front of the forearm and bends the last joints of the fingers? The answer is the flexor digitorum profundus. It arises from the front of the ulna and the interosseous membrane and sends a tendon to the last phalanx of each finger. Its medial half is supplied by the ulnar nerve, the rest by the median nerve.

Question 2059. Which statement about the deep flexor of the fingers is correct: it is supplied entirely by the median nerve, entirely by the ulnar nerve, partly by each, or by the radial nerve? The answer is that it is supplied partly by each. The median nerve supplies the part for the index and middle fingers, the ulnar nerve the part for the ring and little fingers.

Question 2060. A constructed illustration: a break in the shaft of the humerus leaves the hand hanging and the fingers unable to straighten. Which nerve is injured? The answer is the radial nerve, in the spiral groove against the back of the humerus. The extensor muscles of the wrist and fingers lose their supply. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2061. Which structure crosses the front of the lower radius just above the wrist, between the tendons of the brachioradialis and the flexor carpi radialis? The answer is the radial artery. It becomes superficial there and its pulse can be felt against the bone, which is the usual place for taking a pulse at the wrist.

Question 2062. Which of these bones lies on the thumb side of the forearm: the radius, the ulna, the humerus, or the pisiform? The answer is the radius. It is the shorter and thicker of the two forearm bones. The ulna lies on the little finger side and the pisiform is a small bone of the wrist.

Question 2063. Which of these joints is the elbow: the joint between the two forearm bones, the joint between the humerus and the forearm bones, the joint between the forearm and the wrist, or the joint between the carpal rows? The answer is the joint between the humerus and the forearm bones. It is a hinge with the ulna and a pivot with the head of the radius.

Question 2064. Which fibrous arch lies just below the medial epicondyle, arching over the median nerve and the ulnar artery? The answer is the arch of the flexor digitorum superficialis. The median nerve and the ulnar artery pass beneath it as they enter the forearm. It is one of the places where the median nerve can be pressed upon.

Question 2065. A constructed illustration: a person cannot turn the palm upward against resistance and the biceps tendon cannot be felt at the elbow. Which structure is most likely torn? The answer is the tendon of the biceps brachii, torn from the radial tuberosity. With the elbow bent, the biceps is the strongest muscle that turns the palm upward. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2066. Which statement about the anconeus is correct: it lies on the front of the elbow, it helps to straighten the elbow, it turns the palm upward, or it arises from the medial epicondyle? The answer is that it helps to straighten the elbow and steady the joint. It is a small triangular muscle that continues the triceps down the forearm.

Question 2067. A described magnetic resonance image of the elbow shows a small pad of fat lifted forward from the lower humerus. What does that displacement indicate? The answer is fluid in the elbow joint, which has pushed the fat pad out of its usual place. The pad lies between the capsule and the bone and is normally hidden, so its appearance means swelling.

Question 2068. Which structure runs down the medial side of the forearm immediately medial to the ulnar artery? The answer is the ulnar nerve. The artery lies on its lateral side, nearer the thumb. Near the wrist the nerve passes lateral to the pisiform bone, and the artery passes with it. Both enter the hand outside the carpal tunnel.

Question 2069. Which flat muscle lies deepest in the front of the forearm near the wrist, running from the ulna across to the radius? The answer is the pronator quadratus. It pulls the radius across the ulna and so turns the palm downward, and it is the chief muscle of slow, unresisted turning.

Question 2070. Which statement about turning the palm is correct: the supinator acts best with the elbow bent, the biceps with the elbow straight, the pronator quadratus in slow turning, or the pronator teres only with the shoulder raised? The answer is that the pronator quadratus acts in slow turning. The biceps turns the palm upward best with the elbow bent, the supinator with the elbow straight.

Question 2071. Which bone lies in the floor of the hollow at the base of the thumb and is the one most often broken at the wrist? The answer is the scaphoid. The hollow is the anatomical snuffbox, bounded by tendons of the thumb. A break is easily missed because the bone lies hidden and the pain is often slight.

Question 2072. Which of these bones belongs to the row of carpal bones nearest the forearm: the capitate, the scaphoid, the trapezium, or the hamate? The answer is the scaphoid. The proximal row from the thumb side is the scaphoid, the lunate, the triquetrum and the pisiform. The other three bones named lie in the distal row.

Question 2073. A described radiograph shows a bone shaped like a boat beneath the thumb, with its lower part hidden behind the thumb bones. Which bone is described, and why is a break in it easily missed? The answer is the scaphoid. It lies at an angle across the wrist, so the part that breaks is often hidden and the bone is tender at the base of the thumb.

Question 2074. A constructed illustration: a person falls on the outstretched hand and feels pain in the hollow at the base of the thumb. Which bone is injured, and why? The answer is the scaphoid, whose blood vessels enter near its lower end and run backward. A break through the middle can cut off the supply to the proximal fragment. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 2075. Which carpal bone is the largest, lies in the middle of the wrist and takes the base of the third metacarpal bone? The answer is the capitate. It sits at the center of the carpus, with the trapezoid on its thumb side and the hamate on its little finger side. It is the keystone of the wrist.

Question 2076. Which of these structures passes through the carpal tunnel: the ulnar nerve, the median nerve, the radial artery, or the tendon of the flexor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the median nerve. It lies just beneath the flexor retinaculum, with the finger flexor tendons around it. The ulnar nerve and the ulnar artery pass into the hand outside the tunnel.

Question 2077. Which structure lies just beneath the flexor retinaculum inside the carpal tunnel, more superficial than the tendons around it? The answer is the median nerve. It lies directly under the ligament at the wrist. Pressure there is one cause of tingling in the thumb, the index and the middle fingers.

Question 2078. True or false: the tendon of the palmaris longus passes through the carpal tunnel. The answer is false. It crosses in front of the flexor retinaculum and spreads into the palmar aponeurosis. The tendons beneath the retinaculum are those of the flexor muscles of the fingers and thumb, with the median nerve.

Question 2079. A described drawing of the palm shows a raised fleshy ridge at the base of the thumb and a smaller one at the base of the little finger. What are the two ridges called? The answer is the thenar and hypothenar eminences. The thenar eminence is formed by the muscles of the thumb, the hypothenar by those of the little finger.

Question 2080. Which pair belongs together: the flexor retinaculum with the carpal tunnel, the palmar aponeurosis with the extensor tendons, the extensor retinaculum with the flexor tendons, or the fibrous sheath with the wrist? The answer is the flexor retinaculum with the carpal tunnel, over which it arches. The palmar aponeurosis lies in the palm and the extensor retinaculum on the back of the wrist.

Question 2081. Which statement about the blood supply of the scaphoid is correct: the vessels enter near the thumb and flow backward, they enter near the forearm and flow toward the thumb, the bone has no supply of its own, or only the ulnar artery supplies it? The answer is that they enter near the thumb and flow backward, so a break through the middle starves the proximal fragment.

Question 2082. Which artery crosses the floor of the hollow at the base of the thumb, lying on the scaphoid and the trapezium? The answer is the radial artery. It passes between the tendons that bound the hollow and then turns into the palm. Its pulse can be felt there as well as at the front of the wrist.

Question 2083. A constructed illustration: an older person falls on the outstretched hand and the lower radius is bent backward with the hand. Which pattern is described? The answer is a break of the lower radius with the fragment displaced backward. It is an illustration, not a real patient. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2084. A described radiograph of a hand shows two small round bones lying beside the joint at the base of the thumb, in front of the first metacarpal bone. What are they? The answer is the sesamoid bones of the thumb. They lie within the tendons that cross the joint and are normal, not fragments of bone.

Question 2085. Which muscle forms the fleshy mound at the base of the thumb and draws the thumb away from the palm: the adductor pollicis, the abductor pollicis brevis, the first dorsal interosseous, or the flexor digitorum superficialis? The answer is the abductor pollicis brevis, supplied by the median nerve. The adductor pollicis pulls the thumb toward the palm and belongs to the ulnar nerve.

Question 2086. Name the four carpal bones of the row nearest the forearm, spoken as a list from the thumb side to the little finger side. The answer is the scaphoid, the lunate, the triquetrum and the pisiform. The pisiform is the small round bone on the front of the triquetrum. The other row holds the trapezium, the trapezoid, the capitate and the hamate.

Question 2087. Which of these bones is the metacarpal bone of the thumb: the first, the second, the third, or the fifth? The answer is the first. It lies at the outer edge of the hand and is shorter and thicker than the rest. Its base meets the trapezium in a saddle-shaped joint, which allows the thumb to reach across the palm.

Question 2088. Which of these movements of the hand at the wrist carries the hand toward the thumb side: bending the wrist forward, bending it backward, moving it toward the thumb, or moving it toward the little finger? The answer is moving it toward the thumb side. That movement is called radial deviation. Its opposite, toward the little finger, is ulnar deviation.

Question 2089. Which structure passes into the hand just lateral to the pisiform bone and lies outside the carpal tunnel? The answer is the ulnar nerve, with the ulnar artery beside it. It crosses the wrist in a small channel between the pisiform and the hook of the hamate. Because it lies outside the tunnel, pressure inside does not affect it.

Question 2090. A described dissection of the palm shows a tough fibrous sheet sending bands toward the fingers. What is the sheet, and what does it do? The answer is the palmar aponeurosis. It lies just under the skin and gives the palm a firm grip. It also protects the tendons and vessels beneath it.

Question 2091. True or false: the joint at the base of the thumb between the trapezium and the first metacarpal bone is a saddle joint. The answer is true. Its two curved surfaces sit at right angles to each other, so the thumb can move in two planes. The combination of those movements brings the thumb pad against the fingers.

Question 2092. Which of these tendons passes through the carpal tunnel: the flexor carpi radialis, the palmaris longus, the flexor pollicis longus, or the extensor carpi ulnaris? The answer is the tendon of the flexor pollicis longus. The other three lie outside the tunnel. Nine tendons and the median nerve pass through it.

Question 2093. A described section across the wrist shows the carpal bones in an arch with a fibrous band across the top. In which plane was the section cut, and what does the space beneath the band contain? The answer is a transverse plane, and the space is the carpal tunnel. It contains the tendons of the flexor muscles of the fingers and thumb, with the median nerve among them.

Question 2094. Which joint is the wrist joint proper: the joint between the radius and the ulna, the joint between the radius and the first carpal row, the joint between the carpal rows, or the joint at the base of the thumb? The answer is the joint between the radius and the first row of carpal bones. A disc of cartilage separates the ulna from that joint.

Question 2095. Which statement about the short muscles of the thumb is correct: the abductor pollicis brevis and the opponens pollicis are supplied by the median nerve, all of them by the ulnar nerve, the adductor by the median nerve, or all of them by the radial nerve? The answer is that the abductor pollicis brevis and the opponens pollicis are supplied by the median nerve.

Question 2096. A described photograph shows the fleshy mound at the base of the thumb flattened and the thumb in the plane of the palm. Which nerve is injured, and by what route does it reach the thumb muscles? The answer is the median nerve, whose small recurrent branch curls around the flexor retinaculum to the thumb muscles. Injury to it leaves the thumb unable to oppose.

Question 2097. Which of these pairs belongs together: the scaphoid with a boat, the lunate with a hook, the hamate with the moon, or the pisiform with a wedge? The answer is the scaphoid with a boat, since the word means a skiff. The lunate is named for the moon, the pisiform for a pea, and the hamate for its hook.

Question 2098. Which carpal bone lies at the base of the thumb, meets the first metacarpal bone and carries a ridge and a groove on its palmar surface? The answer is the trapezium. The ridge gives attachment to muscles of the thumb and the groove carries the flexor carpi radialis tendon.

Question 2099. A constructed illustration: a fall tears the ligament on the inner side of the thumb joint, and the thumb bends away from the index finger when stressed. Which structure is illustrated as torn? The answer is the ulnar collateral ligament of the joint between the first metacarpal and the first phalanx of the thumb. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 2100. Which statement about the deep branch of the ulnar nerve is correct: it supplies the thenar muscles, it runs with the radial artery, it supplies the muscles between the metacarpals, or it lies in the carpal tunnel? The answer is that it supplies the muscles between the metacarpal bones. It also supplies two small muscles of the ring and little fingers and lies outside the carpal tunnel.

To summarize, the elbow is a hinge between the humerus and the ulna and a pivot between the humerus and the radial head, held by the collateral ligaments and by the annular ligament. The medial epicondyle carries the common flexor tendon with the ulnar nerve behind it, and the lateral epicondyle the common extensor tendon with the deep branch of the radial nerve through the supinator. The cubital fossa holds the biceps tendon, the brachial artery and the median nerve in a row, and the interosseous membrane carries force from the radius to the ulna. At the wrist the scaphoid lies at the base of the thumb and is the bone most often broken, the median nerve passes through the carpal tunnel with the flexor tendons, and the ulnar nerve enters the hand outside it.
