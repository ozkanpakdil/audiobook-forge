# Chapter 4 — Anatomical Variation

No two bodies are built to exactly the same plan, and this chapter is about the differences that count as normal rather than as disease. It covers the variations in number, position, shape and course that a student meets in the dissection room, and then turns to surface anatomy, the landmarks that can be seen and felt through the skin before anything is cut. It holds one hundred questions, and every one gives its answer straight after the question and then explains why that answer is right. Recognizing a variant for what it is, and knowing where a normal structure lies, are two halves of the same skill. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 301. What name is given to the arrangement in which the heart, the liver and the spleen are all mirrored from their usual sides? The answer is situs inversus totalis. Every thoracic and abdominal organ lies as a mirror image of the ordinary layout, with the heart pointing to the right.

Question 302. Which of these is a small separate mass of normal tissue found in about one person in ten: an accessory spleen, an accessory liver, an accessory kidney, or an accessory adrenal gland? The answer is an accessory spleen. It is a rounded nodule of splenic tissue, usually near the upper pole of the spleen.

Question 303. Which of these names a kidney anomaly in which the two lower poles are joined across the midline: a horseshoe kidney, a pelvic kidney, a duplex kidney, or a crossed kidney? The answer is a horseshoe kidney. The joining band crosses in front of the great vessels, and the organ rests lower than usual.

Question 304. A described radiograph of the abdomen shows the two renal outlines joined below by a band of tissue that crosses the midline in front of the aorta. Which anomaly is shown, and why does it sit lower than usual? The answer is a horseshoe kidney, held down because its isthmus catches beneath the inferior mesenteric artery.

Question 305. In a horseshoe kidney the two ureters leave the hila of the fused lower poles. Which part of the fused organ do they cross? The answer is the isthmus, which both ureters cross in front of. That relation explains why the band can obstruct the flow of urine.

Question 306. What is the name of the extra rib that arises from the seventh cervical vertebra and lies in the root of the neck, above the first rib? The answer is a cervical rib. It is present in well under one person in a hundred and is often found only when it presses on the nerves or the vessels of the arm.

Question 307. A constructed illustration: a young adult has wasting of the small muscles of one hand and numbness along the inner border of the forearm, with no injury to the arm. Which variant in the root of the neck explains the picture? The answer is a cervical rib, which stretches the lower trunk of the brachial plexus.

Question 308. Which structures pass over a cervical rib and may be stretched by it: the upper trunk of the brachial plexus, the lower trunk of the brachial plexus with the subclavian artery, the phrenic nerve, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the lower trunk of the brachial plexus with the subclavian artery. Both cross the cervical rib behind the clavicle.

Question 309. A described radiograph of the lower neck shows a slender rib arising from the seventh cervical vertebra and ending free in the soft tissues. Which of these is shown: a cervical rib, a lumbar rib, a bifid rib, or a sternal foramen? The answer is a cervical rib. It may join the first rib, and it is the commonest extra rib.

Question 310. What name is given to a kidney that fails to rise and comes to rest in the pelvis, usually in front of the sacrum and below the brim? The answer is a pelvic kidney. Its artery comes from the nearby iliac vessels instead of the aorta, and the condition is usually discovered by chance.

Question 311. Which vessel may cross in front of the ureter and obstruct it: the inferior mesenteric artery, an accessory renal artery, the gonadal artery, or the middle suprarenal artery? The answer is an accessory renal artery. Extra renal arteries are common and often arise from the aorta below the main one, passing in front of the ureter.

Question 312. A constructed illustration: an examiner asks a person to press the thumb against the little finger and then bend the wrist, and a thin tendon stands out in the middle of the front of the wrist. Which tendon is being tested? The answer is the palmaris longus. That movement tightens it, and its tendon can be felt just above the wrist crease.

Question 313. Which of these muscles is the one most often missing as a normal variant: the palmaris longus, the plantaris, the psoas minor, or the sternocleidomastoid? The answer is the palmaris longus. It is absent in about one person in seven, more often on the left and more often in women, while the others are missing far less commonly.

Question 314. Which small muscle of the calf has a thin belly and a long slender tendon, is absent in about one person in ten, and is therefore used by surgeons as a graft? The answer is the plantaris. It arises above the lateral head of the gastrocnemius and its tendon runs down beside the calcaneal tendon to reach the heel.

Question 315. A described dissection of the axilla shows a muscular slip that crosses from the latissimus dorsi to the pectoralis major, arching over the axillary vessels and the cords of the brachial plexus. What is this variant called? The answer is the axillary arch. It is a normal variant of the latissimus dorsi and it narrows the space of the axilla.

Question 316. In an axilla that contains an axillary arch, which structures lie immediately deep to the arch: the axillary artery and vein with the cords of the brachial plexus, the serratus anterior, the subscapularis, or the clavicle? The answer is the axillary artery and vein with the cords of the brachial plexus. The arch may press on them when the arm is raised.

Question 317. What is the name of the flat vertical muscle that lies on the front of the chest, superficial to the pectoralis major and parallel to the sternum, and is present in only a small minority of people? The answer is the sternalis. Its nerve supply comes from the intercostal nerves, which suggests that it belongs with the muscles of the abdominal wall.

Question 318. Which of these is an accessory head of the flexor pollicis longus, arising from the coronoid process of the ulna: the Gantzer muscle, the anconeus epitrochlearis, an accessory slip of the pronator teres, or an accessory palmaris longus? The answer is the Gantzer muscle. It lies in the deep flexor compartment of the forearm and may press on the anterior interosseous nerve.

Question 319. What is the name of the bony spur that projects from the anteromedial surface of the humerus about 5 centimeters above the medial epicondyle and is present in about one person in a hundred? The answer is the supracondylar process. A fibrous band runs from the spur down to the medial epicondyle and completes a tunnel over the median nerve.

Question 320. A constructed illustration: a person has weakness of the muscles of the thenar eminence and numbness of the palm, and a radiograph shows a small bony spur above the medial epicondyle of the humerus. Which nerve is compressed, and by what? The answer is the median nerve, trapped between the spur and the fibrous band that joins it to the medial epicondyle.

Question 321. A described radiograph of the lower end of the humerus, taken from behind, shows a small oval opening in the thin plate of bone between the olecranon fossa and the coronoid fossa. What is this opening called? The answer is the supratrochlear foramen, also called the septal aperture. It is a normal variant, commoner in women, and it is not a fracture.

Question 322. True or false: the patella may be formed from two separate bony pieces that never unite, so that a radiograph seems to show a broken bone. The answer is true. The condition is a bipartite patella, and the separate piece usually lies at the upper and lateral corner of the bone. Its margins are smooth and dense, unlike the sharp edges of a fracture.

Question 323. What is the name of the small sesamoid bone that lies in the tendon of the lateral head of the gastrocnemius behind the knee and is present in a minority of people? The answer is the fabella. It is a normal sesamoid bone and must not be mistaken for a loose body inside the knee joint.

Question 324. Which of these is an extra ossicle on the medial side of the foot, beside the tuberosity of the navicular bone: the accessory navicular, the os trigonum, the os peroneum, or the os vesalianum? The answer is the accessory navicular. It may be quite separate from the bone or joined to it by cartilage.

Question 325. An accessory navicular bone lies within a tendon on the medial side of the foot. Which tendon is it, and why does that matter? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior, which inserts into the tuberosity of the navicular. The extra bone lies inside the tendon, so the pull of the muscle can irritate the junction.

Question 326. What name is given to the separate ossicle that lies behind the talus, next to its lateral tubercle, and is present in a minority of people? The answer is the os trigonum. It begins as a separate center of bone that never joins the talus, and it can be pinched between the tibia and the calcaneus when the foot is pointed.

Question 327. Which artery is most often missing from the ring of vessels at the base of the brain: the anterior communicating artery, one posterior communicating artery, the basilar artery, or the anterior cerebral artery? The answer is one posterior communicating artery. A complete ring is found in only about one person in five.

Question 328. In a described dissection of the base of the brain the posterior cerebral artery arises from the internal carotid artery rather than from the basilar artery. What is that arrangement called? The answer is a fetal origin of the posterior cerebral artery. Most of its blood comes from the internal carotid, and the first part of the artery behind it is small.

Question 329. Which of these may arise as the last branch of the arch of the aorta and pass behind the esophagus to reach the right arm: the right common carotid artery, an aberrant right subclavian artery, the right vertebral artery, or the thyrocervical trunk? The answer is an aberrant right subclavian artery. It is the commonest anomaly of the arch.

Question 330. A constructed illustration: before an operation on the thyroid gland, imaging shows an artery passing behind the esophagus toward the right arm. Why does that finding matter to the surgeon? The answer is that the right recurrent laryngeal nerve does not recur. With no subclavian artery on that side to loop around, it runs straight from the vagus nerve to the larynx.

Question 331. What is the name of the narrow upward extension of thyroid tissue that runs from the isthmus toward the hyoid bone in many people? The answer is the pyramidal lobe. It is a remnant of the thyroglossal duct, along which the gland descended from the tongue, and it may be left behind when part of the gland is removed.

Question 332. A described magnetic resonance image of the neck shows thyroid tissue in the midline above the isthmus, reaching up toward the hyoid bone in front of the thyroid cartilage. Which structure is shown, and from what does it develop? The answer is the pyramidal lobe, a remnant of the thyroglossal duct, which marks the path the gland took as it descended.

Question 333. Which of these names an artery that ascends in the midline in front of the trachea to reach the thyroid gland: the inferior thyroid artery, the thyroidea ima artery, the superior thyroid artery, or the transverse cervical artery? The answer is the thyroidea ima artery. It usually arises from the brachiocephalic trunk, and a midline incision may meet it.

Question 334. Which of these sutures may persist into adult life as a midline seam running down the middle of the forehead: the sagittal suture, the metopic suture, the coronal suture, or the lambdoid suture? The answer is the metopic suture. It separates the two halves of the frontal bone in the child and usually closes by the end of the second year.

Question 335. A described radiograph of the skull shows a thin straight line running down the middle of the forehead. What is this line, and how is it told from a fracture? The answer is a persistent metopic suture. A suture has smooth, dense margins and lies in the midline, while a fracture has sharp and irregular edges.

Question 336. A described specimen of a skull shows a separate triangular piece of bone at the back of the vault, between the two parietal bones and above the occipital bone. What is this bone? The answer is the Inca bone, or interparietal bone. It is a separate center of ossification of the occipital bone that has not united.

Question 337. True or false: small extra bones may lie within the sutures of the skull vault, so that a suture looks broken on a radiograph. The answer is true. They are called sutural or wormian bones, and they are common along the lambdoid suture. Their rounded and dense margins separate them from the fragments of a fracture.

Question 338. True or false: the body of the sternum may contain a small opening in the midline where the two sternal bars did not fuse completely. The answer is true. It is called a sternal foramen, and it usually lies in the lower part of the body of the sternum, close to the heart and the great vessels.

Question 339. In a described specimen the transverse process of the lowest lumbar vertebra is fused to the sacrum, and the vertebra above carries the lowest free transverse process. What is this arrangement called? The answer is sacralization of the fifth lumbar vertebra, which leaves only four free lumbar vertebrae and moves the landmarks above it.

Question 340. A described radiograph of the abdomen shows a tongue of liver tissue extending below the costal margin on the right. What is this variant called? The answer is the Riedel lobe. It is a downward extension of the right lobe of the liver and is a normal variant rather than a tumor.

Question 341. Which of these describes a normal variant of the gallbladder in which the fundus is folded back on itself: a Phrygian cap, a Hartmann pouch, a double gallbladder, or an intrahepatic gallbladder? The answer is a Phrygian cap. The fold gives the fundus a rounded cap, and it does not by itself cause any symptoms.

Question 342. The appendix may lie in several positions. Which position is the commonest, with the appendix lying behind the cecum and running upward against the back wall of the abdomen? The answer is the retrocecal position. It explains why an inflamed appendix there can cause pain in the flank.

Question 343. A constructed illustration: a person has pain in the right flank and the right hip, made worse by extending the hip, and tenderness is felt deep behind the cecum. Which position of the appendix best explains the picture? The answer is a retrocecal appendix lying against the psoas muscle. Its position against that muscle is what makes stretching the hip painful.

Question 344. What is the name of the variant in which the ventral and dorsal parts of the pancreas keep separate ducts? The answer is pancreatic divisum. The two buds normally fuse and their ducts join, and when they do not, the dorsal duct drains most of the gland.

Question 345. A described magnetic resonance image of the upper abdomen shows two pancreatic ducts that do not join, the larger one opening separately into the duodenum. Which variant is shown, and which duct drains most of the gland? The answer is pancreatic divisum, in which the dorsal duct drains most of the gland through the minor papilla.

Question 346. Which of these is a remnant of the vitelline duct that hangs from the border of the ileum opposite its mesentery: a Meckel diverticulum, the Meckel cartilage, a pharyngeal pouch, or a urachal cyst? The answer is a Meckel diverticulum. It lies on the antimesenteric border of the ileum and it may contain gastric or pancreatic tissue.

Question 347. Which of these describes a left renal vein that divides and passes both in front of and behind the aorta: a retroaortic left renal vein, a circumaortic left renal vein, a double inferior vena cava, or a left-sided inferior vena cava? The answer is a circumaortic left renal vein. The ring it forms around the aorta matters when the renal vessels are clamped.

Question 348. Which of these pairs belongs together: a horseshoe kidney with fusion of the two lower poles, an accessory spleen with fusion of the two kidneys, a cervical rib with fusion of two ribs, or the Inca bone with fusion of two vertebrae? The answer is a horseshoe kidney with fusion of the two lower poles.

Question 349. Which of these pairs belongs together: an aberrant right subclavian artery with a course behind the esophagus, a thyroidea ima artery with a course beside the trachea, a pyramidal lobe with a course behind the aorta, or a cervical rib with a course behind the clavicle? The answer is an aberrant right subclavian artery with a course behind the esophagus.

Question 350. Name two muscles that may be absent in a healthy person as a normal variant. The answer is the palmaris longus and the plantaris. Neither is needed for the ordinary use of the hand or the foot, so their absence causes no weakness that a person would notice.

Question 351. Which surface landmark is formed by the ridge where the manubrium of the sternum meets its body, felt as a horizontal bar across the front of the chest? The answer is the sternal angle, also called the angle of Louis. It can be felt in everyone, and it is the starting point for counting the ribs and the intercostal spaces.

Question 352. The sternal angle lies at the level of which thoracic vertebra: the second, the fourth, the sixth, or the eighth? The answer is the fourth thoracic vertebra. The plane through the sternal angle also passes through the disc between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae, so it marks both that disc and the vertebra.

Question 353. At the sternal angle the second costal cartilage joins the sternum. Which rib therefore lies immediately below that cartilage, and which space lies below that rib? The answer is the second rib, so the space between the second and third ribs is the first one below the angle. Counting from there is how the other ribs are found.

Question 354. Which bony point forms the rounded prominence at the base of the back of the neck, felt best when the head is bent forward? The answer is the spine of the seventh cervical vertebra, called the vertebra prominens. It is the longest and most easily felt of the cervical spines.

Question 355. The highest point of the iliac crest lies at the level of which vertebra: the second lumbar, the fourth lumbar, the first sacral, or the twelfth thoracic? The answer is the fourth lumbar vertebra. The crests of the two hip bones lie on that level, and it is the level at which the lumbar spine is counted on the surface.

Question 356. The inferior angle of the scapula lies against the chest wall. At which vertebral spine does it lie when the arms hang by the sides, and which rib is at the same level? The answer is the spine of the seventh thoracic vertebra, with the seventh rib. The root of the spine of the scapula lies higher, at the third thoracic spine.

Question 357. A described photograph of a back shows a furrow down the midline, two dimples low in the back, and a rounded bony point at the base of the neck. Which structures make these marks? The answer is the spines of the vertebrae, the posterior superior iliac spines, and the seventh cervical spine. The dimples overlie the sacroiliac joints.

Question 358. Which bone can be felt in the front of the neck between the chin and the thyroid cartilage, moves when a person swallows, and lies at the level of the third cervical vertebra? The answer is the hyoid bone. It is the only bone of the body that does not articulate with another bone, being held in place by muscles and ligaments.

Question 359. The cricoid cartilage marks the start of which structure, and at which cervical vertebra does it lie: the pharynx at the fourth, the trachea at the sixth, the larynx at the third, or the esophagus at the seventh? The answer is the trachea at the sixth cervical vertebra. The cricoid is the only complete ring of cartilage in the airway.

Question 360. The carotid pulse is felt in the neck beside the larynx. At which level is it most easily felt, and which bony point of the sixth cervical vertebra lies just behind the artery? The answer is the level of the upper border of the thyroid cartilage, with the carotid tubercle behind. The artery can be pressed against that tubercle.

Question 361. A constructed illustration: a midline swelling of the neck lies below the larynx and moves when the person swallows. Which membrane lies immediately beneath the skin at that point, and between which two cartilages? The answer is the cricothyroid membrane, between the thyroid cartilage above and the cricoid cartilage below. It is the most superficial part of the airway in the neck.

Question 362. A described chest radiograph carries marks at the sternal angle, at the nipple and at the tip of the eleventh rib. Which structures lie at those three marks? The answer is the disc between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae, the fourth thoracic dermatome at the nipple, and the lowest part of the pleural cavity at the eleventh rib.

Question 363. In which intercostal space and which vertical line is the apex beat of the heart normally felt, at the point furthest down and out on the chest wall? The answer is the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line. That point marks the apex of the heart, where the wall of the left ventricle comes closest to the surface.

Question 364. Where is the sound of the mitral valve best heard, and why not over the valve itself: at the apex, at the right edge of the sternum, at the left edge of the sternum, or in the second intercostal space? The answer is at the apex. The valve faces up and to the left, so its sound travels toward the apex of the heart.

Question 365. How far above the medial third of the clavicle do the apex of the lung and the cupola of the pleura reach in a healthy adult: about 1 centimeter, about 3 centimeters, about 6 centimeters, or about 10 centimeters? The answer is about 3 centimeters. Both rise above the clavicle, and the pleura reaches a little higher than the lung itself.

Question 366. What is the name of the triangular hollow on the back of the hand, bounded by the tendons of two muscles of the thumb, that appears when the thumb is fully extended? The answer is the anatomical snuffbox. The radial artery crosses its floor and can be felt there as a pulse.

Question 367. Which tendons form the boundaries of the anatomical snuffbox: the extensor pollicis longus and the abductor pollicis longus with the extensor pollicis brevis, the flexor carpi radialis and the palmaris longus, the extensor digitorum and the extensor indicis, or the flexor pollicis longus and the pronator quadratus? The answer is the extensor pollicis longus and the abductor pollicis longus with the extensor pollicis brevis.

Question 368. Which bones form the floor of the anatomical snuffbox: the scaphoid and the trapezium, the lunate and the triquetrum, the capitate and the hamate, or the pisiform and the triquetrum? The answer is the scaphoid and the trapezium. The radial artery crosses that floor between the tendons, and that is where its pulse is felt.

Question 369. A constructed illustration: after a fall on the outstretched hand, a person has tenderness deep in the hollow at the base of the thumb, though the radiograph looks normal. Which bone is injured, and why does it matter? The answer is the scaphoid. A branch of the radial artery enters it near its far end, so a break through the waist can starve the piece nearest the forearm.

Question 370. A described photograph of a hand shows the wrist bent, with a thin tendon in the middle of the front of the wrist and another running to the base of the thumb. Which tendons are these? The answer is the palmaris longus and the flexor carpi radialis. The palmaris longus is the one that may be absent.

Question 371. Which two bony points are joined by the inguinal ligament, felt as a ridge running across the groin? The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic tubercle. The ligament is the folded lower border of the tendon of the external oblique muscle.

Question 372. Which point marks the femoral pulse in the groin: the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, the mid-inguinal point halfway between the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic symphysis, the pubic tubercle, or the umbilicus? The answer is the mid-inguinal point. The midpoint of the inguinal ligament lies a little medial to it and marks the deep inguinal ring.

Question 373. Which of these lies outside the femoral sheath within the femoral triangle: the femoral artery, the femoral vein, the femoral canal, or the femoral nerve? The answer is the femoral nerve. The sheath encloses the artery, the vein and the canal, while the nerve lies lateral to the artery and outside the sheath.

Question 374. A constructed illustration: a swelling appears in the groin below the inguinal ligament and just lateral to the pubic tubercle, and it enlarges when the person coughs. Which opening has it come through? The answer is the femoral canal. The femoral ring lies just medial to the femoral vein, so a swelling here lies below and lateral to the pubic tubercle.

Question 375. A described photograph shows the back of a knee with a diamond-shaped hollow bounded by two tendons above and two muscle bellies below. Which muscles bound it, and which structure lies deepest within it? The answer is the biceps femoris and the semitendinosus above with the two heads of the gastrocnemius below. The popliteal artery lies deepest, against the bone.

Question 376. A described photograph of the back of the lower leg shows a thick cord running from the calf to the heel, standing out when the person rises on tiptoe. Which structure is shown? The answer is the calcaneal tendon, also called the Achilles tendon, which inserts into the back of the calcaneus.

Question 377. Which artery can be felt as a pulse in the groove between the back of the medial malleolus and the heel: the dorsalis pedis artery, the posterior tibial artery, the anterior tibial artery, or the fibular artery? The answer is the posterior tibial artery. It lies behind the medial malleolus, covered only by skin and a little fascia.

Question 378. Several structures pass behind the medial malleolus, between it and the calcaneus. Which lies nearest the bone, and which lies farthest from it? The answer is the tendon of the tibialis posterior nearest the malleolus and the flexor hallucis longus farthest away. Between them lie the flexor digitorum longus, the posterior tibial artery and the tibial nerve.

Question 379. Which long vein begins at the medial end of the arch of the foot, runs up in front of the medial malleolus, and ends in the femoral vein? The answer is the great saphenous vein. It is the longest vein in the body, it lies just under the skin along its whole course, and it is often used as a graft.

Question 380. Where is the dorsalis pedis pulse felt: immediately lateral to the tendon of the extensor hallucis longus, immediately medial to that tendon, behind the lateral malleolus, or in the sole of the foot? The answer is immediately lateral to the tendon of the extensor hallucis longus. Its course varies, so a missing pulse there does not prove that the artery is blocked.

Question 381. What is the name of the point on the abdominal wall, one third of the way along the line from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus, where the appendix is usually tender? The answer is McBurney point. It overlies the base of the appendix in the usual position, though the appendix itself may lie elsewhere.

Question 382. The fundus of the gallbladder is marked on the surface at which point: the tip of the ninth costal cartilage, the tip of the twelfth rib, the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, or the level of the third lumbar spine? The answer is the tip of the ninth costal cartilage, where the lateral border of the rectus abdominis meets the costal margin.

Question 383. Name the ribs between which the spleen lies when it is normal in size, and say which rib its long axis follows. The answer is the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh ribs, with the long axis along the tenth rib. The spleen lies behind them on the left, with its upper end close to the diaphragm.

Question 384. A constructed illustration: a person has pain under the right costal margin that worsens on deep breathing, and the pain is also felt over the right shoulder. Which organ lies beneath that point, and which nerve carries the second pain? The answer is the gallbladder, with the shoulder pain carried by the right phrenic nerve from the diaphragm.

Question 385. A described photograph of the abdomen has two vertical lines through the middle of the clavicle and two horizontal lines at the lowest ribs and the hip bones. Which scheme do those lines mark? The answer is the nine regions of the abdomen. The upper middle region is the epigastric, the central one the umbilical, and the lowest the hypogastric.

Question 386. Which plane, halfway between the upper border of the sternum and the pubic symphysis, crosses the body at the level of the first lumbar vertebra? The answer is the transpyloric plane. It lies about a hand's breadth below the sternal angle and is a useful level for finding several organs.

Question 387. Which of these lies in the transpyloric plane: the pylorus of the stomach, the fundus of the stomach, the umbilicus, or the place where the aorta divides? The answer is the pylorus. The plane also passes through the neck of the pancreas and the hila of the kidneys.

Question 388. What is the name of the angle on the back where the lower border of the twelfth rib meets the outer border of the muscle that runs up beside the spine? The answer is the renal angle. It lies over the lower pole of the kidney, and tenderness there may come from the kidney or from the pleura above it.

Question 389. A constructed illustration: a person has pain in the renal angle on the right, worse on breathing, and also felt over the right shoulder. Which two structures may be responsible? The answer is the kidney and the diaphragm, because the pleural recess lies just above the kidney. The phrenic nerve supplies both the diaphragm and the shoulder skin.

Question 390. Which spinal segment supplies the skin around the umbilicus? The answer is the tenth thoracic segment. The umbilicus lies at the level of the third and fourth lumbar vertebrae, but its skin is supplied by the tenth thoracic nerve, because the segments do not follow the skeleton.

Question 391. Which dermatome includes the skin of the nipple: the second thoracic, the fourth thoracic, the sixth thoracic, or the eighth thoracic? The answer is the fourth thoracic. The dermatomes run around the trunk in bands, and the nipple is the classic landmark for the fourth thoracic segment.

Question 392. Which dermatome supplies the skin over the great toe and the inner side of the sole: the third lumbar, the fourth lumbar, the fifth lumbar, or the first sacral? The answer is the fifth lumbar. The first sacral segment supplies the outer border of the foot and the little toe, and the fourth lumbar supplies the skin over the medial malleolus.

Question 393. A described map of the hand shades three sensory territories: the little finger and the inner border, the thumb and the outer border, and the middle of the palm. Which nerves supply them? The answer is the ulnar nerve for the inner border, the radial nerve for the outer border, and the median nerve for the middle of the palm.

Question 394. True or false: the skin of the nipple lies in the fourth thoracic dermatome. The answer is true. The fourth thoracic nerve supplies a band that runs around the chest at the level of the nipple, over the pericardium and the pleura within.

Question 395. True or false: the skin around the umbilicus is supplied by the twelfth thoracic dermatome. The answer is false. The umbilicus lies in the tenth thoracic dermatome, while the twelfth thoracic segment supplies the skin just above the pubic symphysis. The two levels are easily confused because both lie near the front of the abdomen.

Question 396. Which of these pairs belongs together: the seventh cervical spine with the vertebra prominens, the fourth lumbar spine with the sternal angle, the seventh thoracic spine with the angle of the jaw, or the second sacral segment with the iliac crest? The answer is the seventh cervical spine with the vertebra prominens. The sternal angle lies at the fourth thoracic vertebra and the iliac crest at the fourth lumbar.

Question 397. Which of these pairs belongs together: the posterior tibial pulse with the groove behind the medial malleolus, the dorsalis pedis pulse with the groove behind the lateral malleolus, the popliteal pulse with the front of the knee, or the radial pulse with the front of the elbow? The answer is the posterior tibial pulse with the groove behind the medial malleolus. The other three are paired with the wrong surface.

Question 398. Which of these pairs belongs together: the femoral triangle with the inguinal ligament above, the popliteal fossa with the inguinal ligament above, the cubital fossa with the calcaneal tendon below, or the anatomical snuffbox with the mastoid process above? The answer is the femoral triangle with the inguinal ligament above. The popliteal fossa lies behind the knee and the cubital fossa in front of the elbow.

Question 399. Name the two bony points that are joined by the inguinal ligament, the ridge felt across the groin. The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic tubercle. The ligament forms the floor of the groin and the upper boundary of the femoral triangle.

Question 400. A described section of the trunk is taken across the chest at the level of the sternal angle, and it shows the heart, both lungs and the vertebral column in cross section. In which plane is the section taken, and which disc does that plane mark? The answer is a transverse plane, at the disc between the fourth and fifth thoracic vertebrae.

To summarize, variation is normal, and the commonest variants each carry a name worth knowing: the horseshoe kidney whose isthmus is caught below the inferior mesenteric artery, the accessory spleen, the cervical rib, the pelvic kidney, the missing palmaris longus or plantaris, the accessory navicular within the tendon of the tibialis posterior, the posterior communicating artery that fails to complete the ring at the base of the brain, the aberrant right subclavian artery behind the esophagus, the pyramidal lobe of the thyroid gland, and the pancreatic divisum that leaves most of the gland draining through the minor papilla. Accessory bones such as the fabella, the os trigonum and the bipartite patella have smooth, dense margins that separate them from fractures, and persistent sutures such as the metopic suture and the wormian bones look the same. On the surface, the sternal angle marks the second costal cartilage and the fourth thoracic vertebra, the iliac crest marks the fourth lumbar vertebra, the inferior angle of the scapula marks the seventh thoracic spine, and the cricoid cartilage marks the sixth cervical vertebra. The femoral pulse lies at the mid-inguinal point, the popliteal artery is the deepest structure in the popliteal fossa, the dorsalis pedis artery is felt lateral to the tendon of the extensor hallucis longus, and the structures behind the medial malleolus run outward from the tibialis posterior to the flexor hallucis longus. Dermatomes close the list: the fourth thoracic at the nipple, the tenth thoracic at the umbilicus, the fifth lumbar over the great toe, and the first sacral along the outer border of the foot.
