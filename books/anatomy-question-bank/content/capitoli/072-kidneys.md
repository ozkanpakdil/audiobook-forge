# Chapter 72 — Kidneys

This chapter examines the kidneys, the paired organs that filter the blood and make urine, and it works inward through the cortex, the medulla, the renal pyramids and the calyces. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer immediately, followed by the reason the answer is right, so nothing has to be carried in mind from one item to the next. The questions begin with the position and coverings of the kidney, then follow the tissue inward to the calyces that collect what the pyramids deliver. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7101. Which paired organs lie behind the peritoneum on the posterior abdominal wall, one on each side of the vertebral column, between the twelfth thoracic and the third lumbar levels? The answer is the kidneys. Each lies against the posterior wall of the abdomen. In the adult each one is about 11 to 12 centimeters long.

Question 7102. Which covering lies directly against the surface of the kidney and can be peeled away from it: the fibrous capsule, the perirenal fat, the renal fascia, or the peritoneum? The answer is the fibrous capsule. It is a smooth sheath wrapped tightly around the organ. The perirenal fat and the renal fascia lie outside it.

Question 7103. Which large organ rests against the upper part of the anterior surface of the right kidney, and how does it affect the position of that kidney? The answer is the liver, which presses on the upper pole and holds the right kidney lower than the left. The right suprarenal gland caps the same pole.

Question 7104. Which structure lies immediately anterior to the lower part of the right kidney: the descending colon, the ascending colon, the spleen, or the stomach? The answer is the ascending colon. It runs up the right side of the abdomen in front of that kidney. The descending colon and the spleen lie on the left.

Question 7105. Which muscle lies immediately behind the kidney, along the medial part of its posterior surface? The answer is the psoas major. The kidney rests on the psoas major and the quadratus lumborum, with the diaphragm above the upper pole. The twelfth rib crosses the same posterior surface. Behind the kidney therefore lies muscle, not peritoneum.

Question 7106. Which statement about the left kidney is correct: it lies against the spleen and the pancreas, it lies in front of the peritoneum, it is related to the duodenum, or it lies lower than the right kidney? The answer is that it lies against the spleen and the pancreas. The stomach lies above and in front of it.

Question 7107. A described coronal section shows a kidney with a smooth outer rim and a central cavity filled with fat, vessels and a funnel-shaped tube. Name the cavity and the tube. The answer is the renal sinus and the renal pelvis. The sinus opens at the hilum, and the pelvis funnels downward to become the ureter.

Question 7108. Which structure lies most anterior in the hilum of the kidney, in front of the artery and the pelvis? The answer is the renal vein. From front to back the hilum holds the vein, then the artery, then the pelvis. The ureter leaves behind those vessels.

Question 7109. Name the outer layer of the kidney that lies immediately deep to the fibrous capsule and contains the renal corpuscles. The answer is the renal cortex. It forms a shell about 1 centimeter thick and dips between the pyramids as the renal columns. The corpuscles and the convoluted tubules lie within it.

Question 7110. Which structure in the renal cortex consists of a cluster of capillaries inside a double-walled cup? The answer is the renal corpuscle. The cup is the glomerular capsule and the tuft inside it is the glomerulus. Blood enters and leaves the tuft through the afferent and the efferent arterioles. The corpuscle is the beginning of the nephron.

Question 7111. Which statement about the renal cortex is correct: it lies deep to the medulla, it contains the loops of Henle, it receives blood from the interlobular arteries, or it is drained by the minor calyces? The answer is that it receives blood from the interlobular arteries. Those vessels run outward from the arcuate arteries toward the capsule.

Question 7112. The band of cortex that lies between two adjacent renal pyramids has a name. What is it called, and which vessels run through it? The answer is a renal column, and it carries the interlobar arteries and veins. The column bridges the outer shell of the kidney and the tissue near the sinus.

Question 7113. Which of these lies at the boundary between the cortex and the medulla: the interlobular artery, the arcuate artery, the segmental artery, or the afferent arteriole? The answer is the arcuate artery. It arches over the base of each pyramid and marks that boundary. The interlobular arteries arise from it. The arcuate artery is the continuation of the interlobar artery.

Question 7114. A described microscopic section of the cortex shows round tufts of capillaries surrounded by coiled tubules, with straight tubules running in bundles toward the surface. What are those bundles called? The answer is the medullary rays, straight tubules extending from the base of a pyramid into the cortex. They carry collecting ducts and loops of Henle.

Question 7115. True or false: the renal cortex is the region of the kidney that contains the renal pyramids. The answer is false. The pyramids belong to the medulla, and each points its apex toward the renal sinus. The cortex is the outer shell that arches over their bases. The columns of cortex also belong to that outer shell.

Question 7116. A described ultrasound image of a normal kidney shows an outer band of medium gray, inner triangular areas that are darker, and a bright central region. Which zone is bright? The answer is the renal sinus, whose fat reflects the sound waves. The dark triangles are the medullary pyramids. Each dark triangle sends its tip toward the bright center.

Question 7117. Which of these pairs belongs together: the renal cortex with the glomeruli, the renal medulla with the glomeruli, the renal cortex with the loops of Henle, or the renal medulla with the convoluted tubules? The answer is the renal cortex with the glomeruli. The corpuscles lie in the cortex and the loops of Henle descend into the medulla.

Question 7118. A described computed tomography scan taken after contrast shows a bright outer rim of kidney tissue and a darker inner zone. Which zone is the bright rim, and why is the inner zone darker? The answer is the renal cortex, and the inner zone is the medulla. Contrast reaches the cortex first because most of the blood flow goes there.

Question 7119. Which structures arch over the base of a renal pyramid, between it and the cortex above? The answer is the arcuate artery and the arcuate vein. They follow the line of the base and give off the interlobular vessels. A renal column runs down on each side of the pyramid. They mark the boundary between the cortex and the medulla.

Question 7120. A constructed illustration: a needle biopsy is taken from the outer part of a kidney. Which layer does the sample come from, and which structures should it contain? The answer is the renal cortex, with renal corpuscles, convoluted tubules and medullary rays. A sample from deeper would contain the straight tubules of the medulla.

Question 7121. Name two structures that lie within the renal cortex, spoken as a list. The answer is the renal corpuscle and the convoluted tubule. Both lie among the coils of the cortical labyrinth between the medullary rays. The collecting ducts pass through on their way inward. The glomerulus sits inside the corpuscle, and the tubule coils around it.

Question 7122. Which structure forms the outer shell of the kidney, lies immediately under the fibrous capsule, and gives the organ a granular surface in the newborn? The answer is the renal cortex. In the fetus and the infant it is divided into visible lobules, and the surface stays bumpy until childhood. In most adults the lobules fuse.

Question 7123. Which statement about the renal columns is correct: they are part of the medulla, they separate the pyramids and carry vessels, they contain the renal papillae, or they drain into the minor calyces? The answer is that they separate the pyramids and carry vessels. The interlobar arteries and veins run through a column toward the arcuate vessels.

Question 7124. A described specimen shows a kidney cut in half, with a band of tissue arching over darker triangular areas and dipping inward between them. What is the dipping part called, and which vessels run in it? The answer is a renal column, carrying the interlobar vessels. The darker triangles are the medullary pyramids.

Question 7125. In the cortex, which vessel gives rise directly to the afferent arterioles of the glomeruli: the arcuate artery, the interlobular artery, the interlobar artery, or the segmental artery? The answer is the interlobular artery. It runs outward through the cortex between the medullary rays. The efferent arteriole leaves the glomerulus behind. The peritubular capillaries then surround the tubules.

Question 7126. Name the structure at the vascular pole of the renal corpuscle where the afferent arteriole meets the distal tubule. The answer is the juxtaglomerular apparatus. It holds the granular cells of the arteriole and the macula densa of the tubule, and it lies close to the glomerulus it serves. The apparatus lies in the cortex, beside its glomerulus.

Question 7127. Which of these zones contains the longest loops of Henle: the outer medulla, the inner medulla, the cortex, or the renal sinus? The answer is the inner medulla, close to the papilla. The juxtamedullary nephrons have these long loops, while the nephrons near the surface have short ones. The longest of them come close to the tip of the papilla.

Question 7128. Which statement about the renal lobule is correct: it is a pyramid with its cortex, it is a medullary ray with the cortex around it, it is bounded by the calyces, or it is another name for a lobe? The answer is that it is a medullary ray with the cortex around it. The renal lobe is the larger unit, a pyramid with its cortex. Several lobules lie within one lobe.

Question 7129. A constructed illustration: a tumor arises from the tubular epithelium of the kidney and grows along the renal vein. From which zone did it arise, and which structure is it invading? The answer is the renal cortex, and it is invading the renal vein. The tubular epithelium lies in the cortex, and the veins of the cortex drain toward the hilum.

Question 7130. Immediately outside the fibrous capsule of the kidney lie two coverings. Name them, in the order in which they are met. The answer is the perirenal fat and then the renal fascia. The fat lies against the capsule, and the fascia wraps the kidney and the fat together. Outside it lies more fat, the pararenal fat.

Question 7131. In a coronal section the cortex is described as arching over the base of each pyramid. Toward which surface of the kidney does the base of a pyramid face? The answer is the outer surface. The base lies against the cortex near the capsule, and the apex points inward to the renal sinus.

Question 7132. Which statement about the blood flow of the renal cortex is correct: it receives less blood than the medulla, it receives most of the renal blood flow, it is supplied only by the renal vein, or it has no capillary network? The answer is that it receives most of the renal blood flow. The medulla receives a much smaller share.

Question 7133. Which vessels mark the boundary between the cortex and the medulla on a cut surface of the kidney? The answer is the arcuate arteries and veins, whose arching line follows the base of every pyramid. The interlobular arteries continue outward from them into the cortex. Each renal column carries vessels up to that line.

Question 7134. Which zone of the kidney lies immediately deep to the fibrous capsule and immediately outside the medulla? The answer is the renal cortex. It is the outer shell of the organ, and the medulla lies within it. The cortex sends columns inward between the pyramids. The columns of cortex are continuous with it.

Question 7135. Which of these pairs belongs together: the renal cortex with the medullary rays, the renal medulla with the glomeruli, the renal cortex with the renal papillae, or the renal medulla with the convoluted tubules? The answer is the renal cortex with the medullary rays, bundles of straight tubules that project into the cortex from the base of a pyramid.

Question 7136. The arcuate arteries arch over the bases of the renal pyramids. At which boundary do they lie? The answer is the boundary between the cortex and the medulla. They mark the corticomedullary junction and give off the interlobular arteries. The interlobar arteries reach them from the columns below.

Question 7137. Name the inner region of the kidney, which contains the triangular structures whose apices point toward the renal sinus. The answer is the renal medulla. It lies deep to the cortex and is divided into pyramids by the renal columns. Its outer and inner parts hold tubules of different length. Each pyramid ends at a papilla, which a calyx receives.

Question 7138. Which statement about the two kinds of nephron is correct: cortical nephrons have the longest loops, juxtamedullary nephrons lie close to the medulla, every nephron reaches the papilla, or all loops lie in the cortex? The answer is that juxtamedullary nephrons lie close to the medulla. Their loops descend far into the medulla beside the vasa recta.

Question 7139. True or false: in a horseshoe kidney the band joining the two kidneys lies below the origin of the inferior mesenteric artery. The answer is true. That band, the isthmus, joins the lower poles in front of the great vessels. It catches beneath the artery as the kidney ascends and holds the organ low.

Question 7140. A constructed illustration: an image of both kidneys shows small streaks of calcium and tiny cysts inside the medullary pyramids, while the cortex looks normal. Which structures are affected? The answer is the collecting ducts within the pyramids. The ducts are dilated and hold urine, and calcium is deposited in the same region.

Question 7141. A described microscopic section shows parallel straight tubules running toward a rounded tip that opens into a cavity. Which region of the kidney is shown? The answer is the medulla, at the tip of a renal pyramid. That tip is the papilla, where the collecting ducts open. The cavity beyond the tip is the beginning of a calyx.

Question 7142. Which structure carries the urine from the tip of a renal pyramid into the next part of the collecting system? The answer is the collecting duct, which opens at the papilla. Several ducts open close together on the surface of each papilla, and beyond them the urine enters a minor calyx. Urine leaves the papilla only through these openings.

Question 7143. Which statement about the loops of Henle is correct: they lie entirely in the cortex, they descend into the medulla and return to the cortex, they open into a minor calyx, or they surround the glomerulus? The answer is that they descend into the medulla and return to the cortex. Their length decides how deep they reach.

Question 7144. Which structure lies between two medullary pyramids, enhances exactly as the cortex does, and can be mistaken for a tumor: a renal column, a renal papilla, a minor calyx, or the renal capsule? The answer is a renal column. It is normal cortical tissue, so it enhances in step with the cortex. The other three structures do not enhance in that pattern.

Question 7145. A described vertical section of the kidney shows a series of cones whose bases meet the cortex and whose tips open into small cups. What are the tips called? The answer is the renal papillae. Each is the apex of a medullary pyramid and is perforated by the openings of the collecting ducts.

Question 7146. Which two regions of the kidney lie on either side of the arcuate vessels? The answer is the cortex above and the medulla below. The arcuate line follows the boundary between them, arching over the base of every pyramid. Renal columns carry the vessels up to that line. The papillae of the pyramids point away from that line.

Question 7147. Which statement about the medulla of a child's kidney is correct: the pyramids are absent, the pyramids are prominent and the surface is lobulated, the medulla has not yet formed, or the calyces lie outside the kidney? The answer is that the pyramids are prominent and the surface is lobulated. The fetal kidney is divided into visible lobules.

Question 7148. Name the anomaly in which the lower poles of the two kidneys are joined across the midline by a band of tissue in front of the great vessels. The answer is a horseshoe kidney. Its isthmus is trapped beneath the inferior mesenteric artery as the organ ascends, so the joined kidney stays low in the abdomen. The collecting systems of the two halves often face forward.

Question 7149. A constructed illustration: a person with long-standing diabetes loses the blood supply to the innermost part of the kidney, and fragments of papillae break away. Which zone has been damaged? The answer is the renal medulla, at the papillae, which receive the least blood flow and suffer first when it falls.

Question 7150. Which structure opens onto the surface of a renal papilla: the proximal convoluted tubule, the collecting duct, the loop of Henle, or the afferent arteriole? The answer is the collecting duct. Between 10 and 25 ducts open on each papilla, forming a small perforated area on its tip. The ducts deliver urine from the nephrons above them.

Question 7151. What name is given to the striped bands of straight tubules that run from the base of a pyramid into the cortex? The answer is the medullary rays. They mark out the lobules within the cortex and carry collecting ducts and loops of Henle. In section they run at right angles to the capsule.

Question 7152. Which statement about the medullary rays is correct: they are part of the medulla, they contain collecting ducts and loops of Henle, they contain the glomeruli, or they lie in the renal sinus? The answer is that they contain collecting ducts and loops of Henle. They project into the cortex from the base of a pyramid.

Question 7153. A described microscopic preparation shows the medulla with two bands: a striped outer band and a paler inner band around the papilla. What are those bands? The answer is the outer zone and the inner zone of the renal medulla. The outer zone carries straight vessels and tubules in bundles. The inner zone consists of thin limbs and collecting ducts.

Question 7154. Which statement about the blood supply of the medulla is correct: it is richer than that of the cortex, it comes only from the renal vein, it comes from the straight vessels and is small, or it is supplied by the interlobular arteries? The answer is that it comes from the straight vessels and is small. The vasa recta descend from the efferent arterioles of the juxtamedullary nephrons.

Question 7155. Name two structures of the renal medulla that run as straight tubes, spoken as a list. The answer is the loop of Henle and the collecting duct. The loop descends from the cortex and returns to it, and the collecting duct runs on to the papilla. Both lie among the straight vessels that serve the medulla.

Question 7156. Which straight vessels run down among the loops of Henle and return blood from the region of the papilla toward the cortex? The answer is the vasa recta. They share the hairpin shape of the loops they accompany, and their thin walls let water move in and out. They carry blood back toward the cortex.

Question 7157. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan shows the cortex as a bright outer band and the medulla as a darker inner zone, while the center of the organ returns a strong signal. What fills that center? The answer is the fat of the renal sinus, with the pelvis and the calyces lying within it.

Question 7158. Which of these pairs belongs together: the renal medulla with the vasa recta, the renal cortex with the vasa recta, the renal medulla with the peritubular capillaries, or the renal cortex with the papilla? The answer is the renal medulla with the vasa recta. Those straight vessels run down among the loops of Henle.

Question 7159. A constructed illustration: in sickle cell disease a period of poor blood flow damages the papillae first. Why do the papillae suffer before the cortex? The answer is that the medulla has a marginal blood supply, carried by straight vessels that take only a small share of the flow. The cortex, with its richer flow, withstands the same period.

Question 7160. Which large vein crosses in front of the aorta, and between which two vessels can it be compressed? The answer is the left renal vein, compressed between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery. It is longer than the right because it must cross the midline. The right renal vein drains into the inferior vena cava.

Question 7161. Which of these explains a single kidney that is larger than usual but shows a normal cortex, medulla and calyces: congenital absence of the other kidney, a horseshoe kidney, a duplex collecting system, or an ectopic pelvic kidney? The answer is congenital absence of the other kidney. The remaining organ carries the whole function and grows larger, but its internal structure stays normal.

Question 7162. A described section through the renal sinus shows rounded profiles of tissue surrounded by fat, with no cortex around them. What are those profiles? The answer is the renal papillae, the apices of the medullary pyramids. They project into the sinus and each is capped by a minor calyx. The cortex lies far away, at the outer border of the organ.

Question 7163. Name the cone-shaped structures of the renal medulla whose bases lie against the cortex and whose apices point toward the renal sinus. The answer is the renal pyramids. A kidney usually holds 8 to 18 of them, and each with its cortex forms a renal lobe. The number of pyramids differs from one kidney to the next.

Question 7164. Which structures separate one renal pyramid from the next: the renal columns, the minor calyces, the medullary rays, or the arcuate arteries? The answer is the renal columns. Each column is a band of cortical tissue that runs from the outer shell to the sinus. The interlobar vessels pass through it between the pyramids.

Question 7165. A described image of the kidney taken after contrast is given shows triangular areas of dense material within the medulla. What are those triangles? The answer is the renal pyramids. The contrast is concentrated within the tubules that run through them. They stand out against the cortex.

Question 7166. What is formed by the union of two or more minor calyces, and into which structure does it open? The answer is a major calyx, which opens into the renal pelvis. A major calyx is a broad channel that carries urine onward from the cups around the papillae. It lies within the renal sinus, behind the renal vessels.

Question 7167. Why are the arteries that climb between the renal pyramids called interlobar arteries: because they pass between the lobes, because they supply both kidneys, because they cross the calyces, or because they lie inside one lobe? The answer is that they pass between the lobes. A lobe is a pyramid with the cortex over its base, and the artery climbs through a renal column between two lobes.

Question 7168. Which statement about the renal pyramids is correct: a normal kidney has about 3 of them, a normal kidney has 8 to 18 of them, they lie within the cortex, or each one drains into the renal vein? The answer is that a normal kidney has 8 to 18 of them. The number varies from person to person.

Question 7169. In a coronal section a pyramid lies with its base at the outer border of the medulla and its apex directed inward. Toward which part of the kidney does the apex point? The answer is toward the renal sinus and the hilum. The base faces the cortex on the outer side. The apex is the papilla, which the calyx receives.

Question 7170. True or false: every renal pyramid is drained by its own separate minor calyx. The answer is false. Two or more pyramids may fuse at their apices to form a compound papilla, and one minor calyx then receives them together. This is common at the upper pole of the kidney. A compound papilla is a normal variation.

Question 7171. Which of these is a pyramid together with the cortex that covers its base: a renal lobule, a renal lobe, a renal column, or a renal papilla? The answer is a renal lobe, the larger unit of the kidney. A lobule is a medullary ray with the cortex around it. A column is cortical tissue, and a papilla belongs to the medulla.

Question 7172. A constructed illustration: an image of a child's kidney shows the pyramids outlined by bands of tissue on every side, so that the surface looks bumpy. What is the most likely explanation? The answer is persistent fetal lobation, a normal variant. The grooves between the lobules usually smooth out in childhood. Those grooves lie over the renal columns.

Question 7173. A constructed illustration: a plane through the lateral border of the kidney is chosen because it crosses few vessels. Which two groups of arteries lie on either side? The answer is the anterior and the posterior branches of the renal artery. They supply the front and the back and do not join one another.

Question 7174. An ectopic kidney lies in the lesser pelvis in front of the sacrum. Which structure lies immediately in front of it, and why is its renal artery unusually short? The answer is the peritoneum, and the artery is short because the kidney never ascended. It kept the blood supply it received at its original level, from a nearby pelvic vessel.

Question 7175. A described dissection shows the medulla divided into cones, each with a striated base and a rounded tip, and bands of paler tissue between the cones. Which two structures are described? The answer is the renal pyramids and the renal columns. The paler bands are the columns of cortical tissue. The base of each cone lies against the cortex above it.

Question 7176. Which of these pairs belongs together: a renal pyramid with the medulla, a renal pyramid with the cortex, a renal column with the medulla, or a medullary ray with the sinus? The answer is a renal pyramid with the medulla. The pyramids are the visible units of the medulla. The columns and rays are cortical.

Question 7177. Which structure forms the apex of a renal pyramid and projects into a minor calyx: the renal papilla, the renal column, the medullary ray, or the arcuate vein? The answer is the renal papilla. Each pyramid ends in a papilla, though the apices of neighboring pyramids may fuse. A fused apex is called a compound papilla.

Question 7178. Which structure of the kidney lies deepest, projecting into the renal sinus at the center of the organ? The answer is the renal papilla, the apex of a medullary pyramid. The base of that pyramid and the capsule lie near the outer surface.

Question 7179. A described computed tomography scan shows a wedge of kidney tissue from the capsule to the sinus that does not enhance, while the rest of the kidney does. Which vessels supply it? The answer is the segmental arteries. Because they do not join one another, one blocked artery leaves a whole wedge without blood.

Question 7180. Which statement about the segmental arteries of the kidney is correct: they join one another freely, they are end arteries supplying separate wedges, they supply only the cortex, or they arise from the renal vein? The answer is that they are end arteries supplying separate wedges. Each serves a defined part with no useful overlap.

Question 7181. Name the cup-shaped structure that surrounds the tip of a renal pyramid and collects the urine leaving it. The answer is the minor calyx. Several minor calyces unite to form a major calyx, and a short neck joins the cup to the rest of the system. The cup is lined by the same epithelium as the ureter.

Question 7182. Which of these comes immediately after the minor calyx as urine leaves the kidney: the major calyx, the ureter, the renal pelvis, or the collecting duct? The answer is the major calyx. Several minor calyces unite to form a major calyx, and the major calyces then unite to form the renal pelvis. The pelvis narrows below and continues as the ureter.

Question 7183. How many minor calyces does a normal kidney usually contain, and how many major calyces does it have? The answer is 7 to 13 minor calyces and usually 3 major calyces. The major calyces are named the upper, the middle and the lower. The pelvis forms where they join. A kidney with a different number of calyces is still normal.

Question 7184. True or false: the collecting ducts, the calyces, the renal pelvis and the ureter all develop from the same outgrowth. The answer is true. That outgrowth is the ureteric bud, which grows into the tissue that becomes the kidney. Its branches form the collecting ducts and its stem forms the pelvis and the ureter.

Question 7185. Which statement about the minor calyces is correct: each one surrounds a renal papilla, each one surrounds a renal column, they drain into the other kidney, or they lie within the cortex? The answer is that each one surrounds a renal papilla. The cup catches the urine that the collecting ducts deliver.

Question 7186. A described image of the kidney shows a central branching structure filled with contrast, with rounded cups at its outer ends. What is that whole structure? The answer is the collecting system within the sinus, the pelvis and the calyces. The cups at the ends are the minor calyces. The channels that join the cups are the major calyces and the pelvis.

Question 7187. Within which cavity of the kidney does the renal pelvis lie, and what else does that cavity hold? The answer is the renal sinus, which also holds the calyces, the renal vessels and a quantity of fat. The sinus is the cavity that opens at the hilum of the kidney.

Question 7188. A constructed illustration: in a child with a duplex system, the ureter from the upper part of the kidney is obstructed, while the ureter from the lower part lets urine flow backward. Which part shows dilated calyces? The answer is the upper part. Its obstructed ureter makes its calyces dilate. The lower part is the one that allows backward flow.

Question 7189. True or false: the renal fascia encloses the kidney, the perirenal fat and the suprarenal gland in one compartment. The answer is true. The fascia passes around the kidney and the fat and also encloses the suprarenal gland above. Because they share that envelope, the two organs move together. The fascia also separates the kidney from the pararenal fat.

Question 7190. Which of these pairs belongs together: the minor calyx with a renal papilla, the minor calyx with the renal cortex, the major calyx with a single papilla, or the pelvis with the perirenal fat? The answer is the minor calyx with a renal papilla. Its cup receives the apex of a pyramid.

Question 7191. A constructed illustration: a stone forms in the collecting system and grows until it fills the pelvis and the major calyces, taking the shape of the cavity. Which structures does it occupy? The answer is the renal pelvis, the major calyces and the minor calyces. A stone of that shape lies within the renal sinus.

Question 7192. Which structure lies between the apex of a renal pyramid and the renal pelvis? The answer is the minor calyx, with the major calyx beyond it. The cup receives the papilla, and its neck leads to the major calyx. It then funnels the urine onward into the ureter.

Question 7193. Which statement about the calyces is correct: the minor calyces are always 3 in number, the major calyces are usually 3 in number, the major calyx surrounds the papilla, or the calyces lie in the cortex? The answer is that the major calyces are usually 3 in number. The minor calyces number 7 to 13.

Question 7194. A described section through the renal sinus shows a funnel-shaped space that divides into three broad channels, each of which divides into several small cups. Name the funnel and the channels. The answer is the renal pelvis and the major calyces. The small cups are the minor calyces. Each small cup embraces the apex of a pyramid.

Question 7195. Name the narrowing at which the renal pelvis becomes the ureter, and state where it lies. The answer is the ureteropelvic junction, at the lower part of the hilum. The pelvis tapers into the ureter there, about level with the second lumbar vertebra. The junction is a common place for a stone to lodge.

Question 7196. When the ureteropelvic junction is blocked, which part of the collecting system dilates first: the minor calyces, the major calyces, the renal pelvis, or the ureter? The answer is the minor calyces. The cups around the papillae lie nearest the obstruction and change first. If the pressure continues, the pelvis and the major calyces dilate as well.

Question 7197. A constructed illustration: urine is forced back into the tissue at the margin of a minor calyx. Which structure normally seals that margin? The answer is the attachment of the minor calyx to the base of the papilla. Where it gives way, urine enters the medulla. The seal keeps the urine inside the cup.

Question 7198. Name two structures that lie within the renal sinus, spoken as a list. The answer is the renal pelvis and the calyces. The sinus also holds the renal vessels and a quantity of fat, and it opens at the hilum of the kidney. They lie behind the vessels. The fat around them fills the rest of the cavity.

Question 7199. Which of these explains why the number of minor calyces need not equal the number of renal pyramids: two papillae may fuse into one compound papilla, one pyramid may have two calyces, the calyces may drain into the cortex, or the pelvis may receive a papilla? The answer is that two papillae fuse into one compound papilla, which a single calyx receives. The major calyx unites several minor calyces.

Question 7200. Which of these structures opens into a minor calyx: the collecting duct, the loop of Henle, the proximal convoluted tubule, or the arcuate artery? The answer is the collecting duct, which ends at the papilla that the calyx cups. The other structures never open into the collecting system. The duct is the last part of the uriniferous tubule.

To summarize, the kidneys are paired retroperitoneal organs on the posterior abdominal wall, each wrapped in a fibrous capsule with perirenal fat and renal fascia outside it, and each divided into an outer cortex and an inner medulla. The cortex contains the renal corpuscles, the convoluted tubules and the medullary rays, and it sends columns of cortical tissue between the pyramids, while the medulla is built of 8 to 18 renal pyramids whose apices form the papillae, where the collecting ducts open. The arcuate vessels mark the boundary between the two zones, the interlobular arteries supply the cortex, and the vasa recta serve the medulla, which has the poorer blood supply. The collecting system begins at the papilla with a minor calyx, minor calyces unite into a major calyx, the major calyces form the renal pelvis, and the pelvis leaves the hilum as the ureter.
