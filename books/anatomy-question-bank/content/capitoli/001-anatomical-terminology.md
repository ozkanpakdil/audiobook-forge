# Chapter 1 — Anatomical Terminology

Every conversation about the body depends on a shared set of words for where things are, and this chapter builds that vocabulary from the ground up. It covers the anatomical position, the directional terms that describe one structure relative to another, the planes in which the body can be cut, the axes about which its parts move, and the named regions of its surface. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. The terms defined here belong to the body as a whole, and later chapters use them to describe particular bones, muscles, vessels, and nerves. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 1. In a body standing in the anatomical position, which bone of the leg lies on the lateral side? The answer is the fibula. The tibia lies medially and carries the weight of the body. The fibula lies on the outer side and bears almost none of it, serving mainly as a place for muscles to attach.

Question 2. In the anatomical position, which way do the palms face: forward, backward, downward, or toward each other? The answer is forward. With the palms forward the thumb points away from the midline. A hand with the palm turned backward is not in the anatomical position.

Question 3. A prepared specimen shows a body standing upright with both palms rotated to face the back. Which way does the radius lie in that specimen? The answer is that the radius crosses over the ulna. That rotation is pronation, and it carries the lower end of the radius across the ulna. In the anatomical position the two bones lie parallel instead.

Question 4. In a body standing in the anatomical position, which forearm bone lies medial to the other? The answer is the ulna. The ulna lies on the little finger side of the forearm and the radius on the thumb side. When the palm turns to face the back, the two bones change places.

Question 5. Which statement about the feet in the anatomical position is correct: the soles are flat and the feet are together, the right foot is in front, the toes point outward, or the heels are raised? The answer is that the soles are flat and the feet are together. Turning the toes outward is a natural stance but not the reference position.

Question 6. What is the name of the position in which a person lies face upward with the limbs extended? The answer is the supine position. The belly and the front of the body face the ceiling, and the back rests against the surface. A person lying face downward is described as prone.

Question 7. A constructed illustration: a patient is brought in lying face upward, and the team must describe a wound on the front of the right thigh. Which posture supplies the words for that description? The answer is the anatomical position. The wound is described as anterior and on the right side, whatever the position of the bed.

Question 8. True or false: in the anatomical position the thumbs point toward the midline of the body. The answer is false. The palms face forward, so each thumb points away from the midline and each little finger points toward it. When the palm faces the body instead, the thumb turns inward.

Question 9. Which of these words means toward the head: superior, inferior, anterior, or posterior? The answer is superior. Superior means nearer the head end of the body and inferior means nearer the feet. Anterior means toward the front and posterior means toward the back.

Question 10. A described radiograph shows one hip in profile, with the outer surface of the joint nearest the film. Which directional word names that surface? The answer is lateral. Lateral means farther from the midline, and the inner surface nearer the midline is the medial surface.

Question 11. In a head viewed from the side, does the tip of the nose lie anterior or posterior to the external ear? The answer is anterior. The nose lies in the midline of the face, while the ear lies further back on the side of the head. The word anterior places the nose toward the front.

Question 12. Which gland lies immediately anterior to the upper part of the trachea in the neck? The answer is the thyroid gland. It wraps around the front and the sides of the airway just below the larynx. The trachea therefore lies posterior to it.

Question 13. In the head, which of these terms means toward the forehead: caudal, rostral, dorsal, or ventral? The answer is rostral. It is used in the head and the neck, where the long axis of the body bends forward. Caudal and dorsal mean toward the tail and the back, and ventral means toward the belly.

Question 14. A described dissection shows a broad muscle rising from the lower spine, crossing the inferior angle of the scapula, and twisting into a flat tendon at the front of the arm. Which muscle is it? The answer is the latissimus dorsi. It pulls the arm downward and backward and forms the posterior fold of the armpit.

Question 15. A constructed illustration: a wound on the side of the neck lies lateral to the midline and superficial to the sternocleidomastoid muscle. Which large vein lies in that described zone? The answer is the external jugular vein. It runs down the surface of the neck over that muscle. The internal jugular vein lies deeper, so the muscle separates the two.

Question 16. Which statement about the words cranial and cephalic is correct: both point toward the head, both point toward the feet, both mean toward the back, or both mean toward the midline? The answer is that both point toward the head. Cranial is usually reserved for structures inside the skull, while superior stays the general term.

Question 17. Which of these pairs belongs together: superior with toward the feet, inferior with toward the head, anterior with toward the back, or lateral with away from the midline? The answer is lateral with away from the midline. A structure farther from the median plane is lateral, and one nearer it is medial.

Question 18. True or false: the word superior always means higher from the floor, whatever the body is doing. The answer is false. Superior means nearer the head end of the body, which is a fact about the body rather than the room. In a person lying face upward, the nose is superior to the umbilicus.

Question 19. Which organ lies immediately inferior to the diaphragm on the right side of the abdomen? The answer is the liver. The right dome of the diaphragm arches over the upper surface of the liver. On the left side the organs below the diaphragm are the stomach and the spleen.

Question 20. Which of these structures lies highest in the abdomen: the urinary bladder, the stomach, the rectum, or the sigmoid colon? The answer is the stomach. It lies in the upper part of the abdomen, just below the diaphragm. The bladder and the rectum lie in the pelvis, the lowest part of the trunk.

Question 21. A constructed illustration: pain that began around the navel later moves lower and to the right. Where has the pain moved, in the language of direction? The answer is inferior and lateral to the umbilical region. Such a shift is described with the words superior and inferior and with medial and lateral.

Question 22. Which two small glands sit immediately superior to the kidneys, one on each side, capping their upper poles? The answer is the adrenal glands. They lie in the fatty tissue around the kidney and are held there by the same layer of fascia. They move with the kidney when it descends.

Question 23. A described specimen shows the lower spine, with separate vertebrae above a fused triangular mass of bone. Which vertebra is the lowest separate one, and where does it lie? The answer is the fifth lumbar vertebra, immediately superior to the sacrum. Their junction is the lumbosacral joint, where the spine changes direction.

Question 24. Which of these is inferior in the normal standing body: the kidneys, the diaphragm, the lungs, or the bladder? The answer is the bladder. It lies in the pelvis, below the level of the other three. The kidneys and the diaphragm lie in the upper abdomen and the lungs fill the chest.

Question 25. Which structure sits immediately above the upper pole of the right kidney, and which organ rests against that same pole? The answer is the right adrenal gland, with the liver against the pole. The right kidney lies lower than the left because the liver occupies the space above it.

Question 26. Which statement holds for a person lying on the back: the nose is superior to the umbilicus, the sternum is inferior to it, the chin is inferior to the navel, or the knee is superior to the hip? The answer is that the nose is superior to the umbilicus. Superior and inferior follow the long axis of the body, not gravity.

Question 27. A described frontal radiograph of the chest shows the heart as a shadow in the middle, with the ribs crossing it and the spine faintly behind. Which surface of the heart lies closest to the film? The answer is the anterior surface. In that view the beam passes from the back to the front, so the film lies against the anterior chest.

Question 28. Which bony structures form the posterior wall of the thoracic cavity and lie behind the heart and the esophagus? The answer is the thoracic vertebrae. Twelve of them lie one above another in the midline of the back, each carrying a pair of ribs. Their bodies and the discs between them protect the spinal cord.

Question 29. Which of these structures lies directly anterior to the trachea in the upper chest: the esophagus, the arch of the aorta, the vertebral column, or the thyroid gland? The answer is the arch of the aorta. It crosses in front of the windpipe before turning backward. The esophagus lies behind the trachea.

Question 30. Immediately posterior to the body of the sternum lies which structure, and which gland occupies the same space in a child? The answer is the heart, with the thymus in front of it in the child. The heart sits behind the sternum and the costal cartilages. The thymus regresses with age.

Question 31. Which large vein lies immediately posterior to the neck of the pancreas, formed where the splenic vein joins the superior mesenteric vein? The answer is the portal vein. It is created behind the neck of the pancreas and runs upward to the liver. The pancreas therefore lies in front of it.

Question 32. A constructed illustration: a blow lands on the back over the lower ribs. Which organ lies in that described zone and is exposed to injury from behind? The answer is the kidneys, which lie against the posterior wall of the abdomen. They sit behind the peritoneum, partly protected by the eleventh and twelfth ribs.

Question 33. Which of these structures lies posterior to the kidney: the twelfth rib, the peritoneum, the pancreas, or the ascending colon? The answer is the twelfth rib. It crosses the upper part of the kidney from behind on both sides. The peritoneum and the colon lie in front of the kidney.

Question 34. True or false: in a standing person the word dorsal points to the same side of the trunk as the word posterior. The answer is true. In the embryo the dorsal surface is the back, and in the trunk of an adult the words keep that meaning. For the hand, dorsal names the back of the hand.

Question 35. Which of these statements about the word medial is correct: it means nearer the midline, nearer the side, nearer the front, or nearer the head? The answer is nearer the midline. The median plane runs down the middle of the body. A structure closer to it than another is medial to it.

Question 36. A constructed illustration: a wound on the medial side of the upper thigh bleeds heavily just below the groin. Which structure lies in that described position within the femoral triangle? The answer is the femoral vein. In the triangle the vein lies medial to the artery, which lies medial to the nerve.

Question 37. Which bone forms the medial wall of the orbit, a thin plate that separates the eye from the nasal cavity and the ethmoid air cells? The answer is the ethmoid bone. Its paper-thin orbital plate is easily broken by a blow to the eye. The maxilla and the lacrimal bone also contribute to that wall.

Question 38. At the wrist, which structure lies medial to the radial artery: the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis, the tendon of the palmaris longus, the ulnar nerve, or the median nerve? The answer is the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis. It lies just medial to the artery, where both can be felt at the pulse point.

Question 39. A described cross section of the forearm shows two bones side by side with a membrane between them. On which side does the bone with the broad lower end lie? The answer is the lateral side. That bone is the radius, whose lower end is broad, the reverse of the ulna. The interosseous membrane joins their shafts.

Question 40. In the hand held in the anatomical position, which digit lies medial to the others and which lies lateral? The answer is the little finger medially and the thumb laterally. With the palm facing forward, the thumb points away from the body and the little finger points toward it.

Question 41. Which of these structures lies immediately lateral to the sternum in the upper chest: the internal thoracic artery, the trachea, the esophagus, or the thoracic duct? The answer is the internal thoracic artery. It descends just lateral to the sternum behind the costal cartilages. The other three lie nearer the midline.

Question 42. A described dissection of the neck shows a long thin muscle running from the upper chest to the mastoid process. Which muscle is shown, and which large vein lies medial to it? The answer is the sternocleidomastoid muscle, with the internal jugular vein medial to it. The vein runs in the carotid sheath.

Question 43. A constructed illustration: a deep cut crosses the front of the wrist just distal to the skin crease, and the thumb cannot be opposed. Which nerve has most likely been divided? The answer is the median nerve. It lies superficially in the middle of the wrist and supplies the muscles that oppose the thumb.

Question 44. Which single bone forms the skeleton of the arm between the shoulder joint and the elbow, and is therefore the proximal bone of the upper limb? The answer is the humerus. Its head fits into the socket of the scapula above, and its lower end joins the radius and the ulna.

Question 45. Along the large intestine, which of these parts lies proximal to the others: the descending colon, the transverse colon, the sigmoid colon, or the rectum? The answer is the transverse colon. The colon runs up as the ascending, across as the transverse, and down as the descending and sigmoid parts. Proximal means nearer the beginning of that path.

Question 46. Which part of the small intestine lies immediately distal to the duodenum, and how is that part recognized at operation? The answer is the jejunum. It begins at the duodenojejunal flexure, which is fixed high in the abdomen. The jejunum is thicker walled and more vascular than the ileum that follows it.

Question 47. Which bone forms the proximal part of the lower limb skeleton and fits into the socket of the pelvis? The answer is the femur. Its head sits in the acetabulum, and its shaft runs down to the knee. The bones distal to it are the tibia and the fibula.

Question 48. Which of these pairs belongs together: the elbow with distal, the shoulder with distal, the wrist with proximal, or the knee with proximal? The answer is the elbow with distal, because the elbow is farther from the trunk than the shoulder. The knee is distal to the hip and the wrist is distal to the elbow.

Question 49. For an internal organ, which statement is correct: proximal and distal describe distance from the mouth along the gut, they describe distance from the skin, or they mean superior and inferior? The answer is that they describe distance from the mouth along a tube such as the gut. In the limbs they measure distance from the trunk.

Question 50. Name two structures that lie distal to the knee in the lower limb, spoken as a list. The answer is the tibia and the fibula. Both begin at the knee and run down to the ankle, where they form the bony frame of the leg. The bones of the foot lie distal to them in turn.

Question 51. True or false: a structure described as superficial lies nearer the surface of the body than one described as deep. The answer is true. In the forearm the skin is superficial and the bone is deep. In the brain the gray matter is superficial to the white matter.

Question 52. Which bone lies immediately deep to the skin along the front of the shin, with almost no muscle covering it? The answer is the tibia. Its sharp anterior border is the ridge felt down the front of the leg. The muscles lie on its lateral and posterior surfaces.

Question 53. Which structure lies immediately deep to the inguinal ligament at the midpoint between the front of the hip bone and the pubic symphysis? The answer is the femoral artery. It enters the thigh there, where its pulse can be felt, and the femoral vein lies just medial to it.

Question 54. Which of these layers lies most superficial on the front of the abdomen: the skin, the deep fascia, the muscle, or the peritoneum? The answer is the skin. Deep to it lie the fatty layer, the membranous layer, and the muscles of the abdominal wall. The peritoneum is the deepest of the four.

Question 55. A described dissection shows the flat muscles of the abdominal wall separated layer by layer. Which layer lies deepest of the three, and what does it become near the midline? The answer is the transversus abdominis, whose fibers run across the abdomen, and it becomes an aponeurosis that joins the rectus sheath.

Question 56. A constructed illustration: a small puncture below the middle of the clavicle lets air collect under the skin of the chest. Which layer has the air entered? The answer is the superficial fascia, the fatty layer just beneath the skin. Air tracks along that loose layer because it offers little resistance.

Question 57. Which vein is the most superficial at the front of the elbow: the basilic, the brachial, the cephalic, or the median cubital? The answer is the median cubital vein, which crosses the front of the elbow just under the skin. The brachial vein lies deep beside the artery.

Question 58. A described dissection identifies a vein crossing the surface of the sternocleidomastoid muscle and a plexus of nerves emerging from beneath it. Which structures are described? The answer is the external jugular vein and the cervical plexus. The plexus emerges from beneath the posterior border of the muscle.

Question 59. Which of these pairs belongs together: ipsilateral with the opposite side, contralateral with the same side, bilateral with both sides, or unilateral with neither side? The answer is bilateral with both sides. Ipsilateral means on the same side of the body and contralateral means on the opposite side.

Question 60. What word describes two structures that lie on the same side of the body, such as the right eye and the right ear? The answer is ipsilateral. The opposite word, contralateral, describes a pair such as the left eye and the right ear.

Question 61. A lesion of one cranial nerve weakens the muscles of the face on the same side as the lesion. Which word describes the relationship between the nerve lesion and the weak muscles? The answer is ipsilateral. The nerve and the muscles it supplies lie on the same side of the face.

Question 62. A described photograph shows a person with a scar on the left cheek and a drooping left eyelid, while the right side of the face moves normally. Which word describes the relation between the scar and the weakness? The answer is ipsilateral, because both lie on the left side.

Question 63. Fibers of the corticospinal tract that begin in the left cerebral hemisphere end where in the spinal cord, and what word describes that relation? The answer is the right side, which is the contralateral side. Most fibers cross in the medulla oblongata, and a small number descend ipsilaterally.

Question 64. Light from the left half of the visual field of each eye is carried to which side of the brain: the left occipital lobe, the right occipital lobe, or neither side? The answer is the right occipital lobe. Fibers from that half of each retina end in the opposite hemisphere.

Question 65. A described illustration: a person cannot lift the right corner of the mouth. Which word describes the side of the weakness relative to the brain lesion that commonly causes it? The answer is contralateral. A lesion of the left motor cortex weakens the right side, because the fibers cross.

Question 66. A constructed illustration: glass enters the upper neck behind the angle of the jaw, and the bleeding is deep. Which vessel lies in that described position, and which word names its course through the skull? The answer is the internal carotid artery, which enters the skull through the carotid canal.

Question 67. A described section through the skull names the outer and inner surfaces of the vault as two tables. Which table lies external and which lies internal? The answer is that the outer table is external and the inner table is internal. A blow may break the thin inner table without breaking the outer one.

Question 68. Which fibrous coat forms the external covering of the kidney and can be peeled away from it? The answer is the renal capsule. It is a smooth fibrous sheath wrapped directly around the organ. Around it lie a layer of fat and the renal fascia.

Question 69. Which word describes a structure lying nearer the center of an organ or a cavity, as opposed to one lying nearer its outer boundary: internal, external, superficial, or distal? The answer is internal. Its opposite is external, which describes the outer boundary. Superficial answers a different question, that of depth from the surface.

Question 70. A described coronal section through the head shows a thick plate of bone on each side of the cranial cavity, separated by a layer of spongy bone. Which plate lies internal? The answer is the inner table, with the outer table on the outside. The spongy layer between them is the diploe.

Question 71. True or false: the external oblique muscle lies superficial to the internal oblique muscle in the abdominal wall. The answer is true. The external oblique is the outermost of the three flat muscles, and the internal oblique lies immediately deep to it. Their fibers cross at a right angle.

Question 72. Which statement about the iliac arteries is correct: the external iliac artery becomes the femoral artery, the external iliac artery supplies the pelvic organs, the internal iliac artery becomes the femoral artery, or both arise from the aorta? The answer is that the external iliac artery becomes the femoral artery. It leaves the pelvis under the inguinal ligament.

Question 73. Which pair belongs together: the median plane with upper and lower parts, the coronal plane with right and left parts, the transverse plane with front and back parts, or the sagittal plane with right and left parts? The answer is the sagittal plane with right and left parts. The median plane is the sagittal plane through the middle.

Question 74. What is the name of the plane that passes through the midline of the body and divides it into equal right and left halves? The answer is the median plane. Every sagittal plane runs from front to back, but only the median one divides the body into two equal halves.

Question 75. Which plane divides the body into an upper part and a lower part: the sagittal plane, the coronal plane, the transverse plane, or the median plane? The answer is the transverse plane. It runs horizontally across the body at right angles to the long axis.

Question 76. A described section of the trunk shows both kidneys at one level, with the aorta and the inferior vena cava in front of the vertebral column. In which plane was this section cut? The answer is a transverse plane. The section lies across the body, so it shows structures from both sides.

Question 77. What name is given to a plane that runs parallel to the median plane but lies to one side of it, cutting the body into unequal right and left parts? The answer is a parasagittal plane. It runs from front to back but does not pass through the middle of the body.

Question 78. The sternum, the vertebral column, and the mediastinum all lie in which plane, and what does a section in that plane divide? The answer is the median plane, which divides the body into right and left halves. All three structures lie in the midline.

Question 79. Which statement about an oblique plane is correct: it cuts the body at an angle to all three reference planes, it parallels the median plane, it divides the body into equal halves, or it is another name for a transverse plane? The answer is that it cuts all three reference planes at an angle. The pelvic inlet is a familiar example.

Question 80. A described sagittal section through the head shows the corpus callosum as a curved band of white matter above the thalamus. Which plane was used, and does the section pass through both halves of the brain? The answer is a sagittal plane lying to one side of the midline, so it shows only one hemisphere.

Question 81. A described fracture line runs diagonally across the shaft of a long bone, from the upper part of one side to the lower part of the other. In which plane does the break lie? The answer is an oblique plane. The fragments can slide past one another along the slope of the break.

Question 82. The superior thoracic aperture is bounded by the first thoracic vertebra, the first ribs, and the top of the sternum. In which kind of plane does it lie, and how is that plane directed? The answer is an oblique plane, tilted downward and forward. The first ribs slope down from the spine to the sternum.

Question 83. Which axis passes through the body from front to back, at right angles to the coronal plane: the longitudinal axis, the transverse axis, the anteroposterior axis, or the vertical axis? The answer is the anteroposterior axis. The transverse axis runs from side to side, and the longitudinal axis runs from head to foot.

Question 84. A constructed illustration: a fall forces the ankle into a twist, and the foot turns outward. About which axis does the movement occur? The answer is a longitudinal axis. Turning the toes outward is rotation of the leg and the foot around a vertical axis.

Question 85. What axis runs from side to side through a joint, and about what movements does rotation around that axis occur? The answer is the transverse axis, which carries the movements of flexion and extension. A hinge joint such as the elbow moves about a single transverse axis.

Question 86. A described series of drawings shows a forearm bending at the elbow and then straightening again, with the hand moving forward and back in a plane that splits the body into right and left halves. About which axis does the movement take place, and in which plane? The answer is a transverse axis, in the sagittal plane.

Question 87. Which movement takes place about a longitudinal axis: flexion of the elbow, rotation of the head, abduction of the shoulder, or extension of the knee? The answer is rotation of the head. Turning the face to one side swings the head around a vertical axis through the neck.

Question 88. What is the fixed relation between an axis of movement and the plane in which the movement occurs? The answer is that the axis is at right angles to the plane. A joint that flexes in the sagittal plane therefore rotates about a transverse axis.

Question 89. Which statement about the long axis of a bone is correct: it runs along the shaft from end to end, it lies parallel to the midline, it crosses the joint at right angles, or it equals the axis of movement? The answer is that it runs along the shaft from end to end. The long axis of the femur runs from hip to knee.

Question 90. Name the plane and the axis for the movement of turning the palm to face the floor while the elbow stays at the side. The answer is the transverse plane and a longitudinal axis. The forearm rotates around its own long axis. The same words describe turning the head to look over one shoulder.

Question 91. Which body region lies between the neck above and the diaphragm below, and is bounded by the ribs and the sternum? The answer is the thorax, or chest. It contains the heart, the lungs, and the great vessels, and the diaphragm forms its floor. The region below it is the abdomen.

Question 92. Which of these regions lies in the middle of the abdomen around the navel: the epigastric region, the umbilical region, the hypogastric region, or the right iliac region? The answer is the umbilical region. It is the central of the nine regions into which the abdomen is divided.

Question 93. A described photograph marks the umbilicus, a vertical line down the middle, and two horizontal lines, one at the lowest ribs and one at the hip bones. Which regions do those lines mark? The answer is the nine regions of the abdomen, three across by three down. The upper middle region is the epigastric.

Question 94. Which pair belongs together: the axilla with the armpit, the popliteal fossa with the front of the elbow, the cubital fossa with the back of the knee, or the groin with the side of the chest? The answer is the axilla with the armpit. The popliteal fossa lies behind the knee.

Question 95. Which abdominal region lies between the umbilicus above and the pubic symphysis below, in the midline? The answer is the hypogastric region, also called the pubic region. It lies between the two iliac regions. In it lie the bladder when full and, in the female, the uterus.

Question 96. Which of these regions of the upper limb lies between the shoulder and the elbow: the forearm, the arm, the cubital fossa, or the axilla? The answer is the arm. In anatomical language the arm is the segment between the shoulder and the elbow, while the forearm lies between the elbow and the wrist.

Question 97. A constructed illustration: pain in the upper middle of the abdomen radiates to the right shoulder. Which region is described, and which structure beneath it can irritate the diaphragm? The answer is the epigastric region, with the gallbladder beneath it. Irritation of the diaphragm can refer pain to the shoulder.

Question 98. Which body region lies between the elbow above and the wrist below? The answer is the forearm. Its two bones are the radius and the ulna, and the muscles that move the wrist and fingers lie in it. The region above the elbow is the arm.

Question 99. A described photograph of the back shows a midline furrow, a rounded prominence at the base of the neck, and two flat triangular bones. Which surface regions are shown, and which vertebra forms the prominence? The answer is the back, with the spine of the seventh cervical vertebra forming it.

Question 100. The perineum is described as a diamond-shaped region divided into two triangles by a line between the two ischial tuberosities. Which triangle contains the anus and which the external urinary opening? The answer is that the anal triangle lies behind the line and the urogenital triangle in front. The perineum lies below the pelvic diaphragm.

To summarize, the anatomical position is the standing reference posture with the palms facing forward and the feet together and flat, and every directional term is defined from it. Superior and inferior follow the long axis of the body rather than gravity, while anterior, posterior, medial, lateral, proximal, distal, superficial, deep, internal, external, ipsilateral and contralateral each name a position relative to another structure. The three reference planes are the sagittal plane, which divides the body into right and left parts, the coronal plane, which divides it into front and back parts, and the transverse plane, which divides it into upper and lower parts, and every movement occurs about an axis at right angles to the plane in which it is seen. The named regions of the body, from the thorax and the abdomen to the axilla, the cubital fossa, the popliteal fossa and the perineum, give a shared vocabulary for the surface. Saying a structure's position aloud, relative to a named neighbor, is what fixes these terms in memory, and later chapters rely on them.
