# Chapter 74 — Urinary Bladder

This chapter follows the urine from the kidney to the bladder. It begins with the ureter, the narrow tube that carries urine from the renal pelvis to the bladder, and then turns to the urinary bladder itself: its shape when empty and when full, the surfaces and relations of each part, the trigone, the detrusor, the two sphincters, the nerve supply, the blood supply and the way the organ develops. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately in the words The answer is, and then explains why that answer is right. Nothing has to be held in mind, and no answer key has to be waited for. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 7301. Which muscular tube carries urine from the renal pelvis to the urinary bladder? The answer is the ureter. Each kidney sends one ureter down to the bladder, where it opens at the upper corner of the trigone. The tube lies behind the peritoneum along its whole abdominal course.

Question 7302. About how long is the adult ureter: 5 to 8 centimeters, 12 to 15 centimeters, 25 to 30 centimeters, or 40 to 45 centimeters? The answer is 25 to 30 centimeters. It begins at the renal pelvis in the upper abdomen and ends at the bladder in the pelvis. The right ureter is a little shorter than the left because the right kidney sits lower.

Question 7303. In the abdomen, the ureter descends immediately anterior to which muscle, which forms the bed on which it lies? The answer is the psoas major. The tube runs down the front of that muscle with the peritoneum in front of it. Because the muscle slopes forward as it descends, the ureter also passes forward on its way to the pelvis.

Question 7304. Which is the narrowest point of the ureter, where a stone may stick and cause colic? The answer is the ureterovesical junction, where the tube passes through the wall of the bladder. The other two narrow points are the ureteropelvic junction and the crossing of the pelvic brim. A stone may lodge at any of the three.

Question 7305. A described radiograph taken after contrast shows two thin curved tubes running from the kidneys to the pelvis. Which structures are shown, and what is the examination called? The answer is the ureters, shown on an excretory urogram. The contrast outlines the collecting system of each kidney and the tube below it. The tubes taper as they approach the bladder.

Question 7306. A constructed illustration: a person has waves of severe pain in the loin that later move toward the groin. Where is the stone most likely lodged at the start? The answer is the ureteropelvic junction. It is the first of the three narrow points of the ureter. As the stone travels down, the pain travels with it.

Question 7307. At the pelvic brim, the ureter crosses in front of which large artery? The answer is the common iliac artery, where it divides. The tube passes over the bifurcation and then continues downward in front of the external iliac artery. This crossing is one of the three narrow points of the ureter.

Question 7308. In the female pelvis, which artery crosses above and in front of the ureter on its way to the uterus? The answer is the uterine artery. The ureter passes below the artery and then runs forward to the bladder, about 2 centimeters lateral to the cervix. Surgeons describe the arrangement as water running under a bridge.

Question 7309. In the male pelvis, which structure crosses from lateral to medial above the ureter before the ureter reaches the bladder: the ductus deferens, the obturator nerve, the middle rectal artery, or the internal pudendal artery? The answer is the ductus deferens. It crosses above and medial to the ureter as it runs to the prostate. The ureter continues below it into the bladder.

Question 7310. True or false: the ureter lies behind the peritoneum throughout its abdominal course. The answer is true. It descends on the psoas major with peritoneum covering only its front. For that reason a surgeon reaches the tube from the front by pushing the peritoneum aside.

Question 7311. Name three arteries that supply the ureter, spoken as a list. The answer is the renal artery, the gonadal artery and the superior vesical artery. The abdominal aorta and the iliac arteries also send small branches to it. The supply is segmental, so each branch feeds only part of the tube.

Question 7312. From which embryonic structure does the ureter develop, and from what does that structure itself arise? The answer is the ureteric bud, which grows out of the mesonephric duct. The bud pushes into the metanephric tissue and induces the kidney to form. Its upper end widens into the renal pelvis and its stalk becomes the ureter.

Question 7313. Which part of the bladder develops from the mesonephric duct, so that its lining differs from the rest: the apex, the trigone, the superior surface, or the urachus? The answer is the trigone. The remaining lining of the bladder comes from the endoderm of the urogenital sinus. The trigone therefore has a mesodermal origin, which is one reason its mucosa is smooth and fixed.

Question 7314. On the right side, which part of the bowel crosses in front of the ureter in the upper abdomen? The answer is the descending part of the duodenum. The right ureter also passes behind the root of the mesentery and is crossed by the right colic vessels. On the left side the sigmoid colon and its mesentery cross the ureter lower down.

Question 7315. A constructed illustration: a person with a stone in the ureter feels pain that begins in the loin and travels to the groin. Why does it travel in that direction? The answer is that the pain follows the nerve supply of the ureter. Its sensory fibers reach the spinal cord between the eleventh thoracic and the second lumbar segments.

Question 7316. A described section of the ureter wall shows a stretchy lining of several cell layers, a middle coat of smooth muscle and an outer fibrous coat. Which lining is described, and how is the muscle arranged? The answer is transitional epithelium over an inner longitudinal and an outer circular layer. In the lower third a third, outermost longitudinal layer appears.

Question 7317. Why does the lower end of the ureter pass obliquely through the wall of the bladder instead of opening straight into it? The answer is that the oblique tunnel is compressed when the bladder contracts. The tube runs about 2 centimeters within the muscle before it opens. Rising pressure in the bladder therefore closes the tunnel and urine cannot pass back up.

Question 7318. In an empty bladder the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice mark the corners of a triangle. How is that triangle placed, and which corner is lowest? The answer is that it lies on the base of the bladder, with the internal urethral orifice at the lowest corner. The two ureteric orifices lie above and behind the internal opening.

Question 7319. Which of these pairs belongs together: the ureteropelvic junction with the renal pelvis, the ureterovesical junction with the pelvic brim, or the intramural part with the psoas major? The answer is the ureteropelvic junction with the renal pelvis. The intramural part lies within the wall of the bladder and the pelvic part runs from the brim to the bladder.

Question 7320. What name is given to the short segment of the ureter that lies inside the wall of the bladder, and about how long is it? The answer is the intramural part, about 2 centimeters long. It runs obliquely through the muscle of the bladder wall. Its slit-like opening at the inner end is the ureteric orifice.

Question 7321. A constructed illustration: during a pelvic operation in a woman the ureter is injured about 2 centimeters from the cervix. Which named vessel marks that dangerous point? The answer is the uterine artery where it crosses the ureter. The ureter passes below the artery as it runs to the uterus.

Question 7322. Which statement about the blood supply of the ureter is correct: one artery supplies the whole tube, the supply is segmental, only the renal artery supplies it, or the supply comes only from below? The answer is that the supply is segmental. Branches arrive from the renal artery above and from the iliac and vesical arteries below.

Question 7323. A described computed tomography scan shows a small rounded dot on the psoas major in front of a lumbar vertebra. Which structure is shown, and what crosses in front of it? The answer is the ureter, with the gonadal vessels in front. The tube appears as a dot because the section is transverse.

Question 7324. In both sexes, which vessels cross the front of the ureter about halfway down its course on the psoas major? The answer is the gonadal vessels, the testicular vessels in the male and the ovarian vessels in the female. They cross the ureter obliquely as they run down toward the pelvis. Below that crossing the ureter continues to the pelvic brim.

Question 7325. A child has a kidney drained by two collecting systems. Which statement explains the double ureter: the ureteric bud divided early, the mesonephric duct failed to form, or the urogenital sinus grew too far? The answer is that the ureteric bud divided early. One bud drains the upper part of the kidney and the other the lower part.

Question 7326. When the urinary bladder is empty, in which part of the body does it lie? The answer is the pelvis, behind the pubic symphysis. It rests on the pelvic floor and lies below the peritoneum. In that state it cannot be felt through the abdominal wall.

Question 7327. About how much urine does a normal adult bladder hold when it is comfortably full: 100 to 150 milliliters, 300 to 500 milliliters, 1,000 to 1,200 milliliters, or 1,500 to 2,000 milliliters? The answer is 300 to 500 milliliters. The first desire to void comes much earlier, at about 150 to 250 milliliters. These figures describe ordinary anatomy.

Question 7328. In the male, which structures lie between the base of the bladder and the rectum? The answer is the seminal vesicles and the ampullae of the ductus deferens. They lie against the lower part of the base. Above them the peritoneum dips down between the bladder and the rectum to form the rectovesical pouch.

Question 7329. A described ultrasound scan shows a rounded black space behind the pubic symphysis with bright echoes below it. What is the black space, and why does it look black? The answer is a full bladder, which looks black because urine does not reflect sound. The bright echoes come from the pelvic floor. A full bladder is a window for the organs behind it.

Question 7330. A constructed illustration: a person cannot pass urine and has a tender swelling rising above the pubic symphysis. Which organ is distended, and why does it rise? The answer is the urinary bladder, which rises into the abdomen as it fills. The peritoneum is pushed upward in front of it. The swelling is dull to percussion because it holds fluid.

Question 7331. The apex of the bladder is joined to the umbilicus by which fibrous remnant? The answer is the median umbilical ligament. It is the remains of the urachus, the channel that once joined the bladder to the allantois. It runs upward in the midline behind the front wall of the abdomen.

Question 7332. Which of these describes the trigone of the bladder: a raised fold at the apex, a smooth triangle between the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice, the fold of peritoneum over the fundus, or the ring of muscle at the bladder neck? The answer is the smooth triangle between the openings. Its mucosa is bound to the muscle and stays smooth.

Question 7333. In the female, which structure lies immediately behind the base of the bladder? The answer is the vagina, with the cervix above it. The peritoneum passes from the uterus onto the bladder and forms the vesicouterine pouch between the two organs. The bladder lies in front of the vagina and below the uterus.

Question 7334. True or false: when it is empty, the bladder lies entirely within the pelvis. The answer is true. The empty bladder sits behind the pubic symphysis and below the pelvic peritoneum. It rises into the abdomen only when it fills, and the peritoneum is carried up with it.

Question 7335. Which of these pairs belongs together: the apex with the pubic symphysis, the base with the abdominal wall, the superior surface with the pelvic floor, or the inferolateral surface with the umbilicus? The answer is the apex with the pubic symphysis. The apex points forward and upward toward that bone. The inferolateral surfaces rest on the pelvic floor.

Question 7336. Which muscle forms the main wall of the bladder and expels urine from it? The answer is the detrusor muscle. Its fibers are smooth and run in interlacing bundles in several directions. When it contracts the bladder empties and the internal urethral orifice opens.

Question 7337. In the male, which structure prevents semen from passing backward into the bladder during ejaculation: the external urethral sphincter, the internal urethral sphincter, the bulbospongiosus muscle, or the levator ani? The answer is the internal urethral sphincter, a thickening of smooth muscle at the bladder neck. It closes while the bladder wall relaxes.

Question 7338. A constructed illustration: a person leaks a little urine when coughing. Which muscle group has most likely become weak, and where does it lie? The answer is the pelvic floor, especially the levator ani. Those muscles support the bladder and the urethra from below. The external urethral sphincter is part of the same supporting mechanism.

Question 7339. A described midline magnetic resonance image of a male pelvis shows the bladder in front, the rectum behind and a rounded gland below the bladder neck. Which section is this, and what is the gland? The answer is a sagittal section, and the gland is the prostate. The base of the bladder rests on the prostate and the urethra leaves through it.

Question 7340. What is the name of the peritoneal pouch between the bladder and the rectum in the male, and why does it matter? The answer is the rectovesical pouch. When a person lies on the back it is the lowest part of the peritoneal cavity. Fluid that escapes into the abdomen therefore collects there and can be felt through the front wall of the rectum.

Question 7341. Which artery is the main supply of the upper part of the bladder? The answer is the superior vesical artery. It arises from the umbilical artery, a branch of the internal iliac artery. The inferior vesical artery supplies the lower part and the base in the male.

Question 7342. Where does blood from the bladder drain: into the portal vein, into the internal iliac veins through the vesical plexus, or into the femoral veins? The answer is into the internal iliac veins through the vesical plexus. The plexus lies in the loose tissue beside the bladder and the prostate. It connects with the prostatic and rectal veins.

Question 7343. Name the three coats of the wall of the bladder, spoken as a list from within outward. The answer is the mucosa, the muscular coat and the adventitia. The mucosa carries transitional epithelium and the muscular coat is the detrusor. The adventitia is the loose fibrous coat that binds the bladder to the structures around it.

Question 7344. A constructed illustration: a person has painless blood in the urine and a growth is found on the inner surface of the bladder. From which tissue does such a growth most often arise? The answer is the transitional epithelium of the mucosa. Growths of this lining are the most common tumors of the urinary tract. The treatment belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7345. A described transverse section is taken through the pelvis at the level of the hip joints. In what order do the pelvic organs lie from front to back in the male? The answer is the bladder, then the seminal vesicles, then the rectum. The bladder lies behind the pubic bones in the midline. The rectum lies against the sacrum and the coccyx behind.

Question 7346. Which nerves carry the parasympathetic fibers that empty the bladder: the hypogastric nerves, the pelvic splanchnic nerves, the pudendal nerve, or the obturator nerve? The answer is the pelvic splanchnic nerves, which come from the second to the fourth sacral segments. They make the detrusor contract and relax the internal urethral sphincter. The hypogastric nerves do the opposite.

Question 7347. Which nerve supplies the external urethral sphincter, the voluntary muscle that holds urine back? The answer is the pudendal nerve. Its branches run to the muscles of the deep perineal pouch, where the sphincter lies. Because that muscle is skeletal, it can be tightened at will.

Question 7348. True or false: the internal urethral sphincter is under voluntary control. The answer is false. It is made of smooth muscle and is controlled by the autonomic nervous system. The voluntary muscle at the outlet is the external urethral sphincter, which lies further down in the deep perineal pouch.

Question 7349. Which of these pairs belongs together: the pelvic splanchnic nerves with contraction of the detrusor, the hypogastric nerves with contraction of the detrusor, the pudendal nerve with relaxation of the detrusor, or the sympathetic fibers with opening of the internal sphincter? The answer is the pelvic splanchnic nerves with contraction of the detrusor. The hypogastric nerves relax the detrusor and close the internal sphincter.

Question 7350. A constructed illustration: a person with an injury of the spinal cord above the sacral segments has no voluntary control of the bladder, yet it still empties reflexly. Which part of the reflex arc remains intact? The answer is the sacral spinal cord with the pelvic splanchnic nerves. Only the path from the brain to that center is interrupted.

Question 7351. A described scan after contrast shows two thin tubes entering the back of a rounded organ in the pelvis at its upper corners. Which organ is shown, and where do the tubes open? The answer is the bladder, with the ureters opening at the upper corners of the trigone. Those corners are the two ureteric orifices.

Question 7352. Which two openings mark the upper corners of the trigone of the bladder, and which opening marks its lowest corner? The answer is the two ureteric orifices above and the internal urethral orifice below. The triangle between the three is smooth and keeps the same size whatever the bladder is doing. Each ureteric orifice is a slit rather than a round hole.

Question 7353. From which embryonic tube does the median umbilical ligament, the remnant that runs from the bladder to the umbilicus, derive? The answer is the urachus, the lower end of the allantois. In the fetus it drains the bladder into the umbilical cord. After birth it closes and becomes a solid fibrous cord.

Question 7354. Why does the trigone of the bladder have a different origin from the rest of the organ: its lining comes from the mesonephric ducts, from the allantois, from the rectum, or from the peritoneum? The answer is that its lining comes from the mesonephric ducts. The lower ends of the ducts are drawn into the growing bladder and form the trigone.

Question 7355. A constructed illustration: an older man cannot pass urine and the bladder is distended. Which gland has enlarged: the prostate, the seminal vesicle, the bulbourethral gland, or the levator ani? The answer is the prostate, which surrounds the first part of the urethra below the bladder. As it enlarges it narrows that channel. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 7356. Name two structures that help to hold the bladder in position, spoken as a list. The answer is the pubovesical ligaments and the pelvic floor formed by the levator ani. The ligaments run from the pubic bones to the neck of the bladder. The pelvic floor carries the organ from below and resists its descent.

Question 7357. At about what volume of urine does a person first feel the desire to empty the bladder: 50 milliliters, 150 to 250 milliliters, 400 to 500 milliliters, or 900 milliliters? The answer is 150 to 250 milliliters. At that point the bladder holds far less than its full capacity. The sensation comes from stretch receptors in the wall of the bladder.

Question 7358. In the male bladder, what is the name of the small elevation seen just behind the internal urethral orifice, and what produces it? The answer is the uvula vesicae, produced by the median lobe of the prostate. It lies below the trigone at the bladder neck. It can lift the lower edge of the trigone and interfere with complete emptying.

Question 7359. A described view through a cystoscope shows a smooth pale triangle with an opening at each corner. Which region is viewed, and what are the openings? The answer is the trigone, with the two ureteric orifices above and the internal urethral orifice below. Its mucosa is smooth because it is fixed to the muscle.

Question 7360. Which muscles form the floor and the lateral wall on which the bladder rests? The answer is the levator ani and the obturator internus. The levator ani forms the pelvic floor under the bladder. The obturator internus lies against the lateral wall of the pelvis, separated from the organ by fascia and veins.

Question 7361. A constructed illustration: a blow to the lower abdomen ruptures a full bladder and urine appears in the peritoneal cavity. Where was the tear, and why did urine reach that space? The answer is the superior surface, which is covered by peritoneum. A full bladder rises into the abdomen and that part of its wall is stretched thin. The urine therefore escapes into the peritoneal cavity.

Question 7362. To which groups of lymph nodes does lymph from the bladder drain: the superficial inguinal nodes, the internal and external iliac nodes, the lateral aortic nodes only, or the deep inguinal nodes? The answer is the internal and external iliac nodes. Some vessels also reach the lateral aortic nodes. Lymph from the upper part of the bladder may follow the ureter upward to those nodes.

Question 7363. Which of these statements is correct: the detrusor expels urine, the trigone stores urine, the urachus forms the pelvic floor, or the uvula vesicae lies at the apex? The answer is that the detrusor expels urine. The trigone is a fixed smooth area and not a store. The urachus is the fibrous remnant that runs from the bladder to the umbilicus.

Question 7364. True or false: the bladder receives no sympathetic nerve fibers. The answer is false. Sympathetic fibers reach it through the hypogastric plexus from the upper lumbar segments. They relax the detrusor and close the internal urethral sphincter. The parasympathetic fibers from the sacral segments do the opposite.

Question 7365. In the female, the peritoneum runs from the uterus onto the bladder and forms which pouch between the two organs? The answer is the vesicouterine pouch. It is a shallow pouch that lies above the bladder and in front of the uterus. The corresponding pouch in the male is the deeper rectovesical pouch behind the bladder.

Question 7366. Which statement about the position of the bladder is correct: it never changes position, it lies in the abdomen when empty, or it rises into the abdomen as it fills? The answer is that it rises into the abdomen as it fills. The peritoneum is pushed upward ahead of it. When full its upper part can be reached above the pubic symphysis.

Question 7367. A constructed illustration: a person has burning on passing urine, goes often and has tenderness above the pubic symphysis. Which organ is inflamed, and which of its surfaces lies against the front of the abdomen when it is full? The answer is the bladder, and its superior surface. Inflammation makes the wall irritable, so even a little urine provokes the desire to void.

Question 7368. A described transverse ultrasound scan shows a rounded black area in the midline of a full bladder, with two thin tubes entering at the back corners. What shape is the bladder here, and why is this view useful? The answer is an ovoid shape, and the full bladder acts as a window for scanning the organs behind it. The tubes are the ureters approaching the trigone.

Question 7369. What lies immediately in front of the empty bladder, and what occupies the space between? The answer is the pubic symphysis, with the retropubic space between the two. That space holds loose fat and the vesical venous plexus. It is the plane through which the bladder is reached from the front, above the pubic bone.

Question 7370. What is the name of the space between the pubic symphysis and the bladder, and what does it contain? The answer is the retropubic space. It contains loose fatty tissue and the veins of the vesical plexus. Because the tissue there is loose, the space can hold a large collection of blood after a fracture of the pelvis.

Question 7371. When the bladder is full, why can a needle be passed into it just above the pubic symphysis: the peritoneum is pushed up out of the way, the pelvis has no peritoneum, or the rectum lies in front? The answer is that the peritoneum is pushed up out of the way. In the empty bladder that fold lies close above the symphysis.

Question 7372. A constructed illustration: after a long labor a woman cannot hold her urine, and a passage is found between the bladder and the vagina. Which two walls were pressed together? The answer is the base of the bladder and the front wall of the vagina. They lay between the pubis in front and the head of the baby behind.

Question 7373. Which of these pairs belongs together: the trigone with a smooth fixed mucosa, the detrusor with a transitional lining, or the urachus with the bladder neck? The answer is the trigone with a smooth fixed mucosa. The detrusor is muscle and not a lining. The urachus is the fibrous remnant that runs from the bladder to the umbilicus.

Question 7374. Which surface of the bladder is covered by peritoneum? The answer is the superior surface. The peritoneum passes over it and then continues onto the anterior abdominal wall or, in the female, onto the uterus. The base and the inferolateral surfaces lie below the peritoneum and are covered only by fascia.

Question 7375. Why is the mucosa of the trigone always smooth while the rest is folded when the bladder is empty: it has no submucosa, it has no epithelium, it is covered by peritoneum, or it has no blood supply? The answer is that it has no submucosa and is fixed to the muscle. The rest of the mucosa has a loose submucosa and gathers into folds.

Question 7376. A described magnetic resonance image of a male pelvis shows the base of the bladder resting on a rounded gland, with a tube passing through the gland from the bladder. Which gland and which tube are shown? The answer is the prostate and the urethra. The ejaculatory ducts pass through the gland behind that tube.

Question 7377. In the male, which two ducts pass through the prostate and open into the urethra just below the neck of the bladder? The answer is the two ejaculatory ducts. Each is formed by the union of the ductus deferens with the duct of the seminal vesicle. They open on a small elevation in the urethra called the seminal colliculus.

Question 7378. True or false: the bladder lies below the peritoneum and outside the peritoneal cavity. The answer is true. Only its superior surface is covered by peritoneum, and that covering is reflected off onto the wall of the abdomen. The organ is therefore described as extraperitoneal rather than intraperitoneal.

Question 7379. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the lower abdomen a person has blood in the urine and cannot pass urine, and the bladder is full. Which structure has most likely been torn: the bladder wall, the ureter, the rectum, or the psoas major? The answer is the bladder wall. A full, tense bladder is torn more easily than an empty one, and the blood then appears in the urine.

Question 7380. Which point of the bladder is lowest when a person stands, and which opening lies there? The answer is the neck of the bladder, where the internal urethral orifice opens. It is the lowest corner of the trigone. Urine therefore collects at the neck and the last of it leaves the bladder there.

Question 7381. In the female, which additional artery commonly supplies the lower part of the bladder: the ovarian artery, the vaginal artery, the superior gluteal artery, or the internal pudendal artery? The answer is the vaginal artery. It is a branch of the internal iliac artery and lies beside the vagina. The superior and inferior vesical arteries still supply most of the organ.

Question 7382. A described sagittal section of a male pelvis shows the bladder as a triangle behind the pubis, with one surface facing the rectum and another facing the abdominal wall above. Which surfaces are these? The answer is the base, which faces backward and downward toward the rectum, and the superior surface, which faces upward and forward. The apex points forward toward the pubic symphysis.

Question 7383. A described radiograph after contrast has been put into the bladder shows a smooth rounded shadow with a flat lower border. Which organ is outlined, and what does an irregular outline suggest? The answer is the bladder, and an irregular outline suggests a growth or a stone. Contrast outlines the inner surface, so anything projecting into the cavity shows as a filling defect.

Question 7384. A constructed illustration: a child born with a defect of the lower spine cannot empty the bladder completely, and the sphincter tightens while the detrusor contracts. Which two sets of nerves are failing to work together? The answer is the pelvic splanchnic nerves and the pudendal nerve. The detrusor and the external sphincter normally act in turn rather than together.

Question 7385. Which of these pairs belongs together: the apex with the umbilicus, the base with the pelvic floor, or the superior surface with the rectum? The answer is the apex with the umbilicus, joined by the median umbilical ligament. The base faces the rectum and the seminal vesicles in the male. The trigone lies on the base near the neck.

Question 7386. What is the name of the venous network that surrounds the bladder? The answer is the vesical venous plexus. It lies in the loose tissue beside the bladder and the prostate and drains into the internal iliac veins. It communicates with the prostatic plexus and with the rectal veins.

Question 7387. From which segments of the spinal cord do the pelvic splanchnic nerves arise: the first to the third lumbar, the eleventh thoracic to the second lumbar, the second to the fourth sacral, or the fourth to the fifth lumbar? The answer is the second to the fourth sacral segments. They pass through the sacral foramina to the pelvic plexus, and their fibers empty the bladder.

Question 7388. Toward which two structures does the apex of the bladder point? The answer is the pubic symphysis and the umbilicus. The apex lies behind the upper part of the symphysis. It is joined to the umbilicus by the median umbilical ligament and is the highest point of the empty bladder.

Question 7389. A constructed illustration: a newborn has an open lower abdominal wall and the back wall of the bladder is exposed. Which statement about this fault is correct: the front of the bladder is open, the trigone is absent, the posterior wall is missing, or the urachus is duplicated? The answer is that the front of the bladder is open, while the posterior wall and the trigone are present.

Question 7390. A described section through the bladder outlet shows a ring of smooth muscle in the bladder wall and a ring of striped muscle around the urethra below it. Which two sphincters are shown? The answer is the internal urethral sphincter and the external urethral sphincter. The inner one is involuntary and the outer one is voluntary.

Question 7391. Which muscle of the bladder contracts to expel urine, and what kind of muscle is it? The answer is the detrusor, which is smooth muscle. Its bundles run in several directions and interlace with one another. It receives both sympathetic and parasympathetic nerves, which have opposite effects on it.

Question 7392. Which structures form the upper corners of the trigone of the bladder: the two internal urethral orifices, the two ureteric orifices, the median umbilical ligament and the uvula, or the two ejaculatory ducts? The answer is the two ureteric orifices. The lowest corner is the internal urethral orifice. The triangle between these three points never changes its shape.

Question 7393. A described cross section of the bladder wall shows a lining that is folded in one region and smooth in another. Which two regions are described, and what lies beneath the smooth one? The answer is the folded mucosa of the body and the smooth mucosa of the trigone, where there is no submucosa. The folds disappear as the bladder fills.

Question 7394. In the male, which structures lie behind the base of the bladder: the prostate and the pubic symphysis, the rectum with the seminal vesicles and the ampullae of the ductus deferens, or the urethra with the vagina and the cervix? The answer is the rectum, the seminal vesicles and the ampullae of the ductus deferens. The peritoneum dips between them as the rectovesical pouch.

Question 7395. A constructed illustration: a tube is passed into the bladder of a man whose prostate is enlarged, and the tube meets an obstacle below the bladder. Which part of the urethra has narrowed: the prostatic part, the membranous part, the spongy part, or the intramural part? The answer is the prostatic part, which the gland surrounds just below the neck of the bladder.

Question 7396. Why does urine not pass back up the ureter when the bladder contracts: the orifice has a flap of mucosa, the intramural part is compressed by bladder muscle, the peritoneum holds it shut, or the kidney draws it back? The answer is that the intramural part is compressed by the muscle of the bladder. The oblique course turns the rising pressure into a closing force.

Question 7397. Which statement about the emptying of the bladder is correct: the detrusor relaxes while the sphincters contract, the detrusor contracts while the internal sphincter relaxes, the detrusor contracts while the external sphincter contracts, or the detrusor does not change at all? The answer is that the detrusor contracts while the internal sphincter relaxes. The external sphincter is then relaxed at will.

Question 7398. A described coronal section of the pelvis shows a rounded organ in the midline with two tubes entering its upper and outer corners and a single channel leaving its lowest point. Which organ and which three openings are shown? The answer is the bladder, with the two ureteric orifices above and the internal urethral orifice below. The three openings mark the corners of the trigone.

Question 7399. A described sagittal section through a male pelvis shows the bladder in front and a smaller rounded organ directly below its neck, wrapped around a tube. Which organ is shown, and how does it relate to the bladder? The answer is the prostate, which lies below the neck of the bladder and surrounds the first part of the urethra.

Question 7400. Which statement about the ureters and the bladder is correct: the ureters open at the apex, they open at the lateral angles of the trigone, or the organs lie above the peritoneum? The answer is that they open at the lateral angles of the trigone and the bladder empties through its neck. The oblique passage of each ureter prevents urine from flowing back.

To summarize, each ureter is a muscular tube about 25 to 30 centimeters long that runs behind the peritoneum from the renal pelvis to the bladder, descending on the psoas major and crossing the pelvic brim in front of the common iliac artery. Its three narrow points are the ureteropelvic junction, the pelvic brim and the ureterovesical junction, and its lower end passes obliquely through the bladder wall so that rising pressure closes it. In the female the uterine artery crosses above the ureter near the cervix, and in the male the ductus deferens crosses above it. The bladder lies in the pelvis behind the pubic symphysis when empty and rises into the abdomen as it fills, and it is extraperitoneal, with peritoneum only on its superior surface. Its base faces the rectum and the seminal vesicles in the male and the vagina in the female, and its smooth fixed trigone is marked by the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice. The detrusor is emptied by parasympathetic fibers from the second to the fourth sacral segments, and the pudendal nerve carries the voluntary control of the external sphincter.
