# Chapter 91 — Abdomen, Part Two

This chapter works through the abdomen below the diaphragm, taking its walls, its organs and its peritoneal spaces one at a time. It covers the anterior abdominal wall and the inguinal canal, the peritoneum and the spaces it creates, the stomach and the intestines, the liver with its biliary passages, the spleen, the pancreas, the kidneys and the posterior abdominal wall with its great vessels. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer immediately after the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be kept in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9001. Which flat muscle of the anterior abdominal wall is the most superficial, with fibers running downward and forward from the lower ribs to the iliac crest and the pubic bone? The answer is the external oblique muscle. Its aponeurosis forms the inguinal ligament along its lower edge, and the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis lie deep to it.

Question 9002. The inguinal ligament is the folded lower edge of which structure: the aponeurosis of the external oblique, the internal oblique muscle, the transversus abdominis, or the fascia transversalis? The answer is the aponeurosis of the external oblique. It stretches from the anterior superior iliac spine to the pubic tubercle and forms the floor of the inguinal canal.

Question 9003. A described dissection of the groin shows a strong band running from the front of the hip bone to the pubic bone, with a canal above it. What is this band, and what does it form? The answer is the inguinal ligament, which forms the floor of the inguinal canal, and below its medial end lies the femoral canal.

Question 9004. Which area lies immediately medial to the inferior epigastric artery, bounded below by the inguinal ligament and medially by the rectus sheath, and is the site of a direct inguinal hernia? The answer is the weak area of the posterior wall of the inguinal canal, known as Hesselbach's triangle. A direct hernia pushes through it, while an indirect hernia enters the canal lateral to the same artery.

Question 9005. A constructed illustration: a man lifts a heavy load and feels sudden pain in the groin, and the swelling that follows passes through the deep inguinal ring. Which structure has the swelling entered, and where does it lie relative to the inferior epigastric artery? The answer is the inguinal canal, lateral to that artery. Management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9006. True or false: in the female the inguinal canal contains the round ligament of the uterus. The answer is true. The canal carries the round ligament in the female and the spermatic cord in the male, and the ilioinguinal nerve runs in it and leaves through the superficial ring.

Question 9007. Which of these pairs belongs together: the superficial inguinal ring with the fascia transversalis, the deep inguinal ring with the fascia transversalis, the deep inguinal ring with the external oblique aponeurosis, or the inguinal ligament with the internal oblique? The answer is the deep ring with the fascia transversalis, which it pierces lateral to the inferior epigastric artery. The superficial ring is a gap in the external oblique aponeurosis.

Question 9008. Which structure forms the roof of the inguinal canal: the external oblique aponeurosis, the arching fibers of the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis, the inguinal ligament, or the fascia transversalis? The answer is the arching fibers of the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis. They pass above the cord from the abdominal wall to the pubis, while the inguinal ligament forms the floor.

Question 9009. Which midline band of connective tissue separates the two rectus abdominis muscles and is formed by the interwoven aponeuroses of the three flat muscles? The answer is the linea alba. It runs from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis, narrow below the navel and broad above it, and it carries few vessels.

Question 9010. A described transverse section of the anterior abdominal wall, cut above the navel, shows the rectus abdominis enclosed in a sheath. Which aponeuroses lie in front of the muscle, and which behind it? The answer is the external oblique and the anterior layer of the internal oblique in front, and the posterior layer of the internal oblique and the transversus abdominis behind.

Question 9011. Which nerve runs within the inguinal canal in front of the spermatic cord and supplies the skin of the upper medial thigh? The answer is the ilioinguinal nerve. It is a branch of the first lumbar nerve and enters the canal lateral to the deep ring, then leaves with the cord through the superficial ring.

Question 9012. Which of these organs lies behind the peritoneum: the stomach, the ascending colon, the transverse colon, or the jejunum? The answer is the ascending colon. It lies against the posterior abdominal wall and is covered by peritoneum only on its front and sides, while the transverse colon and the jejunum hang on mesenteries.

Question 9013. A described dissection of the upper abdomen shows a double sheet of peritoneum hanging from the greater curvature of the stomach down over the intestines. What is this sheet, and which two layers form it? The answer is the greater omentum, formed by the two layers of the dorsal mesogastrium, which fuse below the stomach and enclose a fatty apron.

Question 9014. Which structure forms the posterior boundary of the epiploic foramen, and what lies in front of it? The answer is the inferior vena cava, covered by parietal peritoneum. In front lies the free edge of the lesser omentum, containing the portal vein, the bile duct and the hepatic artery, and the foramen joins the greater sac to the lesser sac.

Question 9015. A constructed illustration: a person with long-standing liver disease has dilated veins radiating from the navel. Which anastomosis has opened, and between which two venous systems? The answer is the portocaval anastomosis around the navel, joining the portal vein by way of the paraumbilical veins to the superficial veins of the anterior abdominal wall. The radiating pattern is called caput medusae.

Question 9016. True or false: in the male the peritoneal cavity is a closed sac that does not communicate with the exterior. The answer is true. The peritoneum forms a closed serous sac in the male, while in the female the cavity communicates with the exterior through the uterine tubes, the uterus and the vagina.

Question 9017. Which of these pairs belongs together: the lesser sac with the space behind the stomach, the greater sac with the bursa behind the stomach, the lesser omentum with the spleen, or the greater omentum with the liver? The answer is the lesser sac with the space behind the stomach. The lesser sac, also called the omental bursa, lies behind the stomach and the lesser omentum.

Question 9018. Through which opening does the lesser sac communicate with the greater sac: the epiploic foramen, the root of the mesentery, the gastrosplenic ligament, or the pyloric orifice? The answer is the epiploic foramen, also called the omental foramen. It is a small opening behind the free edge of the lesser omentum, and the other three are folds of peritoneum or a muscular opening of the stomach.

Question 9019. Which part of the stomach lies above the level of the opening of the esophagus and rises with the left dome of the diaphragm? The answer is the fundus. It is the highest part of the stomach and usually holds a bubble of swallowed air, while the body lies below it and the pyloric part to the right.

Question 9020. Which organ lies immediately behind the stomach, forms the floor of the lesser sac and carries the splenic artery along its upper border? The answer is the pancreas. Its body and tail lie against the posterior wall of the lesser sac, so the stomach lies in front of it, separated by the cavity of the lesser sac.

Question 9021. Which part of the stomach is the narrow funnel that leads into the duodenum, and which ring of muscle surrounds its opening? The answer is the pyloric part, with the pyloric sphincter around the pyloric canal. The canal is about 2 centimeters long in the adult, and the sphincter controls the emptying of the stomach.

Question 9022. Which artery supplies the greater curvature of the stomach from the left: the left gastric artery, the left gastro-omental artery, the right gastric artery, or the short gastric arteries? The answer is the left gastro-omental artery. It arises from the splenic artery and runs along the greater curvature, while the short gastric arteries supply the fundus.

Question 9023. A described contrast study shows the stomach as a curved shadow with a rounded upper part holding gas and a narrow lower end. Which parts are shown, and what does the narrowing mark? The answer is the fundus above, the body in the middle and the pyloric part below, with the narrowing at the pyloric canal. The pyloric sphincter there guards the exit into the duodenum.

Question 9024. Which two arteries run along the lesser curvature of the stomach, one from the left and one from the right? The answer is the left gastric artery and the right gastric artery. The left arises from the celiac trunk and the right from the hepatic artery, and they meet along the lesser curvature and supply both surfaces of the stomach.

Question 9025. A constructed illustration: a person vomits blood and a study shows bleeding from a vessel high on the lesser curvature of the stomach. Which artery is the likely source, and where does it arise? The answer is the left gastric artery, a direct branch of the celiac trunk. It runs down the lesser curvature, and management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 9026. Which of these pairs belongs together: the fundus with the pyloric canal, the body with the cardia, the pyloric antrum with the pyloric canal, or the cardia with the greater curvature? The answer is the pyloric antrum with the pyloric canal. The antrum is the wider part of the pyloric region and narrows into the canal, while the cardia surrounds the opening of the esophagus.

Question 9027. Which part of the duodenum is the only mobile part, with a mesentery of its own: the first part, the second part, the third part, or the fourth part? The answer is the first part, also called the duodenal cap. It runs upward and backward from the pylorus, and the other three parts lie behind the peritoneum and are fixed.

Question 9028. Which structure marks the junction of the duodenum and the jejunum, and by which muscle is it suspended? The answer is the duodenojejunal flexure, suspended by the suspensory muscle of the duodenum. That muscle runs from the right crus of the diaphragm to the flexure, which lies to the left of the aorta behind the peritoneum.

Question 9029. A constructed illustration: a person with an ulcer in the second part of the duodenum has pain that bores into the back. Which structure lying behind that part explains the pain? The answer is the head of the pancreas, which lies behind the second part of the duodenum, and the ulcer may penetrate into it.

Question 9030. A described specimen of the small intestine shows an upper segment with thick walls and few vascular arcades, and a lower segment with thin walls and many arcades. Which segment lies above, and which below? The answer is the jejunum above and the ileum below. The jejunum is thicker walled and more vascular, while the ileum has more arcades and larger lymphoid nodules.

Question 9031. Which part of the small intestine is the widest, thickest and most vascular, and occupies the upper left part of the abdomen? The answer is the jejunum. It begins at the duodenojejunal flexure and forms the upper two fifths of the coiled small intestine, and its walls are heavy and crowded with vessels.

Question 9032. Which statement about the ileum is correct: its walls are thicker than those of the jejunum, its vessels form few arcades, it carries large lymphoid nodules, or its loops lie mainly in the upper left abdomen? The answer is that it carries large lymphoid nodules. The ileum lies mainly in the lower right abdomen and has thinner walls than the jejunum.

Question 9033. A described specimen shows one segment of bowel with long straight vessels running from the last arcade to the wall, and another with short vessels and many arcades. Which segment is which? The answer is the jejunum with the long straight vessels and the ileum with the many arcades. The jejunum is thicker walled and lies higher, and the ileum carries larger lymphoid nodules.

Question 9034. Which vessel crosses immediately in front of the third part of the duodenum, and which vessel lies behind that same part? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery in front and the aorta behind. The third part of the duodenum can therefore be narrowed between the two, squeezed in the narrow angle where they cross.

Question 9035. A constructed illustration: a thin young person vomits bile-stained fluid, and a study shows the duodenum compressed between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery. Which part is compressed? The answer is the third part, which crosses the aorta behind that artery, and the narrow angle between them pinches it.

Question 9036. True or false: the fourth part of the duodenum ascends on the right side of the aorta to reach the duodenojejunal flexure. The answer is false. The fourth part ascends on the left side of the aorta, crosses the left psoas muscle and lies behind the peritoneum before it turns forward at the flexure.

Question 9037. Which of these features distinguishes the large intestine from the small intestine: taeniae coli, a complete longitudinal muscle coat, villi on the mucosa, or a mesentery along its whole length? The answer is taeniae coli, the three flat bands of longitudinal muscle. The large intestine also shows haustra and fatty appendices, while the small intestine bears villi.

Question 9038. Which three flat bands of longitudinal muscle run along the colon and spread out at the rectum to form a complete coat? The answer is the taeniae coli. They are named the mesocolic, the omental and the free taeniae. They are shorter than the colon, so the wall between them is thrown into sacculations called haustra.

Question 9039. Which part of the colon runs upward on the right side against the posterior abdominal wall and is covered by peritoneum only in front and at the sides: the transverse colon, the ascending colon, the sigmoid colon, or the cecum? The answer is the ascending colon. It lies behind the peritoneum from the cecum to the right colic flexure.

Question 9040. A described transverse section of the posterior abdominal wall shows a kidney on each side, capped at the upper pole. Which kidney lies lower in the body, and what caps its upper pole? The answer is the right kidney, which lies lower because the liver occupies the space above it, and the right suprarenal gland caps its upper pole.

Question 9041. Which part of the large intestine is the widest and lies lowest in the right iliac fossa, below the junction with the small intestine? The answer is the cecum. It receives the ileum at the ileocecal junction, where the ileocecal valve guards the opening, and the appendix hangs from its posteromedial wall.

Question 9042. Which line has, one third of the way along it, the surface point that marks the base of the appendix: the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus, the umbilicus to the pubic symphysis, the iliac crest to the umbilicus, or the xiphoid process to the umbilicus? The answer is the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus, at the point named after McBurney, and the tip may lie elsewhere.

Question 9043. A described contrast enema shows a wide tube with sacculations along its wall, three flat bands running lengthwise and small fatty tags. Which part of the bowel is shown, and what are the sacculations called? The answer is the colon, and the sacculations are the haustra. The bands are the taeniae coli and the tags are the appendices epiploicae.

Question 9044. Which muscle lies immediately behind the appendix when its tip hangs downward into the pelvis, and which part of the bowel lies in front of it? The answer is the psoas major behind and the terminal ileum in front. An inflamed appendix lying on the psoas may cause pain on extending the hip, and it may also lie behind the cecum.

Question 9045. A constructed illustration: pain begins around the navel and later settles in the right lower quadrant, where pressing the abdominal wall hurts most. Which structure is inflamed, and why did the pain move? The answer is the appendix. The early pain is referred to the umbilical region and the later pain to the peritoneum of the right iliac fossa.

Question 9046. Which of these pairs belongs together: haustra with the small intestine, taeniae coli with the colon, villi with the colon, or appendices epiploicae with the jejunum? The answer is taeniae coli with the colon. The colon carries three flat bands of longitudinal muscle, while the small intestine bears villi and has no taeniae or haustra.

Question 9047. Which artery supplies the ascending colon and the right half of the transverse colon, and which supplies the rest of the colon? The answer is the superior mesenteric artery for the right side and the inferior mesenteric artery for the left side. The two territories meet near the splenic flexure, and that meeting point is a weak place in the blood supply.

Question 9048. Which named vessel runs along the inner border of the colon and joins the branches of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries? The answer is the marginal artery of the colon. It lies along the mesenteric border and links the two arterial territories, and the junction near the splenic flexure is the weakest point of that chain.

Question 9049. Name two organs of the abdomen that lie behind the peritoneum, spoken as a pair. The answer is the kidneys and the pancreas. The ascending and descending parts of the colon, the duodenum except its first part, and the suprarenal glands are also behind the peritoneum, and such organs are covered by peritoneum on one surface only.

Question 9050. Which two veins unite to form the portal vein: the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein, the splenic vein and the inferior mesenteric vein, the hepatic veins and the inferior vena cava, or the gastric veins and the esophageal veins? The answer is the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein, which join behind the neck of the pancreas.

Question 9051. Which large vein carries the blood of the digestive organs to the liver, lies behind the first part of the duodenum and runs in the free edge of the lesser omentum? The answer is the portal vein. It is formed behind the neck of the pancreas and ends in the liver, and because it has no valves the blood can flow back when the liver is obstructed.

Question 9052. Which structure separates the right lobe of the liver from the left lobe on the front of the organ: the falciform ligament, the ligamentum venosum, the inferior vena cava, or the gallbladder? The answer is the falciform ligament. It runs from the liver to the anterior abdominal wall and carries the ligamentum teres in its free edge.

Question 9053. A described dissection of the upper abdomen shows a large organ under the right dome of the diaphragm, with a fold running from its front to the abdominal wall and a fibrous cord in the free edge of that fold. What are the fold and the cord? The answer is the falciform ligament and the ligamentum teres, and the cord is the remains of the umbilical vein.

Question 9054. Which structure lies between the liver and the stomach, and what does its free edge contain? The answer is the lesser omentum, whose free edge contains the portal vein, the bile duct and the hepatic artery. It runs from the liver to the lesser curvature of the stomach, and behind its free edge lies the epiploic foramen.

Question 9055. A constructed illustration: after a fatty meal a person has severe pain in the right upper abdomen that radiates to the right shoulder. Which structure lies beneath the diaphragm on that side and can irritate it? The answer is the liver, with the gallbladder against its visceral surface. Irritation of the diaphragm refers pain to the shoulder, and management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 9056. Which artery usually supplies the gallbladder: the right hepatic artery by way of the cystic artery, the left hepatic artery, the gastroduodenal artery, or the left gastric artery? The answer is the right hepatic artery by way of the cystic artery, which passes through the triangle bounded by the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the liver.

Question 9057. Which veins drain the liver into the inferior vena cava just below the diaphragm: the hepatic veins, the portal vein, the splenic vein, or the superior mesenteric vein? The answer is the hepatic veins. There are usually three of them, and they open into the inferior vena cava within the bare area of the liver.

Question 9058. Which part of the liver lies against the diaphragm without a covering of peritoneum, between the layers of the coronary ligament? The answer is the bare area. It lies on the posterior surface of the right lobe, and the coronary ligament surrounds it, its two layers meeting at the right and left triangular ligaments.

Question 9059. At which of these places does blood pass from the portal system to the systemic veins: the esophagus, the stomach wall, the small intestine, or the spleen? The answer is the lower end of the esophagus, where the left gastric vein meets the esophageal veins. Other such places are the navel, the rectum and the back of the colon.

Question 9060. A described computed tomography scan of the upper abdomen, known as a CT scan, shows a large organ on the right under the diaphragm, a flat gland crossing the midline in front of the spine, and a rounded organ behind the stomach on the left. Which three structures are described? The answer is the liver, the pancreas and the spleen.

Question 9061. Which organ lies in the left hypochondrium behind the stomach and in contact with the ninth, tenth and eleventh ribs? The answer is the spleen. It lies within the peritoneum and is held by the gastrosplenic and lienorenal ligaments, and its smooth surface faces the diaphragm while its hilum faces the stomach.

Question 9062. Which artery supplies the spleen: the splenic artery from the celiac trunk, the left gastric artery, the superior mesenteric artery, or the left gastro-omental artery? The answer is the splenic artery from the celiac trunk. It runs a tortuous course along the upper border of the pancreas, while the splenic vein runs behind the pancreas.

Question 9063. A described specimen of the spleen cut across shows a dark red interior with small pale spots and a hilum on one side. What enters and leaves at the hilum, and what are the pale spots? The answer is the splenic artery and vein at the hilum, and the pale spots are the white pulp. The red pulp surrounds them.

Question 9064. Which organ lies immediately anterior to the medial surface of the spleen and is joined to it by the gastrosplenic ligament? The answer is the stomach. The gastrosplenic ligament carries the short gastric arteries and the left gastro-omental artery between the two organs, and the lienorenal ligament joins the spleen to the left kidney behind.

Question 9065. A constructed illustration: a blow to the left lower chest is followed by pain in the left upper abdomen and signs of internal bleeding. Which organ has most likely been injured? The answer is the spleen, which lies under the ninth to eleventh ribs, so a fracture of those ribs may tear it. Management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 9066. True or false: the spleen lies behind the peritoneum, fixed to the posterior abdominal wall. The answer is false. The spleen is covered by peritoneum except at its hilum and lies within the peritoneal cavity, held by the gastrosplenic and lienorenal ligaments and by the pressure of the surrounding organs.

Question 9067. Which two ligaments hold the spleen: the gastrosplenic and lienorenal ligaments, the falciform and coronary ligaments, the gastrocolic and phrenicocolic ligaments, or the gastrohepatic and hepatoduodenal ligaments? The answer is the gastrosplenic and lienorenal ligaments. The first joins the spleen to the greater curvature of the stomach and the second to the left kidney.

Question 9068. Which gland lies across the posterior abdominal wall behind the stomach and has a head, a neck, a body and a tail? The answer is the pancreas. Its head lies in the curve of the duodenum and its tail reaches the spleen, and it lies behind the peritoneum with its duct joining the bile duct.

Question 9069. Which duct carries the pancreatic juice to the duodenum, and with which duct does it join: the main pancreatic duct with the bile duct, the accessory pancreatic duct with the cystic duct, the main pancreatic duct with the cystic duct, or the accessory duct with the bile duct? The answer is the main pancreatic duct with the bile duct, which open together at the major duodenal papilla.

Question 9070. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the upper abdomen, known as an MRI scan, shows a flat gland lying across the spine with a duct running through it. Which gland is shown, and where does its duct end? The answer is the pancreas, whose main duct runs the length of the gland and opens into the duodenum with the bile duct.

Question 9071. Which paired organs lie behind the peritoneum on either side of the great vessels, with the right one placed a little lower than the left? The answer is the kidneys. They lie between the twelfth thoracic and the third lumbar levels, and the right kidney is pushed down by the liver above it.

Question 9072. From front to back, which is the order of the structures at the hilum of the kidney: vein, artery, pelvis; artery, vein, pelvis; pelvis, artery, vein; or vein, pelvis, artery? The answer is vein, artery, pelvis. The renal vein lies in front, then the renal artery, and the renal pelvis lies behind them within the renal sinus.

Question 9073. A described computed tomography scan after contrast shows the two kidneys and a tube running down from each hilum toward the pelvis. What is the tube, and how is the urine collected before it? The answer is the ureter, the continuation of the renal pelvis. Urine is collected in the minor calyces, then the major calyces, then the pelvis.

Question 9074. Which gland caps the upper pole of each kidney, and which parts of the bowel lie in front of the right kidney? The answer is the suprarenal gland on each side, with the duodenum and the ascending colon in front of the right kidney. In front of the left kidney lie the stomach, the spleen, the pancreas and the jejunum.

Question 9075. A constructed illustration: a person has severe colicky pain from the loin to the groin, and a study shows a stone in the upper urinary passage. At which three places does that passage normally narrow? The answer is the renal pelvis, the brim of the pelvis and the entry into the bladder. The stone lodges where the tube is narrowest.

Question 9076. Which of these pairs belongs together: the kidney with the peritoneum, the kidney with the renal fascia, the ureter with the peritoneum, or the suprarenal gland with the spleen? The answer is the kidney with the renal fascia. That fascia surrounds the kidney and its fat, while the kidney and the ureter lie behind the peritoneum and are not covered by it.

Question 9077. Which statement about the arteries of the kidney is correct: each kidney usually receives one artery that divides into segmental branches, the segmental branches join freely with one another, the kidney receives two arteries in every person, or the arteries enter at the lower pole? The answer is that each kidney usually receives one artery that divides into segmental branches, and those branches behave as end arteries.

Question 9078. Which gland sits on the upper pole of each kidney, triangular on the right and crescent shaped on the left, and produces hormones? The answer is the suprarenal gland. The right gland lies against the liver and the inferior vena cava, and the left lies against the spleen, the stomach and the pancreas.

Question 9079. Name two structures that pass through the diaphragm at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra, spoken as a pair. The answer is the aorta and the thoracic duct. The azygos vein also passes through the same opening behind the diaphragm, which lies behind the muscle and in front of the vertebral column.

Question 9080. Which statement about the coverings of the kidney is correct: the renal fascia lies outside the perinephric fat, the perinephric fat lies outside the renal fascia, the fibrous capsule lies outside the perinephric fat, or the renal fascia is the same as the capsule? The answer is that the renal fascia lies outside the perinephric fat. The capsule lies on the kidney and the fat surrounds it.

Question 9081. Which muscle of the posterior abdominal wall arises from the bodies of the lower thoracic and lumbar vertebrae and inserts on the lesser trochanter of the femur? The answer is the psoas major. It lies beside the lumbar spine and forms a rounded swelling in the lower abdomen, and the lumbar plexus is buried within its substance.

Question 9082. Which nerve of the lumbar plexus emerges from the medial border of the psoas major and supplies the adductor muscles of the thigh: the femoral nerve, the obturator nerve, the genitofemoral nerve, or the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve? The answer is the obturator nerve. It leaves the medial border and passes through the obturator canal, while the femoral nerve leaves the lateral border.

Question 9083. A described dissection of the posterior abdominal wall shows a large muscle beside the spine with a network of nerves emerging through it, and a large vessel on its medial side. Which structures are shown? The answer is the psoas major with the lumbar plexus and the aorta. The plexus lies within the muscle, and the inferior vena cava lies to the right of the spine.

Question 9084. Which structure lies immediately in front of the right crus of the diaphragm at the level of the esophageal opening, and which nerves run with it? The answer is the esophagus, with the two vagal trunks. The esophagus passes through the opening at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra, and the vagal trunks lie on its front and back.

Question 9085. A constructed illustration: after a stab wound in the flank, a person cannot extend the hip and has numbness over the front of the thigh. Which plexus has been injured? The answer is the lumbar plexus, which lies within the psoas major. Its branches supply the muscles of the thigh and the skin over them, and management belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 9086. True or false: the opening for the inferior vena cava lies in the central tendon of the diaphragm at the level of the eighth thoracic vertebra. The answer is true. The esophageal opening lies at the tenth thoracic vertebra and the aortic opening at the twelfth, and the phrenic nerve supplies the diaphragm from above.

Question 9087. Which three arteries arise from the celiac trunk: the left gastric, the splenic and the common hepatic; the right gastric, the splenic and the superior mesenteric; the left gastric, the inferior mesenteric and the common hepatic; or the splenic, the renal and the common hepatic? The answer is the left gastric, the splenic and the common hepatic arteries, which arise at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra.

Question 9088. Which short artery arises from the front of the aorta just below the diaphragm and supplies the stomach, the spleen, the liver and the upper duodenum? The answer is the celiac trunk. It divides into the left gastric, the splenic and the common hepatic arteries, while the superior mesenteric artery arises a little lower and supplies the midgut.

Question 9089. Which of these arteries is a branch of the superior mesenteric artery: the ileocolic artery, the left colic artery, the middle rectal artery, or the splenic artery? The answer is the ileocolic artery. It supplies the end of the ileum, the cecum and the appendix, while the left colic artery arises from the inferior mesenteric artery.

Question 9090. A described computed tomography angiogram of the abdomen shows three arteries arising close together from the front of the aorta, the lowest of them running downward and to the left. Which arteries are shown? The answer is the celiac trunk, the superior mesenteric artery and the inferior mesenteric artery. The lowest one supplies the descending colon, the sigmoid colon and the upper rectum.

Question 9091. Name the lymph sac at the lower end of the thoracic duct and name one group of vessels that empties into it. The answer is the cisterna chyli, which receives the intestinal lymph trunk and the two lumbar trunks. It lies in front of the first two lumbar vertebrae, and the thoracic duct begins at it.

Question 9092. Which parts of the bowel are supplied by the inferior mesenteric artery: the descending colon, the sigmoid colon and the upper rectum; the cecum, the ascending colon and the appendix; the jejunum and the ileum; or the stomach and the spleen? The answer is the descending colon, the sigmoid colon and the upper rectum, supplied by an artery arising at the level of the third lumbar vertebra.

Question 9093. A described specimen of the lower abdomen shows a wide tube with sacculations descending on the left side toward the pelvis, with fatty tags along it. Which part of the bowel is shown, and what supplies it? The answer is the descending colon and its continuation, the sigmoid colon, supplied by the inferior mesenteric artery. The tags are the appendices epiploicae.

Question 9094. Pain from the midgut is referred to which region of the body surface: the epigastric region, the umbilical region, the suprapubic region, or the left iliac region? The answer is the umbilical region. The midgut is supplied by the superior mesenteric artery and its pain is felt around the navel, while foregut pain is felt in the epigastrium.

Question 9095. Which nerve forms the posterior vagal trunk as it enters the abdomen: the right vagus nerve, the left vagus nerve, the greater splanchnic nerve, or the phrenic nerve? The answer is the right vagus nerve. The left vagus forms the anterior trunk in front of the esophagus, and the posterior trunk is the larger of the two.

Question 9096. Which chain of ganglia lies in front of the lumbar vertebrae and sends the splanchnic nerves to the abdominal organs? The answer is the sympathetic trunk. The greater splanchnic nerve comes from the fifth to the ninth thoracic ganglia, and the lesser and least splanchnic nerves follow below and end in the celiac and related ganglia.

Question 9097. A tumor of the body of the pancreas often causes deep pain in the back because the organ lies against which structure: the celiac plexus, the inferior vena cava, the left ureter, or the transverse colon? The answer is the celiac plexus, which lies behind the pancreas around the celiac trunk and carries the pain fibers of the upper abdominal organs.

Question 9098. Which line on the posterior wall of the rectus sheath marks the level below which the sheath has no posterior wall and the rectus muscle lies against the fascia transversalis? The answer is the arcuate line. It lies about halfway between the navel and the pubic symphysis, and below it all three aponeuroses pass in front of the rectus muscle.

Question 9099. Which space in the lower back can allow a lumbar hernia, bounded above by the twelfth rib, behind by the erector spinae and in front by the internal oblique: the superior lumbar triangle, the inferior lumbar triangle, the femoral ring, or the obturator canal? The answer is the superior lumbar triangle. The inferior lumbar triangle lies between the latissimus dorsi and the external oblique.

Question 9100. A described cross section through the abdomen shows a cavity lined by a thin membrane, with a large organ on the right, a rounded organ behind the stomach on the left, and two bean-shaped organs against the back wall. Which four structures are described? The answer is the liver, the spleen and the two kidneys, the last lying behind the peritoneum.

To summarize, the anterior abdominal wall is built of three flat muscles whose aponeuroses form the rectus sheath, and the inguinal canal runs above the inguinal ligament between the deep ring in the fascia transversalis and the superficial ring in the external oblique aponeurosis. The peritoneum forms the greater sac and the lesser sac, which communicate through the epiploic foramen behind the free edge of the lesser omentum. The stomach lies in front of the pancreas, the colon carries taeniae coli, haustra and appendices epiploicae, and the jejunum differs from the ileum in wall thickness, arcades and lymphoid nodules. The liver receives the portal vein and drains by the hepatic veins, the spleen lies within the peritoneum against the ninth to eleventh ribs, and the kidneys lie behind the peritoneum with the suprarenal glands capping their upper poles. The posterior wall carries the psoas major with the lumbar plexus and the celiac, superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric arteries, and the diaphragm opens for the inferior vena cava at the eighth thoracic vertebra, the esophagus at the tenth and the aorta at the twelfth.
