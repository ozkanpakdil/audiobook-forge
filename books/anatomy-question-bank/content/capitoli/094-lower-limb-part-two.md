# Chapter 94 — Lower Limb, Part Two

This chapter continues the regional anatomy of the lower limb, from the gluteal region below the hip to the tips of the toes. It covers the muscles of the buttock and of the thigh, the femoral triangle and the adductor canal, the popliteal fossa, the compartments of the leg with their nerves and vessels, and the ankle and the foot with their arches and their retinacula. It holds one hundred questions, and every question gives its answer immediately and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. Every clinical item is a constructed illustration rather than a real patient. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 9301. Which muscle forms the bulk of the buttock and is the strongest extensor of the hip? The answer is the gluteus maximus. It arises from the ilium, the sacrum and the coccyx, and it inserts into the iliotibial tract and the gluteal tuberosity of the femur. The inferior gluteal nerve supplies it.

Question 9302. Which of these muscles abducts the hip and is supplied by the superior gluteal nerve: the gluteus maximus, the gluteus medius, the piriformis, or the quadratus femoris? The answer is the gluteus medius. It arises from the outer surface of the ilium between the two gluteal lines and inserts on the greater trochanter. The gluteus maximus is supplied by the inferior gluteal nerve instead.

Question 9303. Which small muscle leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, runs almost horizontally, and rotates the extended hip laterally? The answer is the piriformis. It arises from the front of the sacrum and passes through the greater sciatic foramen to the upper border of the greater trochanter. When the hip is flexed it abducts the thigh instead.

Question 9304. Through which part of the greater sciatic foramen does the sciatic nerve leave the pelvis, and what lies above it? The answer is the part below the piriformis, with the muscle above it. The nerve passes beneath the lower border of the piriformis and then descends behind the hip joint into the back of the thigh.

Question 9305. A constructed illustration: an injection is given into the buttock, and the needle is placed in the upper lateral part of the buttock to spare a large nerve. Which nerve is being avoided, and why is that site chosen? The answer is the sciatic nerve, which lies lower and more medially under the gluteus maximus. The upper lateral part of the buttock lies over the gluteus medius, away from the nerve.

Question 9306. Which of these structures leaves the pelvis below the piriformis: the superior gluteal nerve, the sciatic nerve, the obturator nerve, or the femoral nerve? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It is joined below the muscle by the inferior gluteal nerve and vessels, the pudendal nerve, and the posterior femoral cutaneous nerve.

Question 9307. True or false: the gluteus minimus lies deep to the gluteus medius and shares its nerve supply. The answer is true. Both muscles arise from the outer surface of the ilium and insert on the greater trochanter, and both are supplied by the superior gluteal nerve. Together they steady the pelvis when the opposite foot leaves the ground.

Question 9308. A described magnetic resonance image through the hips shows a broad muscle on the outer surface of the pelvis, with a smaller muscle deep to it and a nerve running between the two. Which muscle is the superficial one? The answer is the gluteus medius. The gluteus minimus lies deep to it, and the superior gluteal nerve runs between them.

Question 9309. Which of these pairs belongs together: the gluteus maximus with the superior gluteal nerve, the gluteus medius with the inferior gluteal nerve, the piriformis with the nerve to the piriformis, or the quadratus femoris with the femoral nerve? The answer is the piriformis with the nerve to the piriformis. The gluteus maximus takes the inferior gluteal nerve and the gluteus medius the superior gluteal nerve.

Question 9310. Name the two gluteal muscles that keep the pelvis level when the opposite foot is lifted, spoken as a sentence. The answer is the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus. Both pull the hip bone down on the standing side while the other side of the pelvis is unsupported, so the pelvis stays horizontal. The tensor fasciae latae helps them.

Question 9311. Which muscle lies on the outer side of the hip, arises from the front of the iliac crest, and tightens the thick band that runs down the outside of the thigh? The answer is the tensor fasciae latae. It inserts into the iliotibial tract and is supplied by the superior gluteal nerve.

Question 9312. Which of these structures lies between the tendon of the gluteus maximus and the outer surface of the greater trochanter: the trochanteric bursa, the hip joint capsule, the iliopsoas tendon, or the obturator externus tendon? The answer is the trochanteric bursa. It reduces friction where that broad tendon crosses the bone.

Question 9313. Which artery leaves the pelvis above the piriformis while the sciatic nerve and the inferior gluteal vessels leave below it? The answer is the superior gluteal artery. It divides into a superficial and a deep branch that supply the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus and the tensor fasciae latae. It is a branch of the internal iliac artery.

Question 9314. A constructed illustration: a patient stands on the right leg and the left side of the pelvis drops. Which muscles are weak, and on which side is the lesion? The answer is the right gluteus medius and the right gluteus minimus. A weakness of the abductors of the standing hip lets the opposite side of the pelvis fall.

Question 9315. A described dissection shows the lower border of the gluteus maximus lifted, with a thick nerve running downward in the middle of the field and a smaller nerve and vessels beside it. Which structures are shown? The answer is the sciatic nerve with the posterior femoral cutaneous nerve and the inferior gluteal vessels.

Question 9316. Which of these muscles is supplied by the same nerve as the quadratus femoris: the inferior gemellus, the piriformis, the obturator internus, or the gluteus maximus? The answer is the inferior gemellus. The nerve to the quadratus femoris supplies both muscles and arises from the sacral plexus.

Question 9317. A constructed illustration: after a fall, a patient holds the hip flexed and turned inward and cannot feel the back of the thigh. Which nerve is most likely stretched, and which movements are lost? The answer is the sciatic nerve. It lies directly behind the hip joint, which is why a dislocation backward can injure it.

Question 9318. Into which two places does the gluteus maximus insert, and which of them lies on the femur? The answer is the iliotibial tract and the gluteal tuberosity. Most of the muscle ends in the tract, and only the deeper lower fibers reach the femur itself. The tract then blends with the fascia of the thigh.

Question 9319. Which long ribbon-like muscle forms the lateral border of the femoral triangle, running from the front of the hip to the inner side of the knee? The answer is the sartorius. The triangle is bounded above by the inguinal ligament, laterally by the sartorius and medially by the adductor longus.

Question 9320. Which of these statements about the femoral sheath is correct: the artery lies medial to the vein, the vein lies medial to the artery, the femoral nerve lies inside the sheath, or the femoral canal lies laterally within it? The answer is that the vein lies medial to the artery. The nerve lies outside the sheath, and the canal is its most medial compartment.

Question 9321. Which vessel becomes the femoral artery as it passes under the inguinal ligament? The answer is the external iliac artery. It changes its name at the midpoint of the inguinal ligament and runs down the front of the thigh, where its pulse can be felt.

Question 9322. At the groin, on which side of the femoral artery does the femoral vein lie, and what separates the two? The answer is the medial side, with the femoral sheath placing them in the same compartment. The artery lies lateral and the vein intermediate, and the femoral canal lies medial to the vein.

Question 9323. A constructed illustration: a swelling appears just below the groin crease, medial to the femoral artery. Which opening has it passed through, and which structure bounds that opening medially? The answer is the femoral canal, bounded medially by the lacunar ligament. Such a swelling lies below and lateral to the pubic tubercle, unlike one that follows the inguinal canal.

Question 9324. Which of these structures does not normally lie in the adductor canal: the femoral artery, the femoral vein, the saphenous nerve, or the sciatic nerve? The answer is the sciatic nerve, which lies behind the thigh and never enters the canal. The other three run in the canal with the nerve to the vastus medialis.

Question 9325. A described transverse section through the upper thigh shows one bone surrounded by three groups of muscles, with strong bands of fascia separating them. Which bone lies in the middle of the section? The answer is the femur. The three groups are the anterior, the medial and the posterior compartments of the thigh.

Question 9326. Which of these pairs belongs together: the adductor longus with the femoral nerve, the gracilis with the obturator nerve, the pectineus with the sciatic nerve, or the adductor magnus with the superior gluteal nerve? The answer is the gracilis with the obturator nerve. The adductor longus and the adductor magnus are also supplied by the obturator nerve, and the pectineus by the femoral nerve.

Question 9327. Which nerve is the longest cutaneous branch of the femoral nerve and runs down the medial side of the leg beside a long superficial vein? The answer is the saphenous nerve. It leaves the adductor canal and becomes superficial on the medial side of the knee, where it supplies the skin of the leg and the foot.

Question 9328. True or false: the great saphenous vein begins at the medial end of the dorsal venous arch and passes in front of the medial malleolus. The answer is true. It runs up the medial side of the leg and the thigh and ends in the femoral vein below the inguinal ligament.

Question 9329. Which of these arteries is the main supply of the head and neck of the femur in the adult: the lateral circumflex femoral, the medial circumflex femoral, the obturator, or the inferior gluteal? The answer is the medial circumflex femoral. Its branches run along the back of the neck and enter the bone, so a fracture there can interrupt them.

Question 9330. Which two vessels pass through the opening in the adductor magnus, and where do they go? The answer is the femoral artery and the femoral vein, which pass through the adductor hiatus into the popliteal fossa. There the artery becomes the popliteal artery, lying behind the knee.

Question 9331. A constructed illustration: a cyclist develops numbness on the inner side of the knee and the upper part of the leg after long rides, with no weakness. Which nerve is compressed, and where does it lie? The answer is the saphenous nerve in the adductor canal, where it lies beside the femoral artery.

Question 9332. A described dissection of the femoral triangle exposes the structures in order from the lateral side to the medial side. Which structure lies most lateral, and which lies most medial? The answer is the femoral nerve laterally and the femoral canal medially, with the artery and the vein between them.

Question 9333. Name the structures that lie in the femoral triangle in order from lateral to medial, spoken as a sentence. The answer is the femoral nerve, the femoral artery, the femoral vein and the femoral canal. The nerve lies outside the femoral sheath, and the canal is the small medial compartment through which lymph vessels pass.

Question 9334. Which of these nerves runs in the adductor canal beside the femoral artery and supplies the vastus medialis: the saphenous nerve, the nerve to the vastus medialis, the obturator nerve, or the medial cutaneous nerve of the thigh? The answer is the nerve to the vastus medialis. It leaves the canal with the artery and crosses to the muscle.

Question 9335. Which is the most medial muscle of the thigh, a long thin strap that runs from the pubis to the inner side of the upper tibia? The answer is the gracilis. It adducts the thigh and flexes the knee, and the obturator nerve supplies it.

Question 9336. Three tendons meet on the inner side of the upper tibia and are arranged one over another. Which lies most superficial, and which lies deepest of the three? The answer is the sartorius most superficial and the semitendinosus deepest, with the gracilis between them. Together they form the pes anserinus.

Question 9337. Which of these statements about the sartorius is correct: it extends the hip and rotates it inward, it flexes the hip and the knee, it adducts the thigh, or it extends the knee? The answer is that it flexes the hip and the knee. It also abducts and laterally rotates the hip, and the femoral nerve supplies it.

Question 9338. The femoral ring is the abdominal opening of the femoral canal. Which structure forms its medial boundary, and what lies immediately lateral to it? The answer is the lacunar ligament medially and the femoral vein laterally. The ring is closed above by peritoneum and by a plug of fat.

Question 9339. A constructed illustration: a long dilated vein is seen running up the inner side of the leg from in front of the medial malleolus. Which vein is it, and where does it end? The answer is the great saphenous vein, which ends in the femoral vein. Its many valves normally keep the blood moving upward.

Question 9340. Which muscle is formed by the union of the psoas major and the iliacus, and on which small bony prominence does it insert? The answer is the iliopsoas, which inserts on the lesser trochanter of the femur. It is the chief flexor of the hip and is supplied by branches of the lumbar plexus and the femoral nerve.

Question 9341. Which muscle forms the upper lateral boundary of the popliteal fossa? The answer is the biceps femoris. Its tendon can be felt as a cord on the outer side of the hollow behind the knee. The semitendinosus and the semimembranosus form the upper medial boundary.

Question 9342. Which of these structures lies in the popliteal fossa: the femoral nerve, the popliteal artery, the great saphenous vein, or the obturator nerve? The answer is the popliteal artery. The fossa also holds the popliteal vein, the tibial nerve and the common fibular nerve, together with fat and lymph nodes.

Question 9343. Within the popliteal fossa, which of the three main structures lies deepest and which lies most superficial: the artery, the vein, or the tibial nerve? The answer is the artery deepest and the tibial nerve most superficial. The vein lies between them, and all three run from above downward.

Question 9344. A constructed illustration: a firm pulsating swelling fills the hollow behind the knee, and the pulse at the ankle is reduced. Which structure is involved, and which nerve lies closest to its surface? The answer is the popliteal artery, with the tibial nerve lying most superficially over it.

Question 9345. A described radiograph of the knee shows two large bones end to end, a slender bone on the outer side, and a small rounded bone in front of the joint. Which bone lies in front? The answer is the patella. It lies within the tendon of the quadriceps and protects the front of the joint.

Question 9346. Which strong band runs from the lower pole of the patella to the tibial tuberosity, and what does it continue above the patella? The answer is the patellar ligament, which continues the quadriceps tendon. It is the part of that tendon that lies below the bone.

Question 9347. Which of these movements locks the extended knee: a medial rotation of the femur on the tibia, a lateral rotation of the tibia on the femur, a forward glide of the tibia, or a backward glide of the femur? The answer is a lateral rotation of the tibia on the femur. It happens in the last few degrees of extension.

Question 9348. True or false: the anterior cruciate ligament prevents the tibia from sliding forward on the femur. The answer is true. It runs from the front of the tibia upward and backward to the outer side of the femur, and a tear lets the tibia move forward when the knee is pulled.

Question 9349. Which of these pairs belongs together: the fibular collateral ligament with a valgus strain, the tibial collateral ligament with a varus strain, the anterior cruciate ligament with forward sliding of the tibia, or the posterior cruciate ligament with forward sliding? The answer is the anterior cruciate ligament with forward sliding of the tibia. It resists that movement. The fibular ligament resists a varus strain and the tibial ligament a valgus strain.

Question 9350. Name the two structures that form the lower boundary of the popliteal fossa, spoken as a sentence. The answer is the two heads of the gastrocnemius. They arise from the back of the femur above the condyles and meet below to form the belly of the calf.

Question 9351. Which of these statements about the menisci is correct: the medial meniscus is more circular than the lateral, the lateral meniscus is attached to the tibial collateral ligament, the medial meniscus is attached to the tibial collateral ligament, or both are attached to the fibular collateral ligament? The answer is that the medial meniscus is attached to the tibial collateral ligament. That attachment makes it the more easily torn.

Question 9352. A described magnetic resonance image of the knee shows a dark wedge of fibrocartilage between the femur and the tibia on the inner side, with a bright line running through it to the joint surface. Which structure is shown, and what does the bright line mean? The answer is the medial meniscus, and the line reaching the surface indicates a tear.

Question 9353. From which bony points does the gastrocnemius arise, and what does it form with the soleus below? The answer is the two condyles of the femur, and the two muscles join in the calcaneal tendon. The medial head is the larger of the two.

Question 9354. Which nerve winds around the neck of the fibula just below the knee, and what lies over it there? The answer is the common fibular nerve, which lies against the bone under the cover of the fibularis longus. That exposed position makes it easy to injure.

Question 9355. A constructed illustration: after a blow to the outer side of the knee, a patient cannot lift the foot and the toes, and the top of the foot is numb. Which nerve is injured, and which muscles have failed? The answer is the common fibular nerve, with paralysis of the muscles of the anterior and lateral compartments.

Question 9356. Which of these statements about the small saphenous vein is correct: it begins behind the lateral malleolus, it begins in front of the medial malleolus, it ends in the femoral vein, or it lies on the inner side of the leg? The answer is that it begins behind the lateral malleolus. It runs up the back of the leg and ends in the popliteal vein.

Question 9357. Which small muscle has a thin belly lying above the lateral head of the gastrocnemius and a very long slender tendon that ends on the calcaneus? The answer is the plantaris. Its belly is small and its tendon is often taken as a graft, and the tibial nerve supplies it.

Question 9358. A described dissection of the popliteal fossa clears the fat and shows, from the surface inward, a nerve, then a vein, then an artery close to the bone. Which nerve is the superficial one? The answer is the tibial nerve, which continues down the back of the leg.

Question 9359. Which of these bursae normally communicates with the cavity of the knee joint: the suprapatellar bursa, the prepatellar bursa, the superficial infrapatellar bursa, or the deep infrapatellar bursa? The answer is the suprapatellar bursa. It lies between the femur and the quadriceps tendon and is a prolongation of the joint cavity.

Question 9360. A constructed illustration: fluid collects in the knee, and the swelling appears above the patella as a soft fullness. Which space has filled, and with what does it communicate? The answer is the suprapatellar bursa, which communicates with the joint. Fluid formed in the joint therefore distends it.

Question 9361. Which muscle lies just lateral to the front edge of the tibia, lifts the foot upward, and turns the sole inward? The answer is the tibialis anterior. It arises from the upper two thirds of the lateral surface of the tibia and inserts on the medial side of the foot.

Question 9362. Which of these muscles lies in the anterior compartment of the leg: the gastrocnemius, the tibialis anterior, the fibularis longus, or the soleus? The answer is the tibialis anterior. The extensor digitorum longus, the extensor hallucis longus and the fibularis tertius lie with it in that compartment.

Question 9363. Which broad flat muscle lies deep to the gastrocnemius and arises from the back of the tibia and the fibula as well as from a tendinous arch between them? The answer is the soleus. It is a powerful plantarflexor of the foot and is supplied by the tibial nerve.

Question 9364. Which nerve and which artery run together in the anterior compartment of the leg, between the tibialis anterior and the extensor hallucis longus? The answer is the deep fibular nerve and the anterior tibial artery. They enter the compartment through the interosseous membrane near its upper end.

Question 9365. A constructed illustration: after a long march, a leg is tense and painful, and pulling the toes upward causes agony while the pulse at the ankle remains. Which compartment is involved, and which nerve is at risk? The answer is the anterior compartment, with the deep fibular nerve at risk.

Question 9366. A described transverse section through the middle of the leg shows two bones joined by a membrane, with muscles grouped in front, outside and behind them. Which bone lies on the outer side and carries no weight? The answer is the fibula, which lies laterally and gives attachment to muscles.

Question 9367. Which nerve supplies the fibularis longus and the fibularis brevis in the lateral compartment of the leg: the deep fibular nerve, the superficial fibular nerve, the tibial nerve, or the saphenous nerve? The answer is the superficial fibular nerve. It also supplies the skin of the lower part of the front of the leg and the dorsum of the foot.

Question 9368. The tendon of which muscle crosses the sole of the foot obliquely to reach the base of the first metatarsal and the medial cuneiform: the tibialis posterior, the fibularis longus, the fibularis brevis, or the flexor hallucis longus? The answer is the fibularis longus. It supports the transverse arch of the foot.

Question 9369. Which muscle lies between the tibialis anterior and the extensor digitorum longus in the lower part of the leg and straightens the great toe? The answer is the extensor hallucis longus. Its tendon crosses the front of the ankle, and the deep fibular nerve supplies it.

Question 9370. Which small muscle belongs to the anterior compartment but arises from the lower part of the fibula rather than the tibia, and helps to lift the outer edge of the foot? The answer is the fibularis tertius. It is a separated part of the extensor digitorum longus and inserts on the base of the fifth metatarsal.

Question 9371. Which of these statements about the calcaneal tendon is correct: it inserts on the calcaneus, it inserts on the talus, it contains the plantaris tendon, or it is formed by the tibialis posterior? The answer is that it inserts on the calcaneus. The gastrocnemius and the soleus form it, and the plantaris tendon lies beside it.

Question 9372. Which muscle of the deep posterior compartment sends its tendon behind the medial malleolus to insert mainly on the navicular bone? The answer is the tibialis posterior. It supports the medial arch of the foot and turns the sole inward, and the tibial nerve supplies it.

Question 9373. Behind the medial malleolus, the tendons of the flexor digitorum longus and the flexor hallucis longus lie one behind the other. Which of these structures lies immediately behind the tendon of the flexor digitorum longus: the posterior tibial artery, the tibialis posterior tendon, the calcaneal tendon, or the sural nerve? The answer is the posterior tibial artery, and the tibial nerve lies behind the artery.

Question 9374. Which of these muscles is the chief plantarflexor of the foot when the knee is bent, because it does not cross the knee joint: the gastrocnemius, the soleus, the plantaris, or the tibialis anterior? The answer is the soleus. It works alone in that position, while the gastrocnemius loses power as the knee bends.

Question 9375. A described dissection of the back of the leg lifts the two heads of the gastrocnemius and shows a broad flat muscle beneath them, with a thick tendon below. Which muscle is exposed? The answer is the soleus, whose tendon joins that of the gastrocnemius to form the calcaneal tendon.

Question 9376. Which artery continues the anterior tibial artery onto the dorsum of the foot, where its pulse is felt beside the tendon of the extensor hallucis longus: the posterior tibial artery, the dorsalis pedis artery, the fibular artery, or the medial plantar artery? The answer is the dorsalis pedis artery.

Question 9377. Where does the anterior tibial artery enter the front of the leg: above the interosseous membrane, through the upper part of the interosseous membrane, through the adductor hiatus, or behind the medial malleolus? The answer is through the upper part of the interosseous membrane. It then descends on the membrane between the tibialis anterior and the extensor muscles.

Question 9378. At which level does the popliteal artery divide into the anterior and the posterior tibial arteries: at the adductor hiatus, at the upper border of the popliteus, at the lower border of the popliteus, or at the neck of the fibula? The answer is at the lower border of the popliteus.

Question 9379. True or false: the tibial nerve supplies every muscle of the posterior compartment of the leg. The answer is true. It gives branches to the gastrocnemius, the soleus, the plantaris, the popliteus, the tibialis posterior and the two long flexors of the toes.

Question 9380. A described magnetic resonance image of the leg shows swelling and bright signal in the muscles that lie against the interosseous membrane in front, while the lateral compartment looks normal. Which nerve is most at risk in that group, and which movement would fail first? The answer is the deep fibular nerve, and lifting the foot upward would fail first.

Question 9381. A described computed tomography scan of the leg shows a break across the neck of the fibula, with the fragments displaced. Which nerve is most likely injured, and how would the foot look? The answer is the common fibular nerve, with the foot hanging down and turned inward.

Question 9382. Behind the medial malleolus, several structures pass from the front to the back within one sheath. Which structure lies most forward, and which lies most backward? The answer is the tibialis posterior tendon most forward and the flexor hallucis longus tendon most backward, with the flexor digitorum longus tendon, the artery and the nerve between them.

Question 9383. Which bone forms the heel and is the largest of the tarsal bones? The answer is the calcaneus. It transmits the weight of the body to the ground behind the ankle and gives attachment to the calcaneal tendon and to the muscles of the sole.

Question 9384. Which of these is a bone of the tarsus: the navicular, the first metatarsal, the proximal phalanx of the great toe, or a sesamoid bone? The answer is the navicular. It lies between the talus behind and the cuneiform bones in front, and it takes part in the medial arch of the foot.

Question 9385. Which bone of the foot transmits the weight of the body from the leg to the foot, has no muscle attached to it, and forms the lower part of the ankle joint? The answer is the talus. Its rounded upper surface, the trochlea, fits into the socket formed by the tibia and the fibula.

Question 9386. Which of these bones takes part in the medial longitudinal arch of the foot: the calcaneus, the cuboid, the lateral cuneiform, or the fourth metatarsal? The answer is the calcaneus. The arch runs from the calcaneus through the talus, the navicular, the cuneiform bones and the first three metatarsals.

Question 9387. A described radiograph of the foot taken from above shows one bone overlapping another at the ankle, with a row of small bones in front and long bones beyond them. Which small bone lies on the outer side of the middle of the foot? The answer is the cuboid, which lies between the calcaneus and the fourth and fifth metatarsals.

Question 9388. On which side of the ankle does the strong deltoid ligament lie, and on which side are the three separate lateral ligaments? The answer is the deltoid ligament on the medial side and the lateral ligaments on the outer side. The lateral ones are the anterior and the posterior talofibular ligaments and the calcaneofibular ligament.

Question 9389. Which ligament of the ankle is the one most often torn when the foot is turned inward: the deltoid ligament, the anterior talofibular ligament, the calcaneofibular ligament, or the posterior talofibular ligament? The answer is the anterior talofibular ligament. It is the weakest part of the lateral group.

Question 9390. Which of these structures lies behind the medial malleolus within the tarsal tunnel: the sural nerve, the tibial nerve, the superficial fibular nerve, or the tendon of the fibularis longus? The answer is the tibial nerve. The tunnel also holds the tibialis posterior, the flexor digitorum longus and the flexor hallucis longus tendons with the posterior tibial artery.

Question 9391. Which thick band of deep fascia runs from the medial tubercle of the calcaneus forward to the bases of the toes and helps to hold up the arch of the foot? The answer is the plantar aponeurosis. Tightening it when the toes bend raises the arch and supports the foot in walking.

Question 9392. Which vein begins at the medial end of the dorsal venous arch of the foot and ascends in front of the medial malleolus: the small saphenous vein, the great saphenous vein, the popliteal vein, or the anterior tibial vein? The answer is the great saphenous vein, which ends in the femoral vein.

Question 9393. Which of these pairs belongs together: the navicular with the sustentaculum tali, the calcaneus with the sustentaculum tali, the cuboid with the trochlea, or the talus with the groove for the fibularis longus tendon? The answer is the calcaneus with the sustentaculum tali, a shelf that supports the talus. The navicular carries a tuberosity, and the cuboid carries the groove for the fibularis longus tendon.

Question 9394. The tendon of which muscle runs in a groove on the back of the talus and beneath the sustentaculum tali on its way to the great toe: the flexor digitorum longus, the flexor hallucis longus, the tibialis posterior, or the fibularis longus? The answer is the flexor hallucis longus. Its tendon is the one that lies most backward behind the medial malleolus.

Question 9395. True or false: the medial longitudinal arch of the foot is higher and more mobile than the lateral arch. The answer is true. The medial arch is made of the calcaneus, the talus, the navicular, the cuneiform bones and the first three metatarsals, while the lateral arch is flatter and takes the weight.

Question 9396. A described dissection of the sole shows the plantar aponeurosis removed and four layers of muscle beneath it, with two nerves and two arteries running between them. Which two nerves are shown? The answer is the medial plantar nerve and the lateral plantar nerve, both branches of the tibial nerve.

Question 9397. Which of these nerves supplies most of the muscles of the sole of the foot: the deep fibular nerve, the superficial fibular nerve, the medial plantar nerve, or the sural nerve? The answer is the medial plantar nerve. With the lateral plantar nerve it supplies the muscles of the sole, and both come from the tibial nerve.

Question 9398. A described magnetic resonance image of the ankle shows a thickened band behind the medial malleolus, with a bright swollen nerve beneath it and wasted muscle in the sole. Which nerve is compressed, and which muscles are wasted? The answer is the tibial nerve, with wasting of the small muscles of the sole.

Question 9399. Which of these pairs belongs together: the abductor hallucis with the first layer of the sole, the quadratus plantae with the first layer, the dorsal interossei with the second layer, or the lumbricals with the fourth layer? The answer is the abductor hallucis with the first layer. The quadratus plantae and the lumbricals lie in the second layer, and the interossei in the fourth.

Question 9400. A described dissection of the dorsum of the foot shows a small muscle arising from the upper surface of the calcaneus and dividing into four tendons that join the long extensor tendons. Which muscle is shown, and which nerve supplies it? The answer is the extensor digitorum brevis, supplied by the deep fibular nerve.

To summarize, the gluteal region is built around the gluteus maximus, supplied by the inferior gluteal nerve, and the gluteus medius and the gluteus minimus, supplied by the superior gluteal nerve, which steady the pelvis when the opposite foot leaves the ground. The sciatic nerve leaves the pelvis below the piriformis and the superior gluteal artery leaves above it. In the thigh, the femoral triangle holds the femoral nerve, artery, vein and canal from lateral to medial, the femoral vein lies medial to the artery, and the femoral artery passes through the adductor hiatus to become the popliteal artery. The popliteal fossa holds the artery deepest, the vein next and the tibial nerve most superficial, and the common fibular nerve winds around the neck of the fibula. The leg is divided into an anterior compartment supplied by the deep fibular nerve, a lateral compartment supplied by the superficial fibular nerve and a posterior compartment supplied by the tibial nerve, with the gastrocnemius and the soleus forming the calcaneal tendon. Behind the medial malleolus lie the tendons of the tibialis posterior, the flexor digitorum longus and the flexor hallucis longus with the posterior tibial artery and the tibial nerve, and the sole of the foot is supplied by the medial and the lateral plantar nerves. The arches of the foot and the plantar aponeurosis spread the weight of the body, and this chapter is educational material that does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.
