# Chapter 11 — Vertebral Column, Part Two

This chapter follows the column upward to the two vertebrae that carry the skull and then traces the passages and the ligaments that hold the rest of the column together. It covers the atlas, the axis, the vertebral canal, the intervertebral foramina, and the vertebral ligaments. It holds one hundred questions, and each one gives its answer in the same breath as the question and then explains why that answer is right, so nothing has to be held in mind until the end. This is educational material, and it supports rather than replaces an anatomy course, a dissection, a clinical training program, or the judgment of a teacher.

Question 1001. Which vertebra of the column has no body and no spinous process, and is built as a ring of bone instead? The answer is the atlas, the first cervical vertebra. It carries the skull rather than bearing weight along the column, so it has no body of its own. Its ring is made of an anterior arch, a posterior arch, and two thick lateral masses.

Question 1002. Which statement about the ring of the atlas is correct: it carries the spinal cord and the dens, it carries the spinal cord alone, it carries the vertebral artery, or it is closed by bone? The answer is that it carries the spinal cord and the dens. The dens rises into the front part of the ring, and the vertebral artery passes through the transverse foramen instead.

Question 1003. A described radiograph of the upper neck in side view shows a ring of bone with a small knob on its front and no body behind it, and the skull rests on two curved facets above. Which vertebra is shown? The answer is the atlas. The knob is its anterior tubercle, and the facets above receive the occipital condyles. No other vertebra has this shape.

Question 1004. The dens of the axis lies immediately behind the anterior arch of the atlas. Which structure lies between the dens and the spinal cord? The answer is the transverse ligament of the atlas. It stretches between the two lateral masses and holds the dens against the back of the anterior arch.

Question 1005. Which smooth facet on the back of the anterior arch of the atlas receives the front of the dens? The answer is the facet for the dens, a small oval surface. It faces backward into the ring of the atlas. The dens turns against it whenever the head rotates.

Question 1006. Which statement about the superior articular facets of the atlas is correct: they are flat and face upward, they are concave and face upward and medially, they are convex and face downward, or they face sideways? The answer is that they are concave and face upward and medially. They receive the occipital condyles and form the joint that nods the head. The inferior facets face downward and receive the axis.

Question 1007. A constructed illustration: a fall onto the top of the head breaks the ring of the atlas in two places, one at the front and one at the back. Which two parts of the bone are described? The answer is the anterior arch and the posterior arch. The ring gives way at its thinnest points, where the arches join the thick lateral masses.

Question 1008. True or false: the atlas has a spinous process that can be felt in the midline at the back of the neck. The answer is false. The atlas has no spinous process, because its ring is closed behind by the posterior arch. A small posterior tubercle marks the midline of that arch instead.

Question 1009. Which small bony knob marks the middle of the back of the atlas and stands in place of a spinous process? The answer is the posterior tubercle. It projects backward from the posterior arch and can be felt through the muscles at the top of the neck. The rest of that arch is smooth and rounded.

Question 1010. Which structure passes through the transverse foramen of the atlas: the spinal cord, the vertebral artery, the dens of the axis, or the eighth cervical nerve? The answer is the vertebral artery. It enters the foramen from below and leaves it to lie in a groove on the upper surface of the posterior arch. The vertebral vein runs with it.

Question 1011. A described dried atlas shows a deep groove on the upper surface of the posterior arch, just behind each lateral mass. What is that groove for? The answer is the vertebral artery. The artery leaves the transverse foramen and winds backward and medially in the groove before it enters the skull.

Question 1012. After it leaves the transverse foramen of the atlas, where does the vertebral artery lie, and through which opening does it reach the skull? The answer is that it lies in the groove on the posterior arch and enters through the foramen magnum. It pierces the membrane behind the atlas on the way. The artery never passes through the ring of the atlas.

Question 1013. Which of these pairs belongs together: the atlas with the dens, the axis with the dens, the atlas with a body, or the axis with the occipital condyles? The answer is the axis with the dens. The dens is the upward projection of the body of the second cervical vertebra. The atlas has no body and receives the dens in its ring.

Question 1014. Which movement takes place at the joints between the occipital condyles and the atlas: nodding the head, turning the head from side to side, opening the mouth, or shrugging the shoulders? The answer is nodding the head. The curved facets of that joint allow the head to bend forward and backward. Turning the head takes place at the joint below.

Question 1015. A constructed illustration: a blow to the head fractures the anterior arch of the atlas. Which structure lies immediately behind the broken arch and is at risk? The answer is the dens of the axis. It sits directly behind the anterior arch inside the ring. The transverse ligament lies behind the dens and in front of the cord.

Question 1016. Name the two arches that join the lateral masses of the atlas and complete its ring. The answer is the anterior arch and the posterior arch. The anterior arch carries the facet for the dens on its inner surface. The posterior arch carries a groove for the vertebral artery.

Question 1017. A described computed tomography section of the upper neck at the level of the first cervical vertebra shows a bony ring with two thick side masses. Which two structures lie within that ring? The answer is the dens of the axis and the spinal cord. The dens lies in the front part of the ring and the cord behind it.

Question 1018. Which statement about the transverse processes of the atlas is correct: they are the widest in the cervical region, they carry prominent front and back knobs, they are missing altogether, or they carry facets for the first ribs? The answer is that they are the widest in the cervical region. They can be felt between the mastoid process and the angle of the jaw. Their foramen carries the vertebral artery.

Question 1019. Which statement about the joints of the atlas is correct: it joins the occipital condyles above and the axis below, it joins the axis above and the skull below, it joins the third cervical vertebra below, or it forms no joint at all? The answer is that it joins the occipital condyles above and the axis below. It is the only vertebra with no disc above it and no disc below it.

Question 1020. Which part of the atlas gives attachment to the anterior longitudinal ligament and can be felt through the back wall of the pharynx? The answer is the anterior tubercle. It is the small knob in the middle of the front of the anterior arch. The long ligament that runs down the front of the bodies begins on it.

Question 1021. Which process rises from the body of the second cervical vertebra and acts as a pivot for the head? The answer is the dens, also called the odontoid process. It stands upward inside the ring of the atlas. The transverse ligament of the atlas passes behind it and holds it in place.

Question 1022. The dens of the axis lies immediately behind which structure: the posterior arch of the atlas, the anterior arch of the atlas, the spinal cord, or the occipital condyle? The answer is the anterior arch of the atlas. The facet on the back of that arch meets the front of the dens. The spinal cord lies behind the dens, not in front of it.

Question 1023. A described radiograph of the open mouth view of the upper neck shows a central peg of bone with a block on each side of it at the same level. Which vertebra is shown? The answer is the axis. The peg is the dens and the two blocks are its superior articular facets. The atlas lies above it as a ring without a body.

Question 1024. The transverse ligament of the atlas spans the gap between which two structures? The answer is the two lateral masses of the atlas. It runs behind the dens and turns the front of the ring into a socket for it. The spinal cord lies behind the ligament.

Question 1025. Which spinous process is the first one felt in the midline of the back of the neck below the skull? The answer is the spinous process of the axis. It is large and split into two points, and it is the first spine below the external occipital protuberance. The atlas above it has no spine to feel.

Question 1026. Which statement about the superior articular facets of the axis is correct: they face upward and laterally to meet the atlas, they face downward to meet the third cervical vertebra, they receive the occipital condyles, or they carry the dens? The answer is that they face upward and laterally and meet the atlas. They are flat surfaces, not the curved sockets of the atlas above them.

Question 1027. A constructed illustration: a sudden forced turn of the head drives the dens backward against the spinal cord. Which ligament normally prevents that movement? The answer is the transverse ligament of the atlas. It holds the dens forward against the anterior arch. When that band gives way, the dens can press on the cord.

Question 1028. True or false: the axis has a body and a spinous process. The answer is true. Its body carries the dens above it, and the large spinous process projects backward and can be felt. It is the atlas above it that lacks both a body and a spine.

Question 1029. Which smooth surface on the front of the dens of the axis articulates with the atlas? The answer is the anterior articular facet. It meets the facet on the back of the anterior arch. There is a second facet behind, where the transverse ligament runs.

Question 1030. At which joint does most of the rotation of the head take place: between the occipital condyles and the atlas, between the atlas and the axis, between the second and third cervical vertebrae, or between the skull and the temporal bone? The answer is between the atlas and the axis. About one half of the turning of the head happens there. The joint above it nods instead.

Question 1031. A described dried axis seen from behind shows a large spine with two points and a rounded peg rising above the body. Which bone is shown? The answer is the axis, the second cervical vertebra. The two points belong to its split spinous process. The peg above the body is the dens, which the atlas turns around.

Question 1032. The alar ligaments run from the upper part of the dens to which structures? The answer is the margins of the foramen magnum. They pass upward and outward, one on each side. They check rotation of the head and are slack in the middle position.

Question 1033. Which of these pairs belongs together: the dens with the transverse ligament of the atlas, the dens with the supraspinous ligament, the atlas with the alar ligament, or the axis with the ligamentum nuchae? The answer is the dens with the transverse ligament of the atlas. That ligament crosses behind the dens and holds it against the front of the ring. The other pairs name structures that never meet.

Question 1034. The median joint between the atlas and the axis lies between the dens and which two structures: the anterior arch and the transverse ligament, the posterior arch and the ligamentum flavum, the occipital condyles and the tectorial membrane, or the two lateral masses and the alar ligaments? The answer is the anterior arch and the transverse ligament. The dens turns between them like a pivot in a ring.

Question 1035. A constructed illustration: a radiograph of a child of about six years shows a small separate piece of bone at the very tip of the dens. What is the most likely explanation? The answer is a normal ossicle at the apex of the dens. It is a separate center of bone that usually joins the dens by about the twelfth year. Such a piece is a common variation.

Question 1036. About which axis does the turning of the head between the atlas and the axis take place, and in which plane is the movement seen? The answer is a longitudinal axis, with the movement in the transverse plane. The dens forms the pivot and the atlas turns around it. The two lateral joints move with it.

Question 1037. A described computed tomography section at the level of the atlas shows the dens in the middle of the ring with a band passing directly behind it. Which band is described? The answer is the transverse ligament of the atlas. It runs from one lateral mass to the other behind the dens. It is the strongest part of the cruciate ligament.

Question 1038. Which statement about the axis is correct: its spinous process is large and split and is the first felt below the skull, its spinous process is small and single, it has no body, or it has no transverse processes? The answer is that its spinous process is large and split and is the first felt below the skull. The atlas above it has no spine at all.

Question 1039. Which statement about the dens is correct: it is held against the anterior arch of the atlas by the transverse ligament, it is held by the ligamentum flavum, it belongs to the atlas, or it carries the occipital condyles? The answer is that it is held against the anterior arch by the transverse ligament. Without that band it could move backward toward the cord.

Question 1040. Which small ligament runs from the apex of the dens to the front margin of the foramen magnum? The answer is the apical ligament. It is a slender band in the midline, hidden between the alar ligaments. It carries little strength and is a remnant of the notochord.

Question 1041. What is the name of the tube that runs down the column from the skull to the sacrum, formed by the vertebral foramina of successive vertebrae? The answer is the vertebral canal. Each vertebra adds its own opening to the tube. The tube ends below at the sacral hiatus.

Question 1042. Which statement about the walls of the vertebral canal is correct: the bodies and discs lie in front and the laminae behind, the bodies lie behind and the laminae in front, the transverse processes form its floor, or it is bounded only by ligaments? The answer is that the bodies and the discs lie in front and the laminae behind. The pedicles form the sides of the canal.

Question 1043. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the lower back shows a bony tube with a cord inside it, wrapped in a dark sheath and lying free in fluid. What is the tube, and what runs in it? The answer is the vertebral canal, with the spinal cord inside it. The dark sheath is the dura mater and the fluid is cerebrospinal fluid.

Question 1044. The spinal cord lies inside the vertebral canal. Which three coverings and which fluid surround it there? The answer is the dura mater, the arachnoid mater and the pia mater, with cerebrospinal fluid between them. The fluid fills the space deep to the arachnoid. Outside the dura lie the fat and the veins of the epidural space.

Question 1045. What is the name of the space between the dura mater and the wall of the vertebral canal? The answer is the epidural space. It surrounds the dura from the foramen magnum down to the sacral hiatus. It contains fat and a network of veins.

Question 1046. Which statement about the contents of the epidural space is correct: it holds fat and the internal vertebral venous plexus, it holds cerebrospinal fluid, it holds the spinal cord, or it is empty? The answer is that it holds fat and the internal vertebral venous plexus. The plexus drains the vertebrae and has no valves. The fluid and the cord lie inside the dura.

Question 1047. A constructed illustration: a needle is placed in the lower back and its tip comes to rest outside the dura but inside the canal. Which space has it entered, and what lies there? The answer is the epidural space, which holds fat and the internal vertebral venous plexus. The needle has not passed the dura. Any such procedure belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol.

Question 1048. True or false: the vertebral canal carries the spinal cord and the roots of the spinal nerves. The answer is true. The cord fills the upper part of the canal, and its roots leave it through the intervertebral foramina on each side. Below the end of the cord the roots continue as the cauda equina.

Question 1049. Which opening forms the lower end of the sacral canal and gives access to it from behind? The answer is the sacral hiatus. It lies where the laminae of the lowest sacral vertebra fail to meet in the midline. It is closed by a ligament rather than by bone.

Question 1050. Which statement about the shape of the vertebral canal is correct: it is triangular in the cervical and lumbar regions, it is round throughout, it is triangular only in the thoracic region, or it is oval in the lumbar region? The answer is that it is triangular in the cervical and lumbar regions. The thoracic part of the canal is smaller and more rounded. The cord is thinnest there.

Question 1051. A described transverse section through the middle of the neck shows a triangular bony canal with a round cord inside it and a large body in front. Which region of the column is shown? The answer is the cervical region. The triangular shape and the thick body in front are typical of the neck. The cord here is wide because the nerves of the upper limb arise at this level.

Question 1052. Below the level at which the spinal cord ends, which structures occupy the vertebral canal? The answer is the roots of the lower spinal nerves, gathered as the cauda equina. They descend in the fluid inside the dura. Each one leaves through its own intervertebral foramen.

Question 1053. Which of these pairs belongs together: the epidural space with fat and veins, the subarachnoid space with fat and veins, the vertebral canal with the trachea, or the sacral canal with the spinal cord? The answer is the epidural space with fat and veins. It lies outside the dura, between that membrane and the wall of the canal. The subarachnoid space holds cerebrospinal fluid instead.

Question 1054. At about which level does the spinal cord end in the adult column: the twelfth thoracic vertebra, the first lumbar vertebra, the third lumbar vertebra, or the sacrum? The answer is the first lumbar vertebra. The cord tapers there into the conus medullaris. In a newborn it ends one or two vertebrae lower.

Question 1055. A constructed illustration: an older person has a narrow canal in the lower back, and the roots inside it are pressed. Which space has narrowed, and what does it normally contain? The answer is the vertebral canal, which normally contains the cauda equina and its coverings. Bone and thickened ligament have closed in on it. Any treatment belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 1056. Name two structures that form the posterior wall of the vertebral canal. The answer is the laminae of the vertebrae and the ligamentum flavum between them. The laminae are the flat plates that meet in the midline. The ligament joins them from one vertebra to the next.

Question 1057. A described CT section of the neck at the level of the first cervical vertebra shows a wide canal with a small peg in its front part. What is the peg, and what does the rest of the canal hold? The answer is the dens of the axis, with the spinal cord behind it. The canal at this level is the widest in the column.

Question 1058. Which statement explains why the vertebral canal is widest in the cervical region: the cervical cord is thickest because the nerves of the upper limb arise there, the cervical vertebrae are the largest, the canal carries the vertebral artery, or the canal holds the dens? The answer is that the cervical cord is thickest because the nerves of the upper limb arise there. The ring must be wide enough for that swelling.

Question 1059. At which level can the canal of the lower back be expected to hold roots and no cord in a healthy adult: at the twelfth thoracic vertebra, below the first lumbar vertebra, at the fourth lumbar vertebra, or at the sacrum? The answer is below the first lumbar vertebra. The cord ends there, and its roots continue downward to their own foramina.

Question 1060. Which part of a vertebra forms the lateral wall of the vertebral canal and carries the notch that helps to bound the intervertebral foramen? The answer is the pedicle. It runs backward from the body to meet the lamina. The notch on its lower border is part of that opening.

Question 1061. Which two notches come together to form the intervertebral foramen? The answer is the inferior vertebral notch of the vertebra above and the superior vertebral notch of the vertebra below. Each notch is a smooth curve on the border of a pedicle. The two vertebrae together close the opening.

Question 1062. Which of these structures passes through the intervertebral foramen: the spinal nerve, the spinal cord, the vertebral artery, or the ligamentum flavum? The answer is the spinal nerve. It leaves the canal through the foramen on its way to the body wall and the limbs. The vertebral artery runs through the transverse foramina of the neck instead.

Question 1063. A described pair of dried vertebrae held together shows an opening between the two pedicles, with the disc in front of it and a joint behind it. What is the opening, and what is its front wall? The answer is the intervertebral foramen, with the disc and the adjoining bodies in front of it. The joint behind it belongs to the articular facets.

Question 1064. What forms the front wall of the intervertebral foramen? The answer is the intervertebral disc together with the adjoining parts of the two vertebral bodies. The disc lies directly in front of the emerging nerve. Its backward bulge can therefore narrow the opening.

Question 1065. Which swelling on the spinal nerve lies within the intervertebral foramen? The answer is the dorsal root ganglion. It is the rounded enlargement on the sensory root as the nerve passes through the foramen. It is one reason a disc that bulges backward presses on sensation first.

Question 1066. Within the intervertebral foramen, where does the spinal nerve lie: above the disc, below the disc, behind the disc, or through the disc? The answer is above the disc. The nerve occupies the upper part of the foramen, close to the pedicle above. The disc forms the lower and front part of the opening.

Question 1067. A constructed illustration: a disc between the fourth and fifth lumbar vertebrae bulges backward and outward. Which nerve root is compressed? The answer is the fifth lumbar root. That root is crossing the disc on its way to the foramen below. The fourth lumbar root has already left the canal at a higher level.

Question 1068. True or false: the first cervical nerve leaves the canal above the first cervical vertebra, and the eighth cervical nerve leaves below the seventh. The answer is true. The eighth cervical nerve has no vertebra of its own, so it leaves below the seventh. This is why the neck has eight nerves and seven vertebrae.

Question 1069. Which blood vessels run through the intervertebral foramen with the spinal nerve? The answer is the spinal artery and the intervertebral veins. The artery is a small branch of a segmental vessel and follows the nerve root. The veins drain the cord and its coverings into the plexus outside.

Question 1070. Which structure forms the posterior wall of the intervertebral foramen: the capsule of the joint between the articular facets, the intervertebral disc, the ligamentum flavum, or the pedicle? The answer is the capsule of the joint between the articular facets. That joint lies behind the nerve. When its edges enlarge with age, they can narrow the opening.

Question 1071. A described radiograph of the neck in side view shows a row of oval openings between the pedicles, one above each disc. What are those openings, and what do they contain? The answer is the intervertebral foramina, containing the spinal nerves. They lie between the pedicles of neighboring vertebrae. Their shape in the neck is oval rather than round.

Question 1072. In the cervical region, does a spinal nerve leave the canal above or below the pedicle of the vertebra of the same number? The answer is above it. The first cervical nerve leaves above the first cervical vertebra. The rule holds down to the seventh, after which the eighth nerve leaves below.

Question 1073. Which of these pairs belongs together: the intervertebral foramen with the spinal nerve, the vertebral foramen with the spinal nerve, the transverse foramen with the spinal cord, or the sacral hiatus with the dens? The answer is the intervertebral foramen with the spinal nerve. Each nerve leaves the canal through the foramen of its own level. The vertebral foramen holds the cord.

Question 1074. In the lumbar region, where does a spinal nerve leave the canal: above the pedicle of the vertebra of the same number, below it, through it, or behind the joint? The answer is below it. The fifth lumbar nerve passes below the pedicle of the fifth lumbar vertebra. This is the opposite of the rule in the neck.

Question 1075. A constructed illustration: an older person has pain along one leg, and imaging shows a foramen narrowed by bone at its back and by a disc at its front. Which two structures have closed the opening? The answer is the joint between the articular facets and the intervertebral disc. The facet joint forms the back wall and the disc the front wall. Any treatment belongs to the treating clinician.

Question 1076. Which small nerve runs back into the vertebral canal through the intervertebral foramen to supply the disc and the ligaments? The answer is the recurrent meningeal nerve, also called the sinuvertebral nerve. It branches from the spinal nerve just outside the foramen. It carries sensation from the outer fibers of the disc.

Question 1077. A described computed tomography scan of the lower back shows a foramen shaped like a keyhole and narrowed from behind by bone. Which structure has grown into the opening? The answer is the joint between the articular facets. Its enlarged edges press on the nerve in front of them. The disc may narrow the same opening from the other side.

Question 1078. The nerves of the neck leave the canal above their own vertebra and those of the lower back leave below it. What single difference in growth explains this? The answer is that the column grows longer than the spinal cord. The lower roots must therefore run downward inside the canal before they reach their foramina. In the neck the roots lie almost at the level of their own openings.

Question 1079. Why does a disc that bulges backward and outward in the lower back press on the root that leaves at the level below rather than on its own root? The answer is that the root of its own level has already left the canal above the disc, while the next root is still crossing it. The bulging disc therefore meets the traversing root.

Question 1080. Which structure forms the upper boundary of the intervertebral foramen: the inferior vertebral notch of the vertebra above, the superior vertebral notch of the vertebra below, the intervertebral disc, or the joint between the facets? The answer is the inferior vertebral notch of the vertebra above. The superior notch of the vertebra below forms the lower boundary.

Question 1081. Which long ligament runs down the front of the vertebral bodies and discs from the base of the skull to the sacrum? The answer is the anterior longitudinal ligament. It is broad and strong and is firmly attached to the front of each disc. It checks backward bending of the column.

Question 1082. Which statement about the posterior longitudinal ligament is correct: it runs inside the canal on the back of the bodies and discs, it runs on the tips of the spinous processes, it lies outside the canal, or it joins the transverse processes? The answer is that it runs inside the canal on the back of the bodies and discs. It lies between the bone and the dura. It checks forward bending.

Question 1083. A described dried specimen shows a series of short yellow bands bridging the gaps between the laminae of neighboring vertebrae from the second cervical vertebra downward. Which ligament is shown? The answer is the ligamentum flavum. Its color comes from elastic fibers. It forms the posterior wall of the vertebral canal.

Question 1084. Which structure lies immediately behind the ligamentum flavum, outside the vertebral canal: the deep muscles of the back, the spinal cord, the epidural fat and veins, or the dura mater? The answer is the deep muscles of the back. Fat and vessels lie between them and the bone. The ligament is the deepest layer of the back wall.

Question 1085. Which ligament runs along the tips of the spinous processes from the seventh cervical vertebra down to the sacrum? The answer is the supraspinous ligament. It links the spines in the midline and is felt as a firm cord in the lower back. In the neck it is replaced by a broader sheet.

Question 1086. Which sheet of elastic tissue takes the place of the supraspinous ligament in the neck and gives attachment to muscles: the ligamentum nuchae, the ligamentum flavum, the interspinous ligament, or the posterior longitudinal ligament? The answer is the ligamentum nuchae. It runs from the skull down to the spines of the cervical vertebrae. It checks forward bending of the head.

Question 1087. A constructed illustration: a neck is forced suddenly into full backward bending. Which long ligament on the front of the bodies is stretched and may tear? The answer is the anterior longitudinal ligament. It lies against the front of the discs and bodies. Behind the torn ligament lie the disc and the body of the vertebra.

Question 1088. True or false: the ligamentum flavum is made largely of elastic fibers and helps the column to straighten after it has been bent forward. The answer is true. Its elastic recoil assists the return to the upright position. That same elasticity lets it stretch and fold instead of buckling.

Question 1089. Which membrane closes the space between the occipital bone above and the posterior arch of the atlas below? The answer is the posterior atlanto-occipital membrane. It is pierced by the vertebral artery. It takes the place of the ligamentum flavum at that level.

Question 1090. The tectorial membrane inside the skull is the upward continuation of which ligament: the posterior longitudinal ligament, the anterior longitudinal ligament, the ligamentum flavum, or the ligamentum nuchae? The answer is the posterior longitudinal ligament. It runs up over the dens and into the cranial cavity. It is broad above and narrow below.

Question 1091. A described magnetic resonance imaging scan of the neck shows a dark band lying along the back of the vertebral bodies inside the canal, with a clear space between it and the cord. Which structure is the band? The answer is the posterior longitudinal ligament. The space in front of the cord is filled with cerebrospinal fluid. The ligament lies between the bone and that fluid.

Question 1092. Which ligament bridges the gap between the transverse processes of two neighboring vertebrae? The answer is the intertransverse ligament. It runs from one transverse process to the next on each side. It is best marked in the lumbar region and checks sideways bending.

Question 1093. Which of these pairs belongs together: the ligamentum flavum with the laminae, the ligamentum flavum with the bodies, the anterior longitudinal ligament with the spines, or the supraspinous ligament with the discs? The answer is the ligamentum flavum with the laminae. It bridges the gap between the flat plates of neighboring vertebrae. The anterior longitudinal ligament runs on the bodies, not on the spines.

Question 1094. Which of these ligaments checks backward bending of the vertebral column: the anterior longitudinal ligament, the posterior longitudinal ligament, the ligamentum flavum, or the interspinous ligament? The answer is the anterior longitudinal ligament. It lies on the front of the bodies, where backward bending stretches it. The other three lie behind the bodies and check forward bending.

Question 1095. A constructed illustration: a fall forces the trunk into strong forward bending and the spines of the lower back are pulled apart. Which ligaments are stretched in that movement? The answer is the supraspinous and interspinous ligaments, together with the ligamentum flavum. All of them lie behind the bodies. The anterior longitudinal ligament is slack in that position.

Question 1096. Which band of the vertebral column is narrow in the lower back and leaves the outer part of the disc uncovered? The answer is the posterior longitudinal ligament. It is wide in the neck and narrow in the lumbar region. Because of this, a disc can bulge outward around its edge.

Question 1097. A described dissection of the back of the neck shows a broad elastic sheet in the midline between the skull above and the cervical spines below, with muscle attached to both of its surfaces. What is the sheet? The answer is the ligamentum nuchae. It is the thickened continuation of the supraspinous ligament above the seventh cervical vertebra.

Question 1098. Why does a lumbar disc bulge backward and outward rather than straight backward: the posterior longitudinal ligament is narrow there, the anterior longitudinal ligament is weak there, the ligamentum flavum pushes the disc outward, or the disc has no outer ring? The answer is that the posterior longitudinal ligament is narrow there. It covers only the middle of the disc. Its outer part is covered only thinly.

Question 1099. Name two ligaments that lie behind the vertebral bodies and check forward bending of the column. The answer is the posterior longitudinal ligament and the ligamentum flavum. The supraspinous and interspinous ligaments help as well. All of them are stretched when the trunk bends forward.

Question 1100. Which statement about the cruciate ligament of the atlas is correct: it is the transverse ligament of the atlas with bands above and below it, it is the two alar ligaments, it is another name for the ligamentum flavum, or it lies outside the vertebral canal? The answer is the transverse ligament of the atlas with bands above and below it. It lies inside the canal, behind the dens.

To summarize, the atlas is a bony ring with no body and no spinous process, carrying the skull on its concave superior facets and receiving the dens of the axis in the front of its ring. The axis carries the dens upward and is the first vertebra whose spine can be felt in the midline below the skull, and the transverse ligament of the atlas holds the dens against the anterior arch while most rotation of the head takes place between these two vertebrae. The vertebral canal is formed by the vertebral foramina of successive vertebrae and holds the spinal cord, its coverings, cerebrospinal fluid, the roots of the spinal nerves, and, in the epidural space, fat and the internal vertebral venous plexus. Each intervertebral foramen lies between the notches of two neighboring pedicles and transmits the spinal nerve, the spinal artery, the intervertebral veins, and the recurrent meningeal nerve, with the intervertebral disc forming its front wall. The ligaments of the column, from the anterior and posterior longitudinal ligaments to the ligamentum flavum, the supraspinous and interspinous ligaments, the ligamentum nuchae and the ligaments of the dens, bind the vertebrae together and check their movements.
