# Anatomy — A Narrated Question and Answer Bank — Style Contract

This file is the binding contract for every chapter of *Anatomy — A Narrated Question
and Answer Bank*. It overrides habits, preferences, and anything else you have read.
Each chapter is written by a different author and read aloud by the same voice, so the
rules below are what make one hundred and nine chapters sound like one bank.

## What this book is

A bank of eleven thousand six hundred anatomy questions, written to be answered out
loud. It is not a textbook read aloud: it is a question, the answer, and the reason for
the answer, in that order, for every item, from the first question of chapter one to
the last question of chapter one hundred and nine.

The listener is a student or a clinician revising anatomy by ear: walking, driving,
at the gym, or between patients. Nothing can be re-read, no option can be scanned, no
diagram can be consulted, and nothing can be highlighted. If an item only works when
seen on a page, it does not belong in this bank. There are no figures, no tables, and no
image references anywhere in it.

The bank follows the twenty categories of its source blueprint, in order: general
anatomy, the skeleton, the joints, the muscles, the heart and vessels, the lymphatic
system, the nervous system, the respiratory, digestive, urinary, reproductive and
endocrine systems, the special senses, regional anatomy, surface and clinical anatomy,
cross-sectional and imaging anatomy, embryology, anatomical relationships, clinical
anatomy, and the integrated questions that close the bank.

## The one rule that matters most

**Every question is answered immediately, in the same paragraph, before the next
question begins.** The listener must never have to hold an answer in mind, and must
never have to wait for an answer key. The shape of every single item is:

1. the question, with its options spoken inside the sentence when it has options;
2. the words **The answer is** followed by the correct answer;
3. two to four sentences saying why that answer is right, and, where it helps, why the
   tempting wrong answer is wrong.

That three-part shape is the whole book. It never varies.

## Contract of style

- One heading per file, exactly the heading given in the chapter brief, in the form
  `# Chapter N — Title`. Nothing else in the file may be a heading.
- After the heading comes one short opening paragraph, with no heading of its own, of
  three to six sentences: what this chapter covers, how many questions it holds, and a
  reminder that the answer follows each question.
- Then the questions, in the order the brief gives, numbered continuously from the
  number the brief states. Question numbers are digits: `Question 407.`
- One question per paragraph. Each question paragraph is three to six sentences and
  between about forty-five and seventy-five words. No question paragraph may run past
  eight lines on the page; a question that needs more than that is two questions.
- The last paragraph of the chapter begins with exactly `To summarize, ` and carries no
  heading. It is three to six sentences that restate the highest-yield facts the chapter
  tested — the facts, not the questions.
- Prose only. No bullet points, no numbered lists, no lettered options on their own
  lines, no tables, no arrows, no markdown emphasis, no footnotes, no citations, no web
  addresses, and no references to figures, tables or boxes.
- No question may refer to another question, to a chapter number, or to anything the
  listener cannot hear in this chapter. A chapter is listened to on its own.
- Vary the openings of the questions. One hundred questions that all begin "Which
  structure" would be heard as one long question. Vary the stems, the structures and the
  order in which options are spoken, and keep the correct answer from drifting to the
  same spoken position every time.
- Never write the question number as a list marker or start a line with a numeral
  followed by a full stop, because the build check reads that as a numbered list.
  `Question 407.` is fine because the line begins with a word.

## The six question types

The chapter brief states how many of each type the chapter must contain. Match those
counts, and vary the phrasing within each type.

- **Identification.** Name the structure, the bone, the muscle, the vessel or the nerve
  described. One question, one answer, one reason.
- **Multiple choice.** Speak four options inside the question sentence, then give the
  answer. The three wrong options must be plausible and must be the ones a student
  actually confuses, not filler. Never give the answer by its position in the list, and
  never say "option B": name the answer, because a listener cannot see a letter.
- **Verbal image description.** The source blueprint marks a large group of questions as
  image-based. Nothing can be shown, so every one of them is written as a description
  instead: describe what the radiograph, the section or the specimen would show, and ask
  which structure or which interpretation fits. Never write "in the figure", "as shown",
  or "in the image above".
- **Relationships.** Which structure lies immediately medial to, deep to, anterior to,
  or supplied by another. These test the three-dimensional map, so they must be answered
  with directions, not with a diagram.
- **Clinical anatomy.** A short constructed scenario that asks which structure is
  injured, compressed or involved. The scenario is an illustration and must be described
  as one; it is never a real patient and never an identifiable case.
- **True or false, matching, short answer and spatial reasoning.** The remaining types.
  True or false states the proposition, then answers with true or false and the reason.
  Matching asks which of four named pairs belongs together. Short answer asks for a name
  or a list of two or three structures, spoken as a sentence. Spatial reasoning asks about
  planes, axes, sections and the position of one structure relative to another.

## Difficulty

The brief gives the mix of basic, intermediate, advanced and expert items. Honour it.

- **Basic** is single-fact recall: which bone, which nerve, which chamber, which vessel.
- **Intermediate** needs two steps: a structure identified from its relations, or a
  function inferred from a feature.
- **Advanced** integrates several structures, or turns on a less common detail, a
  variation, or a distinction students routinely get wrong.
- **Expert** is applied reasoning: a discriminating clinical correlation, a
  developmental explanation for an adult arrangement, or a question whose wrong answers
  are all defensible until the last step.

An advanced question is not a basic question with longer words. It is a question that
cannot be answered by recognising a single term.

## Numbers, units and symbols

- Numbers are digits. Units are always written out in full: "5 centimeters", "12
  millimeters", "2 liters", "70 beats per minute".
- Never a unit abbreviation: not cm, mm, kg, mL, mg, or any other.
- Ordinals and centuries in words: "the fourth cranial nerve", "the nineteenth century".
  Never a Roman numeral standing alone, and never a figure such as "4th". Write "cranial
  nerve IV" nowhere in this bank: the build check reads IV, IM and PO as abbreviated drug
  routes, so the cranial nerves are always named by their ordinal word.
- Fractions as words: "one half", "one third", "two thirds". Ranges use "to": "8 to 12
  centimeters". Ratios are spoken: "one in three".
- Symbols are never used: no per cent sign, equals, plus, greater than, less than,
  arrow, degree sign, ampersand, or slash used to mean "or". Write "per cent", "equals",
  "leads to", "greater than", "less than", "and".
- Latin letters, ordinary punctuation, and the em dash in the heading only. Never
  Cyrillic, Greek, or mathematical characters.

## Acronyms, short forms and spelling

A synthesized voice reads an uppercase token as a sequence of letters, which is
sometimes what a clinician would say and sometimes nonsense.

- The only short forms this bank tolerates are **CT** and **MRI**.
- Spell everything else out at its first mention in every chapter, and prefer the full
  form throughout: "central nervous system", "peripheral nervous system", "autonomic
  nervous system", "cerebrospinal fluid", "cranial nerve", "spinal nerve", "computed
  tomography" (after which CT is allowed), "magnetic resonance imaging" (after which MRI
  is allowed), "anterior cruciate ligament", "temporomandibular joint", "gastrointestinal
  tract", "range of motion".
- Never write CNS, PNS, ANS, CSF, CN, GI, GU, ENT, TMJ, ACL, PCL, MCL, LCL, ROM, IV, IM,
  PO, or any other invented short form. A chapter is listened to on its own, so the full
  term is spelled out again in each chapter, however often it appeared in the one before.
- Use AMERICAN spelling throughout, never British: "hemorrhage", "edema", "anemia",
  "tumor", "color", "behavior", "center", "defense", "pediatric", "fetal", "fibers",
  "esophagus", "anesthesia", "gray matter", "mold".

## Accuracy and safety rules

These rules are not decoration. A question bank that teaches a wrong relation or a wrong
nerve will be believed, because it is being memorised.

- **Every item must be anatomically correct**, including the three wrong options, which
  must be wrong for the stated reason and not merely unlikely.
- **No drug, no dose, no route, no frequency, no treatment.** This bank teaches anatomy.
  Where a clinical correlation touches management, name the structure involved and say
  that the management belongs to the treating clinician and the local protocol. A
  listener must not be able to take a prescription from this book.
- **No numbers that a listener might act on.** Measurements are given only to describe
  normal anatomy, and any figure must be the ordinary textbook range, spoken as an
  illustration of normal structure.
- **Variation is normal.** Where a structure has a common variant, say so in the reason
  rather than teaching the variant as the rule.
- **Describe the construction, not a real case.** No real patient and no identifiable
  case, and clinical scenarios must be described as illustrations.
- **This is educational material.** It does not replace an anatomy course, a dissection,
  a clinical training programme or the judgment of a teacher, and no chapter may suggest
  otherwise.

## Length

- The chapter must contain exactly the number of questions its brief states. Every
  chapter in this bank holds one hundred questions.
- Its word count must land between three thousand two hundred and seven thousand five
  hundred words, which is what the build check enforces. Aim for five thousand nine
  hundred to six thousand five hundred, and never exceed seven thousand three hundred.
  A chapter of one hundred questions is about six thousand two hundred words, which is
  roughly sixty words for each question including its options, its answer and its reason.
- Write the file once, carefully. Do not rewrite, restructure, or trim it afterwards.
